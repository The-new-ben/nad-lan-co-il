Israel's Discounted-Apartment Lottery (Dira BeHanacha, formerly Mechir LaMishtaken): Can You Use It?

Dira BeHanacha ("discounted apartment") is the government housing programme of the Ministry of Construction and Housing and the Israel Land Authority. Developers build on state land and sell part of the flats to eligible buyers at a reduced price; the buyers' place in line is set by a lottery. Mechir LaMishtaken ("price for the resident") is the programme's earlier and still widely used name, and today it is one of four tracks inside it, next to Mechir Matara (target price), Mechir Mufchat (reduced price) and Dira LeHaskir (long-term rental).

A foreign buyer cannot use it. The programme is for Israeli households without a home: married or cohabiting couples, couples marrying within three months, singles from 35, a parent with a child under 21, and a few more groups listed by the ministry. An Israeli citizen married to a foreign citizen is eligible with a sworn declaration. The ministry's eligibility page sets no separate rule for new immigrants: the same family-status and home-ownership tests apply, checked against the Population Authority record, so a new immigrant should confirm the case with a registration company before relying on it. Owning more than a third of a home in the last three years makes you a "housing improver", who may register only for continuation lotteries.
