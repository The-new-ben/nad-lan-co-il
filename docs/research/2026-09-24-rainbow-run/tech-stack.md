**Method.** Read-only web research (WebSearch, WebFetch), plain GETs of public HTML/JS, HEAD requests for file sizes, the npm/PyPI/GitHub registries for versions (all checked 24.9.2026), and a read of our own repo. No browser pane, no Chrome extension, no sign-ups.

- **[doc]**: an official or vendor documentation page I read.

## Summary (15 lines)

1. **Pre-rendering wins on phones.** The leaders that look cinematic on a phone pre-render. Plyo says its views are "pre-rendered at final quality" from 3ds Max, Blender, Corona or V-Ray, at "a few MB" [claim]. The EcoCity configurator is a three.js r186 sphere that shows one pre-rendered equirectangular JPG per room and finish choice, low-res first [code].
2. **Real-time rivals look like games.** Hauzd runs its own C++ engine compiled to WebAssembly: 2.05 MB of wasm plus texture packs for each GPU family [code]. It puts drone 360° photos behind the model [eyes]. Unreal pixel streaming looks best, but it costs about $1.50 to $9 per viewer-hour and needs 5 to 15 Mbit/s [doc, calc].
3. **Our rule:** pre-render whatever the buyer looks at, render live only what the buyer moves through, and turn every choice into masks so the phone only swaps images.
4. **Picking an apartment.** Render 72 Cycles orbit frames for each light (day, dusk), with a unit mask per frame generated by Blender, so any unit can be tapped on any frame. The existing three.js Rainbow stage stays as the free-rotate mode.
5. **Real view, version 1.** A live Google 3D view (Map3DElement) at the exact floor height and bearing, with our models of the approved Sde Dov towers placed in it. Google's imagery still shows the quarter as empty lots [eyes].
6. **Google's terms shape the layout.** No caching and no saved stills. Videos may run 30 s at most and must be marked "for promotional purposes only". Nothing may appear "with or near a non-Google map". So the view opens on its own screen, away from the Mapbox beam [doc]. Cost: $6 per 1,000 visits after 1,000 free (Map Tiles), or $7 after 5,000 free (3D Maps) [doc].
7. **Real photos from every height.** A drone library at 3 to 5 heights for the whole Sde Dov quarter, shot once and shared by every project there. Rainbow sits about 0.5 km from Reading power station, so the flight permit is the first risk [calc; the permit is unverified].
8. **Clickable surroundings.** Our projects become clickable Google 3D models and polygons. Google's mesh has no building objects to click. For our own 3D, the Tel Aviv GIS buildings layer (513) has footprints and roof heights; its licence is still to be confirmed [code].
9. **Walking inside.** Cycles 360° rooms in Photo Sphere Viewer 5.15.1 with a glide between rooms. Finish choices are masked overlays that add up per option instead of rendering every combination, and a real view is composited into the windows. Phase 2 is a walkable Gaussian splat of the same rendered flat.
10. **Facilities** use the same 360° pipeline. The buyer reaches them by tapping the pool or the gym on the orbit frames.
11. **Touring together.** LiveKit is free up to 5,000 participant-minutes a month, then $50 a month buys 150,000 [doc]. The camera syncs over data packets 10 to 15 times a second. This replaces today's REST polling every 1.6 s [code].
12. **The quarter as a world you can move through like a game.** three.js with Blender-baked lighting and Tel Aviv GIS context; camera-controls and GSAP 3.15 for the camera; SunCalc 2.0.2 for the sun; Spark 2.2 splats (500K on phones) for real places captured on foot.
13. **Everything runs on the owner's PC:**
    - Blender 5.2 LTS, rendering with HIP on the Radeon;
    - glTF-Transform 4.5 and KTX2 for web assets;
    - Brush 0.3 to train splats on AMD, then SplatTransform 3.6.4;
    - DaVinci Resolve for films;
    - one Python manifest per project.

    After Rainbow, another project in the same quarter should take about a week [est].
14. **Phone budgets:**
    - first view under 1.5 MB and under 2 s;
    - one orbit set under 12 MB, loaded lazily;
    - a building model under 150k triangles and 2 MB;
    - KTX2 textures up to 2048²;
    - at most 140 draw calls and 500K splats on screen.
15. **Honesty and law.** Every render says הדמיה; every photo shows its height and date; the Google view is named as Google imagery; future towers show their plan status; drone shots avoid neighbours' windows (privacy law s.2(3)); calls are not recorded by default (Amendment 13). A new defect: /earth/ loads Cesium OSM Buildings without a valid ion token, so check it.

**Lesson.**
- **Pre-rendering** gives film quality at a few MB, with no GPU cost per viewer.
- **Live engines** give freedom but look like games.
- **Streaming** gives both, at a price per viewer and a bandwidth need that a public phone page cannot carry.

So we mix all three roles, as the table above shows. Streaming stays optional, for a representative-led session on a sales-lounge screen.

## 2. Technology facts that decide the design

### 2.1 Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles and 3D Maps
- **Coverage at Sde Dov:** present. Our /earth/ page renders it, and the attributions read "Google • CNES / Airbus • Data SIO, NOAA…" [eyes]. The imagery shows the quarter as **empty lots**, so it predates construction. Any "today" view must carry our models of approved towers. Detail is good from a few hundred metres up and soft at street level. Google itself pitches the tiles for "city block to citywide scale" [doc].
- **Two ways in:**
  - (a) **Map Tiles API** plus our own renderer: CesiumJS, as /earth/ does, or 3DTilesRendererJS 0.5.3 in three.js. Enterprise SKU: **1,000 root requests a month free, then $6.00 per 1,000** (0–100k). One root request covers "up to three hours" of tiles; the cap is 10,000 root requests a day [doc, pricing page updated 17.9.2026].
  - (b) **Maps JavaScript 3D Maps** (`gmp-map-3d`, `Map3DElement`), which is GA. "Immersive Maps" Pro SKU: **5,000 a month free, then $7.00 per 1,000** [doc]. It supports `Model3DElement` (glTF), clickable models and `Polygon3DInteractiveElement` (`gmp-click`), `flyCameraTo` and `flyCameraAround` [doc]. It also has `cameraPosition` and `fov` 5–80°, added February 2026 [secondary: spatialized.io].
- **What you cannot click:** the photoreal mesh carries no per-building objects. You click your own overlays (models, polygons) [doc: overlay rule; mesh semantics unverified].
- **Rules** [doc]:
  - Attribution: the Google logo, plus "aggregate, sort, and display in a line, all attributions for displayed tiles".
  - No caching: "must not pre-fetch, index, store, or cache any Content".
  - Overlays: "You may overlay your own 3D objects… as long as the 3D objects aren't extracted, traced, or otherwise derived… from Photorealistic 3D Tiles".
  - Promotional videos: "no more than 30 seconds", "clearly marked, 'for promotional purposes only'", "about the capabilities of your application(s)".
  - Cesium's pass-through terms repeat that you may not use the content "with or near a non-Google map".
  - **Consequences:** no saved stills of the Google view, no Google footage in marketing films, and the Google view must not sit next to the Mapbox beam.
- **EEA:** since 8.7.2025, projects with an EEA billing address get no 3D tiles. Keep the billing account in Israel [doc].
- **Aerial View API** (ready-made flyover videos) covers US addresses only [doc]. Not usable here.

### 2.2 Mapbox (the beam map and today's window view)
- GL JS **3.31.0** is current [npm]. The plugin loads **four different versions**: 3.7.0 (23 references), 3.5.1, 3.6.0 and 3.14.0 [code].
- The Interactions API and featuresets arrived in **3.8** [doc]. With them, the Standard style's `buildings` featureset supports hover and select states (`colorBuildingHighlight` and `colorBuildingSelect`), which do not apply to 3D landmark models [doc].
- Standard style properties [doc]:
  - `lightPreset` takes dawn, day, dusk or night;
  - `show3dBuildings`, `show3dLandmarks`, `show3dFacades`, `show3dTrees`;
  - a directional light and `model-cast-shadows` for glTF models.
- **Price:** 50,000 map loads a month free, then $5.00 per 1,000 (up to 100k) [doc].
- **Attribution:** the Mapbox logo, which may not be restyled, plus "© Mapbox", "© OpenStreetMap" and "Improve this map" [doc].

### 2.3 Cesium ion and OSM Buildings
- **Plans** [doc]:
  - Community: free, "Personal and non-commercial use", 1,000 Google root tiles a month;
  - Commercial: $149 a month;
  - Premium: $499 a month.
- `createOsmBuildingsAsync()` needs your own ion token; the built-in token is for evaluation only [doc].
- In Tel Aviv, OSM building heights are sparse: "only FEW buildings with height data" (2018 thread) [doc, old]. Cesium OSM Buildings would therefore use default heights there. Do not use them for view blocking.

### 2.4 Context data for Tel Aviv
- **Tel Aviv GIS**, `IView2/MapServer/513` "מבנים", polygons [code]. Fields:
  - `ms_komot` (floors);
  - `max_height` (roof) and `min_height` (ground);
  - `gova_simplex_2019`, `dsm_mean`, `dsm_max`;
  - `year`, `shem_mivne`.
- Queries return JSON, GeoJSON or PBF, at most 2,000 records each [code]. **Licence:** the open-data site says data may be shared and used "בהתאם לרישיון השימוש", but I found no licence text for this layer [doc]. Ask the municipality in writing before using it commercially [unverified].
- **Future buildings:** our own massing from approved plans, tagged with the plan number. The GLBs for Dimri Yama, Ashira, Utopia and Einstein already exist (engine-journey §3).

### 2.5 Gaussian splatting in 2026
- **Standard:** `KHR_gaussian_splatting` is "Complete, Ratified by the Khronos Group" [doc].
- **Spark 2.2.0** (World Labs, MIT, three ≥0.180) [doc, npm]:
  - reads PLY, SPZ, SPLAT, KSPLAT and SOG;
  - LoD is new in 2.x: default splat budget "500K on mobile", "1.5 M on desktop"; LoD needs three ≥0.179;
  - `.RAD` streams chunks over HTTP Range requests;
  - it is **834 KB brotli** [code, measured on jsDelivr], so load it only when a splat opens.
- **three.js r186** (8.9.2026) adds a native `GaussianSplat` with loaders for PLY, SPZ, KSPLAT and glTF KHR, with WebGPU and a WebGL fallback [doc]. It has no LoD and no streaming, and it is tiny: "7KB" [claim]. Good for small splats.
- **PlayCanvas SuperSplat** (3.4.2) [doc]:
  - SOG is "15-20× smaller than PLY" [doc];
  - WebGPU doubles splat speed on an iPhone 13 Pro Max: 1M splats at 38 fps on WebGL2 and 78 fps on WebGPU; 4M at 20 and 42 [doc, June 2026].
- **Trainers on AMD:**
  - **Brush** runs on "AMD/Nvidia/Intel cards" through WebGPU and Burn, and reads COLMAP or Nerfstudio data [doc]. Latest release found: v0.3.0 (14.9.2025).
  - **Postshot** is NVIDIA-only [doc, secondary].
  - **RealityScan 2.2** (June 2026) added AMD support on Windows [doc].
- **Splats made from renders** are proven in archviz: Atlux used 1,886 cameras, 6M splats for interaction, and a 3.46 GB PLY at 15M splats. Mirrors "end up smeared" [doc]. Size rule [calc]: a full PLY is 62 floats (248 B) per splat, so 1M splats are about 248 MB as PLY and about 12–17 MB as SOG.

### 2.6 Real-time engines and browsers
- Engine versions [npm]:
  - three.js **0.186.1**, released today; r186 came out 8.9.2026; three core plus module are 194 KB brotli [code];
  - Babylon.js 9.28 (9.0 shipped in March 2026 with splat shadows and SOG);
  - PlayCanvas 2.22.4;
  - Needle Engine 5.1.13 stable.
- We stay on three.js because our code, Spark, 3DTilesRenderer and Photo Sphere Viewer are all three-based.
- **WebGPU** is on by default in Safari 26 (iOS 26) and on Android Chrome with recent hardware [doc, secondary]. Use `WebGLRenderer` now, because Spark targets WebGL2. Moving to WebGPU is an upgrade for later.
- **Memory on iOS:** practitioners report a WebGL heap of about 300–500 MB before the context is lost [secondary, unverified]. KTX2 is the main defence [calc]:
  - one 2048² RGBA8 texture with mipmaps takes 21.3 MiB of GPU memory;
  - ASTC 4×4 takes 5.3 MiB;
  - ETC2 RGB takes 2.7 MiB.

### 2.7 Drone views in Israel
- **Licences:** commercial work needs a CAAI pilot licence and an operator certificate [doc, secondary].
- **Where you can fly:** keep at least 250 m from neighbourhoods and public buildings unless approved. Flying near power stations and airports is forbidden [doc, secondary].
- **War-time rules:** the March 2026 emergency directive allowed licensed commercial flights up to 100 m in line of sight; anything higher needs a NOTAM. The blanket ban was lifted on 30.3 [doc, secondary]. Rainbow's top floors are at about 110–120 m (38 × 3.05 m) [calc].
- **Reading power station:** Wikipedia puts it at 32.10278, 34.77889. Rainbow at 32.103168, 34.784441 is about **525 m** away [calc]. Expect a restriction there; check the DronesIL no-fly map before any plan [unverified].
- **Method** used for US condo sales: drone altitude matched to the unit's finished floor elevation, heading matched, bracketed shots at blue hour [claim].
- **Price:** Israeli price lists show about ₪800 for 2 hours and ₪1,000–3,000 for a full day, before permits [doc, secondary].
- **Privacy:** photographing a person in a private space is a privacy infringement under s.2(3) [doc, secondary]. Shoot outward from the building, never into neighbours' windows, and blur people.

### 2.8 Video and co-browsing
- **LiveKit Cloud** [doc]:
  - Build: $0, 5,000 WebRTC minutes, 100 concurrent users;
  - Ship: $50, 150,000 minutes;
  - Scale: $500;
  - overage $0.0005 a minute; recording $0.02 a minute.
- **LiveKit data packets** [doc]:
  - reliable: in order, 15 KiB at most;
  - lossy: "recommended maximum of 1300 bytes";
  - addressed with `topic` and `destinationIdentities`.
- **Daily:** 10,000 minutes a month free [doc].
- **Twilio Video:** Twilio reversed its end-of-life decision in October 2024, and the SDK is maintained (2.36.0) [doc].
- **Precedent:** Realsee Live Connect, where up to 5 people share voice and a synced tour [claim].
- **Today:** our `inc/cotour.php` pushes the shared state every 1.6 s over REST, with no voice or video [code].

## 3. The recommended stack

### 3.1 How it fits together
1. **Page:** a WordPress project page with DOM UI and i18n. The data payload holds units, prices and deals, and the consent text lives there too.
2. **Hero layer (pre-rendered):** the orbit frames with their unit masks, and the films.
3. **Live layer:** the three.js stage and the quarter world, with the sun.
4. **View layer:** the Google Map3DElement view on its own screen, the drone panorama library, and our models of planned towers.
5. **Inside layer:** Photo Sphere Viewer rooms, overlays and window compositing. Phase 2 adds a Spark splat.
6. **Together layer:** a LiveKit room.

All layers read and write **one state object**: layer, unit, camera or yaw/pitch, room, finish choices, sun time and language. A co-tour then only mirrors that object; there is no sync code per feature.

## 4. Production pipeline (runs on the owner's machine)

**4.1 One manifest per project** (`projects/<slug>/manifest.json`):
- location, bearing and footprint polygon;
- floors × floor height, plus ground elevation;
- unit table (floor, facade, rooms, m²), with only real rows shown as real and demo rows labelled;
- facade materials and style;
- apartment types, with their plan sources;
- facilities and the licence of each image source;
- languages.

Everything below is generated from it, headless: `blender -b <scene>.blend -P tools/<step>.py -- --project <slug>`.

**4.2 Blender (Cycles):**
- **Orbit frames.** A camera ring of 72 steps × 2 lights (day HDRI, dusk). Output WebP at 1600 and 1280 px.
- **Unit masks.** An Object Index or Cryptomatte pass for every orbit frame. Python and OpenCV turn it into per-frame SVG polygons plus an ID map. The same pass gives facility masks (pool, gym, roof).
- **Interiors.** A panoramic camera at every room point, each rendered as cube faces.
  - **Finish variants:** re-render with the variant swapped, then keep only that variant's masked area. The browser layers the masked tiles over the base render, so a floor, 3 kitchens and 3 bath tiles cost 7 layers, not 9 full renders. Bounce light onto other surfaces is a small approximation [est; this method was not seen at a competitor].
  - **Windows:** rendered with alpha, so the view layer shows through. That layer is our own context render in v1 and the drone panorama in v2.
- **Live GLBs.**
  - Exterior: bake AO only, because the sun moves live.
  - Interiors, as a dollhouse: bake full light into the base colour and export unlit; that is cheapest and looks like the render. A small environment map handles glass and metal.
  - Export with the 5.2 glTF exporter (Meshopt) and keep a second UV set for lightmaps.
- **Camera paths.** One path per film. Animate it in Blender, export it in the glTF, play it live with GSAP, and render the same path in Cycles for the film.

**4.3 Packaging:**
- GLBs: `gltf-transform optimize in.glb mid.glb --compress meshopt`, then the README's `uastc` (normal, occlusion and metallic-roughness slots) and `etc1s` steps.
- Frames: WebP q80. Cube tiles for rooms: 512 px tiles for the cubemap-tiles adapter.
- A `budget.json` check fails the build if any file breaks section 3.5.

**4.4 Splats from renders (phase 2):**
1. Render 300–1,500 views of one flat with BlenderNeRF.
2. Train in Brush on the Radeon.
3. `splat-transform flat.ply --filter-floaters --filter-nan flat.sog` (or streamed SOG), or Spark's `build-lod` for `.RAD`.
4. Check it on two phones at a 500K budget.

Mirrors and glass need masks or cheating geometry [doc: Atlux notes].

**4.5 Real places** (beach, promenade, park; for amenities and the "10 minutes to the sea" walk):
1. Capture 4K phone video on foot at dawn, which needs no drone permit.
2. Run COLMAP (or RealityScan 2.2), then Brush, then SuperSplat to remove people.
3. Convert to SOG.

**4.6 Numbers that nobody else shows** (Python):
- **Sun hours:** rays from each window centre against context plus planned massing, for 21 Dec, 21 Mar and 21 Jun (pvlib for sun positions, trimesh with embree).
- **View score:** the share of sky, sea, green and buildings in the window frame. It is written into the unit JSON and labelled as a calculation.

**4.7 QA gates (before any release):**
- Playwright screenshots at 375 px in all 5 languages.
- Zero console errors.
- Budget check.
- The language-dna blacklist grep over new strings.
- Real-phone check on one Android mid-range and one iPhone.
- `source_audit.py` before and after (iron law 9a).
