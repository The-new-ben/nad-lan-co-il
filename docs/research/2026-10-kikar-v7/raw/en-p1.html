Tel Aviv’s Kikar Hamedina Towers (מגדלי כיכר המדינה) comprises three towers with 453 apartments beside a public park of about 40 dunam. The landowners’ buildings rise 40, 40 and 37 floors, with each floor turning 1.25 degrees. The frame was recorded as completed on 23 April 2026. Three published deals from April to December 2024 ranged from ₪9.58 million to ₪10.63 million. Your choice comes down to the apartment’s position, layout and contract, including the specification, what the asking price covers and the seller’s agreed delivery date.

Tower, floor and direction

Which tower, floor and direction to choose at Kikar Hamedina Towers

Begin with the tower’s position on the plot. Tower A stands to the southwest, Tower B to the southeast and Tower C to the northeast. The tower positions are about 75 to 78 m from the plot center. A compass direction alone does not describe the whole choice: compare the tower, floor and apartment orientation together. You can choose tower, floor and apartment by direction on this page, then ask for the plan of the specific home you are considering.

What the turning floors change

The buildings are also called the Spiral Towers. Each residential floor steps back slightly and turns relative to the floor below, following the circular character of the square. The architects’ treatment of the floor slabs and white façades is a modern interpretation of the buildings around the ring. The 1.25-degree turn accumulates to about 50 degrees over 40 floors, or about 46 degrees over 37 floors. This makes the exact floor relevant to how a balcony is aligned, and to the direction you face when looking out from it. Read its position from that apartment’s plan rather than assuming that a balcony farther up the building faces precisely the same way.

Insulated glass includes built-in electric shading; assess sun and shade at the hours you expect to use the balcony.

Lower floor or higher floor?

For a lower-floor home, make the relationship with the park and surrounding buildings part of the viewing. For a higher-floor home, establish which part of the advertised outlook you will enjoy from the rooms you use most. The published height comparison places the average tower deal at about ₪65,000 per m² and high floors and penthouses at ₪80,000 to ₪150,000 per m². The comparison’s date is not published. Compare that premium with the actual view and outdoor space, rather than treating height alone as the benefit you are buying.

The lifts have a published speed of 4 to 6 m per second. For a high-floor choice, include the complete route between the apartment, lobby and allocated parking in your assessment. A floor number describes elevation; your own route through the building helps you judge the practical fit, especially when you expect to use the facilities below as part of your daily routine.

Glass, finishes and the mamad

The façade combines floor-to-ceiling glass and white aluminum curtain walls, with full-height pivot windows and inner railings. Its aluminum-and-glass fins are 300 mm deep. Inside, listings describe fishbone parquet, designer marble, central heating, smart-home controls and high ceilings. Air conditioning is specified as Daikin VRF, a central multi-split arrangement. These details help you frame a specification review without treating an advertisement as the apartment’s contractual specification.

Listings also describe a safe room, or mamad, with a full bathroom, alongside a lobby with a security guard and round-the-clock security. Numerical ceiling heights, floor-to-floor heights and a green-building rating for the residential towers are not published.

Typical listings include 2 parking spaces and a storage room; another advertises 4 parking spaces and 2 storage rooms. Match the allocation to the apartment being sold rather than assuming that every home comes with the same package.

What to ask the seller

  • The apartment plan, showing the room layout being offered and any difference from the original plan.
  • The tower, floor and direction of the apartment, with its position clearly marked.
  • The apartment area and balcony area separately, together with the basis used for those measurements.
  • The parking and storage allocation, including the locations and the items included in the sale.
  • The specification appendix, so the agreed finishes and equipment can be checked against the listing.
  • The delivery date written into the seller’s contract.
  • Whether the price buys a right to an apartment or a finished apartment, and whether construction costs remain payable on top.

Life in the building

Pool, spa, gym and parking: daily life in Kikar Hamedina Towers

A morning here can be planned around a swim or workout, the school run and coffee in the square. The apartment’s route to those places is what makes that routine practical. When viewing a home, give the shared areas a place in the visit alongside the rooms upstairs, particularly the facilities you expect to use regularly.

The basement facilities

A swimming pool, gym, spa, treatment rooms and multipurpose halls are planned on a basement level. The recorded pool volume is 290 m³. Which basement level holds these facilities is not published. Ask to follow the route from the residential lifts to the facilities and discuss how access, opening hours and bookings will be handled. The guide to the pool in a residential project can help you consider the pool as part of daily life, rather than simply as an item in a sales description.

Moving through the building

There are 15 high-speed lifts serving the apartments. Start the practical part of a viewing in the lobby, then follow the route you would use with shopping, luggage or a stroller. The guide to lifts in a residential tower offers further reading for this part of the comparison. Garbage chutes and entrance doors are supplied by Rav Bariach. Include air conditioning in the same review by asking about the apartment’s controls, servicing and maintenance responsibilities.

Ask what the lobby service will cover when you are away and how visitors or deliveries will be received. For an overseas owner, put those arrangements alongside the upkeep of the apartment itself when assessing whether the building suits your pattern of visits.

Private parking and public parking

The underground provision includes about 906 spaces for apartment owners and about 720 public spaces, including 14 for disabled drivers. That distinction matters when reviewing the bays attached to the apartment. Ask to see their location and the route to the residential lifts, and clarify how you would use the public parking when receiving visitors. An allocation on paper and the parking routine you prefer should be considered together.

The school run and time outside

The new state primary school is inside the square. A dedicated internal access road serves school drop-off at set hours; ask about those hours when planning a family routine. The park plan includes lawns, playgrounds and shaded seating, giving you outdoor places to consider alongside the indoor facilities. For exercise outdoors, the park plan includes a 750 m running track, offering a separate option from the basement gym. The project’s own commercial space is limited to a few small café kiosks in the park, not a shopping mall. The shops remain on the surrounding ring.

The management company and its monthly fee are not published. Ask for the proposed arrangements when comparing homes, and use the guide to management fees in a new project to keep the ongoing cost of shared facilities in your budget.
