Kikar Hamedina Towers (מגדלי כיכר המדינה) rise in the heart of Kikar Hamedina, the great round square of north Tel Aviv: three twisting residential towers of 40, 40 and 37 floors with 453 apartments, each floor turned 1.25° from the one below. The developers are the landowners, the architects Yaski Mor Sivan and the builders Electra and Ashtrom. The frame was completed on 23.4.2026, per the municipal building-site record. Around the towers lies a park of about 40 dunams with an ecological pond, a school and a community centre. Three 4-room apartments on floors 38 and 39 sold for ₪9.58M to ₪10.63M (Globes, 2.5.2025). Here you choose a tower, a floor and a facing, and see an illustration of the view and the hours of sun from the window.

For sale and for rent

Apartments for sale in Kikar Hamedina Towers

The apartments belong to about 250 rights holders, who received them in exchange for their land, so there is no developer price list: apartments are sold and rented directly by their owners, usually through brokers (Globes, 23.9.2025). Many owners plan to live in the towers, and per Bizportal (19.4.2025) at most 200 to 250 apartments are expected to reach the market. It is a rare chance to live on the renewed Kikar Hamedina, above a green park in the heart of north Tel Aviv.

The park

The park and the pond at the heart of the square

Between the towers a public park of about 40 dunams is taking shape: an ecological pond about one metre deep with perennial planting, bridges and paths, lawns, playgrounds, a dog garden, game tables and shaded seating. Around it runs a boulevard with a cycle path, a 750 m running track and three rows of trees. 560 new trees were planted and 36 mature trees kept, in a public investment of about ₪150 million (Mako, 11.11.2025 and 24.9.2026).

On the west side of the square stand two new public buildings: a primary school of 18 classes and 6 special-education classes, with a green roof and an underground sports hall, and a community centre with a dance studio, activity rooms, a multi-purpose hall and a café (Tel Aviv-Yafo municipality, 4.8.2021; Mako, 11.11.2025).

The square

Kikar Hamedina: luxury, shops and cafés

Kikar Hamedina is considered the largest square in Israel. It was designed by Oscar Niemeyer with Israel Lotan and Abba Elhanani, and its ring of buildings, built mostly in the early 1970s, made it the centre of international fashion in Tel Aviv (Wikipedia). The ring’s shops host international fashion and jewellery brands, among them Gucci and Dior (ynet, 17.2.2023; TheMarker, 3.1.2025), and in December 2025 a boutique of luxury watches and jewellery opened on the square (Jerusalem Post, 27.12.2025).

On the ring, a minute’s walk from the towers, there are cafés, bakeries and restaurants, among them Lechem Erez and Bakery Kikar Hamedina, as well as supermarkets and a pharmacy. Within a 10-minute walk there are 84 cafés and restaurants, per the place registry in the area map (municipal data, findplace and OpenStreetMap; walking times by Mapbox).

Frequently asked questions

How tall are Kikar Hamedina Towers?

Towers A and C rise to about 160 metres over 40 floors, and tower B to about 157 metres over 37 floors, per Wikipedia and Ashtrom. The Tel Aviv-Yafo municipality’s buildings register lists all three at 40 floors and 158 to 160 metres. Every floor turns 1.25°, so the façade twists by about 50° from the ground to the roof.

What do apartments in Kikar Hamedina Towers cost?

Three 4-room apartments of 140 m² on floors 38 and 39 sold in 2024 for ₪9.58M to ₪10.63M, up to about ₪75,900 per m², per Globes (2.5.2025). Published listings ask, for example, ₪13.7M for a 168 m² apartment (1.2026) and ₪43M for a penthouse on floor 39. There is no developer price list, as the owners are the landowners.

Who built the towers in Kikar Hamedina?

Electra Construction and Ashtrom are building the towers together, under a contract of about ₪1.4 billion signed at the end of 2021 (Calcalist, 28.11.2021). The developers are the landowners of the square; the architects are Yaski Mor Sivan, the project manager is WXG, and the finance comes from Bareket Capital with Clal and Migdal, per Globes.

What is Kikar Hamedina in Tel Aviv?

Kikar Hamedina is a grand round square in north Tel Aviv, encircled by He Be’Iyar Street, with Weizmann and Jabotinsky streets running into it. It is considered the largest square in Israel, was designed by Oscar Niemeyer with Israel Lotan and Abba Elhanani, and the buildings of its ring host luxury shops of international brands, per Wikipedia.

What is the Kikar Hamedina project?

The Kikar Hamedina project is the residential complex built at the heart of the square: three twisting towers with 453 apartments, 1,620 underground parking spaces, a pool, a gym and a spa for residents, and a public park of about 40 dunams with a pond, a school and a community centre. It follows plan TA/MK/2500/A of 2013, and its frame was completed on 23.4.2026.

Is there a café in Kikar Hamedina?

Yes. On the square’s ring, a minute’s walk from the towers, there are cafés such as Lechem Erez and Bakery Kikar Hamedina, and 84 cafés and restaurants lie within a 10-minute walk, per municipal data and OpenStreetMap. Kiosks will operate in the new park, and the municipality’s design plan (4.8.2021) includes a café of about 60 m² in the community centre.

Where can I find kosher restaurants in Kikar Hamedina?

Kosher restaurants around Kikar Hamedina are identified by the current kashrut certificate displayed at each place. On the ring and along Weizmann, Jabotinsky and Ibn Gabirol streets there are dozens of restaurants and cafés within walking distance, all shown on this page’s area map with their walking times. It is best to check the certificate on site before a visit, as it can change.

How many apartments are in Kikar Hamedina Towers?

The three towers hold 453 apartments, per Ashtrom, Wikipedia and Globes, about 150 m² on average. The published deals and listings show mostly 4 and 5-room apartments of 132 to 200 m², alongside large penthouses with roof terraces. An official unit mix and the number of apartments in each tower have not been published.

What facilities will residents enjoy?

Per Ashtrom, one of the basement levels will hold a pool, a gym, a spa, treatment rooms and multi-purpose halls. Fifteen high-speed lifts will serve the apartments, per Electra, and the façades are glass curtain walls with built-in electric shading. Beneath the towers are 1,620 parking spaces, and listings usually show two parking spaces and a storage room per apartment.

The ring itself has cafés, supermarkets and a pharmacy. Herzliya Hebrew Gymnasium is about a 4-minute walk from the square, Ichilov hospital about 9 minutes and Park HaYarkon about 13 minutes.
