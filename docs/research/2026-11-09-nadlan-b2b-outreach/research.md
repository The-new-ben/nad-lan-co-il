## 1. Israeli law on B2B commercial email (Communications Law, section 30A)

### What the sources agree on
- Section 30A bans sending an advertisement by email, SMS, fax or automatic dialing without the recipient's prior consent. Courts count a message as an advertisement by its purpose, not its label. (https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פיצוי_בגין_משלוח_דברי_פרסומת_ללא_הסכמה_של_הנמען_%28חוק_הספאם%29 ; https://barlaw.co.il/advertising-message-or-informational-content-how-israeli-courts-interpret-the-spam-law/)
- Lecture or conference invitations, and in some rulings even greetings, have been treated as advertisements. The trend is stricter. (https://barlaw.co.il/advertising-message-or-informational-content-how-israeli-courts-interpret-the-spam-law/ ; https://www.pearlcohen.com/unprecedented-recovery-of-damages-for-violation-of-the-israeli-spam-law/)
- An advertisement must carry the word for "advertisement" at the start or in the subject line of an email. Sources on this: https://www.law.co.il/en/news/2016/08/19/israeli-anti-spam-law-amended-for-first-time/ ; a 2020 small-claims ruling found a breach for a missing subject label and for ignoring removal (https://www.law.co.il/media/computer-law/raz15.pdf).
- The recipient may refuse. Continued sending after refusal, or a message with no way to refuse, creates a claim. (https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פיצוי_בגין_משלוח_דברי_פרסומת_ללא_הסכמה_של_הנמען_%28חוק_הספאם%29)
- Statutory damages: up to NIS 1,000 per message, no proof of harm needed, adding up per message. Criminal fines also exist (kolzchut cites up to NIS 226,000). Claims can be filed in small claims without a lawyer. Class actions are possible. (kolzchut URL above ; https://www.pearlcohen.com/unprecedented-recovery-of-damages-for-violation-of-the-israeli-spam-law/)
- When the recipient denies consent, a 2020 decision put the burden on the sender to prove it. (secondary, via https://www.law.co.il/media/computer-law/ search result set; verify in the ruling)

### The business exception (one-time invitation)
- The law allows a one-time approach by the advertiser to a recipient that is a business, offering to agree to receive advertisements later. (https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פיצוי_בגין_משלוח_דברי_פרסומת_ללא_הסכמה_של_הנמען_%28חוק_הספאם%29 ; https://www.law.co.il/en/news/2016/08/19/israeli-anti-spam-law-amended-for-first-time/)
- What it may contain: case law holds the request must not itself be an advertisement. It may only offer the future-consent option. (https://www.law.co.il/en/news/2016/08/19/israeli-anti-spam-law-amended-for-first-time/ , describing the case law). Consequence: a first message that pitches advertising on the portal, or attaches the marketing film, is at real risk of being the advertisement itself.
- A 2015 small-claims ruling appears to have rejected a claim on the business-recipient exception for a single message to a business address (https://www.law.co.il/media/computer-law/adi_maoz2_4RgtsCC.pdf; the snippet was not conclusive).
- The business exception covers recipients that are businesses. Whether a broker, lawyer or appraiser acting as a sole proprietor, or an address of a named individual at a firm, counts as a "business" recipient is not settled in anything I read.

### Public addresses
- I found no source stating that an address published on a business website may be mailed without consent. The question was left open by every source I read. Treat as: NOT permitted unless counsel says otherwise.

### Cases
- Supreme Court: the cited case is רע"א 1954/14, Ilan Hazani v. Shimon Hanegbi (decided 4.8.2014), not "Hazani v. Sagi". It is used by lower courts as a guide to size of damages and attorney fees under the spam law. Reported: NIS 10,000 for 27 messages in one cited use. (https://www.toledano.co.il/רעא-1954-14-אילן-חזני-נגד-שמעון-הנגבי/פסיקה-חינם/ ; secondary summaries via the law.co.il search results). I could not read the full text; verify on Nevo. The case concerns damages, not specifically B2B.
- Other: Glasberg v. Psagot (2017, free-course links held to be advertisements, class certified), Cohen Halala v. AIG (2014, policy-expiry SMS, settled). (https://barlaw.co.il/advertising-message-or-informational-content-how-israeli-courts-interpret-the-spam-law/)
- Supreme Court language that the law is read in favour of recipients: secondary (https://www.pearlcohen.com/unprecedented-recovery-of-damages-for-violation-of-the-israeli-spam-law/).

### Still unclear, needs counsel
1. Exact current text of the business exception subsection and its conditions (I could not load the statute; my searches pointed at Nevo and Knesset PDFs such as https://fs.knesset.gov.il/17/law/17_lsr_299991.pdf).
2. Whether the one-time message may name the portal's services at all, include a link to a page, or a thumbnail of a film.
3. Whether a mail to a sole proprietor's personal-looking address is a "business" recipient.
4. Whether follow-up reminders are allowed when the first invitation gets no answer (no source supports it; assume no).
5. Whether a "reply to the owner's own contact form / phone / WhatsApp / LinkedIn" is a safer first channel (the law lists email, SMS, fax, auto-dial; a personal phone call or personal LinkedIn message is outside that list per kolzchut's wording, but verify).
6. Treatment of a recipient who has an existing relationship (past advertiser, past enquiry): the customer-details exception applies to people who gave details during a purchase or negotiation with notice and a chance to opt out. (kolzchut URL)

## 3. Video in email

- Support for the HTML5 video tag is small: about 24% of tracked client combinations per caniemail (last tested 2019). Apple Mail plays it (partly, controls needed on some; autoplay not on iOS). Gmail removes it. Yahoo and AOL remove it. Outlook is mixed and unreliable. Thunderbird, HEY, Samsung (controls needed) play it. (https://www.caniemail.com/features/html-video/)
- Gmail is a big share of mailboxes, so an embedded player would not play for many recipients. Hence the standard method: a still image with a play button that links to a landing page that hosts the video. (https://wistia.com/blog/embedding-video-in-email , a vendor that sells hosting)
- Lift claims, all vendor or secondary, no method seen: play-button overlay 12 to 21% click lift versus plain thumbnail (attributed to Litmus; secondary) and 14.5% (secondary) via https://sproutvideo.com/blog/?p=13264 summary; Campaign Monitor via Wistia: open rate +19%, click +65%, unsubscribes -26% (https://wistia.com/blog/embedding-video-in-email). Treat as direction only.
- GIF previews: no controlled evidence found in this session. Unknown.
- Practical risk from sources: video and large images raise HTML weight and can hurt deliverability (Hunter above, vendor).

## 5. Best-practice structures for a B2B outreach program

Built from sections 1 to 3; the structure is my synthesis, not a measured standard.
- Segments: developers, brokers, lawyers, appraisers, mortgage advisers, inspectors. One owner, one list, one message per segment; separate consented list from the not-yet-consented list.
- Sequence: (1) one-time invitation that offers to receive future messages and is not itself an advertisement (law, section 1); (2) only after a clear yes, a short plain message with the film link and a one-page proposal; (3) consented follow-ups at day 3 to 4 and day 9 to 10, then stop (Backlinko and Woodpecker; vendor). Any follow-up to a non-responder needs counsel.
- Personal one-page proposal per consenting company: their project or specialty, what the portal offers, price and terms, one next step.
- Booking link and a phone/WhatsApp number from a named person.
- Reply handling: same-day human reply; "stop" works in one click and by reply; every refusal logged.
- Suppression list: global, permanent, checked before every send; includes refusals, bounces, complaints.
- Measure replies, booked meetings and signed advertisers per 100 invitations; opens are unreliable (Apple Mail privacy and image blocking), so do not manage by opens. (principle; no source needed beyond the baselines above that use replies)
- Test properly: randomise within a segment, one variable at a time, pre-register the metric, report counts and intervals.

## Requirements

Each is testable. "Counsel" means a lawyer must confirm before launch.

- L1. Before the first live send, a written opinion from Israeli counsel exists on: the business exception text, what the one-time invitation may contain, public addresses, sole proprietors. (Section 1, "still unclear".)
- L2. Every recipient row records: source of the address, date collected, segment, legal basis (consent, existing relationship, or one-time business invitation), and a consent timestamp where relevant. Test: export shows no empty field. (burden of proof on sender, section 1)
- L3. The one-time invitation contains no sales pitch for advertising, no film, no price; it only offers to receive future messages and links to a one-click yes. (law.co.il 2016 article, case-law point)
- L4. Only one invitation goes to any address, ever. A second send to the same address without consent is blocked by the system. Test: the send queue rejects a duplicate. (NIS 1,000 per message; kolzchut)
- L5. Every message that is an advertisement has the advertisement word at the start of the subject and body, the sender's identity and a working refusal route. Automated check on each template. (law.co.il 2016 article; raz15 ruling)
- L6. A refusal (link click, reply "stop", or any wording meaning no) is written to the suppression list within one minute and no further message passes. Test with 10 seeded refusals. (kolzchut)
- L7. No message goes out under the "existing customer" exception unless the notice-and-opt-out record exists for that person. (kolzchut)
- L8. First-touch email is plain text, from a named person's address on a domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, with no images and one link at most. (Hunter and Puzzleinbox, vendor; deliverability; unverified in Hebrew)
- L9. The first touch is 50 to 100 words, in the 20 to 100 range that every length source supports; no more than 100. (Gong, Hunter; vendor)
- L10. The subject is 1 to 5 words and does not use buzzwords. (Gong, vendor; Backlinko 36 to 50 characters conflicts, so A/B test both)
- L11. The message names the recipient's own project, office or specialty. Test: a human checks 20 random messages per segment. (Backlinko +32.7%, Belkins 7% vs 3%; vendor)
- L12. Each message asks for one low-effort action framed as value, not a meeting. (Gong CTA finding; vendor)
- L13. The consented follow-up sequence has at most 3 steps, spaced 3 to 4 days apart, and then stops. (Backlinko 3+ messages best; Woodpecker emails 5 to 6 fall to 30% of the first; vendor)
- L14. The marketing film is shown as a still image with a play button that links to a landing page on nad-lan.co.il; no embedded player. The page loads under 3 seconds on mobile. (caniemail 24% support; Gmail strips it; Wistia)
- L15. The film thumbnail goes only in the consented step, never in the one-time invitation. (L3 and legal risk)
- L16. The landing page for each recipient shows a personal one-page proposal with price, inclusions, contract length and a booking link. (Rightmove guide shows lifecycle packages and account manager; price transparency gap in section 4)
- L17. A named human replies to every reply within one business day. Measured from the mailbox timestamps. (principle from the program structure; Rightmove account-manager model)
- L18. Send windows are tested, not assumed: Sunday to Thursday, 8:00 to 12:00 Israel time as the starting hypothesis; the system logs results per day and hour. (Belkins: 0.54% for 8 to 12; Sunday figure noisy; no Israeli data)
- L19. Success is measured as replies, booked meetings and signed advertisers per 100 invitations; opens are shown but never used to decide. (section 5)
- L20. Each segment starts with a pilot of at most 50 invitations and a control, and scales only after a result is reported with counts. Small lists reply better (Woodpecker secondary: 5.8% vs 2.1%). No sitewide-scale single-day mass send (owner rule).
- L21. A claim that this is "the best system in the world" appears nowhere until a dated public comparison with named competitors exists. Test: grep of all templates and pages. (project editorial rule on unsupported superiority claims)
- L22. Brevo is configured so list unsubscribe headers, a sender address and a postal/contact line appear on every send, and a test message passes inbox tests on Gmail and Outlook. (L5, L8)
