How Israeli mortgages work

An Israeli mortgage is rarely one loan at one rate. The bank splits it into tracks (מסלולים, maslulim), each with its own rate, its own link or no link to the consumer price index (CPI), and often its own term. You pay all the tracks together every month, so the payment depends on the mix as much as on any one rate. Nearly all of it is in shekels: in August 2026 loans in foreign currency were 0.1% of new mortgage money (Bank of Israel).

The Bank of Israel rate and prime

The Bank of Israel rate has been 3.25% since 3 September 2026. In 2026 it was set at 4.00% on 8 January, 3.75% on 28 May, 3.50% on 9 July and 3.25% on 3 September. Prime is that rate plus 1.5 points, 4.75%, and a prime track follows every change: a cut lowers the payment, a rise lifts it. The next rate decision is on 23 November 2026, and the Bank of Israel's October 2026 forecast has the rate at 3.0% on average in the third quarter of 2027.

The average rate in the prime track was 4.15% in September 2026, below prime itself: the track is priced as prime plus or minus a margin, and on average the margin was a discount.

The three uniform baskets in every approval in principle

Since 31 August 2022 every approval in principle (אישור עקרוני, ishur ekroni) from an Israeli bank must price the same three uniform baskets, so offers from different banks can be compared on equal terms (Bank of Israel Directive 451, circular 2694). Each basket is offered for 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 years on a Spitzer schedule, and the approval must show the predicted total interest, the total predicted repayment and the highest predicted monthly payment.

Paying off early: fees and when they apply

Early repayment of an Israeli housing loan is governed by the Banking Order (Early Repayment of a Housing Loan), 2002, as amended in 2014. The main points:

  • An operational fee of up to ₪60.
  • 0.1% of the amount if you give less than 10 days' notice; it is not charged when the bank itself refinances the loan or after the borrower's death. Notice can be given up to 45 days ahead.
  • A capitalization fee on fixed-rate tracks, only when the current average rate is below your loan's rate; the bank charges the lower of two formulas.
  • No capitalization fee on tracks whose rate changes at least once a year or at unknown dates, such as prime, and none on a rate-change date.
  • On CPI-linked tracks repaid between the 1st and the 15th of the month: half the average monthly CPI change of the last 12 indices.
  • The capitalization fee is cut by 20% after 3 years and by 30% after 5 years (on complementary loans by 10%, 20%, 30% and 40% after 1, 2, 3 and 4 years).
  • State eligibility loans (directed loans): no fee.

The average rates used for the fee are published monthly by the Bank of Israel. For loans not linked to the CPI, the September 2026 averages in force from 19 October 2026 ran from 4.35% for terms up to one year to 4.89% for terms over 25 years.

Olim and returning residents: what to check before you borrow

The rules above apply to every borrower at an Israeli bank. A few of them matter more when the Israeli system is new to you.

Citizenship decides the single-home cap

Directive 329 allows up to 75% of the value for a single home, 70% for a replacement home and 50% for an additional home. A buyer who is not an Israeli citizen cannot count as a single-home buyer, so the cap is 50%. If that is your case, read Israel mortgage for foreigners.

How the bank counts your income

The payment test is against disposable income: net income minus fixed commitments that run longer than 18 months, alimony, and rent if you will not live in the home you are buying. A spouse who lives with you counts in full; half of the income of a first-degree relative who guarantees the loan may count. The payment may be up to 50% of that income, and above 40% the bank must hold more capital against the loan.

The eligibility loan counts years since immigration

The state eligibility loan (משכנתא בזכאות, mashkanta bezakaut) is a CPI-linked, fixed-rate loan whose amount and terms the government sets by points: years of marriage or since immigration, children, siblings and service, plus a place loan in priority areas. It can be repaid early with no fee and no notice. Ask the bank for the amount that applies to you.

Four mortgage rules that are often stated wrong

Some answers about Israeli mortgages, including summaries shown in search results, repeat rules that have changed or never existed. This is what Directive 329 of the Bank of Israel says today.

Only a third in prime? There is no such cap

No rule today limits prime to a third of the mortgage. The limit is on the whole variable part, prime and the other variable tracks together: at most 66.66% of the loan, so at least a third at a fixed rate (Directive 329, s.7). In September 2026 prime was 26.5% of new mortgages across the banks, and 42.2% at First International (Habeinleumi), according to the Bank of Israel.

Payment up to 40% of income? The limit is 50%

The monthly payment may be up to 50% of disposable income. Above 40% the bank must hold more capital against the loan, which is why most loans stay below it: in August 2026 the average payment on new mortgages was 29.23% of income (Directive 329, s.5; Bank of Israel).

A 40-year mortgage? Banks lend for up to 30 years

The longest term for a bank housing loan is 30 years (Directive 329, s.8). A longer term lowers the payment and raises the interest: ₪1,000,000 over 30 years costs ₪4,931 in the first month against ₪5,428 over 25 years, at the September 2026 average mix.

60% for buyers who are not citizens? The cap is 50%

A buyer who is not an Israeli citizen cannot count as a single-home buyer, so the maximum is 50%, as for an additional home. A single home: up to 75%; a replacement home: up to 70% (Directive 329, s.2 and its definitions).

Frequently asked questions

What are mortgage rates in Israel today?

The latest Bank of Israel figures are for September 2026. Across all banks the forecast all-in rate (IRR) on new mortgages averaged 5.09%; by track, fixed not linked averaged 4.72%, the prime track 4.15%, fixed CPI-linked 3.26% and variable CPI-linked 3.28% (effective annual rates). The Bank of Israel rate is 3.25% and prime 4.75%. The table by bank on this page updates every month.

How much is the monthly payment on a 1 million shekel mortgage in Israel?

At the all-banks average mix and rates of September 2026, the first payment on ₪1,000,000 is ₪5,428 over 25 years, ₪6,202 over 20 years, ₪7,532 over 15 years and ₪4,931 over 30 years (Spitzer schedule, CPI-linked tracks with the index unchanged).

How much of a down payment do I need to buy a home in Israel?

At least 25% of the value for a single home, since banks may lend up to 75%; 30% for a replacement home and 50% for an additional home. For reduced-price homes such as Mechir LaMishtaken the buyer pays at least ₪100,000 of their own money, or ₪60,000 with the new grant (Directive 329). Purchase tax, the lawyer and other costs are paid from your own money on top.

How much mortgage can I get on my salary in Israel?

The payment can be up to 50% of disposable income, and above 40% the loan costs the bank more capital, so most loans stay below. For example, the September 2026 average payment on ₪1,000,000 over 25 years, ₪5,428, needs disposable income of at least ₪10,856 at the 50% limit, or ₪13,570 to stay at 40%. The calculator's monthly payment mode shows the loan a payment carries.

What is the prime rate in Israel?

Prime is the Bank of Israel rate plus 1.5 points: 4.75% since 3 September 2026, when the Bank of Israel rate was cut to 3.25%. The next rate decision is on 23 November 2026.

What is the Bank of Israel interest rate now?

3.25%, in force since 3 September 2026. In 2026 it was set at 4.00% on 8 January, 3.75% on 28 May, 3.50% on 9 July and 3.25% on 3 September. The Bank of Israel's October 2026 forecast has it at 3.0% on average in the third quarter of 2027.

Which Israeli bank has the lowest mortgage rates?

There is no single answer, because each borrower gets a personal rate. By the Bank of Israel's averages for September 2026, the lowest forecast all-in rate among the five largest banks was 4.75% at First International (Habeinleumi) and the highest 5.16% at Mizrahi-Tefahot. An average is not an offer: get approvals in principle from several banks and compare the three uniform baskets.

What is a mashkanta?

Mashkanta is the Hebrew word for a mortgage, a loan secured on a home. In Israel it is usually split into tracks with different rates and linkage, such as prime, fixed not linked (kalatz) and fixed CPI-linked (katz).

Can my whole mortgage be in prime?

No. Prime counts as a variable rate, and the variable part of the loan, prime and the other variable tracks together, may be at most 66.66% (Directive 329, s.7). There is no separate one-third cap on prime.

What is the longest mortgage term in Israel?

30 years at the banks (Directive 329, s.8). At the September 2026 average mix, ₪1,000,000 over 30 years costs ₪4,931 in the first month against ₪5,428 over 25 years, but the total interest is higher: ₪775,170 against ₪628,489.

Can olim get a mortgage in Israel?

Yes, from the same banks and under the same Directive 329 limits as any borrower. An Israeli citizen buying a single home can borrow up to 75% of the value; a buyer who is not a citizen is limited to 50%. Years since immigration count toward the points of the state eligibility loan.

How does inflation affect an Israeli mortgage?

Only the CPI-linked tracks move with inflation: their balance and payment rise with the consumer price index. The CPI rose 1.64% in the 12 months to September 2026, and the Bank of Israel's October 2026 forecast is 2.0% over the next four quarters. Prime and the other tracks that are not linked do not move with the CPI, but prime moves with the Bank of Israel rate.

Is there a penalty for paying off a mortgage early in Israel?

Sometimes. Fixed-rate tracks can carry a capitalization fee when the current average rate is below your loan's rate; prime and tracks whose rate changes at least once a year have none. There is also an operational fee of up to ₪60, and 0.1% if you give less than 10 days' notice. The capitalization fee is cut by 20% after 3 years and by 30% after 5 years (Banking Order, 2002).

What are the three uniform baskets?

Three standard mixes that every approval in principle must price, so banks can be compared: basket 1 is all fixed not linked; basket 2 is a third fixed not linked, a third prime and a third variable every 5 years, CPI-linked; basket 3 is half fixed not linked and half prime (Directive 451, in force since 31 August 2022). At the September 2026 averages their first payments on ₪1,000,000 over 25 years are ₪5,625, ₪5,269 and ₪5,471.

What is a Spitzer amortization schedule?

A Spitzer schedule keeps the payment on each track constant, so early payments are mostly interest and later ones mostly principal. On ₪1,000,000 over 25 years at the September 2026 average mix, year one costs ₪65,140, of which ₪42,215 is interest and ₪22,925 principal. The full yearly schedule is on this page.
