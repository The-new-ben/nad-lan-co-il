Marketing a new real estate project in Israel: costs, budget and reaching buyers abroad

How much does it cost to market a real estate project in Israel? The anchor that recurs in Israeli market guides is a marketing budget of about 1% of the project's total sales value, about 0.75% of it online; when the sales team and the advertising are counted together, the range quoted is 2% to 4%. For 60 apartments at an average of ₪2.4 million, 1% is about ₪1.44 million, or about ₪24,000 per apartment. A lead in a project campaign actually costs ₪300 to ₪800, according to marketing agencies, and an exclusive-lead price list published on 4 November 2026 quotes ₪128 to ₪173 before VAT. Every number on this page comes with its source and date.

Israel's new-build market in 2026: why developers' marketing budgets jumped

Anyone planning a project marketing budget today walks into a crowded market. A Globes report from July 2026 described more than 85,000 new apartments left unsold and a sharp rise in advertising spend by the large building companies. The figures in the report come from Ifat's advertising monitor and are calculated at rate-card prices, not at what was actually paid. So they show a direction, not an invoice, but the direction is clear: Aura went from ₪3.5 million in 2023 to almost ₪62 million in 2025, and Israel Canada almost doubled its spend within a year.

The same report lists single campaigns: about ₪5 million for the launch of DIMRI YAMA in Sde Dov, an estimated ₪5 million for UTOPIA in the same area (billboards, television and digital), about ₪3 million for a Tidhar campaign across all media, about ₪3 million for a Risdor campaign that included a 5% discount on five-room apartments, and about ₪2 million for a Barzani project in Katzrin. For 51PARK in Herzliya, the report described production costs double the Israeli norm and a presenter's fee estimated at $1 million to $1.5 million.

What it means for a developer planning a budget

  • A campaign does not replace an offer. The experts quoted in the report said it plainly: without a discount, a financing benefit or a real offer, a campaign alone does not turn a trend. Money spent on exposure without a reason to buy now is money burned.
  • When everyone is in the same channels, exposure costs more. Companies with budgets in the tens of millions buy the same audiences on Facebook, on Google and on television. A mid-sized project will not beat them on volume; it wins on fast response, on a project page that answers every question and on tracking all the way to the signed contract.
  • Buyers check before they call. They compare the price per square metre, read about the Sale Law security, and their questions about a project's price already get a written answer inside Google itself, before they reach your site. Our own test of this is further down the page.
  • There are buyers outside the local market. According to the Chief Economist at the Ministry of Finance, foreign residents made 487 purchases in the first quarter of 2026, 18% more than a year earlier. That is about 162 deals a month across the country: a small audience, but one that buys, and one that almost no project speaks to in its own language.

The bottom line: marketing new apartments in Israel in 2026 is less about how many shekels you have and more about where each shekel works. That means taking the budget apart, line by line, and knowing what is normal for each line.

Real estate project marketing budget: how much to set aside and how to decide

There is no official price list for marketing a project. Israeli marketing companies do not publish prices, and the numbers passed around are anchors: a percentage of the project's total sales value. The big difference between the numbers you will hear is not how expensive they are but what they include. So before comparing, separate four pots: marketing only (research, assets, media and management), marketing with a sales team, marketing with a sales commission, and a sales office with a model apartment. Most Hebrew guides mix these pots, which is why one says 1% and another says 4%, and both are right.

Example: 60 apartments at ₪2.4 million

This example appears in market guides; the arithmetic on it is ours. The sales value is ₪144 million. At the 1% anchor, the marketing budget for the life of the project is about ₪1.44 million, of which about ₪1.08 million goes online. Over a sales window of 12 to 24 months that is ₪60,000 to ₪120,000 a month for every marketing line together. With the team and the selling included, at 2% to 4%, it comes to ₪2.88 million to ₪5.76 million.

A check per apartment sold

Another way to test the number is per apartment sold. Reports on large developers quote ₪3,000 to ₪23,000 of advertising per apartment sold, without the team and without the sales office. In one published comparison, one developer spent seven times as much per apartment as another, on a similar project. That gap is the whole story: the same market, the same product, and a budget that depends on the quality of the offer, the speed of response and what buyers find when they reach the page. A per-apartment figure built from public data, the companies' advertising spend set against the apartments they sold, has not yet been published in Israel.

Working back from the sales target

The professional method starts at the end: how many sales you need, and by when. An example published in one guide: 12 sales in six months. If each sale takes 6 meetings, you need 72 meetings. If 8 leads produce one meeting, you need 576 leads. At ₪100 a lead, that is ₪57,600 of media. And if one lead in six reaches a meeting, 432 leads are enough. Working back is also a sanity check on any proposal: a proposal that does not say how many meetings it expects to deliver gives you no way to measure it.

What moves the number up or down

  • The number of apartments and their price: a fixed percentage of a small luxury project gives too little for the assets it needs; in a large project the same percentage leaves room.
  • The time allowed for selling: a target of a third of the apartments in presale needs a larger early budget than a target of an even pace.
  • The stage: before the permit, after the permit or under construction. The more real the building becomes, the less persuasion and the more information buyers need.
  • Location and competition: when several towers are marketed in the same quarter at the same time, every audience is bought twice.
  • Assets you already have: renders, sales plans, a film and a project page that are ready save the production lines.
  • Response in the sales office: a rep who calls back after two days doubles the cost of a meeting, as the chapter on leads shows.
  • The developer's brand: a known developer buys trust; a new one has to earn it on the page, in the guarantees and in openness.

Reserve and the cost of time

Keep a managed reserve in the budget, in case one channel works better than expected or the market shifts. A usual reserve rate in Israel has not been published. And the sum few developers do: what one more month of unsold stock costs. In a published Australian example, a 12-month delay raised the marketing budget from $720,000 to $880,000, from 1.2% to 1.47% of sales value, and cut the margin on cost from 19.5% to 19.1%. In Israel, add the cost of the accompanying bank's financing and the holding costs. A detailed Israeli example has not been published, so build one from your own project's cash flow before you cut marketing.

Market research, pricing and the unit mix

The cheapest line on the sheet, and the most important. Before a shekel of media, look at the competing projects nearby, at recent deal prices, at the audience and at the offer. Many guides advise starting at the planning stage, while the unit mix, the balconies and the lobby can still change: an apartment planned for real demand saves a discount at the end. A starting point for market data: new projects in Israel and the prices developers publish, by city. No Israeli price for this service has been published; it is usually sold as part of the marketing agreement.

Branding, name and creative

A name for the project, a visual language, a sales brochure, a campaign idea and the ad copy. It is a one-off cost that keeps being updated along the way: every extra language, every construction stage and every change in the offer needs a new version. Worth checking in a proposal: how many versions are included, whether translation and adaptation into other languages are in the price, and who holds the open files. No Israeli price has been published.

Renders and sales plans

Here there are open price lists. One studio publishes ₪4,000 to ₪6,000 for an exterior set of three views, ₪1,500 to ₪2,500 for each extra view and ₪2,000 to ₪3,500 for an interior render. A price guide on a comparison site gives ₪800 to ₪3,000 per image, ₪3,000 to ₪5,000 for six images, ₪1,500 to ₪3,000 for an exterior and ₪1,000 to ₪2,500 for an interior. A colour sales plan, the plan of each apartment that buyers receive, costs ₪500 to ₪750 per apartment, and a rendered sales plan, a view from above into the furnished apartment, ₪1,000 to ₪1,500. Published turnaround: 5 to 7 working days for a single render, and two to three weeks for a large set. For comparison, $3,000 to $10,000 per single render is quoted in Australia.

What sets the real price is less the image and more the rounds: how many corrections are included, whether the usage rights cover all media and use abroad, and whether the renders are updated when the specification changes. And every render in an ad has to match what will actually be delivered, for the reasons set out in the chapter on the rules.

Virtual tour, 360 tour and choosing an apartment on the building

A tour of a single property is already priced in the market: OnMap publishes for its clients ₪899 for one property, ₪2,427 for three and ₪3,820 for five, plus VAT, delivered within 5 business days of the shoot. That suits a model apartment. For a whole project, with a building on which buyers pick a floor, see the view from each floor and open an apartment, no Israeli price has been published, and none of the guides we read links what buyers did in the tour to the enquiry they send.

What to ask of any supplier: that buyers can pick a floor and a direction themselves, that their choice reaches the sales rep together with the enquiry, that the tour opens fast on a phone abroad, and that it exists in every language you market in. On our project pages, choosing on the building and the enquiry that carries the floor and the direction already work, in five languages, and a page like this is included in the annual plans further down.

Project film and construction progress footage

A render film of 30 to 60 seconds is priced at ₪7,000 to ₪15,000, and one of one to three minutes at ₪12,000 to ₪30,000, according to a studio price list. Another price guide gives ₪2,000 to ₪7,000 per film, and a further studio writes "from ₪3,000". The gap between the price lists is length, quality and the number of scenes. In practice you need two versions: a short one for ads and a long one for the project page, and for each market abroad a voice-over or subtitles in its own language. Construction progress footage is the cheapest content that holds trust for years; no Israeli price has been published.

Project page and landing pages

The project page is where every shekel of media lands, so build it before you pay for media. What it needs for a buyer to get in touch: a real price or price range, the apartment plans, a summary of the specification, a map and travel times, the payment schedule, the type of Sale Law security, information about the developer, questions and answers, and a fast way to reach you on WhatsApp and by phone. Each campaign gets its own landing page, with tracking that shows which channel every enquiry came from. Make sure the domain, the page and the tracking code are registered in your name. No Israeli price has been published.

CRM, call tracking and WhatsApp

Every enquiry, from every channel, into one pipeline: customer management (CRM), a virtual number for each channel so you know where a call came from, and WhatsApp Business. It is a set-up cost plus a monthly one, and in most proposals it is pushed into an appendix. No Israeli price has been published; the full detail is in the chapter on handling leads.

Paid digital media

Google, Facebook and Instagram, the property listing sites, sponsored articles, YouTube, TikTok and LinkedIn. The figures published in Israel are agencies' claims, with no public research behind them: ₪90 to ₪150 plus VAT per lead in lead suppliers' price lists, against ₪300 to ₪800, and sometimes over ₪1,000, per lead in an actual project campaign. One agency reports good leads at ₪42 and leads at ₪200, another reports under ₪100. LinkedIn was reported at over ₪1,000 per enquiry, YouTube at a few agorot (hundredths of a shekel) per view, and a banner on a large site at thousands of shekels.

A published monthly sum: 20 meetings a month, 5 leads per meeting and ₪250 per lead make ₪25,000 of media a month. And with a proposal of ₪20,000 a month, ask what is inside it: it can be media alone, or media together with management and production. On Google, the daily budget times 30.4 is the monthly billing cap, so a single day can cost more than the daily budget without the month going over.

Billboards, the site fence and signs

Billboards, the fence around the site and the project sign at the entrance. The site fence is the cheapest long-term ad, because it stands there for all the years of construction. No Israeli price has been published, and the fence and the sign are sometimes priced in the construction budget rather than the marketing budget, so they fall between the two pots. Permits for signs are set by the local authority; we did not check them for this page.

Print: the sales brochure, flyers and local press

A printed sales brochure is still needed in the sales office, and a flyer or an ad in a local paper reaches people who live in the neighbourhood and want to move up within it. For buyers abroad the brochure becomes a digital version in their own language. No Israeli price has been published.

Radio, television and presenters

This is where the large sums are. The campaigns described by Globes, ₪2 million to ₪5 million across all media, relied on television, billboards and digital, and some on a well-known presenter. That is a tool for the brand and for the launch of a large project, not for a project of a few dozen apartments. And what was said there matters more than the number: without a real offer, the presenter does not sell.

PR, content, SEO and answers inside Google

Articles, media presence, expert content on the site and a place in the search results. Over a sales window of 12 to 24 months, according to one guide, paid media carries the presale and the launch, and organic content starts to pay its way from the middle of the project. No Israeli price has been published. The new question here is who gets cited in the written answers of Google and ChatGPT; a separate chapter further down shows what we tested.

Influencers

Influencers appear in market guides in a single line, among the forgotten costs. We found no Israeli price, and no data on results in projects. If you are testing the channel, measure it like any other: to the meeting.

Events, sales conferences and exhibitions abroad

A launch event, a presale sales conference, fairs and exhibitions. Published data: the Yad2 real estate fair at the Tel Aviv exhibition grounds drew close to 18,000 visitors and about 40 developers in one of its earlier editions; a series of Israeli real estate events in Jewish communities in the US, Canada and the UK expected 15 to 20 stops a year, with deposits collected at the event; the property salon of the French-speaking community in Paris holds its 95th edition on 15 November 2026; and an Israeli building exhibition was held in New York on 28 and 29 May 2026 and in Jerusalem on 30 September 2026. Stand prices have not been published. A live launch for buyers from China starts at $5,500, with four weeks of campaign before the event.

Events have a drawback that is easy to forget: they end. The day after, no asset is left that keeps bringing enquiries, and the exhibitions do not publish what they share with exhibitors. And a deposit collected at an event is subject to the Sale Law's 7% rule.

Sales office, model apartment and the sales team

Abroad this is usually the largest marketing line. In Australia a range of $150,000 to well over $700,000 is quoted for a sales office with a display apartment, and in a published example $320,000 to fit it out plus $90,000 to run it for 18 months. In Miami a sales centre of $5 million to $8 million was reported, on leases of two to five years. In Israel no price has been published. The sales staff's salaries, commissions and training sometimes sit outside the marketing budget; put them on the same sheet to see the full picture.

The marketing company's fee and the contract terms

Four ways of paying recur in the guides: a monthly retainer, payment per lead, a commission on each sale, and a mix of two of them. Some packages are campaigns only; others are a full service with a sales team. The commission rate Israeli marketing companies charge on a new project was not published anywhere we read. What was published: a broker's fee of 1% to 2% of the apartment price plus VAT when a broker brings the buyer, and about 2% plus VAT from each side in a resale. Abroad: 2% to 3% of the sale price in Australia, and 4% to 8% when the sale goes through a third party; among US home builders, 1.5% for in-house sales (2% in a slowdown) and 2% for outside brokers.

The contract matters as much as the percentage. Points to settle in writing: the length of exclusivity; what the company is owed on a deal signed after the agreement ended; when the commission is earned and when it is paid, at signing or at handover; what happens to the budget you committed if the project stops; and who holds the ad account, the tracking code, the landing page and the lead data. The full checklist is in the chapter on comparing proposals.

The costs missing from the first proposal

  • Set-up fees that sit in an appendix rather than on the first page of the proposal.
  • A media fee charged as a percentage of the media budget, on top of the management fee.
  • Extra renders, versions in other languages and updated plans.
  • Refreshing the materials in the late construction stages and ahead of occupancy.
  • Subscriptions to listing sites, call tracking and short dial numbers.
  • The sales office, the model apartment and the site sign, which fall between the marketing budget and the construction budget.
  • Salaries, commissions and training for the sales staff.
  • VAT, when the proposal does not say whether it is included.
  • Delays in the schedule: every extra month adds a retainer and media.

Marketing stages and what you spend at each one

A project is not marketed all at once. The timeline that recurs in the guides: 4 to 8 weeks of strategy and branding, with 4 to 8 weeks of preparing the digital assets alongside; 4 to 8 weeks of pre-launch; 2 to 6 weeks of presale; a launch week; and from there, selling to the last apartment over a window of 12 to 24 months. These are agencies' claims rather than research, but they are consistent across the sources.

Soft launch and the interest list

Before spending on wide exposure, build a list: the team's friends and family, people living in the neighbourhood, the developer's past buyers and warm audiences on Facebook and Google. The goal is twofold: to test whether the price works, and to reach the presale with people who already know the project. This is also the stage to test the response: everyone who signs up gets a call, and every call is logged.

A real presale and a marketing presale

A real presale gives a real discount on part of the apartments; industry reports talk about 15% to 20% of the apartments at an early price. It helps meet the early sales targets the accompanying bank sets, and it builds a price for the next stages. A marketing presale, in which the "early price" is the regular price, works once: buyers already recognise it, and the trust it wears away does not come back at the launch. One more thing: any deposit above 7% of the apartment price needs a security under the Sale Law, even when it is collected at a conference.

How much of the budget goes out before launch

A monthly spending curve for an Israeli project has not been published. In a detailed Australian example, about $530,000 of $720,000, almost three quarters of the marketing budget, went out before the launch. The logic is plain: the assets, the project page, the sales office and the presale are paid for before the first apartment is sold. Plan the cash flow accordingly, and do not let the launch begin with a budget that is already spent.

End of stock: the last apartments and a relaunch

The last apartments sell one by one, with a message for each: the floor, the direction, the balcony, the price. At this stage the warm audiences, the people who enquired and did not buy, are worth more than any new exposure. And a stalled project needs a new reason to come back to it: a financing offer, new material from the rising building, or a new market, for example buyers abroad who have not yet heard of it.

Advertising channels: what each one does and what is known about its price

Google captures demand that already exists: people searching for a project in the area. Facebook and Instagram create demand: people who were not searching and see the project at the right moment. The property listing sites bring buyers who are already in the process. In all three the test is the meeting, not the enquiry. A form that opens inside Facebook brings enquiries easily; a landing page with a project page filters more. Measure both to the meeting, and decide by that number.

Google or Facebook: how to split the budget

For the local market, no split tested on data has been published, and every agency recommends its own. For a campaign aimed at buyers abroad we suggest a starting point: 55% to Facebook and Instagram, 25% to Google, 15% to community press and newsletters and 5% to international property portals. It is not a formula; after the first weeks, move the split according to the meetings each channel actually brought.

How Google charges a daily budget

On Google, the average daily budget times 30.4 is the billing cap for the month. On a busy day Google can charge more than the daily budget, and at the end of the month the charge does not go over the cap. If you plan a monthly budget of ₪30,400, you set about ₪1,000 a day.

The only click price published in Israel

The only Israeli click price we found, ₪4 to ₪12 according to Google's keyword planner, is for the Hebrew search שיווק פרויקטים נדל״ן (real estate project marketing): developers looking for a marketing company, not buyers looking for an apartment. No dated Israeli figure has been published for buyers' searches, so any promise of a lead price before the campaign runs is an estimate.

Lead economics: cost per lead, per meeting and per sale

A cheap lead is not necessarily a good lead. The ladder to measure in every channel: cost per enquiry, cost per suitable enquiry, cost per meeting in the sales office, and cost per signed contract. The number that decides is the last one, and it almost never appears in the advertising agency's report.

The channel that looked cheap in the monthly report cost two and a half times as much per sale. A second example of the same kind: a ₪100 lead of which only 10% are suitable and 25% of those reach a meeting gives a meeting at ₪4,000, while a ₪350 lead that filters in advance, with 40% suitable and 50% of those arriving, gives a meeting at ₪1,750.

Speed of response is part of the price

The simplest sum on this page, and the most important: a ₪150 lead of which one in ten reaches a meeting gives a meeting at ₪1,500. If you call back after two days and only one in twenty arrives, the meeting costs ₪3,000. A late response doubles the cost of a meeting without one more shekel of media. That is why the response time has to be written into the agreement with whoever handles the enquiries, and measured every week.

Buy leads or run a campaign

A bought lead is fast: you pay and you receive. It suits a quick test or a small project, and it is worth checking whether it is exclusive or shared with competitors. A campaign of your own is slower at first, but it leaves an asset: audiences, a tracking code and data on what worked, which keep working in the next stage and in the next project. In most projects the right answer is a mix: bought leads for the opening, and a campaign that grows along the way.

What has not been published yet

Israeli conversion rates from real data, from lead to meeting and from meeting to sale, have not been published. Every ratio going around, including the ones on this page, is an example or an agency's claim. The only way to know your own project's numbers is to collect them from the first week: how many enquiries, how many suitable, how many meetings and how many sales, for each channel separately.

Handling enquiries: CRM, response and tracking to the contract

A good marketing budget gets burned by poor lead handling. The principle: one pipeline for every enquiry, from every channel, with a named owner and with tracking all the way to the contract.

  • One pipeline: Facebook, Google, the listing sites, WhatsApp, the phone and the sales office all feed the same CRM, and duplicates are merged.
  • Where each enquiry came from: a virtual number for each channel and tracking on the landing page, so that a phone call is also credited to its campaign.
  • Routing: each enquiry to the right rep, in the right language and in the buyer's hours. A buyer in Paris who writes in the evening needs someone who answers in French.
  • Screening in the first call: equity, a bank's approval in principle, and whether they need to sell a home first. Three questions that save empty meetings.
  • A follow-up sequence: messages and return calls by stage, nothing sent on Shabbat, and a record of every consent to receive marketing messages.
  • A weekly report: enquiries, suitable enquiries, meetings and sales for each channel, the cost of each, and the response time.
  • Owning the data: the list, the history and the consents belong to the developer, and stay with the developer when a supplier is replaced.

What no guide connects is the context: what the buyer did before getting in touch. On our project pages, when a buyer picks a floor and a direction on the building and taps through to WhatsApp, the message already carries the project's name, the floor and the direction, in the language of the page. The rep knows what the conversation is about before the first word. The privacy duties that apply to the lead list are in the chapter on the rules.

Selling Israeli property to foreign buyers: who buys, from where and what they need

Marketing to overseas buyers is not a translation of the Israeli campaign into English. You choose countries and cities, a language for each market and a defined audience, and you build them a page that answers the questions they ask from far away: price, tax, financing, the security on their payments, signing from abroad and handover. In the first quarter of 2026, foreign residents made 487 purchases in Israel, 18% more than a year earlier. The market is smaller than the local one, but almost no project speaks to it in its own language, and the competition for it is weak.

Trust from a distance

A buyer in Toronto or Lyon will not come to the sales office until they are convinced. What builds trust from far away: a virtual tour in which they pick a floor and see the view, a video call with a rep who speaks their language, answers on WhatsApp in the hours of their time zone, and full openness about the price, the payment schedule and the Sale Law security. How to compare marketing agency proposals: a checklist before you sign

The same total at the bottom of a proposal can hide completely different scopes. Before signing, ask every supplier the same questions, and get the answers in writing, in the same order, so the proposals can be laid side by side.

  1. What the price includes: media, management, production, or all three. And what it leaves out.
  2. Media fee: whether a percentage of the media budget is charged on top of the management fee.
  3. Whose accounts: the ad account, the tracking code, the landing page, the domain and the lead list. The right answer: yours.
  4. Set-up fees and appendices: what sits in an appendix and not on the first page.
  5. Renders and versions: how many views, how many rounds of corrections, and what each extra language costs.
  6. Refreshing by stage: who pays for new materials as the building rises and ahead of occupancy.
  7. Call tracking and listing sites: virtual numbers, short dial numbers and listing subscriptions, in or out.
  8. VAT: for every number in the proposal, before or after.
  9. The weekly report: enquiries, suitable enquiries, meetings and sales for each channel, and the cost of each.
  10. Response time: how soon every enquiry is called back, by whom, and in which languages.
  11. Exclusivity and tail: for how long, and what the supplier is owed on a deal signed after the agreement ended.
  12. When the commission is earned: at signing, at the first payment or at handover, and what happens if a deal is cancelled.
  13. Exit: what happens to the budget you committed if the project stops, and what each month of delay costs.
  14. Who checks the claims in the ads: view, distances, price, occupancy date.
  15. Licence: if the supplier takes a commission on sales, whether it is a licensed broker.
  16. What the supplier promises: a promised number of enquiries before the campaign runs is a warning sign. A promise of openness and of tracking to the sale is a good sign.

A list like this does not win a negotiation, but it makes sure you compare like with like. It also shows quickly which supplier is ready to commit to measurement.

Frequently asked questions about project marketing and budgets

How much does it cost to market a real estate project in Israel?

The usual Israeli anchor is about 1% of total sales value for the marketing itself, about 0.75% of it online, and 2% to 4% when the sales team is counted too. For 60 apartments at ₪2.4 million that is about ₪1.44 million for marketing, about ₪24,000 per apartment. These are marketing agencies' claims, not public research.

How much does a real estate campaign cost per month?

It depends on the target. A published sum: 20 meetings a month, 5 enquiries per meeting and ₪250 per enquiry make ₪25,000 of media a month, before management and production. With ₪1.44 million for marketing over 12 to 24 months of sales, the average is ₪60,000 to ₪120,000 a month for every marketing line.

What does a real estate lead cost in Israel?

An exclusive-lead price list published on 4 November 2026 quotes ₪128 to ₪173 before VAT, by pack size. Agencies report that a lead in a project campaign actually costs ₪300 to ₪800, sometimes over ₪1,000. For French and English speakers, immobilier.co.il starts at ₪69 a lead at the largest volumes. Compare cost per meeting, not lead price.

Which matters more, cost per lead or cost per meeting?

Cost per meeting, and in the end cost per sale. In a published example, a channel with ₪250 leads brought two sales on ₪100,000 of media, and a channel with ₪500 leads brought five. A sale through the seemingly cheap channel cost ₪50,000, and through the dear one ₪20,000. So measure every channel all the way to the contract.

Does the media budget include the marketing agency's fee?

Usually not. A proposal of ₪20,000 a month can be media alone, or media with management and production. Ask whether a percentage of the media budget is charged as a fee, and ask to see the ad account. In our plans you pay Facebook and Google directly, so you know what went to ads and what to the work.

Should the marketing budget be a percentage of expected sales?

As a starting point, yes: a share of sales value gives a sensible ceiling. The working budget comes from the sales target: how many sales, meetings per sale and enquiries per meeting. When working back lands far above the percentage, the problem is usually the offer or the response. Without a written target, no proposal can be tested.

When should project marketing start, and when do you start paying for media?

Research, pricing and the unit mix start at planning. Renders, the project page and a film are prepared 4 to 8 weeks before the soft launch. Media spend starts in the pre-launch with a small campaign for the interest list, then grows in the presale and the launch. The sales office and model apartment must be ready for the presale.

How long until the first enquiries arrive?

A digital campaign brings first enquiries in the first days, but a real picture takes weeks. immobilier.co.il, for example, sets up a campaign in 5 to 7 business days and delivers the enquiries over 4 to 6 weeks. Our target: the campaign live seven days after payment and your materials. From day one, measure to the meeting, not the enquiry.

Can we start with a small budget?

Yes, at the soft launch: a project page, a sign-up form and a small campaign to warm audiences test price and interest. Do not cut tracking and response: without tracking to the meeting, even a small budget burns. In our planner the ad budget starts at ₪3,000 a month and grows as you see which channels bring meetings.

Google or Facebook for a new development?

Both, for different jobs. Google catches people already searching for a project in the area; Facebook and Instagram create demand among people who were not. For a campaign to buyers abroad we suggest starting at 55% Facebook and Instagram, 25% Google, 15% community press and newsletters and 5% international portals, then moving the split by the meetings each channel brought.

A marketing company or the developer's own sales team?

A marketing company brings a ready team, experience and contacts, usually for a commission, a monthly fee or both; the rates are not published. An in-house team gives control and data, but needs hiring, training and evening cover. It depends on project size and how many you sell at once. Either way, keep the accounts and data in your name.

What is the difference between a real estate broker and a project marketing company in Israel?

An Israeli broker earns a fee only with a licence, a written order and being the effective cause of the deal; the quoted fee is 1% to 2% plus VAT. A project marketing company runs the whole sale for the developer, from pricing to contract, and is sometimes also a brokerage. Check the licence before you sign.

How fast should a lead be called back?

The same day. In a published example, a ₪150 lead of which one in ten reaches a meeting gives a meeting at ₪1,500. Call back after two days and only one in twenty arrives: the meeting costs ₪3,000. A late response doubles the cost of a meeting with no extra media, so write the response time into the agreement.

Does a presale hurt the project's price?

A real presale, with a real discount on part of the apartments, helps meet the accompanying bank's early sales targets and builds a price; industry reports mention 15% to 20% of the apartments. A fake presale, where the price does not change, wears away trust. And any deposit above 7% needs a security under the Sale Law.

How do you market an Israeli project to overseas buyers, and do you need an English site?

You need a page in the language of every market, not only English: French and Russian are whole markets. The page answers on price, tax, financing, the payment security and signing from abroad, and has a virtual tour. Choose countries and cities, reach communities through press and newsletters, and answer on WhatsApp in the buyer's hours. Measure each market separately.

How much purchase tax does a foreign resident pay on a new apartment from a developer?

8% on the part of the price up to ₪6,055,070 and 10% above it, from the first shekel, under the brackets in force from 16 January 2024 to 31 December 2026. For a new apartment from a developer the price includes VAT, and banks do not finance the tax. On ₪4 million, a foreign resident pays ₪320,000.

Can ads be targeted at Jewish communities abroad?

Not through Facebook and not through Google. Since 19 January 2022, Meta has not allowed ads to be aimed by religion or origin anywhere in the world, and on Google religion is a sensitive category. Aim by country, city and language, and reach communities through community press, newsletters, events and lists of people who agreed to receive information.
