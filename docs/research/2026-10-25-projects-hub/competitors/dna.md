## 4. Top 15 things that would make our page beat all ten

1. Content-first order on every width (our law): one H1 with the head term, then a short answer paragraph that already gives the numbers (how many projects listed, price range, which areas), then CTAs, then the grid. Socity has no H1, Ken Hator puts FAQ first, developers have no answer at all.
2. The biggest multi-developer inventory on one page. Every rival here is a single developer (or a broker with 12 unnamed projects); we list all marketed projects across developers, with the developer name and a link to the developer page on each card.
3. The densest honest card: from-price, price per sqm, room range, unit count, stage (permit / construction / occupancy), expected delivery, project type (new neighbourhood, TAMA 38, pinui-binui, chizuk), neighbourhood, developer, each with an "as of" date. Unknown fields are omitted, never guessed (honesty law). Nobody here shows delivery dates; only Dimri shows prices.
4. Real filters plus sort: city, neighbourhood/rova, rooms/type, price, stage, delivery year, renewal type, presale / immediate occupancy, and sort by price, per-sqm, delivery, newest. No competitor has sort; most have no filters at all.
5. A map of all projects with the official plan layers (Sde Dov, plan 3700, renewal neighbourhoods), not a separate map page as at Tidhar and Ken Hator. Must not capture page scroll (our smooth-scroll law).
6. A Tel Aviv section that beats Socity's table: per quarter/neighbourhood, project count, from-price range, per-sqm range and delivery range computed from our own listed projects, dated, with sources kept in the repo; link each row to our neighbourhood or city page.
7. The planning layer explained for buyers: Rova plan (quarters 3-6), Sde Dov, plan 3700, the renewal neighbourhoods, Kikar Yam, Ayalon roofing, with links to the municipal pages as sources and the marketed projects that sit inside each plan. This is what keeps the municipality and Socity in the AI overview.
8. Freshness as a feature: visible "updated" date, a "new launches this month" strip and a short dated market-news row (nadlan.com refreshes its homepage several times a month; Tidhar TLV was edited 6 days ago).
9. Two-sided buyer guidance: pros and cons, a due-diligence checklist (developer record, Sale Law guarantee, permit stage, spec, timelines, payment terms), presale and buying on paper, boutique vs large, living vs investment. Only Socity is two-sided today.
10. An FAQ that answers the full union above, including all seven People-also-ask questions, with FAQPage markup. No competitor has FAQ schema.
11. Structured data no one has: ItemList of the listed projects (name, url, address/geo, developer as Organization, published lowPrice in an Offer only where a price is published), plus CollectionPage and BreadcrumbList. AggregateRating only if it is real (Hagag's self-rating is a risk, not a model).
12. Status and track record: show marketed, presale, under construction and recently delivered projects with a status filter, and per-developer counts (Hagag shows 10 occupied towers as trust; Gabay and Ken Hator publish company totals).
13. Chips that mirror the related searches: on paper, up to 2 million, presale, immediate occupancy, by the sea, south Tel Aviv, Ramat Gan, centre, in marketing. Each chip is a filter state on the same page, not a new URL (URL word law, no rival URLs, cannibalisation check before any new slug).
14. A link architecture rivals only half-build: hub to city pages, to Tel Aviv neighbourhood sections, to project pages, to developer pages, to the buying and urban-renewal guides; each card links to its project page and its developer. Ken Hator's farm of sub-area pages shows Google rewards that sub-intent; we cover it with sections and existing pages first.
15. Tools none of the ten has: side-by-side compare of projects, price-per-sqm and mortgage calculators, new-launch and price-change alerts, favourites, one-tap WhatsApp (our existing law), and our per-project 3D/view and map stage as the moat. All inside the one hub, server-rendered (the municipality's JS-only tiles show the risk of client-only content).

Findings to report only (no action implied): Dimri has no canonical tag while linking filtered URLs; Tidhar's Tel Aviv hub has no meta description and omits Zirelson that its national hub lists; Hagag's phone CTA is a broken relative link; Google's AI overview quotes YAMA TLV at 8,700,000 while Dimri's own page shows that number on the Netanya YAMA Ir Yamim card.
