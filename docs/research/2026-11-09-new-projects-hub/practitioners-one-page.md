# One page for one intent: what SEO practitioners have published

Research date: 2026-11-09. Read-only web research for the /projects/ hub (HAD-689).
Scope: practitioners only (named SEO people, agencies, data studies, forum threads). Google
spokespeople and Google documentation were deliberately left out as evidence.

How to read this file
- Every item has: who, their role, the date, the link, what they did, what they measured.
- Evidence class per item: **measured** (before and after numbers published), **study** (data set,
  usually correlation), **observed** (practitioner reports a result but gives no numbers),
  **opinion** (experience-based advice, no data). Anything I could not check is marked **unverified**.
- Search Engine Land returned 403 to the fetch tool, so two SEL articles were read through a
  mirror or a forum thread about them. This is noted where it applies.
- Most published merge cases used redirects as part of the method. This file reports that as a
  fact of the case only. It makes no redirect recommendation (owner law).

## Part 1. Case studies: several competing pages merged into one, with results

**1. Ahrefs, broken link building guides (measured, no exact numbers)**
Joshua Hardwick, then Head of Content at Ahrefs, reviewed by Patrick Stox. Guide updated 2024-01-10.
https://ahrefs.com/blog/keyword-cannibalization/
In 2018 Ahrefs had two guides on the same topic. They wrote one new guide that covered both and
published it on one of the old addresses. After the merge only that page got traffic, and it got more
than the two pages had together. The chart has no numbers in the text. The same article gives a
counter case: two similar "submit your website" guides were NOT merged, because the stronger page
already ranked first and had reached its traffic ceiling, so a merge could only lose the small page's
traffic. Hardwick also advises against deleting, de-optimizing or noindexing pages just because they
share keywords.

**2. Ahrefs multiple rankings study: most overlap is harmless (study)**
Mateusz Makosiewicz, SEO and Marketing Educator at Ahrefs, reviewed by Ryan Law and Patrick Stox.
Published 2024-02-14. https://ahrefs.com/blog/multiple-rankings-study/
About 9,700 cases where ahrefs.com ranked with two or more pages for one keyword. A hand check of 80
of them found only 1 that needed action. Merging helped only where the pages were close in content and
Google kept swapping them in and out of the results. Where two pages serve different needs and hold
steady, merging brought no benefit. This is the strongest counter-evidence against merging by reflex.

**3. Backlinko, two "SEO tools" articles merged (measured, thin method detail)**
Shreelekha Singh for Backlinko (Semrush owned). Page updated 2026-02-05, original date not shown.
https://backlinko.com/keyword-cannibalization
A long "SEO tools" list and a newer "best free SEO tools" list served nearly the same intent. They were
merged into one URL. In the eight weeks after, traffic was up 466% against the same weeks a year
before. The page calls it both clicks and traffic, and a year-over-year comparison mixes in other
growth, so treat the size of the number with care.

**4. Keyword Insights for Zerodown, US real-estate portal (measured, partial)**
Andy Chadwick, co-founder of Keyword Insights. Page updated 2024-10-25, work date not stated.
https://www.keywordinsights.ai/blog/keyword-cannibalization-case-study-no-1/
The closest case to ours. Zerodown had about 50 million pages. Property-type landing pages such as
"homes for sale in", "houses for sale in" and "properties for sale in" competed with each other. A
spot check showed about 70% top-10 overlap between those three phrases. The team clustered 524
keywords by shared search results, picked one page per cluster and cut the property-type pages from
413 to 85 (about 15 million URLs across states, towns and postcodes). Reported result: organic traffic
up 110%. No absolute figures and no timeframe. Important detail: they merged SYNONYMS of the same
property intent; the article implies the location layer (state, town, postcode) was kept. That is my
reading, the article does not say it in plain words (**partly unverified**).

**5. Chris Long, three consolidation cases (measured, no timeframes)**
Chris Long, then VP of Marketing at Go Fish Digital (now co-founder of Nectiv). Search Engine Land,
2017-11-14/15. https://searchengineland.com/3-case-studies-duplicate-content-consolidation-286263
(read through a mirror because SEL blocked the fetch).
A franchise directory. Case one: two identical "low-cost franchises" pages merged; the kept page's
organic traffic rose more than 200%. Case two: "food franchises" and "fast food franchises", which
Google treated as the same thing, were tied together with a canonical tag while both stayed open to
users; traffic to the main page rose 47%. Case three: URL parameter duplicates cleaned, traffic over
800% above baseline, but the author says other work also contributed.

**6. Inflow, two ecommerce pages merged (measured, small numbers)**
Alex Juel, Inflow (ecommerce SEO agency). Published 2022-07-18.
https://www.goinflow.com/blog/content-consolidation-pruning-case-study/
Five-week test against a control period: clicks per day 0.88 to 1.5, impressions per day 306 to 590,
queries per day 67 to 106, average position 50 to 47. Very small volumes, and the article itself mixes
up which metric rose 70% and which 92%. Useful only as direction.

**7. Amsive, publisher recovery through consolidation (measured, confounded)**
Cullen Smith and Paul VanHevel, Amsive (US digital agency). Published 2021-07-21.
https://www.amsive.com/insights/seo/core-algorithm-recovery-tactics-content-consolidation-part-1/
A publisher hurt by core updates merged, removed or improved hundreds of overlapping URLs each week.
After the June 2021 core update, clicks rose 35% (about 3 million) against the prior period. The gain
coincides with a core update, so it cannot be credited to merging alone. Method worth copying: pick
the page that already covers the topic most broadly as the host, and move the useful parts of the
weaker pages into it under their own subheadings.

**8. Joy Hawkins, boilerplate city pages removed (observed, no numbers)**
Joy Hawkins, owner of Sterling Sky (local SEO agency). Search Engine Land, 2021-04-30, read through a
mirror: https://marketing-and-tech-news.blogspot.com/2021/04/case-study-more-content-is-not-always.html
Discussion: https://localsearchforum.com/threads/joys-article-more-content-is-not-always-better-why-the-increase.57812/ (2021-05-10 to 12)
A Florida disability law firm had dozens of template city pages (city name swapped, no links, no
traffic). They were removed and their internal links pointed to the one main topic page. Local pack
and organic rankings for the main phrase rose within weeks. In the forum thread practitioners
disagreed on why: Carrie Hill (who worked on it) credited the internal links now feeding one page;
Phil Rozek doubted that removal alone explains a jump.

**9. Joy Hawkins, two offices in one city served by one page (observed, no numbers)**
Joy Hawkins, Sterling Sky. Published 2025-03-31.
https://www.sterlingsky.ca/expand-in-a-large-city-without-hurting-seo/
A home-services company opened a second office in Charlotte. Instead of a second Charlotte page, both
offices were put on one city-wide page. Organic rankings across the new area rose sharply. Lesson: same
place plus same service is one intent and gets one page.

## Part 2. How practitioners make one page rank for hundreds of queries of one intent

**10. Grouping by shared search results (SERP overlap): origin and thresholds (method, practitioners disagree on the number)**
- Origin: the Russian SEO Alexey Chekushin set out the method in 2015 (article "Clustering alphabet",
  searchengines.ru, 2015-12-03), with the Just-Magic tool the same year. Source: Wikipedia summary,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyword_clustering (I did not read the Russian original, **unverified**
  beyond Wikipedia). Two keywords belong on one page if Google shows enough of the same URLs in the top
  10 for both. "Soft" compares every phrase to the biggest phrase; "hard" requires every pair to match.
- Keyword Insights default: 3 shared URLs puts two phrases in one group.
  https://docs.keywordinsights.ai/learning-center/the-features/keyword-clustering/the-advanced-settings/keyword-grouping-accuracy (undated docs)
- Serpstat, Ekaterina Hordiienko (Customer Support and Education Specialist), 2022-04-15: compares top
  30, with levels of 3 (weak), 8 (medium) and 12 (strong) shared URLs.
  https://serpstat.com/blog/keyword-clustering-algorithms-and-approaches-of-popular-seo-tools
- Morteza Najafi, freelance SEO, Oncrawl, 2025-06-03: recommends 4 to 6 shared URLs in the top 10; 2 to 3
  gives loose groups, 7 and up near-identical ones. Says to check the groups by hand.
  https://www.oncrawl.com/on-page-seo/keyword-clustering-using-python-serp-api/
- Nozzle documentation (via search snippet, **unverified**): 4 shared URLs is a loose cluster, 6 to 8 tight.
Where they agree: 3 to 4 shared URLs is a broad, "pillar" grouping; 5 and up is strict. Nobody has a
published test showing which threshold earns more traffic. The number is a dial, not a law.

**11. Ahrefs: one main keyword, many secondary ones, check the results page (method + example)**
Mateusz Makosiewicz, Ahrefs, 2023-01-11. https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-many-seo-keywords/
Pick one main phrase and cover its subtopics, synonyms and long variants on the same page. To decide if
two phrases share a page, compare their Google results; near-identical results mean one page. Their
own example page targets a 1,400-search phrase and ranks for 463 keywords, 156 of them in the top 10.

**12. Koray Tuğberk Gübür, topical authority (self-reported results, disputed)**
Koray Tuğberk Gübür, founder of Holistic SEO. "Importance of Topical Authority", published
2021-02-11, updated 2022-11-24. https://www.holisticseo.digital/?p=4946
Method: map every question and sub-intent of a topic, order them in a clear heading hierarchy, connect
related entities, and cover more intents with fewer, deeper pages. He warns that two pages chasing the
same main query split the ranking signals. In his words, "you will create fewer pages to satisfy users
more." Results he reports from his own Search Console: one site from 0 to about 128,000 visits in 123
days, a second site to 200,000+ monthly visits in six months. No outside audit of these numbers exists.
Counter view: Casey Keith, 2025-08-28, https://caseykeith.substack.com/p/exposing-the-hype , collects
negative reviews of his course and argues the jargon repackages relevance and internal linking. That
piece attacks the course, not the case data, and its comments dispute it. His newer column on query
templates (Search Engine Land, about 2026-08-12, **date unverified**, SEL blocked the fetch) extends the
idea to repeating patterns such as "[thing] in [place]".

**13. Internal links: variety of anchor text (study, correlation only)**
Cyrus Shepard, Zyppy. Page last updated 2026-02-23 (first published earlier, original date **unverified**).
https://zyppy.com/seo/internal-links-study/
About 23 million internal links on 1,800 sites, 520,000 URLs, matched to Search Console clicks. Pages
with more different anchor texts pointing at them got more clicks. Pages with at least one exact-match
internal anchor had about five times more clicks. Clicks rose with link count up to about 40 to 45 links,
then fell. The author says it is a correlation study and should be read as direction, not proof.

**14. Sections as separately scored passages (single example, method)**
Mike King, founder and CEO of iPullRank. Published 2026-01-15. https://ipullrank.com/misinformation-about-chunking
Argues that search and AI systems score passages, not just whole pages, so each section should answer
one sub-question on its own. His one test with his own tool: splitting a paragraph that mixed two
subjects into two paragraphs raised the match score for one subject by 19%, and adding a heading raised
the other by a further 18%. One example, not a ranking test. He openly disagrees with advice from
Google on this point.

## Part 3. How many keywords does one top page rank for

**15. Ahrefs, 3 million queries (study)**
Tim Soulo, CMO of Ahrefs. Published 2017-05-08. https://ahrefs.com/blog/also-rank-for-study/
The average page that ranks first also ranks in the top 10 for nearly 1,000 other phrases; the median is
about 400. Pages that rank first for a 10,000+ search phrase almost never rank first for a second phrase
that big; ranking first for two or three 1,000+ phrases is common. Pages with more links and longer
content rank for more phrases (correlation).

**16. Ahrefs, the first position is not where the traffic is (study)**
Tim Soulo, Ahrefs. Published 2019-03-28. https://ahrefs.com/blog/ranking-number-one-is-overrated/
Over 100,000 phrases: the page in first place got the most total search traffic only 49% of the time,
because pages lower down rank for more long variants. His conclusion: aim at total traffic of the
topic, cover it in depth, match the intent.

## Part 4. One hub with city sections, or separate city pages

**17. Lily Ray: template pages multiplied across places fail over time (study)**
Lily Ray, SEO consultant. Published 2026-05-13. https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks
More than 220 sites that used AI content platforms: 54% lost 30% or more of their peak traffic, 39%
lost half or more. One of the named failure patterns is a single template repeated across many states,
cities or languages with little unique content per page, which she says has drawn algorithmic trouble
for about ten years.

**18. Local SEO camp: separate city pages can work, but few and unique (opinion and observation, they disagree with each other)**
- Darren Shaw, founder of Whitespark, 2025-02-10: build separate pages for the 10 to 15 most important
  cities, each unique and linked from navigation; no test data.
  https://whitespark.ca/blog/rank-in-cities-with-no-physical-address/
- Colan Nielsen, Sterling Sky, 2023-01-20: one business with 35 service-area pages that were about 84%
  identical still brought traffic and leads; says it often fails in competitive markets; no numbers.
  https://www.sterlingsky.ca/service-area-pages-duplicate-content/
- This conflicts with item 8 (same agency, 2021) and item 17. The forum view that bridges them: what
  matters is how the pages are added, linked and made useful, not the count.
- Forum anecdotes (Reddit r/SEO and Moz Q&A, various dates, no numbers, **unverified**): the common
  answer is a mix: one strong hub covers most places, and a place earns its own page only when it has
  real local substance of its own.

**19. SearchPilot controlled split tests on location pages (measured, controlled)**
Noe Servin, SearchPilot (SEO A/B testing platform). Published 2022-05-04, updated 2023-08-09.
https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/seo-testing-local-landing-pages
Rare controlled evidence. On about 8,000 regional pages, linking each page to its six nearest regions
raised organic traffic about 7%. Adding review stars did nothing. Putting "near you" in titles lost about
2%. A separate SearchPilot test (search snippet only, **unverified** detail) removed a map from location
pages and lost about 7%. None of their published tests compares one hub against city pages directly.

## Part 5. Real-estate and listing-portal examples

**20. Real-estate specific write-ups (mostly agency reports, weak to medium evidence)**
- Zerodown (item 4) is the strongest: merge property-type synonyms, keep places, +110%.
- Matthew Woodward, Search Logistics, published 2018-06-20 (campaign 2015-11 to 2016-07):
  https://www.searchlogistics.com/case-studies/real-estate/ . A UK estate agent with thin city and
  region pages. They filled those pages with unique content and merged pages that competed for the
  same phrase. Organic sessions 591 to 1,146, leads 72 to 210. Many changes at once, so no single cause.
- Reflect Digital (UK agency), undated (about 2025):
  https://www.reflectdigital.co.uk/blog/top-trends-in-property-seo . For developers: national, county,
  town, then development pages. Argument: several developments in one town compete with each other, so
  one town page should be the single entry point that compares them. No results given (**opinion**).
- Charli, Marketing Manager at Adaptable, 2026-03-25:
  https://weareadaptable.com/insights/homebuilder-seo-website-strategy/ . Regional hubs that pull live
  developments, prices and availability; finished developments are kept live and indexable in a "past
  developments" section rather than deleted. Early result +68% click-through and +8% average position,
  no baseline (**weak**).
- SUSO Digital for Housing.com (India), undated: https://susodigital.com/work/201-organic-traffic-for-leading-indian-property-portal/ .
  Flattened site structure so city rental pages sit closer to the home page, plus links; +201% organic
  traffic, no baseline or timeframe (**weak**).
- I found no practitioner teardown of how Zillow, Realtor.com, Rightmove or Madlan build their
  "new homes" hubs. Kevin Indig's Growth Memo Zillow deep dives are paywalled (**not read**).

## Where practitioners disagree

1. **Is overlap a problem at all?** Ahrefs (items 1, 2) says almost never, merge only when pages are near
   twins and Google swaps them. Israeli agency writers (item 21) and most agency case studies treat
   merging as the default fix.
2. **How much overlap means one page?** 3 shared URLs (Keyword Insights), 4 to 6 (Najafi), 8 of 30
   (Serpstat). No one has tested which threshold wins.
3. **City pages.** Lily Ray (2026) and Joy Hawkins (2021) show template city pages failing; Colan Nielsen
   (2023) and Darren Shaw (2025) say a limited set of city pages works. The bridge: few, unique, linked.
4. **Topical authority.** Koray's results are self-reported; critics say it is relevance under a new name.
5. **Can one page win everything?** Soulo's data (item 15) says a single page very rarely ranks first for
   more than one big head phrase. A hub can own one head intent plus hundreds of long variants, but city
   phrases whose Google results differ behave like separate intents.

## What this means for one /projects/ page

1. Before writing, test overlap in Israel for each phrase family (new projects, new apartments from a developer, immediate occupancy, off-plan): 4 or more shared top-10 URLs means one section on /projects/.
2. Run the same test for each city phrase against the main phrase; high overlap means a city section on the hub, low overlap means Google treats that city as its own intent (Zerodown kept places, item 4).
3. Give every sub-intent and every city its own heading and a self-contained answer with real data (projects, developer, price range, delivery date), not a sentence with the city name swapped (items 14, 17, 21).
4. Expect the page to win one head phrase plus hundreds of long variants, not first place for every big phrase; judge it by total queries and clicks in Search Console (items 15, 16).
5. Point internal links to /projects/ with varied, natural anchors (the synonyms and city phrases), added gradually, not stamped sitewide in one day (items 13, 19).
6. Keep sold-out and delivered project pages live and linked from the hub as "delivered" rather than deleting them (items 1, 20).
7. Do NOT mint many new city pages from one template; that is the failure pattern in items 8 and 17.
8. Do NOT treat every page that shares a keyword as a rival; check that Google actually swaps them before acting, since Ahrefs found 79 of 80 cases harmless (item 2).
9. Do NOT delete, de-optimize or noindex old pages as a cannibalization fix (item 1); this file makes no redirect recommendation, by owner law.
10. Do NOT judge pages by click count or read one agency's percentage as a promise; most numbers here are uncontrolled, and only SearchPilot (item 19) ran a controlled test.
