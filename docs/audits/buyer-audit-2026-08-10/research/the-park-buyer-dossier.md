# THE PARK, Bnei Brak — buyer due-diligence dossier and product specification

**Research cut-off:** 2026-08-10  
**Buyer persona:** a US startup evaluating an Israeli headquarters, with no prior knowledge of Israel, Bnei Brak, local leasing conventions, local taxes, transport, or Hebrew-language planning records.  
**Evidence rule:** no public claim is treated as a building fact merely because it appears in a rendering, brochure, owner page, broker listing, or 3D model.  
**Contact rule:** no owner, broker, municipality, consultant, retailer, or transit operator was contacted during this research.

## 1. Executive decision

### Current verdict: candidate property, not yet decision-ready

THE PARK appears to be a large, architecturally distinctive office-and-retail project beside Bnei Brak railway station, with broad floor plates, terraces, an on-site retail base, and an owner-stated LEED Platinum ambition. Those are meaningful reasons to keep it on a shortlist.

It is **not possible, from the public evidence reviewed, to answer the questions required to approve a headquarters location, price an occupancy, select a floor, or sign an LOI**. The critical blockers are:

1. There is no authoritative, timestamped floor availability stack. The owner page advertises three zone archetypes—not specific legally numbered floors—and does not distinguish available, held, under LOI, leased, delivered, or unavailable space.
2. The owner materials conflict on whether the tower has 44 or 52 floors; an official company report describes 45 office floors above three retail floors. The 2023 floor-plan PDFs and 2024 brochure also shift every zone by seven floors.
3. Public move-in dates range from Q4 2024 and Q1 2025 to Q3 2026 and 2027. The latest official investor presentation reviewed expects project completion in 2026; the live Allied page still says “under construction.”
4. There is no owner quote for rent, management fees, parking, fit-out, free rent, deposits, indexation, or total occupancy cost. Broker asking indications are not a substitute.
5. The public package omits the technical data a startup needs: usable area, efficiency, occupancy capacity, floor-to-floor and clear height, slab load, electrical density, backup power, HVAC capacity and hours, telecom diversity, elevator performance, loading, security, and completed accessibility/fire approvals.
6. “Minutes from the Green Line” can be read as a current benefit, but the official NTA timetable places full Green Line commercial operation in 2030 and the Pinhas Rosen bridge completion in 2029. Current and future transit must be separated.
7. The project is marketed as LEED Platinum, but no public certification ID or scorecard was located in the reviewed owner materials. “Designed/built to a level” must not be presented as certified without the certificate.

## 2. Evidence taxonomy used in this dossier

- **Verified primary disclosure:** stated in an owner statutory filing, municipality record, official transit dataset, or other authoritative primary source. It remains limited to the scope and date of that source.
- **Owner-claimed:** stated on an owner project page or marketing brochure but not independently demonstrated by a certificate, permit, executed availability schedule, or as-built document.
- **Derived from primary open data:** computed from an official dataset; methodology and snapshot date must remain visible.
- **Third-party indication:** broker, retailer, contractor, press, or market-research information. Useful for orientation, never a substitute for owner or authority confirmation.
- **Missing / unresolved:** no reliable answer found, or primary sources conflict. The product must show this state explicitly; it must not convert “unknown” into “available,” zero, “coming soon,” or a guessed value.

## 4. What a skeptical headquarters buyer can and cannot establish

### 4.1 Identity, ownership and development state

**What is supportable**

- Allied Real Estate and Amot present THE PARK as their joint project. Amot’s statutory materials show a 50% company share.
- Allied identifies Yaski Mor Sivan as architect. The owner brochure and façade contractor describe an architectural envelope including a closed double-skin curtain wall, cable wall and concrete honeycomb elements.
- The 2025 annual report says the retail component had been handed over and stores had opened, while final finishes and marketing of the office tower continued.
- Amot’s Q1 2026 investor presentation reports 86,920 sqm, 100% build/marketable area, total construction cost of approximately ILS 1.3 billion, expected completion in 2026, expected NOI of ILS 92 million and expected yield of 7.1%. These are project-level investor metrics, not a tenant price quote or guarantee.

**What remains unresolved**

- Precise legal address, parcel, permitted use mix, floor numbering, certificate status and usable entrances.
- Whether any office area is legally occupiable today and, if so, which levels and under what conditions.
- Whether “45 office floors above three retail floors,” “44 floors,” and “52 floors” count roof plant, lobby, lifestyle, parking or marketing levels differently.

**Website treatment**

The project header should display one canonical legal identity card. Until reconciled, show: **“Address and floor numbering under owner verification”**, not a false single-value answer. The technical data room should preserve older documents but mark them superseded.

### 4.2 Floor programme and the meaning of a click

The public marketing documents support three physical floor archetypes:

| Zone | 2023 plan numbering | 2024 brochure numbering | Example gross floor | Terrace data shown | Public move-in claim |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| LOWZONE | 3–11 | 10–18 | 2,620 sqm | 35 sqm + 110 sqm | Q4 2024 on live LOW page |
| MIDZONE | 12–28 | 19–35 | 1,800 sqm | 33 sqm + 33 sqm | 2027 on live MID page |
| HIGHZONE | 29–44 | 36–51 | 1,740 sqm | 73 sqm + 55 sqm | 2027 on live HIGH page |

The brochure also states approximately 1,600–3,000 gross sqm per office floor, 42 office floors, two retail floors, one lifestyle/welfare floor and two roof technical floors. Elsewhere it states 44 floors. These are marketing descriptors, not a legal stack.

**A floor click is not valid unless it answers, on the same page:**

1. What immutable suite/floor ID did I select?
2. What legal floor, elevator-button label, marketing floor and zone correspond to it?
3. Is it available now, held, under offer/LOI, leased, delivered, unavailable, or unknown?
4. When was that status confirmed, by whom, and when does a hold expire?
5. Is the offer a full floor, subdivision, contiguous multi-floor block, or not divisible?
6. Gross rentable, certified usable/net, common-area load and efficiency percentage.
7. Terrace/storage allocation and whether they are charged in rent, management fee and arnona.
8. Exact compass orientation, view cones, present obstructions and known planned obstructions—not only a generic panorama.
9. Core, columns, façade depth, refuge/shelter, toilets, risers, shafts, electrical rooms, freight path and accessible route.
10. Clear ceiling height, raised-floor zone, structural loading, planning grid and maximum permitted occupancy.
11. Delivery condition, landlord works, fit-out start, statutory occupancy and earliest operational date.
12. Rent and area basis, service charge, arnona assumption, parking, fit-out allowance, VAT and indexed total monthly/annual cost.
13. Downloadable current plan, technical sheet, measurement certificate, offer and evidence links.
14. A visible **Ask about this floor / Request floor pack** action that preserves the selected floor ID and every filter in the lead payload.

### 4.3 Availability — the most serious commercial gap

The public owner interface advertises 1,740, 1,800 and 2,620 sqm zone examples as “for rent.” It does not disclose which individual floors are vacant. The official annual materials reviewed disclose contracts for approximately 13,000 sqm in the retail context; they do not provide a signed office availability stack.

Therefore:

- Do not infer that all floors in a displayed zone are available.
- Do not infer that a missing price means “contact for price” while showing an available colour.
- Do not turn the owner’s zone card into a floor-level status.
- Do not infer office vacancy from a press article saying office leases were not announced.
- Default every floor to `unknown` until a current owner record says otherwise.

**Required availability feed**

At minimum: `building_id`, `legal_floor_id`, `elevator_label`, `marketing_label`, `suite_id`, `zone`, `gross_rentable_sqm`, `usable_sqm`, `divisibility`, `contiguous_group_id`, `availability_state`, `available_from`, `fitout_access_date`, `occupancy_certificate_scope`, `asking_rent`, `service_charge_budget`, `parking_ratio`, `hold_expiry`, `source_document_id`, `source_owner`, `verified_at`, `next_review_at`.

Canonical public state machine:

`unknown → verified_available → soft_hold → under_offer → under_loi → leased → delivered`

Private source states are adapted, not exposed as a competing enum: `owner_reserved → not_marketed` with reason `owner_reserved`; `not_released → not_marketed`; `under_construction → not_marketed` until an owner-verified marketed/delivery state exists; `unavailable → unavailable`. `not_marketed` and `unavailable` are canonical side statuses. Every transition needs actor, timestamp and source. Expired soft holds degrade to `unknown` unless an owner-controlled workflow supplies the next verified state; a front-end timer never promotes availability.

### 4.4 Area, capacity and test-fit

The public plans provide gross archetype sizes and some dimensions, but no certified net usable area or adopted measurement standard. A US buyer cannot translate 1,740–2,620 “gross sqm” into desks, rooms, cost per workstation or US rentable square feet without:

- the exact Israeli lease measurement convention;
- full common-area loading and exclusions;
- core and service areas;
- subdivision rules and dedicated versus shared toilets;
- legal occupant load and egress capacity;
- current architectural CAD/BIM rather than a marketing PDF.

**Product requirement:** the selector must report both sqm and sq ft, clearly labelled `rentable gross` and `usable/net`; never use a single unqualified “area.” Show efficiency as `usable ÷ rentable gross`. A scenario tool should allow headcount, attendance ratio, desks, meeting rooms, labs, secure rooms, kitchens and growth reserve, then produce a test-fit range. It must never claim an executable layout unless it is based on current CAD/BIM and validated against code, egress, accessibility, MEP and landlord rules.

### 4.5 Price and total occupancy cost

**Owner-published commercial terms found:** none at floor level.

**Third-party asking indication:** a TAG brokerage page advertises approximately ILS 75/sqm/month rent, ILS 18/sqm/month management and ILS 1,200/month parking, with offered blocks between roughly 1,000 and 4,000 sqm. A related listing contains inconsistent availability language and no reliable floor number. These values are only broker asking indications at the access date—not achieved rents, owner quotes, net-effective rents or a confirmed Park offer.

**Municipal arnona:** the official Bnei Brak 2026 order publishes annual rates per assessed sqm. Examples relevant to an office inquiry include:

| Potential classification | 2026 annual rate | Monthly equivalent | Qualification warning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Standard office | ILS 373.86/sqm | ILS 31.16/sqm | Do not assume a lower category without an assessment. |
| Office over 5,000 sqm | ILS 311.79/sqm | ILS 25.98/sqm | Threshold application and measured holding must be confirmed. |
| Office over 10,000 sqm | ILS 263.84/sqm | ILS 21.99/sqm | Same. |
| Office over 15,000 sqm | ILS 211.09/sqm | ILS 17.59/sqm | Same. |
| Office over 20,000 sqm | ILS 193.46/sqm | ILS 16.12/sqm | Same. |
| Qualifying software house | ILS 194.67/sqm | ILS 16.22/sqm | The order’s definition is restrictive; “startup” does not automatically qualify. |
| Covered paid parking | ILS 79.23/sqm/year | ILS 6.60/sqm | This is a parking-area tax classification, not the tenant’s parking licence price. |
| Covered free parking | ILS 33.06/sqm/year | ILS 2.76/sqm | Same. |

The municipal definition of non-residential measured area is broad and can include walls, covered/uncovered areas used by the holder, balconies, archives, basements, shelters and other service areas. Only the actual municipal assessment and lease allocation can establish the tenant’s liability.

**Illustrative—not quoted—1,000 sqm occupancy frame using the broker indications:**

- Asking base rent: ILS 75,000/month.
- Asking management fee: ILS 18,000/month.
- Arnona illustration: approximately ILS 16,223/month at the published software-house rate or ILS 31,155/month at the standard-office rate, before resolving assessed area and eligibility.
- Recurring subtotal before parking: approximately ILS 109,223–124,155/month.
- Add parking, electricity, water, after-hours HVAC, internet, security extras, cleaning, insurance, fit-out amortisation, indexation and VAT.

This illustration must not be shown as a Park quote. The website calculator should force the user to acknowledge the source and assumption basis, and should distinguish `asking`, `owner-confirmed`, `executed comparable`, and `user-entered` values.

**Missing commercial inputs**

Base rent basis; net-effective rent; index and base month; VAT; security deposit/bank guarantee; service-charge budget and exclusions; year-end reconciliation; cap on controllable expenses; parking ratio and allocation; EV charging tariff; fit-out contribution; rent-free period; early access; after-hours HVAC; utilities; storage; signage; insurance; legal/agency fees; restoration; expansion rights; delay remedies.

### 4.6 Delivery, occupancy and fit-out

“Shell” is shown on Allied’s LOW/MID/HIGH pages, but a shell label does not answer what the landlord delivers. The buyer needs a room-by-room and system-by-system delivery matrix:

- slab/floor tolerance and raised-floor allowance;
- ceiling and exposed-services condition;
- perimeter and core walls;
- fire sprinklers, smoke detection and tenant branch scope;
- electrical intake, panels, metering and spare capacity;
- HVAC plant, distribution termination, controls and after-hours operation;
- plumbing and pantry connection points;
- toilets, showers and accessibility scope;
- telecom entry, risers and meet-me rooms;
- lighting, emergency lighting and signage;
- permits, landlord approvals and inspection responsibility;
- waste, deliveries, noisy-work hours, lifts and contractor access;
- fit-out deposit, insurance and reinstatement.

Dates must be represented as separate fields: `permit_occupancy_date`, `landlord_possession_date`, `tenant_early_access_date`, `fitout_ready_date`, `target_business_open_date`, and `contractual_long_stop_date`. Public materials currently mix these concepts.

### 4.8 Sustainability claim

Allied and Amot market the project at LEED Platinum level/standard. The public package reviewed does not show a USGBC/GBCI project ID, rating system/version, certificate date or scorecard. The website must use one of these precise states:

- `targeting LEED Platinum`;
- `registered / pre-certified`, with ID and date;
- `certified LEED Platinum`, with ID, version, points and certificate link;
- `owner claim — evidence requested`.

Until evidence is supplied, use the last state. Do not render a certification seal that implies final certification.

## 5. Location, access, transport and employee commute

### 5.1 What the official/open data supports

- The building is marketed on HaLehi Street at the northern edge of Bnei Brak, by the Yarkon and near Ayalon Mall and the Ramat Hahayal employment area.
- A retail-centre Waze link decodes to approximately **32.103875, 34.829299**. This is a navigation point, not a legal entrance coordinate.
- In the Ministry of Transport GTFS snapshot retrieved on 2026-08-10, the Israel Railways Bnei Brak station point was approximately 130 metres straight-line from that navigation point; a bus stop named for Bnei Brak railway station was approximately 103 metres straight-line away. Straight-line distance is not a verified walking route.
- For active services on Tuesday 2026-08-11, the GTFS snapshot contained 85 train departure events at Bnei Brak station from 05:52 to 24:27. Between 07:00–10:00 the mean departure-event gap was about 10.2 minutes and the maximum 15 minutes; between 16:00–19:00 the mean was about 12.2 minutes and the maximum 23 minutes. These are station departure events across directions, not a promise of frequency to a particular destination.
- The same dated feed showed nearby bus services including Dan routes 4, 9 and 81 and Metropoline 524. Route 81 had frequent peak-period departures in the sampled stop data. A live journey planner is still required because routes, days, stop assignments and disruptions change.
- NTA’s current Green Line page states full commercial operation in 2030 and the southern section in 2028. Its Pinhas Rosen bridge page estimates completion in 2029. The Pinhas Rosen/Green Line benefit is therefore future infrastructure, not a current ride-to-office option as of this research cut-off.

Indicative scheduled service in the same Tuesday snapshot, at the sampled railway-adjacent stop(s):

| Route/operator | Scheduled peak indication | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| 81 / Dan | 24 departures between 07:00–10:00 in the sampled stop data; mean gap approximately 7.2 minutes; PM mean approximately 8.9 minutes | Stop/direction and loop pattern must be checked for the employee’s destination. |
| 4 / Dan | Approximately 15–22 minute AM gaps depending on sampled direction | Not a promise; day and direction specific. |
| 9 / Dan | Approximately 22–24 minute sampled peak gaps | Same. |
| 524 / Metropoline | Approximately 30–60 minute sampled gaps on the Ra’anana service | Same; route and timetable can change. |

These figures are schedule-derived, not observed reliability. The website should query a live origin/destination journey rather than expose the averages as a permanent frequency badge.

### 5.2 What the brochure claims but does not prove

The brochure claims approximately two minutes to the railway station, two minutes to a bicycle path, three minutes to the Pinhas Rosen Green Line station, five minutes to a future transport terminal, and two vehicular entrances. None of those time claims supplies:

- the exact start/end entrance;
- pedestrian crossings and signal wait;
- barriers, stairs, gradients or step-free route;
- night-time comfort and lighting;
- construction diversions;
- platform entrance and train direction;
- peak vehicle queue or turning restrictions.

### 5.3 Buyer questions still unanswered

1. Where are the lobby, accessible entrance, parking ramp, loading dock, taxi/ride-hail point and bicycle entrance?
2. Is the shortest rail route legal, lit, shaded and step-free in both directions?
3. Which rail destinations are direct at the employee’s actual commute time, and what is the reliability/cancellation history?
4. Which bus stops serve each direction and where is the safe crossing?
5. How long does a door-to-desk trip take after platform exit, lobby security and elevator wait?
6. What is the peak arrival/departure queue at each vehicle entrance?
7. Are there parking allocations by tenant, visitor spaces, accessible bays, motorcycle/bicycle counts, showers and EV charging counts/terms?
8. What happens during Yarkon flooding, major events, rail disruption or road works?
9. What does the future Green Line change, and what construction disruption precedes it?

### 5.4 Website map specification

The map should not be a decorative cluster of icons. It needs:

- distinct verified pins for pedestrian lobby, accessible entrance, parking entry/exit, loading and taxi drop-off;
- a step-by-step entrance-to-platform route with distance, typical walking time, crossings, stairs/ramps and accessibility state;
- `current` and `future` transit layers; future assets must show operator and target year;
- directions by mode and departure/arrival time using a live routing provider, with a dated fallback timetable;
- commute comparison by employee origin, not only distance from the building;
- categories for food, grocery, pharmacy/health, hotel, childcare, gym, park, bicycle parking, public parking and urgent services;
- each place’s source, last verification, hours, accessible route, distance from the correct entrance and open/coming-soon/closed state;
- an equivalent sortable list for keyboard, screen-reader and map-averse users;
- traffic and disruption disclaimers; do not cache “current traffic” as permanent content.

## 6. Traffic and parking

The brochure states four underground parking levels, EV provision, motorcycle and bicycle spaces, and two vehicle entrances. It does not publish counts, tenant ratios, turning geometry, loading separation, visitor policy, ramp queues or price.

Required evidence:

- approved municipal traffic and parking appendix;
- as-built parking schedule by type and level;
- exact entrance/exit coordinates and permitted turns;
- tenant allocation and waiting-list policy;
- accessible, visitor, motorcycle and bicycle counts;
- EV charger count, power, operator, tariffs and reservation rules;
- peak queue study and building arrival/departure management plan;
- loading dock and courier plan;
- flood/drainage resilience for underground levels.

The site should offer an employee commute import: anonymised origin neighbourhood/postcode, working days, arrival window, mode and mobility needs. Results should show median and 90th-percentile time, transfers, walking, current/future network, parking demand and carbon—not a single “X minutes from Tel Aviv” badge.

## 7. Amenities and surrounding area

### Verified or supportable at the research cut-off

- The official annual report says the retail area was handed over and stores were open.
- Park Design and retailer pages place an operating design/furniture retail centre at THE PARK/HaLehi 1 or 3. Public retailer examples include Kaufman, Oknin and Dr Gav.
- The owner brochure promises an on-site lifestyle floor with fitness/training, changing rooms and lounge, plus cafés/restaurants/shops in the retail component.

Their existence is supportable, but a safe, accessible entrance-to-entrance route and actual journey time from THE PARK were not verified.

### Not established

- A current owner tenant directory with suite, category, opening state and hours.
- Operational status, operator, access rights, capacity, cost and hours for the lifestyle/fitness floor.
- On-site daily food range, coffee, grocery, pharmacy, clinic, hotel, childcare, lockers, showers and employee services.
- Safe and accessible walking routes to Ayalon Mall, Ramat Hahayal services, healthcare and hotels.

Furniture/design retail is not evidence of a complete employee amenity ecosystem. The page should show **“verified open,” “announced,” “under construction,” “temporarily closed,” or “unverified”** for every amenity, with hours and source. When an essential category has no verified answer, say so and provide `Ask owner to confirm`, rather than filling the map with generic nearby search results.

## 8. Office market context — useful but not a substitute for property facts

Cushman & Wakefield Inter Israel’s H2 2025 office-market summary describes a flight to core prime Tel Aviv and softer satellite markets with pipeline/oversupply pressure. Globes reported in 2025–2026 that office leases at THE PARK had not been publicly announced and that the project was having difficulty finding tenants. These are contextual signals and secondary reporting; they do **not** prove a particular floor is vacant or establish the achievable rent.

The buyer should request:

- an owner stack and leasing schedule;
- anonymised executed office comparables with floor, date, size, term, condition, face rent, incentives, parking and effective rent;
- competing-building proposals on a like-for-like gross/net and fit-out basis;
- sublease availability and tenant-credit context;
- an independent tenant-representative market opinion.

Do not derive an office rent from the reported retail contracts or project NOI. Retail and office economics, operating costs and area bases differ.

## 9. Lease and RFP data room

### Minimum downloadable buyer pack

1. Project identity sheet and ownership/authorised signatory.

## 10. UX blueprint for THE PARK page

### 10.1 Information architecture

1. **Decision header:** project identity, current legal/occupancy state, owner-confirmed last update, key unresolved warnings, save/compare/request pack.
2. **Availability explorer:** zone → floor → suite, with legal numbering and state legend.
3. **Floor inspector:** area, capacity/test-fit, orientation/views, technical, delivery, cost, evidence.
4. **Total cost:** transparent calculator with owner-confirmed and assumption modes.
5. **Commute and area:** entrance-aware map, employee-origin matrix, current/future transit, amenities.
6. **Building systems:** structured technical cards, not promotional prose.
7. **Lease and documents:** data-room checklist, version, date, issuer and download/request status.
8. **Questions and lead:** selected-floor context, routed commercial request, consent and promised response SLA.

### 10.2 Desktop interaction

- Left: filterable stack/3D building with zone legend and compass.
- Centre: selected floor plan with north arrow, scale, core and exposure/view overlays.
- Right: persistent inspector showing identity, availability, area, price, delivery and evidence.
- Bottom comparison tray: up to four floors/buildings; missing values remain “Not supplied,” never zero.
- Every model selection updates the URL and can be shared/bookmarked.

### 10.3 Mobile interaction

- Begin with a simple zone/floor list; 3D is optional enhancement, not the only navigation.
- Do not use a draggable bottom sheet. Selection creates one viewport-height decision surface containing the live model strip, exact identity/status, exposure scene, evidence facts, tool doors and CTA; full-screen evidence tools return in one action to the same floor/camera/focus.
- Put floor identity and availability above the fold; cost, area and move-in immediately below.
- Keep `Compare` and `Request floor pack` inside the bounded decision surface. They must not cover map controls, plan legends, legal warnings or form fields.
- Use at least 44 px controls, visible focus, text alternatives for colours and a table/list equivalent for all 3D/map data.
- Do not load a heavy 3D model before the decision data. Provide low-bandwidth plan images and downloadable files.

### 10.4 Orientation and view experience

When a user rotates the tower, keep a locked north compass. Highlight north/east/south/west façade segments and label the selected suite’s exposures. Show view cone, terrace, camera elevation and known obstruction layer. Offer four fixed cardinal views plus a freely rotatable model. A full floor should not be described as simply “facing west.” If view photography is not verified from the actual floor, label it `illustrative rendering`.

### 10.5 Evidence UI

Every material field should carry:

- value and units;
- status badge: `Authority verified`, `Owner disclosed`, `Third-party indication`, `Derived snapshot`, or `Missing`;
- source name and direct link;
- effective/as-of date and last checked date;
- scope/qualification;
- request action and answer owner if missing.

### 10.6 Canonical envelope in a derived Park view

The server wire contract remains the exact 18-field `provenance.fact_envelope` in `data/data-dictionary.csv` and `README-FIRST.md`. ### 10.7 Cost calculator output contract

Return monthly and annual totals in ILS and a selected reporting currency, but preserve the exchange-rate provider and timestamp. Show:

`base rent + service charge + arnona + parking + utilities + after-hours HVAC + security/cleaning extras + fit-out amortisation + insurance + VAT`

For each component show area basis, quantity, unit rate, source/status and whether recoverable/reconciled. Never silently combine per-rentable-sqm, per-usable-sqm and municipal assessed-area rates.

### 10.8 Lead routing and response accountability

The public page has a contact form, but the reviewed public evidence does not establish who receives the lead, CRM routing, response time or whether the selected floor context is transmitted.

Required implementation:

- attach project, floor/suite ID, source page, compared alternatives, target size/date and buyer language;
- ask only high-value qualification fields first; allow detailed RFP upload after confirmation;
- obtain marketing/privacy consent separately from the service request;
- create a CRM record with owner, SLA deadline, status and audit trail;
- send the buyer a confirmation containing the exact request and a reference number;
- route English-language HQ requests to a named commercial queue, with absence/failover rules;
- measure delivery, first human response, qualified meeting, owner pack sent and outcome;
- never claim “we will get back to you within X” until the workflow is monitored and staffed.

## 15. Research limitations

- Public web research cannot prove a unit’s live legal or commercial status; availability can change between page load and transaction.
- No owner confirmation, data-room access, municipal building-file inspection, physical site visit, route walk, accessibility audit, network test, elevator study, traffic observation, lease review or engineer review was performed.
- GTFS is a dated schedule snapshot, not real-time operations or reliability history. Straight-line distances were calculated from an unverified navigation point and official stop coordinates; they are not walking times.
- Broker prices are asking indications and may be stale, negotiable, conditional or measured on a different area basis.
- The CSV fact matrix accompanying this dossier is the machine-readable implementation source. Missing values are deliberate and must remain missing until evidence is supplied.
