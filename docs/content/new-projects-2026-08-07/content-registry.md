# New Projects Technical Content Registry

Updated: 2026-08-07

This registry is the source of truth for the supporting content package around `/new-projects/`. Every public article must exceed 5,000 words in its own language, have one H1, a Title and Meta description block, primary sources, a unique search intent, and a contextual link back to the anchor. No draft URL may be linked or submitted for indexing before the page is complete, self-canonical, indexable and live.

## Ownership law

- `/new-projects/` owns the broad national intent: new projects, new apartments from a developer, comparison and the full purchase journey.
- A guide owns one technical or operational question only.
- Existing live owners are expanded rather than duplicated: construction input index, delayed delivery, presale and 20/80, bank accompaniment and Sale Law guarantee, home inspection, occupancy approval, Sale Law rights and general building-rights checks.
- City inventory, project inventory and country-abroad content remain separate from this guide package.
- The same Israel question in multiple languages receives self-canonical language URLs and reciprocal hreflang only after every declared alternate is complete and live.
- A country-of-origin comparison or a guide about property outside Israel is original market content, not an hreflang alternate of an Israel guide.

## Minimum article anatomy

1. Immediate answer to the search question.
2. Who the guide is for and what decision it supports.
3. Terminology and the difference between marketing language and a binding project document.
4. Evidence hierarchy: law, standard, authority guidance, engineering calculation, editorial benchmark and project commitment.
5. Step-by-step document and physical review.
6. Measurements, calculations and test method, with the status and source of every numerical threshold.
7. Interfaces with adjacent building systems.
8. Operating cost, maintenance, replacement and failure scenarios where relevant.
9. Exact questions and document requests for the developer or management company.
10. Red flags and stop conditions.
11. Comparison worksheet or printable checklist.
12. Ten to twelve language-native FAQs that do not duplicate the anchor FAQ.
13. Official sources, review date and professional disclosure.

## Language rule

Foreign-language articles are researched and written for the search behavior of that language. They are not sentence-by-sentence translations. Israeli legal and technical terms remain visible in transliteration where a foreign buyer must recognize the Hebrew document. French content may explain why VEFA expectations do not automatically apply in Israel. Russian content may explain why DDU or local construction norms do not govern an Israeli purchase. Arabic content must explicitly identify Israel in the title and preserve the Israeli document name beside the Arabic explanation.
