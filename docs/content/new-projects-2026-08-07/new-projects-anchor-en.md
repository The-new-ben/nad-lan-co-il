# New-Build Projects in Israel: The Complete Foreign Buyer Guide

A new-build project in Israel is a residential development sold by a developer before construction, during construction, or close to completion. Buying from a developer can give you a modern apartment, a binding technical specification, staged payments and statutory buyer protections, but it also creates risks that do not exist in the same form in a completed resale home. You are buying a legal promise, a future building and a defined apartment, not merely the furnished image in a brochure. This guide shows you how to compare live projects, calculate the real cost, investigate the developer and land, understand Israeli contracts and guarantees, inspect every promised amenity, manage a remote purchase, prepare for handover and protect your position after delivery.

## How to search for new-build projects in Israel

The efficient way to search is to narrow the market in layers: purpose, location, timing, budget, legal readiness and apartment quality. A page showing dozens of attractive renderings may be useful for discovery, but it cannot tell you whether a particular unit is the right purchase. A serious shortlist should contain specific apartments, not just project names.

Start by deciding what the property must do for you. A primary home, a family base for extended visits, a future retirement home, a home for a child, a long-term rental and a short-term capital allocation are different briefs. The answer changes the best city, neighborhood, unit size, delivery date, management level and financing structure. A large pool and a staffed lobby may be valuable to a buyer who visits for two months each year, while a resident family may prefer lower monthly charges, practical storage and a short school route.

Then define a maximum all-in budget in New Israeli Shekels, abbreviated NIS or ILS. Do not define it only in dollars, pounds, euros, Canadian dollars or Australian dollars. The contract and most project payments are normally denominated in NIS, so your home-currency budget can move even when the developer's price does not. The all-in budget should include purchase tax, legal advice, mortgage and bank costs, currency conversion, possible indexation, upgrades, interim accommodation, inspection, furnishing and the first period of building management charges.

For every live listing, record the following information in one comparison sheet:

| Item | What to record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Exact unit | Building, entrance, floor and provisional apartment number | Different units in one project can have very different light, noise and legal attachments |
| Israeli room count | Advertised room count plus actual bedrooms and living room | In Israel the living room is normally included in the count |
| Area | Contract area, balcony, storage and an estimated usable internal layout | Area labels are not interchangeable |
| Orientation | Every exposure and principal window direction | Determines sun, wind, privacy and cooling load |
| Legal stage | Land rights, plan status, permit status and financing status | Affects risk and delivery certainty |
| Construction stage | Before work, excavation, structure, finishes or near occupancy | Affects choice, risk, inspections and payment timing |
| Delivery | Contractual delivery date and any grace wording | A sales estimate is not a contractual obligation |
| Payment | Deposit, milestones, indexed portion and final payment | Determines cash flow, FX exposure and financing needs |
| Attachments | Parking, storage, roof, garden or other rights | Must be identified legally, not assumed from marketing |
| Running cost | Estimated monthly management budget and municipal tax basis | Affects affordability long after closing |

A useful shortlist usually has three levels. The first is a broad location shortlist. The second is a project shortlist based on legal and construction facts. The third is a unit shortlist based on the actual plan, specification, orientation and price. Do not spend weeks comparing lobby photographs while the building permit, payment protection or apartment schedule remains unclear.

The key documents should be requested before you become emotionally committed. Ask for the draft sale agreement, statutory disclosure documents, binding technical specification, apartment plan, floor plan, parking and storage plans, project finance details, land-rights evidence, permit status, expected management budget and developer company details. A salesperson may need time or approval to release some materials. The way the request is handled is itself information. Vague answers, moving document versions or pressure to sign a reservation before legal review are warning signs.

## What counts as a new-build, pre-sale or off-plan apartment

The term new-build covers several legal and practical stages. A unit offered before a full building permit is not the same product as an almost completed unit awaiting occupancy approval. Both may be advertised as a new apartment, but the evidence available, uncertainty, payment profile and buyer leverage differ.

### Before a full building permit

An early sale may provide broader unit choice or a commercial incentive, but the buyer must understand what remains conditional. The approved planning scheme, developer's land rights, permit application, expected design changes and contractual exit mechanism matter. Ask what happens if the permit is delayed, granted with a smaller apartment or balcony, or requires a changed building configuration. Do not accept a broad statement that all changes will be minor. The contract should define permissible changes, measurement tolerances, notice obligations and the buyer's remedies.

Marketing at this stage is especially dependent on renderings. A sea view may rely on a future public space that has not been built. A landscaped courtyard may change during detailed design. A pool may depend on final systems and operating approvals. The legal review must separate the approved planning position, the permit application, the anticipated permit and the developer's current concept.

### After the permit and before substantial construction

Once a permit has been issued, more information should be stable, but the permit alone does not answer every question. Verify that it covers the building and unit being sold, that the project finance and payment-security mechanism are active, and that the contract package matches the permitted scheme. Ask whether demolition, excavation or enabling works are still required and whether third-party rights, infrastructure relocation or neighboring works could affect the schedule.

### During construction

Construction progress lets the buyer compare promises with physical reality. You may be able to see actual floor heights, distances, views and street conditions. Unit choice may be narrower and buyer-change deadlines may have passed. A building that looks advanced may still have significant systems, testing, utility, public-realm and occupancy-approval work ahead. Progress should be assessed from evidence and contractual milestones, not from a few photographs.

### Near completion or ready for occupancy

A near-complete apartment gives more certainty about space, light, noise and finishes. The payment period may be short, leaving less time to arrange funds or a mortgage. Ask whether the building already has the relevant occupancy approval, whether permanent water and electricity connections are ready, what common areas remain unfinished, and when the statutory and contractual handover conditions will actually be satisfied.

Immediate possession is not the same as completed title registration. A buyer can receive keys while subdivision, condominium registration and registration of the individual apartment remain outstanding. The contract should define who completes each registration stage, the target timing, documents held in trust and any security that remains until registration.

## Israeli property language that foreign buyers must decode

Israeli residential advertising uses familiar words in unfamiliar ways. Translating each word literally is not enough. The buyer needs to know which definition controls the contract, tax, municipal bill, mortgage valuation and daily use.

### Room count includes the living room

An Israeli four-room apartment usually means a living room plus three bedrooms, not four bedrooms. A half-room may refer to a smaller space that met an older or particular convention, and labels can vary. Never compare a four-room Israeli unit with a four-bedroom home abroad without opening the plan.

For each apartment, write the room count as: three bedrooms, one living room, two bathrooms, one balcony. Check whether the kitchen is open to the living room, whether a study is a legal room, and whether the Mamad is used as a bedroom. A Mamad, short for Merhav Mugan Dirati, is the apartment's protected room. It has specific structural and protective functions, but buyers should still check its net dimensions, window, ventilation, access and furniture layout.

### One apartment can have several area figures

The contract and Sale Law specification may state an area under one measurement convention. The local authority may use a different area for Arnona, the municipal property tax. A marketing page may combine internal space with walls, balcony or other elements. An appraiser may describe yet another figure. None should be treated as the apartment's walkable floor area without explanation.

Ask the developer to identify every figure and measurement method. Then test the plan room by room. A large stated area can still produce a narrow living room, long corridor, unusable corner or deep internal zone. The practical questions are whether a standard bed fits with circulation, whether wardrobes fit without blocking doors, where the dining table goes, and how much of the balcony is deep enough for seating.

### Tabu, the Israel Land Authority and a registering company are different systems

Tabu is the common name for the Israel Land Registry. A current official Tabu extract can show registered owners, mortgages, attachments, court orders and restrictions as of its issue date. The Ministry of Justice explains and provides the service for obtaining a [Land Registry extract](https://www.gov.il/en/service/land_registration_extract). A new project may not yet have a separate sub-parcel for the apartment, so the legal review may begin with the parent parcel and the contractual route to future registration.

The authority's Hebrew acronym is commonly transliterated as RMI and pronounced Rami. Its official English name is the Israel Land Authority, ILA. Some rights are long-term leasehold or are managed through the ILA rather than registered as simple private ownership. An ILA information printout does not necessarily constitute legally binding proof of rights, a distinction stated on the official [ILA property information page](https://www.gov.il/en/service/property-information). Your lawyer should identify the exact rights, capitalization status, transfer requirements, payments and registration route.

In some developments, rights may temporarily be recorded by a housing company, registering company or other rights administrator until land and condominium registration is completed. Ask for the current confirmation of rights, the party responsible for registration, the completed and outstanding stages, fees, undertakings and contractual deadline. The expression registration in the company's books is not a substitute for understanding what can be registered today and what remains promised.

### Form 4 is a common label, not the whole handover test

Buyers often use Form 4 as shorthand for permission to connect and occupy a new building. Regulatory terminology and documents can vary with the process and project. A salesperson saying that Form 4 is expected soon does not prove that the apartment is ready for contractual delivery. Ask for the relevant official approval, permanent utility status, safety-system completion, common-area readiness and the contract's precise handover conditions.

### Two Sale Laws and Madad Tesumot HaBniya

The shorthand Chok HaMecher is often used for two distinct statutes. The Sale (Apartments) Law governs matters such as the sale specification, nonconformity, defect periods and delayed delivery. The Sale (Apartments) (Assurance of Investments of Home Purchasers) Law governs statutory protection of buyer payments. They work together but are not one guarantee. Your lawyer must identify which statute and provision applies to each issue.

Madad Tesumot HaBniya is the Construction Input Index published by Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics. If the contract permits part of the unpaid price to be linked to the index, the NIS amount due can change over time. Index linkage is different from exchange-rate exposure, mortgage interest and the apartment's market value. A foreign buyer may face all four at once.

## Location due diligence: city, neighborhood, plot and walking route

Choose the exact plot, not just a fashionable city name. Two buildings in the same marketed neighborhood can differ in school access, construction disturbance, sea wind, road noise, future view, station access and municipal development timing. A project can be well located on a regional map and inconvenient on foot.

### Start with the daily itinerary

Define the routes that will matter: home to school, train, light rail, beach, synagogue, supermarket, medical care, park and family. Measure the actual entrance-to-destination walk, including crossings, slopes, barriers and the future construction site. A claim of five minutes to the sea may measure a straight line or the edge of the neighborhood, not the walk from the correct lobby to a usable beach entrance.

Walk the area at different times. Morning traffic, school collection, nightlife, aircraft routes, delivery bays and construction vehicles may not be visible during a quiet sales appointment. For a remote buyer, commission a recorded route or live video call that begins at the future building entrance and follows the relevant path without cuts.

### Read the neighboring plans

The current view is not the future view. Use Israel's official planning systems, including [Mavat](https://www.mavat.iplan.gov.il/) and [XPLAN](https://ags.iplan.gov.il/xplan/index.html), to examine the project parcel and surrounding parcels. Look for approved and deposited plans, building heights, public buildings, roads, commercial fronts, utilities, open space and phasing.

A promised open view may face a parcel designated for a future tower. A quiet side street may become a transit or access route. A public school may improve family convenience but also create traffic and noise. The correct question is not only what is there now, but what can lawfully be built, what is already approved and what the municipality intends to deliver.

Planning information requires professional interpretation. A colored map is not a complete legal opinion. Ask an architect, planner or lawyer to confirm the status of the relevant plan, whether an amendment is pending and how it affects the apartment's outlook and access. Save dated copies or links in the purchase file.

### Tel Aviv and Sde Dov as a research example

Large new districts illustrate why neighborhood-level claims need plot-level verification. Tel Aviv publishes information on [new neighborhoods and development areas](https://www.tel-aviv.gov.il/Residents/Development/Pages/NewNeighborhoods.aspx), while its [Sde Dov planning page](https://www.tel-aviv.gov.il/Residents/Development/Pages/SdeDov.aspx) provides public context for that district. These pages are starting points, not substitutes for the binding plan, permit and contract.

In a district built in phases, the buyer should ask when roads, schools, parks, retail, transit, beach links and adjacent buildings are expected, who is responsible for each item, and whether the apartment can be occupied before all neighborhood works finish. Construction for years after handover may affect access, dust, noise, rental demand and the experience of using amenities.

### Noise, wind, sun and environmental checks

Map major roads, rail lines, light-rail tracks, bus routes, schools, sports areas, entertainment, waste rooms, loading areas, mechanical plant and emergency infrastructure. Check whether helicopters or aircraft use nearby routes. Investigate visible power infrastructure and any project-specific environmental reports. Do not rely on a generic statement that glazing is high quality. Ask for the specified glazing and acoustic performance for the exact facade.

Sun and wind affect comfort and costs. A west-facing glazed living room can receive intense afternoon sun. A high coastal balcony may be windy even when it has an open view. A north-facing apartment may have softer light but less direct sun. Request a shadow or sun study if the orientation and surrounding towers make it material, and ask how the facade, shading, air-conditioning and balcony drainage respond to the conditions.

## The real cost of a new-build apartment, as of August 2026

The real cost is the contract price plus every compulsory or reasonably foreseeable amount required to acquire, finance, complete, equip, hold and use the apartment. A discount on the headline price can disappear when indexation, currency movement, upgrade costs or high management charges are added.

No single price range is reliable for all new-build projects in Israel. City, micro-location, permit stage, construction stage, floor, orientation, view, developer, payment terms and specification can produce major differences. Current project prices and availability belong in live inventory, so consult [PROJECT_LINK] and obtain a dated written quotation for the exact unit.

### Build an all-in acquisition budget

| Cost group | Questions to answer before signing |
|---|---|
| Contract price | What exactly is included, and is parking, storage or a balcony attachment priced separately? |
| Mas Rechisha | What purchase tax applies to this buyer on the expected signing date? |
| Indexation | Which installments or portions are linked, from which base index, and until when? |
| Foreign exchange | In which currency is each payment due, and what happens if the buyer's home currency weakens? |
| Legal and professional | Buyer lawyer, tax advice, translation, notary, Apostille, appraisal, engineer and inspection |
| Financing | Arrangement, valuation, registration, insurance, transfer and other lender costs |
| Changes and upgrades | Kitchen, flooring, electrical, air-conditioning, smart-home, plumbing and late-change charges |
| Interim costs | Rent, travel, storage and duplicate housing if delivery is later than planned |
| Completion | Furniture, lighting, appliances, curtains, networking and moving |
| Ownership | Arnona, management fees, insurance, maintenance and reserve contributions |

Obtain two totals. The first is the expected all-in cost under the base schedule. The second is a stress case using a weaker home currency, a higher lawful index amount, more conservative delivery timing and realistic upgrades. If the purchase only works in the optimistic case, the budget is not robust.

### Mas Rechisha, Israeli purchase tax

Mas Rechisha is the Israeli tax imposed on the acquisition of rights in real estate. The applicable calculation depends on facts such as the buyer's status, the property, other ownership and the law in force on the transaction date. A non-resident should not assume access to the same treatment as an Israeli resident or an Oleh Hadash, a new immigrant, and should not rely on an old blog table or a salesperson's estimate.

Use the Israel Tax Authority's official [real-estate purchase-tax simulator](https://www.gov.il/en/service/real_eatate_taxsimulator) as a reference, then obtain transaction-specific advice before signing. The government page itself notes that English information may be available while procedures and forms are handled in Hebrew. Confirm the effective date, residency evidence, any relevant immigration status, ownership held directly or indirectly and reporting deadline. This guide intentionally does not publish unstable tax bands as if they applied universally.

### Currency exposure is part of the property decision

A buyer earning in another currency should map every scheduled NIS payment against expected liquidity. If the contract uses a large final installment, the buyer is effectively exposed to the exchange rate on that future amount. A favorable headline price can become unaffordable if the home currency weakens before completion.

Consider buying NIS in stages, maintaining a suitable buffer or using a regulated currency provider after obtaining independent financial advice. Compare the quoted exchange rate, spread, transfer fee, receiving-bank fee, payment cutoff and beneficiary details. Never send funds based only on instructions in an email. Confirm beneficiary and voucher details through a known channel because property-payment fraud can involve altered bank instructions.

Do not confuse FX planning with speculation. The objective is to make the contractual obligations fundable, not to predict the perfect rate. Record the rate and source used for any illustrative conversion, and keep the legally binding price in NIS at the center of the decision.

### Management charges can change the affordable price

A pool, spa, staffed concierge, landscaped podium, multiple elevators and complex mechanical systems must be operated, cleaned, insured and renewed. Ask for a draft annual operating budget, the allocation method, expected monthly charge for the exact unit, reserve policy and services excluded from the base charge. Also ask who controls the management company at first, how the contract can be replaced and whether the developer retains commercial or other units that affect voting and cost allocation.

Treat the estimate as a forecast, not a cap, unless the contract clearly provides otherwise. Model a higher operating cost and major future replacement items. An apartment that fits the acquisition budget but not the annual holding budget is not affordable.

## Buying remotely: lawyer, power of attorney and document control

A remote purchase can often be organized, but distance increases the importance of independent representation, identity procedures and evidence. Whether every step can be completed without travel depends on the bank, lender, tax filings, project documents, place of signature and the authorities involved. Do not plan on a fully remote closing until each institution confirms its process.

### Appoint your own Israeli lawyer

The developer's lawyer acts for the developer and may perform project registration work. That role does not replace a lawyer who owes duties to the buyer. Your lawyer should review the land and company records, permit and plan, statutory payment protection, sale agreement, specification, tax position, payment schedule, POA, mortgage interface, handover and registration route.

For a foreign buyer, confirm that the lawyer routinely handles non-resident identification, cross-border funds and bilingual communication. Agree how advice will be delivered, who can approve changes, which language version is controlling, how urgent notices are handled across time zones and where originals will be stored.

### Power of attorney is useful only when its scope is understood

A power of attorney, or POA, may authorize a lawyer or representative to sign documents, receive notices, complete tax filings, register protections, interact with a lender or perform other defined actions. The precise scope matters. A broad irrevocable POA connected to a property transaction is not a routine administrative form to sign without advice.

Ask who receives the power, whether substitution is allowed, what documents may be signed, whether commercial terms can be changed, whether funds can be received or transferred, when the authority ends and how misuse is controlled. Signature formalities may involve a notary, an Apostille or Israeli consular verification depending on the document, jurisdiction and receiving body. Confirm the exact route before arranging an appointment abroad. Do not assume that a notarization accepted by one bank will satisfy the Land Registry, Tax Authority or another lender.

### Create one controlled deal room

Store every document in a structured folder with its date and version. Include passports and identity evidence, tax-residency documents, POAs, corporate or trust documents if relevant, the reservation, contract drafts, final signed agreement, schedules, plans, specification, disclosure forms, bank vouchers, guarantees, transfer confirmations, tax filings, permit evidence, progress notices, change orders, invoices, inspection reports and handover protocol.

For every translated document, preserve the original and translation together. Mark whether the translation is informational or certified. An English courtesy translation can be extremely useful, but the signed Hebrew contract may control. Your lawyer should explain any inconsistency before signature, not after a dispute.

Use a written approval protocol for material decisions. A relative visiting the sales office should not approve a layout change or substitute finish unless expressly authorized. Keep commercial discussions in writing and ask for every agreed point to appear in the signed agreement or a signed schedule.

## Source of funds, banking and anti-money-laundering preparation

Begin source-of-funds preparation before signing. An Israeli bank, project lender, mortgage lender, lawyer, currency provider or other regulated party may need to understand the buyer, beneficial owner, source of wealth and source of the particular transfer. A delay in compliance does not usually cancel the buyer's contractual payment deadline.

The exact evidence varies by institution and facts. It may include bank statements, employment income, tax returns, investment statements, a business sale, property-sale agreement, inheritance documentation, trust or company records and proof of the path funds took between accounts. Documents may need translation, certification or recent issue dates. Ask each relevant institution for its own list and do not treat a generic checklist as guaranteed acceptance.

Map the payment chain from the beneficial owner to the paying account, currency provider, Israeli account and project voucher. Avoid unexplained third-party transfers. If a parent, company, trust or family member will fund part of the purchase, obtain legal and tax advice on the structure and disclose it early. The name on the purchase contract, the tax reporting, the mortgage application and the sending account should not be allowed to diverge without a documented explanation.

Before the first payment, conduct a small operational test if appropriate and allowed. Confirm international transfer limits, time-zone cutoffs, bank holidays, conversion timing and how the voucher reference must appear. Keep enough time for a rejected or queried transfer to be corrected. Save the SWIFT or transfer confirmation, the receiving evidence and the payment-security document that follows.

## Investigating the developer, contractor, land and permit

The developer's brand is not the legal seller. Identify the exact company signing the agreement, the group behind it, the landowner, the executing contractor, the project lender and any guarantor. A respected group name does not automatically make every special-purpose project company equally supported.

### Check the seller and corporate structure

Obtain a current company extract and review incorporation, status, registered charges and authorized signatories with your lawyer. Israel provides an official [company extract service](https://www.gov.il/he/service/company_extract). Ask whether the seller owns the land, holds an option, participates in a combination transaction with landowners, or is developing under another structure. Understand which consents are needed to complete the sale and who bears the risk if a third party does not cooperate.

Ask whether a parent company provides a contractual guarantee. Do not infer a guarantee from shared branding, a website logo or group letterhead. If support is important, its scope, amount, expiry, conditions and enforcement must be documented.

### Review the developer's delivery record

Choose several completed projects that resemble the proposed building in height, complexity and amenities. Speak with residents or owners where possible. Ask about actual delivery timing, defect response, common-area quality, acoustic performance, water penetration, elevator reliability, management budgets and the transition from developer control to owners.

Complaints alone do not prove systemic failure, and polished testimonials do not prove quality. Look for patterns, the seriousness of issues and the developer's response. Ask for the official names and addresses of completed buildings, then verify that they were actually delivered by the same group or entity being presented.

### Verify the contractor

The executing contractor may change, so identify the current contractual position and what happens if replacement is required. Check registration and classification through the official [Contractors Registrar](https://www.gov.il/he/departments/Units/contractors_registrar_and_head_of_sales_laws_moch). Your professional advisers should confirm that the registration and classification fit the work and remain current.

Ask who is responsible for quality control, who supervises systems, which consultants designed waterproofing and acoustics, and how defects are documented. The developer remains the buyer's contractual address under the sale agreement, but the contractor's capability affects execution risk.

### Separate land rights, planning approval and building permit

These are different questions:

1. Does the seller have enforceable rights to develop and sell on the land?
2. Does the approved planning scheme allow the proposed use, scale and configuration?
3. Has a building permit been issued for the relevant works?
4. Does the apartment sold match the permitted and contractual plans?
5. Are conditions, third-party consents or infrastructure works still outstanding?

Request a written status rather than a label such as approved project. If the permit is pending, the contract must address the consequences. If the permit exists, verify its date, scope and amendments. If the project is an urban-renewal scheme, also understand evacuation, demolition, existing-owner agreements and conditions that could affect commencement.

## Closed project finance and statutory buyer-payment protection

The central payment question is simple: what statutory protection will secure each amount you pay, when will it be issued and what event does it actually protect? Never transfer money merely because the developer is reputable or construction is visible.

Under Israel's Sale (Apartments) (Assurance of Investments of Home Purchasers) Law, a seller may not receive more than 7 percent of the apartment price unless a statutory protection mechanism is provided. The [consolidated assurance-law record](https://main.knesset.gov.il/apps/legislation/main/laws/2000385) should be read with current legal advice and the actual contract.

### What closed project finance means

In a conventionally financed project, the financing institution monitors the project, payments flow through a designated structure and buyers receive approved payment vouchers. The lender's role and its internal feasibility report do not mean it has appraised the apartment for the buyer or guaranteed every contractual promise. A lender protects its own credit position and provides only the protections stated in the relevant instruments and law.

Before paying, confirm:

* the identity of the project lender or insurer;
* that the exact apartment and buyer are recognized in the project system;
* that the voucher belongs to the designated project account;
* the deadline and process for receiving the guarantee or policy after payment;
* how prior encumbrances are addressed;
* what happens to protection at handover and registration;
* whether any requested payment sits outside the protected price and why.

Pay through the correct voucher channel. Verify changed bank instructions independently. After each payment, track receipt of the corresponding guarantee or insurance document and check the buyer name, apartment, amount and terms. Under the statutory voucher framework, the financing entity must issue the bank guarantee or insurance policy for the protected amount within 14 business days after payment, or verify that another qualifying protection was provided. If it does not arrive, notify the seller, financing entity and your lawyer in writing. Do not withhold a contractual payment unilaterally without advice, because the buyer can also create a default.

For a bank guarantee or approved insurance policy, the statute excludes the VAT component included in the payment from the instrument itself. Sections 3g1 and 3g2 establish a separate arrangement under which the seller's right to a VAT refund is pledged to the state and a government fund is intended to return an amount equal to that VAT component when qualifying protection is realized, subject to the statute and fund rules. Reconcile the payment, guarantee amount and VAT component, and preserve the fund documentation. The [consolidated assurance-law record](https://main.knesset.gov.il/apps/legislation/main/laws/2000385) contains the relevant provisions.

### The lender's zero report is not a buyer's valuation

Israeli project-finance discussions may mention a Doch Efes, literally a zero report. This is an economic feasibility report used by the financing institution when assessing land rights, planning, expected construction costs, projected sales, profitability and credit needs. It is prepared for the finance process and its assumptions can change as the project progresses.

The existence of a zero report does not prove that your apartment is fairly priced, technically well designed or certain to be delivered on time. It is not the buyer's appraisal and not a Chok HaMecher guarantee. Ask whether closed finance has actually been signed and activated, who monitors construction and whether the voucher and protection mechanism for your unit are ready.

### The statutory protection mechanisms are not identical

| Mechanism | Basic idea | What the buyer must examine |
|---|---|---|
| Bank guarantee | A bank-issued instrument protects paid amounts in specified statutory circumstances | Beneficiary, amount, wording, realization events, exclusions, expiry and replacement |
| Approved insurance policy | An insurer provides statutory payment protection | Insurer approval, policy terms, beneficiary, amount and claim process |
| First-ranking mortgage | Security is registered over the property or relevant rights | Priority, scope, pre-existing rights and practical realization |
| Cautionary note | A note protects the buyer's transaction where statutory conditions are met | Superior liens, seller rights, parcel identity and ability to register the note |
| Transfer of ownership or another right | The purchased right is transferred as security for payment | Exact right, registration status and remaining encumbrances |

The appropriate mechanism depends on the project structure. Do not choose based on the shortest name. Your lawyer should explain why the offered mechanism meets the statute and how it interacts with the land, lender and future registration.

### What a bank guarantee does not automatically cover

A Chok HaMecher bank guarantee is not an unconditional refund for every delay, defect, disagreement or fall in value. Realization depends on the law and instrument, including specified circumstances such as attachment, insolvency proceedings or an absolute inability to transfer the right or deliver possession. A cracked tile, ordinary contractual dispute or late handover does not automatically let the buyer draw the guarantee.

This distinction explains why buyers need several protections at once: payment security, a precise contract, a binding specification, delay remedies, inspection rights, defect duties, registration undertakings and independent representation. One guarantee cannot replace the rest of the purchase file.

## The contract, specification and the gap between advertising and obligation

The legal product is defined by the signed sale agreement and its schedules, including the Sale Law specification and plans. A rendering, model apartment, WhatsApp message or oral assurance can help explain the offer, but it may not create the same enforceable obligation. Every feature that materially affects the decision should be traced into a signed document.

Create a promise register with four columns: marketing claim, salesperson clarification, binding document and unresolved gap. Use it for the apartment, view, ceiling, kitchen, appliances, parking, storage, amenities, lobby, landscape, management services and delivery. If the developer will not place an important promise in the contract or specification, value the apartment as though the promise will not be delivered.

### Read every schedule as a connected system

The apartment plan may show a feature that the specification excludes. The specification may name a generic quality level while a finish schedule names a model. The parking plan may be provisional. The contract may allow substitutions. Review the documents together and resolve inconsistencies in writing.

Check the order of precedence between documents. Ask what happens if the plan, specification, permit and sales brochure conflict. Review the developer's right to substitute materials or systems, the required quality of a substitute, notice process and buyer remedy. Equivalent is too vague unless the comparison criteria are defined.

### English explanations do not replace the controlling Hebrew

Foreign buyers should obtain a careful explanation or translation of the contract. A summary is useful for orientation but cannot safely replace clause-by-clause advice. Identify which language version is binding. Keep a glossary for recurring terms and require your lawyer to explain provisions that do not map neatly to your home jurisdiction.

Pay special attention to default interest, cancellation, assignment, notices, force-majeure wording, delivery, permissible changes, area tolerances, indexation, taxes, legal fees, mortgage cooperation, buyer-change deadlines, management agreement, inspection, defect notices, registration, POAs and dispute provisions. Do not sign a short reservation or application on the assumption that only the later full contract matters. It may create a payment, timetable or other legal consequence.

### The sales office is not your evidence file

Ask for documents by email and preserve the response. Date screenshots of web claims that materially influence the purchase, but focus negotiations on inserting the obligation into the signed package. If a feature is still under design, require a clear minimum outcome or a meaningful approval and remedy process.

The right sales question has three parts: what exactly is included, where is it stated in the binding documents, and what happens if it is changed. That pattern should be used for every promise in the remainder of this guide.

## Payment schedules, the Construction Input Index, mortgages and FX

A payment schedule is not merely a convenience. It allocates financing risk, index exposure, exchange-rate exposure and leverage between buyer and developer. Compare the present value and risk of the schedule, not only the nominal apartment price.

### Map every payment before signing

Build a calendar showing the contractual due date or trigger, amount in NIS, whether any portion is indexed, funding source, expected transfer lead time and payment-security document. Include tax and professional costs on the same calendar. A foreign buyer should add the home-currency amount under a base exchange rate and a stress rate.

| Stage | Evidence to obtain | Funding question | Buyer-protection question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reservation or application | Signed terms, refund rules and unit identification | Is the amount immediately available? | Is it payable, to whom, and how is it protected? |
| Contract signing | Final agreement and complete schedules | Is equity cleared and compliant? | Which statutory mechanism applies to the first payment? |
| Construction installments | Contractual milestone and developer notice | Will cash, FX conversion or mortgage funds be used? | Has the correct voucher been issued and the prior guarantee received? |
| Pre-handover balance | Readiness evidence, inspection rights and occupancy documents | Can the lender release on time? | What remains secured until legal handover? |
| Handover and registration | Protocol, keys, documents and undertakings | Are taxes and fees settled? | What security remains for title registration? |

Avoid milestones that are subjective or controlled only by a sales declaration. If an installment is linked to construction progress, define the milestone and evidence. If a payment is due on a fixed date regardless of progress, understand that clearly. Keep a reserve for bank processing and do not assume a mortgage draw will occur the same day it is requested.

### Construction Input Index linkage

For contracts signed from July 7, 2022 within the current Amendment 9 framework, the legal source and contract should be reviewed together. The first 20 percent paid at signing or before another payment is not linked to the Construction Input Index, and no more than half of each later payment may be linked. The arithmetic ceiling is therefore 40 percent of the apartment price for contracts to which this rule applies. The precise contract, signing date and law control.

Indexation should generally not continue beyond the contractual delivery date under the applicable framework, except where delay is caused by the buyer or qualifying frustration circumstances under section 18(a) of the Contracts (Remedies for Breach of Contract) Law apply. Do not accept a spreadsheet without checking the clause, base index, publication timing, rounding, negative-index treatment, final calculation and legal applicability.

The Central Bureau of Statistics publishes the official index. Its continuously updated [residential construction input index page](https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/subjects/Pages/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93-%D7%9E%D7%97%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%AA%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%91%D7%91%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D.aspx) is the source for calculation, not a salesperson's forecast. The Knesset's [consolidated Sale Law record](https://main.knesset.gov.il/apps/legislation/main/laws/2000386) and government [Amendment 9 summary](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/9th-amendment) provide official context. Obtain advice on the version in force for your contract.

Ask whether the developer will reduce, cap or remove linkage as part of negotiation. A lower headline price with broad index exposure may be worse than a slightly higher fixed amount. Record any concession in the signed agreement.

### Deferred-payment offers such as 20/80 or 10/90

A structure in which a small portion is paid at signing and a large balance near delivery can improve short-term liquidity. It can also concentrate mortgage, appraisal, FX and affordability risk at the end. The buyer may have committed long before knowing the lender's final valuation, future income position or exchange rate.

Ask whether the offer involves a developer-subsidized loan, balloon finance, interest reimbursement or another arrangement. Identify the actual borrower, lender, interest payer, repayment date, security and consequence if the buyer cannot refinance. The Bank of Israel has addressed risks in developer financing and payment-deferral offers through supervisory measures, including its [developer-financing circular](https://www.boi.org.il/roles/supervisionregulation/circulars/h2816/). A marketing phrase such as no interest does not eliminate the credit, tax, fee or end-payment analysis.

Stress test the final balance under three conditions: the mortgage valuation is below the contract price, the home currency is weaker, and delivery is earlier or later than expected. If any scenario creates a funding gap, decide before signing how it would be covered.

### Mortgages for non-residents

Mortgage availability is buyer-specific. Israeli banks may review residency, citizenship, tax residence, source of income, credit history, debt, property type, project lender, construction stage and collateral. Loan-to-value limits and bank policy may differ by borrower and can change. Do not publish or rely on a universal non-resident percentage without a current written indication for the actual case.

Obtain early feedback from lenders or a suitably regulated mortgage adviser before committing, but treat an initial indication as conditional. Ask what documents must be refreshed before drawdown, whether foreign income is discounted, which currencies are acceptable, how affordability is calculated, whether life or property insurance is required, and what happens if the apartment valuation is lower than the contract price.

The bank's appraiser works for the lending decision. The valuation does not replace the buyer's commercial analysis, engineering review or lawyer. If the appraised value is lower, the lender may calculate finance using its own value rather than the developer's price, leaving the buyer to supply more equity.

Coordinate the mortgage with the project finance system. The lender may require documents from the developer or project bank, undertakings, registration of security and a specific draw process. Set responsibility and lead times in writing. A payment due date should not arrive before the developer has delivered the cooperation documents the buyer's lender reasonably needs.

### Four different risks that buyers often combine

Keep these calculations separate:

1. Construction Input Index risk changes an eligible NIS balance under the contract.
2. FX risk changes what that NIS balance costs in the buyer's home currency.
3. Mortgage interest and product risk change the cost and affordability of borrowing.
4. Market-value risk changes what another buyer or appraiser may pay for the apartment.

A developer concession can reduce one risk while increasing another. For example, deferred payment may reduce near-term index exposure but increase future FX and mortgage uncertainty. Compare the entire structure.

## How to inspect a new-build like an experienced hotel guest

The word luxury proves nothing. A serious buyer converts every amenity and apartment promise into dimensions, capacity, equipment, operating rules, cost and a binding document. The difference between an impressive sales image and a usable building is found in the details.

For each category below, use the same five-part test:

1. What exactly is being supplied?
2. What is its measurable capacity or performance?
3. How many residents will share it, and when can they use it?
4. Who operates, maintains and replaces it, at what expected cost?
5. Where does the binding contract or specification require it?

### Swimming pool: exercise facility or decorative water

Why it matters: a pool can be valuable for exercise, children and occasional use, but it can also consume substantial operating and renewal budgets. The label pool does not reveal whether it is 25 meters long, an eight-meter plunge pool or a shallow decorative feature. Size should be evaluated against the number of apartments and intended users.

Request internal water dimensions, depth profile, lane plan, total water area and a scaled drawing. Ask whether the pool is indoor or outdoor, heated year-round or seasonally, covered, shaded and protected from coastal wind. Clarify whether there is a separate children's pool, accessible entry, showers, changing rooms, lockers and toilets.

Operations matter as much as construction. Ask for intended opening hours, seasonal closure, lifeguard requirements, maximum occupancy, guest policy and rules for children. The final operating regime may depend on law, insurer and management company, so distinguish a developer forecast from a guaranteed service.

Calculate a simple sharing ratio: number of residential units divided by pool water area and, where useful, by the number of usable swimming lanes. It is not a statutory quality standard, but it exposes a project where hundreds of homes share a small pool. Also examine pool-plant location, pump noise, chemical storage, humidity control for an indoor pool and the effect of maintenance on management fees.

Marketing claim: residents' swimming pool. Binding evidence: dimensions and location on signed plans, pool and systems description in the specification, and any contractual right to change or omit it. Exact sales question: "Please provide the pool's internal length, width and depth, whether it will be heated and covered, the planned operating season and hours, and the signed document that requires those features."

### Gym: real training space or symbolic room

Why it matters: a gym needs enough net clear area for safe circulation, multiple users, equipment movement and ventilation. A gross room area may include columns, storage or inaccessible zones. Two treadmills in a small room can satisfy a vague marketing image while failing the buyer's practical expectation.

Do not rely on the claim that 30 square meters is an international luxury-building standard. No universal number proves quality. Thirty square meters may support a limited equipment layout, but the correct capacity depends on shape, clearances, ceiling height, equipment and resident count. Ask for net usable area and a scaled equipment plan.

Request the equipment schedule, quantities, models or minimum specifications. Check cardio machines, resistance machines, cable station, benches, racks, free weights, stretching area and accessible equipment. Ask about flooring, vibration isolation, mirrors, ventilation, cooling, drinking water, sanitation and storage. Determine whether heavy free weights are permitted and whether the room sits above or beside an apartment.

If yoga, Pilates or meditation is advertised, ask whether there is a separate quiet studio or merely open floor beside the treadmills. A six to twelve square meter room may be useful for one person or limited practice, but the dimensions alone do not make a serious communal studio. Request occupancy, booking rules, acoustic separation and equipment.

Marketing claim: fully equipped residents' gym. Binding evidence: room dimensions, finish and required equipment level in signed schedules. Exact sales question: "What is the net clear gym area, which equipment items and minimum models are contractually included, how many users does the layout support, and is the studio a separate room?"

### Spa: define every installed component

Why it matters: spa can mean a functioning wellness suite or an empty relaxation room. A credible spa description identifies the dry sauna, steam room, cold plunge, Jacuzzi, treatment rooms, showers, changing facilities, ventilation, drainage and plant. Each component has construction, safety, staffing and maintenance implications.

Ask whether a dry sauna and steam room are both included. Request their internal dimensions, bench capacity, heater or generator specification, operating control and ventilation. If a cold plunge is shown, confirm whether it is a built, filtered and cooled basin or a conceptual image. If treatment rooms exist, ask whether they include plumbing, beds, storage and an operator, and whether treatments are separately paid.

Investigate operating hours, booking, guest access, age rules, cleaning and closure periods. High humidity and heat require good waterproofing and mechanical systems. Ask where the plant is located relative to apartments and how faults are handled.

Marketing claim: private residents' spa. Binding evidence: named installations and locations in the signed package. Exact sales question: "List every installed spa component, its capacity and required infrastructure, state what is only shown in the rendering, and identify which services will require an additional fee."

### Lobby and concierge: architecture is not service

Why it matters: a double-height stone lobby can affect the arrival experience, but a concierge promise creates recurring payroll and management costs. A desk in a rendering does not mean a person will be there.

Ask for the finished ceiling height, material schedule, furniture responsibility, lighting, mailboxes, package room, accessible route and security arrangement. Check whether the lobby shown serves your entrance or another building. If art or branded furniture appears in a model, confirm whether it is included.

For concierge or reception, ask who employs the staff, planned hours, coverage during holidays and breaks, and actual duties. Will staff accept packages, manage guests, hold keys, arrange services, monitor security or simply sit at reception? What privacy rules apply? Is the service guaranteed for an initial period, subject to owners' approval, or only a management-company proposal?

Marketing claim: 24-hour concierge. Binding evidence: a contractual service obligation, budget and management terms. Exact sales question: "Who will staff the desk, for which exact hours and duties, for how long is that service committed, and what monthly cost assumption is included in the operating budget?"

### Parking and electric vehicles: inspect the exact space

Why it matters: a parking right can be ordinary, tandem, mechanical, narrow, obstructed or difficult to reach. The number of spaces on a price sheet says nothing about ramp geometry, turning, columns, ceiling height or legal attachment.

Identify the exact numbered space on a signed parking plan. Ask for width, length, clear height, adjacent columns and walls, turning route and ramp gradient. Drive the route in a similar completed project if the system is complex. For tandem parking, define who controls the other space. For mechanical parking, examine vehicle size and weight limits, retrieval time, outage procedure, maintenance contract, backup power and replacement reserve.

For electric vehicles, distinguish a working private charger from an electrical preparation. Preparation may mean only a conduit, a cable route, an allocated board position, shared capacity or part of those items. Ask how many chargers can operate simultaneously, total available load, load-management system, metering, billing, approval process and cost to complete a private connection. Determine whether infrastructure can expand as adoption grows.

Ask about visitor parking, accessible spaces, motorcycle parking, bicycle rooms, delivery stopping and car-wash restrictions. Confirm whether parking is included in the apartment price or sold separately and how it is registered.

Marketing claim: EV-ready private parking. Binding evidence: exact attachment plus electrical scope in the specification. Exact sales question: "Is my numbered space supplied with a functioning charger or only preparation, and exactly which conduit, cable, board capacity, load management, meter and future installation costs are included?"

### Storage: legal right and usable volume

Why it matters: a storage room can add practical value, but quoted area may not reflect pipes, sloping ceilings, columns or access. A use right is not necessarily the same as a legally attached storage unit.

Request the exact numbered storage space and plan. Measure internal width, depth, clear height, door opening, steps and any service pipes. Ask whether it is dry, ventilated, lit, powered and protected by sprinklers. Check the route from parking and elevator, access hours and security. Confirm what may legally be stored.

Ask whether storage is included in the base price, whether it can be substituted, and how it will be registered. Marketing claim: private storage. Binding evidence: identified space on the signed plan and legal schedule. Exact sales question: "Show the exact storage unit, internal dimensions and obstructions, confirm whether it is legally attached to the apartment, and identify every separate charge."

### Ceiling height: ask for net finished height

Why it matters: ceiling height affects light, volume, storage and comfort, but the structural slab-to-slab height is not what the resident experiences. Finishes, suspended ceilings, ducts, sprinklers and air-conditioning bulkheads reduce the net height.

Request the minimum finished height for living room, bedrooms, kitchen, corridors, bathrooms, Mamad and balcony. Ask for a reflected ceiling or systems plan showing drops and bulkheads. A high general ceiling can coexist with a low corridor or large air-conditioning enclosure.

Marketing claim: three-meter ceilings. Binding evidence: finished-height requirement and permitted local reductions. Exact sales question: "What is the minimum net finished ceiling height in every room, and where will ducts, sprinklers or services create lower zones?"

### Mamad: protected room and everyday bedroom

Why it matters: the Mamad is part of the apartment's safety system and is often used as a bedroom or study. Its thick walls, protective window and door affect the usable layout. The room count alone does not show whether a bed, wardrobe and desk fit.

Ask for internal finished dimensions, door swing, window opening, ventilation, electrical points, data points and air-conditioning solution. Confirm that furniture does not interfere with protective components or required operation. Measure the clear route to the room from the main living areas.

Marketing claim: spacious Mamad bedroom. Binding evidence: signed apartment plan and specification. Exact sales question: "Please provide the Mamad's finished internal dimensions and show the protective door, window, ventilation, electrical and air-conditioning arrangement on the binding plan."

### Balcony: depth, sun, wind, drainage and future privacy

Why it matters: balcony area can look large while a narrow shape makes outdoor dining impossible. Coastal height can bring strong wind. An open outlook can disappear behind a future building.

Request dimensions at the narrowest and widest points, not only total area. Place scaled furniture while preserving door movement and circulation. Check orientation, overhang, sun exposure, prevailing wind, balustrade type, privacy screens, water point, electrical outlet, lighting and drainage. Ask where air-conditioning equipment, service cupboards or structural columns reduce use.

Review adjacent planning and facade relationships. A side balcony may look toward another unit at close distance. Ask whether pergolas, glazing, shading, grills or planters are permitted under planning rules and building regulations.

Marketing claim: large sun balcony with open view. Binding evidence: dimensions and attachments on the signed plan, plus no unsupported guarantee about external land. Exact sales question: "What is the minimum usable balcony depth, where are all fixed obstructions and drains, what is the sun and wind orientation, and what approved building rights exist opposite it?"

### Kitchen and sanitary ware: models beat adjectives

Why it matters: premium, designer and European standard are not measurable specifications. A kitchen's value depends on cabinet length and height, internal hardware, worktop, sink, faucet, appliance openings, lighting and installation.

Request the supplier, model range, cabinet material, finish, hardware brand or performance, drawer count, worktop material and thickness, backsplash, sink, faucet and waste arrangement. Ask whether the kitchen plan is fixed or based on a credit, and whether delivery and installation are included. Confirm appliance dimensions and whether changing supplier affects warranty or schedule.

For bathrooms, identify toilet, concealed cistern, basin, faucet, shower system, bathtub, screen, cabinets, mirrors, tiles and drainage. Ask for quantities, sizes, manufacturer and permitted alternatives. Inspect slip resistance and floor gradients at handover.

Marketing claim: luxury branded kitchen and bathrooms. Binding evidence: named products or objective minimum specifications in signed schedules. Exact sales question: "Which exact product lines, quantities, materials and installation items are included in the base price, and what credit applies if we choose another supplier?"

### Appliances: included, credited or only illustrated

Why it matters: renderings routinely show ovens, refrigerators, wine coolers, washers and lighting that may not be supplied. An appliance package can have voltage, warranty and servicing implications for an overseas buyer.

List every appliance separately. Ask for manufacturer, model or minimum specification, installation, delivery timing and warranty start. Confirm whether the apartment includes only an opening and electrical point. Check extractor ventilation, induction power, refrigerator water line, dishwasher connection, washer and dryer ventilation.

Marketing claim: fully equipped kitchen. Binding evidence: appliance schedule attached to the contract. Exact sales question: "Which appliances are delivered and installed in the base price, by model or minimum specification, and which items in the model apartment are display only?"

### Smart home: infrastructure is not an installed system

Why it matters: smart-home preparation may mean conduits or neutral wires, while a functioning system requires controllers, switches, sensors, network, software and commissioning. Proprietary systems can create replacement and subscription costs.

Ask which circuits and devices are controlled, whether the system handles lighting, shutters, climate, entry, intercom, leak detection and energy monitoring, and whether remote access works outside Israel. Identify the controller, communication standard, local manual override, cybersecurity update policy, cloud dependence, subscription, installer access and warranty.

Check network infrastructure: communications cabinet, fiber entry, wired data points, Wi-Fi access-point locations and mobile reception. Marketing claim: complete smart apartment. Binding evidence: installed hardware and functions in the specification. Exact sales question: "Which smart-home devices, controllers, licenses and commissioning are included, what works without a subscription or cloud service, and what is merely prepared for later installation?"

### Acoustics: ask about the assembly, not the slogan

Why it matters: traffic, aircraft, elevators, waste rooms, plant and neighboring apartments can undermine daily comfort. Double glazing alone does not solve wall, junction, ventilation or impact-noise transmission.

Ask for the facade and party-wall assemblies, glazing build-up, acoustic door performance where relevant, floating-floor or impact-noise treatment, pipe isolation and mechanical-plant controls. Identify units beside elevators, stairs, waste shafts, generators, pumps, loading areas and communal rooms. Israel's official [acoustic construction regulations](https://www.gov.il/files/iplan/construction_regulation/105/) provide a regulatory baseline, but a buyer should investigate the specific design and not assume a marketing statement exceeds it.

For a high-noise facade, ask whether windows must remain closed to achieve the modeled interior level and how fresh air is supplied. Marketing claim: exceptional acoustic insulation. Binding evidence: defined assemblies and consultant design. Exact sales question: "What wall, floor, glazing and service-isolation systems are specified for this unit, and which modeled external or building noise sources determined them?"

### Orientation, windows and unit position

Why it matters: floor number alone does not determine quality. The exact position relative to elevators, refuse rooms, plant, neighboring balconies, podium roofs and future buildings affects privacy and comfort.

Mark every window on the plan and its true direction. Ask which windows open, their opening type, sill height, glazing, shutters and screens. Check cross-ventilation rather than counting marketing directions that have only a small service window. Examine the apartment above and below as well as beside it. A roof, pool deck or commercial space can introduce different risks.

Marketing claim: three air directions and panoramic view. Binding evidence: signed plan and current planning context. Exact sales question: "Identify every opening and orientation for this exact unit, and show its relationship to elevators, shafts, plant, communal areas, commercial uses and permitted future buildings."

### Air-conditioning, hot water and utilities

Why it matters: an apartment may be delivered with full systems, partial systems or preparation. Cooling needs, control zones, noise, maintenance access and energy consumption affect daily life.

Ask whether equipment is supplied and installed, the system type, zones, indoor and outdoor unit locations, condensate drainage, fresh-air provision and service access. Check whether changing ceiling layouts affects the system. For hot water, identify solar, electric, heat-pump or central systems, storage capacity, backup and metering. Review water-pressure design for high floors and backup pumping.

The official [water-system construction regulations](https://www.gov.il/files/iplan/construction_regulation/52/) provide regulatory context, but the project design and specification remain decisive. Marketing claim: advanced climate and water systems. Binding evidence: equipment scope and performance in the specification. Exact sales question: "Which climate and hot-water equipment is installed, how is each room controlled, where is plant located, and what recurring cost or common-system dependency should we expect?"

### Elevators: count, speed, capacity and waiting time

Why it matters: three elevators can be inadequate in one tower and ample in another. The relevant inputs include floor count, unit count, elevator capacity, speed, zoning, parking levels, service use and move-in traffic.

Request elevator quantity, rated speed, passenger capacity, cab dimensions, accessible features, floors served and whether one is suitable for furniture or emergency service. Ask whether a traffic study was performed and what peak waiting assumptions it used. Determine how an elevator outage affects upper floors and whether pool, parking or commercial users share the same bank.

Marketing claim: high-speed elevator system. Binding evidence: elevator schedule and building plans. Exact sales question: "How many lifts serve my floors, at what rated speed and capacity, what peak waiting time was modeled, and which lift remains available during moves or maintenance?"

### Generator, water resilience and emergency systems

Why it matters: backup generator does not mean the entire apartment continues normally during an outage. The generator may support only emergency lighting, life-safety systems, pumps and one elevator.

Ask for the exact backed-up loads, runtime assumptions, fuel arrangement, test schedule and generator location. Confirm which elevators, water pumps, access controls, communications, garage gates and apartment circuits remain active. Ask about water storage or pressure continuity, fire systems and protected-area access.

Marketing claim: full emergency backup. Binding evidence: electrical and emergency-system scope. Exact sales question: "List every load supported by the generator, expected operating duration and the systems that will not operate during a prolonged outage."

### Waste, deliveries, packages and mixed-use conflicts

Why it matters: practical service routes determine smell, noise and congestion. A beautiful lobby can be undermined by deliveries crossing the entrance or waste collection below a bedroom.

Inspect waste-room location, ventilation, wash-down, collection route and separation from homes. Ask about recycling, bulky waste, moving access, package storage, courier entry and food-delivery waiting. If the building includes retail, offices, hotel use or public facilities, identify loading hours, extraction ducts, signs, external seating and shared entrances.

Israel's official [waste-related construction regulations](https://www.gov.il/files/iplan/construction_regulation/87/) provide baseline context. Marketing claim: discreet service design. Binding evidence: signed service plans and permitted uses. Exact sales question: "Show every waste, loading, delivery and commercial-service route and explain which apartments are exposed to its noise, odor or traffic."

### Management company and the lifetime cost of amenities

Why it matters: the quality of a complex building depends on continuing operation. A management company selected by the developer may provide a smooth opening, but owners need transparent contracts, budgets and replacement rights.

Request the draft management agreement, term, termination provisions, related-party disclosures, staffing, service levels, allocation formula and first-year budget. Ask whether the developer guarantees any estimate, subsidizes an opening period or leaves an operating shortfall to owners. Examine sinking-fund assumptions for elevators, pool plant, facade access, mechanical parking, smart systems and generators.

Marketing claim: managed by a leading company. Binding evidence: signed management framework and disclosed budget. Exact sales question: "Which company is proposed, is it related to the developer, what services and reserve contributions are budgeted, and how can owners replace it?"

### The developer and legal protection are part of the amenity test

Why it matters: even a detailed specification depends on execution and remedy. Ask which comparable complex projects the developer has delivered, what defects occurred and how the company responded. Request the project finance and statutory protection details before valuing the promise.

A generous amenity package can be a burden if the project lacks the resident base or budget to operate it. Compare the promised experience with the number of units, service charges and management structure. The most important exact question is: "Which parts of the advertised experience are binding construction obligations, which are proposed operating services, and what does the buyer pay to sustain them?"

## 50 questions to ask the developer before buying

Print this list, add the answer, document reference, date and name of the person who responded. A persuasive oral answer is not complete until the corresponding obligation appears in the contract, specification, plan, official record or other appropriate written evidence.

### Legal status, finance and contract

1. What is the full legal name and company number of the seller, and what relationship does it have to the advertised developer group?
2. What rights does the seller hold in the land, where are those rights recorded, and which third-party consents remain necessary?
3. What is the current approved planning status, has a full building permit been issued for this building, and may we receive a copy or identifying details?
4. Which entity is the executing contractor, what is its current Contractors Registrar classification, and has the construction agreement been signed?
5. Is the project under closed financial supervision, who is the lender or approved insurer, and when did that arrangement become operative?
6. Which Chok HaMecher buyer-payment protection will secure each payment, when will it be issued, and in what circumstances can it be realized?
7. Will every payment be made through a project voucher, and what verification procedure applies if beneficiary or bank instructions change?
8. Which documents control if the sale agreement, specification, plans, permit, model apartment and advertising conflict?
9. Which clauses allow the developer to alter the apartment, building, amenities or materials, and what objective standard governs a substitute?
10. What are the complete cancellation, buyer-default, late-payment and default-interest provisions, including any effect on guarantees and prior payments?

### Price, tax, payment and delivery

11. What is the exact NIS contract price for this unit, and are parking, storage, balcony rights, registration expenses or other mandatory items charged separately?
12. What is the complete payment schedule, including fixed dates or milestones, and what evidence supports each construction milestone?
13. Which portion of each payment is linked to the Construction Input Index, what is the base index, and when does indexation stop?
14. Does the offer include a balloon loan, developer loan, interest subsidy or deferred-payment facility, and who bears every interest, fee and refinancing risk?
15. What happens if the buyer's bank declines the mortgage, approves less than expected or values the apartment below the contract price?
16. What is the precise contractual delivery date, and what conditions must be satisfied for the apartment to be legally placed at the buyer's disposal?
17. Which clauses permit delivery to be postponed, what notice and evidence are required, and what compensation mechanism applies during each period?
18. Is there any planned charge at occupancy or handover that is not included in the price, and what contract or legal basis supports it?
19. What is the first-year management budget for the exact unit, which services are included, and what reserve or setup payments are expected?
20. What payment-security and registration documents remain in place after handover until the apartment right is finally registered?

### Apartment identity, area and layout

21. What is the exact building, entrance, floor and provisional apartment number, and how will the unit be identified in the final registration?
22. How many actual bedrooms, living rooms and bathrooms are included, and which room is the Mamad?
23. What is the apartment area under the binding Sale Law specification, and how does it differ from net usable area, balcony area and future Arnona measurement?
24. What are the finished internal dimensions of every bedroom, living area, corridor, kitchen, bathroom and service space?
25. What is the minimum net finished ceiling height in each room, and where will beams, ducts, sprinklers, bulkheads or access panels reduce it?
26. What are the Mamad's net finished dimensions, door swing, window, ventilation, air-conditioning arrangement and electrical layout?
27. What are the balcony's minimum and maximum depths, orientation, fixed obstructions, drainage points, water and electrical services?
28. Which exact orientations and openings belong to this unit, and what are the approved or proposed building rights on facing parcels?
29. Where are the elevators, stairs, waste shafts, pumps, generators, pool plant, loading areas and communal rooms relative to the unit?
30. Which wall, floor, glazing and service-isolation assemblies address neighbor, traffic, aircraft, elevator and mechanical noise?

### Specification, systems and changes

31. Which kitchen supplier, cabinet material, hardware, worktop, sink and faucet are included, and what is the buyer's credit if a standard item is declined?
32. Which sanitary fixtures, tiles, faucets, cabinets, shower screens and accessories are included by model, quantity or objective minimum specification?
33. Which appliances, light fittings, wardrobes, curtains, screens and decorative items shown in the model apartment are actually included in the base price?
34. Is full air-conditioning equipment supplied and installed, which system and zones are included, and where are indoor and outdoor units located?
35. What hot-water system, storage, backup and metering are included, and how is water pressure maintained on this floor?
36. Which electrical outlets, data points, communications cabinet, fiber connection, shutters, intercom and smart-home devices are installed rather than merely prepared?
37. What is the deadline for buyer changes, which works are prohibited, who prepares revised plans, and how do changes affect delivery and warranty?
38. May we receive the complete upgrade price list and standard-item credits before signing, and do prices include design, installation, tax and coordination?
39. How are material substitutions approved and recorded, and can the developer replace a named product without the buyer's written consent?
40. Which professional inspections may the buyer conduct before handover, with how much notice, and how are inaccessible or unfinished items recorded?

### Parking, storage, amenities and management

41. Which exact numbered parking and storage spaces belong to the unit, what are their internal dimensions, and how will each right be registered?
42. Is the parking ordinary, tandem or mechanical, and what turning, vehicle-size, outage, maintenance and replacement limitations apply?
43. Does the parking include an operating EV charger or only preparation, and which cable, capacity, metering, load-management and connection costs remain?
44. What are the pool's internal dimensions, depth, heating, enclosure, operating season, planned hours, capacity and resident-to-water ratio?
45. What is the gym's net usable area, exact equipment schedule, ventilation and vibration design, and is the yoga or studio area a separate room?
46. Which spa components are physically installed, including sauna, steam, plunge, Jacuzzi or treatment facilities, and which services cost extra?
47. What are the lobby's finished ceiling height and materials, and is concierge staffing contractually committed by hours, duties and duration?
48. How many elevators serve the unit, what are their speed, capacity and floors served, and what peak waiting time was modeled?
49. Which loads are backed by the generator, how are water, access and life-safety systems maintained during an outage, and which systems will stop?
50. Who is the proposed management company, is it related to the developer, what does the draft agreement require, and how can owners replace it?

## The purchase process in 12 controlled steps

Buying a new apartment becomes manageable when each commitment follows the evidence required for that stage. The sequence below is a framework, not a substitute for transaction-specific advice.

### Step 1: Define the buyer and the purpose

Decide who will acquire the rights: an individual, spouses, family members, company, trust-related structure or another permitted buyer. Ownership structure affects tax, banking, estate planning, financing, signatures and future sale. Take Israeli and home-country tax advice before a reservation creates pressure. Record whether the home is for personal use, immigration, family, rental or a mixed purpose.

For a Toshav Chutz, a non-resident, confirm passport details, tax residence, contact address and beneficial ownership. For an Oleh Hadash or intended immigrant, ask advisers which status and dates may be relevant, without assuming a benefit before eligibility is confirmed.

### Step 2: Set the all-in NIS budget and funding plan

Calculate the contract price, tax, professional fees, indexation scenario, FX buffer, upgrades, completion costs and annual ownership cost. Identify which funds are immediately liquid, which require sale or notice, and which may be borrowed. Begin source-of-funds documentation and mortgage discussions.

Set a walk-away limit for the total expected cost and a separate limit under stress. Do not let a payment concession obscure an unaffordable final balance.

### Step 3: Shortlist locations and live projects

Use [PROJECT_LINK] for current inventory, then reduce the list by daily routes, planning, construction stage, delivery, building type and management intensity. Record the source and date of every commercial figure. Visit the plot or commission an independent video route.

Check nearby plans and public infrastructure. Separate approved, under construction, planned and merely discussed items.

### Step 4: Compare exact units

Request apartment, floor, parking and storage plans. Compare usable layout, bedroom count, Mamad, orientations, sun, wind, privacy, noise, future outlook and legal attachments. Place scaled furniture on the plan. Compare total cost, not just price per square meter.

At this stage, reject units with a fundamental physical problem. Contract negotiation cannot move an elevator shaft or create a second exposure.

### Step 5: Appoint independent advisers

Engage the buyer's Israeli property lawyer before signing a reservation, registration form or memorandum. Add tax counsel, engineer, architect, appraiser or mortgage adviser according to the risk. Confirm scope, fee, language, conflicts and responsibility.

The lawyer examines legal rights and obligations. The engineer or architect examines design and specification. The appraiser examines value and market evidence. A lender decides credit. No single adviser replaces the others.

### Step 6: Open legal, planning and corporate due diligence

Obtain land records, company records, contractor information, plan and permit status, project finance documents, draft contract, specification and plans. Identify the seller's legal rights and the route to final registration. In an urban-renewal project, add the existing-owner agreements, conditions and evacuation status relevant to commencement.

Write unanswered questions in a due diligence list with an owner and deadline. Silence is not an answer.

### Step 7: Review the commercial and technical package

Translate the headline promise into the binding apartment and building specification. Compare kitchen, bathrooms, glazing, acoustics, HVAC, electrical, smart-home, elevators, pool, gym, spa, lobby, parking, storage and management. Obtain upgrade prices and credits early.

Negotiate the exact unit, price, payment, indexation, included items, delivery, change rights and special conditions as one package. A free appliance may be immaterial beside a broad delivery clause or weak storage right.

### Step 8: Complete banking, tax and remote-signature preparation

Confirm purchase-tax advice and reporting process. Complete bank and lender onboarding, source-of-funds review, payment-voucher process and currency-transfer plan. If using a POA, settle the form, scope, notarization, Apostille or consular steps and delivery of originals before the signing deadline.

Test names and passport numbers across documents. A transliteration inconsistency can delay banking or registration.

### Step 9: Sign only the complete package

Sign the final contract with all referenced schedules, plans, specifications, disclosures, payment terms and negotiated amendments. Ensure blanks are completed and every page or attachment is identified. Retain a complete executed copy.

Do not treat a side email as a substitute for a contract amendment. Confirm who has authority to sign for the seller.

### Step 10: Pay only through the verified mechanism

Use the official voucher and designated route. Independently verify beneficiary instructions and preserve payment evidence. Track the statutory guarantee or policy for each installment and reconcile its amount. Update the funding and index spreadsheet after every payment.

Report delays or document mismatches immediately. The next payment should not become due while a prior protection remains unexplained.

### Step 11: Monitor construction and buyer changes

Read progress notices, attend permitted inspections and keep dated photographs. Track change-order plans, prices, approvals, execution and effect on delivery. Do not enter the active site without authorization.

Several months before expected handover, refresh mortgage approval, source-of-funds documents, FX plan, insurance and interim-housing arrangements. Avoid an inflexible move date until official readiness is credible.

### Step 12: Inspect, hand over and follow registration

Arrange a professional inspection, prepare a defect list and attend handover with the contract, plans and specification. Verify occupancy approval and permanent utilities, meter readings, keys, remotes, manuals, warranties and common areas. Sign only a protocol that accurately records the condition and preserves rights.

After receiving keys, follow defect repairs, tax certificates, release of project-lender encumbrances, subdivision, condominium registration and registration of the apartment right. Completion of construction is not completion of the legal file.

## Buyer changes, upgrades and credits

The cheapest time to plan a change is before the relevant construction work. The safest time to agree it is when design, price, responsibility and delivery effect are all written. Buyer-change departments can improve the apartment, but they can also create unexpected cost and delay.

### Ask for the rules and price list before signing

Request the buyer-change manual, deadlines, meeting entitlement, design fees, drawing fees, contractor margins, cancellation charges, supplier credits and tax treatment. Ask whether the price includes materials, labor, installation, coordination, testing and warranty. A low credit for removing a standard item can make an outside supplier expensive even when its retail price looks attractive.

Identify choices that must be made early: plumbing positions, electrical points, air-conditioning zones, kitchen layout and wall changes. Finishes such as paint or cabinet colors may have later deadlines. Missing a deadline may force the standard selection even if handover is far away.

### Never approve a change from a sketch alone

Require an updated scaled plan showing dimensions, doors, furniture implications, electrical, plumbing, HVAC and ceiling effects. A moved wall can reduce a corridor, conflict with a sprinkler or create an inaccessible air-conditioning filter. Confirm which consultant approved the change.

Every order should show the final price, schedule effect, warranty and controlling plan revision. Keep a master list because multiple small orders can contradict each other.

### Understand what cannot be changed

Protected-room elements, structural walls, facade, shafts, common systems, drainage stacks and permitted openings may be restricted. The developer may also stop changes after procurement or execution. Do not assume a refusal is arbitrary, but ask for the technical reason and alternative.

Avoid unofficial site arrangements with workers. They may breach safety, plans, warranty and payment controls. All work should pass through the authorized process.

### Credits and external suppliers

If you remove the standard kitchen, sanitary item or flooring, ask for the exact credit and which preparation remains. Determine who measures, delivers, stores and installs the replacement, and whether the apartment can be handed over without the item. Coordinate warranties at the boundary between developer work and outside work.

For imported appliances or fixtures, check Israeli electrical compatibility, water connections, standards, spare parts, service and warranty. A foreign model number may not match local infrastructure.

## Occupancy approval, handover and snagging inspection

Handover is a legal and technical event, not a ceremonial key collection. The apartment should satisfy the applicable occupancy or completion requirements, have the necessary utility connections and be available under the contract. The official [completion and handover regulations](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/takanot-chok-hamecher-04032021/he/documents_laws_takanot_chok_hamecher_04032021.pdf) provide relevant regulatory context.

### What to verify before accepting possession

Ask for evidence of the relevant occupancy approval or completion certificate, permanent water and electricity status, fire and elevator approvals where relevant, and readiness of the access route and common systems. Confirm that the apartment can legally and practically be occupied. A temporary construction connection or permission to place furniture is not necessarily contractual delivery.

There is no universal nationwide Form 4 fee that every apartment buyer automatically owes. If the developer requests a separate occupancy, meter, connection or handover charge, ask for the contractual clause, legal basis, calculation and invoice. Your lawyer should distinguish a legitimate buyer obligation from a developer's project cost.

### The pre-handover inspection

Engage an engineer or qualified inspector familiar with new Israeli apartments. Provide the contract plan, specification, approved buyer changes and prior correspondence. The inspector should examine accessible structural finishes, floors, tiles, walls, ceilings, doors, windows, shutters, glazing, cabinetry, plumbing, drainage, waterproofing signs, electrical points, communications, HVAC, hot water, balcony gradients, railings, storage and parking.

Test dimensions and supplied items against the binding documents, not the model apartment. Use water tests and equipment operation only where permitted and safe. Photograph every issue with location and scale. Inspect common property relevant to the unit, including lobby, corridor, elevator, access, parking route, storage and advertised facilities, while recognizing that the owners' representative may later conduct a broader common-area inspection.

### The handover protocol

The protocol should record date and time, attendees, meter readings, keys and remotes, documents delivered, defects, incomplete works, agreed repair dates and any item that could not be tested. Attach numbered photographs and the professional report. Do not sign that the apartment is flawless if defects exist.

If asked to sign a broad waiver of defects, delay compensation or other claims as a condition of receiving keys, stop and obtain advice. Taking possession does not inherently require surrendering unrelated statutory or contractual rights. The wording and circumstances matter.

### Safety and habitability first

Separate critical items from cosmetic items. Water penetration, unsafe electricity, missing protective components, unusable sanitation, serious railing issues or inability to secure the apartment require urgent treatment. A paint mark can follow a controlled repair schedule. Prioritization should not erase the lower-priority list.

After handover, inspect again after first rain, first extended air-conditioning use and occupancy. Report new defects in writing with dates and evidence. Allow reasonable access for repairs while maintaining a record of visits and outcomes.

## Defect periods, warranty and repair management

Israeli new-apartment law distinguishes the inspection period, often called the Bedek period, from the following warranty period. The length of the inspection period depends on the defect category. It is not correct to say that every item has one year or that the apartment has a single seven-year blanket warranty.

The government's official [new-apartment buyer guide](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/new_apartment_buyer?chapterIndex=7) summarizes the categories. Under the general framework, during the applicable inspection period the seller is responsible unless it proves that the defect was caused by the apartment owner. A three-year warranty period follows, during which the buyer generally must prove that the defect originates in design, work or materials. Specific facts and law should be reviewed by counsel.

| Defect category | General inspection period stated in the official guide |
|---|---|
| Other non-fundamental nonconformity not listed separately | 1 year |
| Carpentry, metal, plastic and aluminum products | 2 years |
| Interior floor and wall finishes | 2 years |
| Machines and boilers | 3 years |
| Yard development, including walls, paving, water, sewer and drainage components | 3 years |
| Thermal insulation | 3 years |
| Plumbing, water, heating, drains, sewer and leaks | 4 years |
| Waterproofing of underground areas, walls, ceilings and roofs | 4 years |
| Cracks wider than 1.5 millimeters in non-load-bearing components | 5 years |
| Detachment, peeling or crumbling of exterior cladding | 7 years |

The table is a navigation aid, not a diagnosis. The classification of a specific defect may be disputed, and separate rules can apply to instability, safety or fundamental nonconformity. Report defects promptly instead of waiting for a period to expire.

### Run a repair log

For each issue record discovery date, location, description, photo, notice date, developer response, visit, work performed and result. Keep email copies and delivery evidence. If a repair fails, record recurrence rather than opening an unrelated informal conversation.

Give the seller a reasonable opportunity to inspect and repair where required, subject to urgency and advice. For repeated water intrusion, safety concerns or major disagreement, involve an independent expert and lawyer early. Do not cover the evidence with your own renovation before documenting causation unless urgent mitigation is necessary.

### Common property requires organized action

Facade, roof, garage, pool, elevators, lobby, plant and waterproofing may be common property. The owners' committee or representative should commission a common-area inspection and maintain a consolidated claim. Individual complaints can miss system-wide patterns.

Obtain as-built documents, system manuals, warranties, maintenance contracts and consultant reports at transition. Good maintenance is essential and should not be confused with accepting an original construction defect.

## Delayed delivery and compensation

The applicable compensation regime depends on the contract date. Search results often repeat the old 60-day rule as though it applies to every purchase. The current Amendment 9 framework applies to contracts signed from July 7, 2022, while earlier contracts may fall under the prior wording.

### Contracts signed from July 7, 2022

If the seller has not placed the apartment at the buyer's disposal and one month has passed after the contractual delivery date, the statutory framework provides compensation without proof of loss, using the higher of agreed compensation and the statutory measure, subject to the law and exceptions. The general tiers are:

| Delay period | General statutory measure |
|---|---|
| Second through fourth months | Rent for a comparable apartment in size and location, for each month or part of a month |
| Fifth through tenth months | Comparable rent multiplied by 1.25 |
| Eleventh month onward | Comparable rent multiplied by 1.5 |

The exemption is limited. Under the amended framework it concerns delay caused solely by the buyer's act or omission, or circumstances meeting the frustration conditions in section 18(a) of the Contracts (Remedies for Breach of Contract) Law. A label such as war, force majeure or labor shortage does not by itself establish an automatic exemption. The facts, foreseeability, prevention, causation and legal test matter.

### Contracts signed before July 7, 2022

Do not use the new table for an older contract. Under the prior wording, a delay exceeding 60 days generally triggered compensation without proof of loss from the first day of delay, at 150 percent of comparable rent for the initial period and 125 percent after eight months. The previous exception wording was also different. Review the [Knesset's explanation of the former and amended rules](https://main.knesset.gov.il/News/PressReleases/Pages/press08062022C.aspx), the exact contract, subsequent case law and any limitation issue with a lawyer.

### How to document comparable rent and the delay

The reference is a comparable apartment in size and location, not automatically the rent the buyer personally pays. Preserve dated listings, signed leases, broker evidence and, where the amount is disputed, an appraisal. Use several relevant observations rather than one exceptional advertisement.

Request every delay notice in writing with the new date, asserted cause, period and contractual basis. Do not sign a postponement, waiver or change order without advice. Preserve the contract, payment records, marketing delivery claims, progress notices, occupancy status and evidence of interim housing or storage.

Delivery compensation and indexation are separate issues. For contracts under the Amendment 9 index framework, linkage generally should not continue beyond the contractual delivery date except for buyer-only delay or qualifying frustration circumstances. Audit the final account rather than assuming that an asserted delay extension automatically permits more indexation.

### Handover under protest and waiver language

Receiving keys does not inherently waive compensation. If the handover document says the buyer has no claims, obtain advice before signing. It may be possible to receive possession, document defects and reserve claims, but a casual handwritten phrase is not a substitute for a considered legal position.

## New-build or resale apartment

Neither category is automatically better. A new-build can offer modern systems, statutory specification and a staged purchase, while a resale apartment lets the buyer inspect a completed home, building and neighborhood. Compare the risk you can evaluate today with the outcome promised for the future.

| Question | New-build from a developer | Resale apartment |
|---|---|---|
| Physical inspection | Plans and specification may dominate until late construction | The actual apartment and common areas can be inspected before purchase |
| Delivery | Future contractual date with construction and approval risk | Usually a negotiated completion date for an existing home |
| Payment | May be staged and partly indexed | Often concentrated around closing and registration steps |
| Protection | The Sale (Apartments) Law and the separate buyer-payment assurance law may apply | Rights protected through resale contract, registration and trust mechanisms |
| Condition | New finishes and systems, with snagging and defect follow-up | Existing wear, renovation history and building maintenance must be assessed |
| Choice | Early buyers may choose floor, orientation and upgrades | Supply is limited to homes currently offered |
| Neighborhood | New public realm may improve but remain under construction | Daily environment and services are visible |
| Running cost | New towers and amenities can mean high management costs | Costs and building accounts may be easier to review historically |
| Registration | Individual title may not yet exist | Existing registration can often be examined directly, but problems still occur |

A resale home is not free of risk. Check title, planning violations, liens, building condition, common-property liabilities, urban-renewal proposals, taxes and seller authority. A new-build is not automatically more expensive or better designed. Compare exact alternatives on an all-in basis and value the certainty of immediate evidence.

For a buyer relocating soon, a completed resale home or a new-build with verified occupancy may reduce interim-housing risk. For a buyer with a long horizon and strong liquidity, an earlier-stage project may provide more choice. The decision should follow the buyer's use date, financing and tolerance for construction uncertainty.

## Apartment and project types

Project labels describe a physical or commercial category, not a quality guarantee. Each requires additional checks.

### Standard apartment in a mid-rise or tower

Compare floor efficiency, unit count per core, elevator demand, orientation and management cost. A higher floor may improve view while increasing wind and elevator dependence. A lower floor may have better connection to the street but more noise or less privacy.

### Garden apartment

Identify whether the garden is legally attached, its exact boundaries, level changes, drainage, irrigation, privacy, retaining walls and rights of access for common-system maintenance. Check what may be built, planted, shaded or fenced. Examine units above, public paths, street exposure and responsibility for waterproofing below the garden.

### Mini-penthouse or penthouse

The label has no fixed legal quality. Check the exact roof or terrace rights, privacy, wind, shading, waterproofing, plant, elevator access and permitted structures. Ask whether common equipment or maintenance staff require access through or beside the terrace. Review the upper roof and facade-maintenance arrangements.

### Duplex

Measure usable area on each level and the internal stair footprint. Check bedroom distribution, accessibility over time, air-conditioning balance, acoustic separation and whether both levels have practical bathrooms and storage. A large total area can lose efficiency to stairs and double-height voids.

### Urban-renewal project

In Pinui-Binui, demolition and reconstruction, or another renewal structure, the developer's obligations to existing owners, satisfaction of conditions, evacuation and demolition status can affect timing. Check the approved plan, permit, signatures and litigation, as appropriate. Understand whether your building is one stage in a larger compound and which common facilities are shared.

### Mixed-use building

Retail, office, hotel, assisted-living or public uses can add convenience and activity. They can also create deliveries, odors, extraction, signage, visitors, security and cost-allocation issues. Identify separate and shared entrances, elevators, parking, systems, management and voting. Review the permitted uses, not only the current proposed tenant mix.

## Pre-sale, active construction or immediate occupancy

The best stage depends on what the buyer values most.

### Pre-sale

Potential advantages include wider unit selection and more time to plan funds. Risks include permit or design uncertainty, a longer exposure to FX and project timing, and limited physical evidence. Insist on clear conditions, buyer-payment protection and remedies for material changes.

### Active construction

Progress can be observed and the legal package may be more developed. The buyer-change window may be narrow or closed. Ask which milestones are complete, whether the schedule remains realistic and what systems or neighborhood works are still outstanding.

### Immediate occupancy

The apartment can be inspected and funding certainty becomes urgent. Verify official occupancy readiness, utility connections, common areas, unsold inventory, management launch and title route. A developer's phrase ready now should be reconciled with the contract and official documents.

The commercial price at each stage must be checked live through [PROJECT_LINK]. Do not assume early is always cheaper or late always safer. Compare the exact apartment, current incentives, financing cost and remaining uncertainty as of the quotation date.

## Buying a new-build as an investment

An investment decision starts with sustainable net income and exit liquidity, not a rendering or a forecast of automatic appreciation. The apartment must work under conservative assumptions after purchase tax, vacancy, management, maintenance, insurance, financing, tax compliance and currency effects.

### Define the likely tenant and competing supply

Identify who would rent the unit: local family, professional, student, diplomat, short-term visitor or relocating executive. Check whether the layout, school route, transit, parking, Mamad, storage and management suit that tenant. Review projects completing at the same time because a large wave of similar new units can create leasing competition.

Do not base a long-term rental case on a short-term rental rate without confirming planning, building rules, management policy, insurance and local regulation. A staffed lobby or pool may raise rent, but the increase must exceed the owner's share of operating cost.

### Calculate net, not gross, return

Start with a supportable annual rent range from comparable completed apartments. Subtract realistic vacancy, management, repairs, insurance, professional administration, common charges borne by the owner, and applicable tax. Compare the net result with the total equity invested, including acquisition and completion costs.

For a foreign owner, add currency translation and cross-border administration. Rent received in NIS can move against home-currency expenses. Obtain advice on Israeli and home-country income reporting, treaty interaction and estate planning. This page does not provide a universal tax return because personal circumstances control.

### Model delayed delivery and a slower lease-up

If completion is late, rental income begins late while financing or opportunity costs continue. If the neighborhood is still under construction, the first tenant may demand a lower rent. Model both effects. Contractual delay compensation and investment loss are not necessarily the same amount or recovered through the same mechanism.

### Plan the exit before entry

Ask who is likely to buy the apartment later, what competing units exist and when the right can be cleanly transferred or registered. Review restrictions on assignment before completion, developer consent, lender requirements, taxes and fees. A unit with unusual layout, extremely high management charges or mechanical parking may appeal to fewer future buyers even if it looks distinctive today.

No yield, appreciation or rental-demand outcome is guaranteed. Use an independent appraiser or market professional when the investment case is material.

## Red flags that justify stopping the process

A red flag is a reason to pause and obtain evidence, not always proof that the project is unacceptable. Several unresolved red flags together can show that the risk cannot be priced reliably.

* Pressure to sign a reservation or transfer funds before the buyer's lawyer sees the document.
* Refusal to provide the full draft contract, specification or exact unit plans.
* A request to pay outside the approved project voucher or to an account that cannot be independently verified.
* More than 7 percent requested without a clear statutory buyer-payment protection mechanism.
* The advertised developer brand differs from the seller and no support relationship is documented.
* The building permit is described as approved in principle, but no exact status or conditions are provided.
* A delivery date appears in advertising but the contract uses a materially different or undefined date.
* Broad delay clauses are presented as an automatic exemption from compensation for war, labor or any event beyond control.
* A statutory buyer-payment guarantee is described as covering defects, market value and every delay.
* The exact parking or storage is not identified, or the right can be moved without a meaningful standard.
* A sea, park or skyline view is sold as permanent without checking the facing parcel's building rights.
* Pool, gym, spa or concierge appears in renderings but not in the binding specification or operating framework.
* Management charges look implausibly low for the elevators, staffing, water facilities and mechanical systems promised.
* The model apartment contains finishes and appliances with no base-specification list or credit schedule.
* Area figures are compared without explaining contract area, usable area, balcony and Arnona conventions.
* A foreign buyer is told that an English summary makes independent Hebrew contract review unnecessary.
* A bank or lawyer raises source-of-funds questions shortly before payment because onboarding was postponed.
* The buyer's mortgage plan depends entirely on a future valuation matching the developer's price.
* Changed bank details arrive by email and the salesperson discourages independent verification.
* The buyer is asked to accept keys or move belongings before the relevant occupancy approval and permanent services.
* Handover is conditioned on a broad waiver of defects, delay or registration claims.
* There is no clear route, responsibility or timetable for subdivision, condominium registration and final title.

The correct response is a written question and a deadline for evidence. If the answer changes the commercial value, renegotiate. If it changes legal safety or fund protection and cannot be resolved, walk away.

## A final 10-step decision test

Before signing, the buyer should be able to answer yes to all ten statements:

1. I know the exact legal buyer, seller, land-rights structure and permitted registration route.
2. I understand the approved planning position, permit stage and future development around the exact unit.
3. I have compared the unit's actual bedrooms, usable layout, orientations, noise, parking and storage, not just its marketed room count and area.
4. I have a complete all-in NIS budget with tax, index, FX, mortgage, upgrades, completion and annual management stress cases.
5. My source-of-funds, banking, POA and remote-signature arrangements are accepted in principle by the institutions that matter.
6. Every payment uses a verified voucher or lawful route and receives the applicable statutory protection under the assurance law.
7. Every material promise about the apartment, view, finish and amenities appears in an appropriate binding document.
8. I understand delivery, delay compensation, handover, inspection, defect periods and the effect of any buyer change.
9. Independent advisers have reviewed the legal, tax, technical and financing issues within their own expertise.
10. I would still buy this exact apartment under the conservative cost and delivery scenario, without relying on promised appreciation.

If one answer is no, identify whether it is missing information, negotiable commercial risk or a fundamental reason not to proceed. The purpose of due diligence is not to eliminate every uncertainty. It is to know which uncertainty you are buying, what protection applies and whether the price compensates you for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions About New-Build Projects in Israel

### Can a foreigner buy a new-build property in Israel?

Foreigners can acquire many Israeli residential properties, but eligibility and structure depend on the land, rights, buyer and transaction. Private Tabu ownership, ILA leasehold and company-managed rights are not identical. A foreign person or entity should obtain Israeli legal and tax advice before reserving a unit, particularly where ILA consent, corporate ownership, beneficial ownership, financing or future immigration status may affect the purchase.

### Can I buy an Israeli apartment entirely remotely?

Much of the process can often be handled remotely through an independent Israeli lawyer and a carefully drafted power of attorney. Full remote completion is not guaranteed. Banks, lenders, tax filings, notarization, Apostille, consular verification or original-document rules may require specific steps or attendance. Confirm each institution's requirements before signing, and arrange source-of-funds review and identity checks well before the first contractual payment.

### What does a four-room apartment mean in Israel?

An Israeli four-room apartment usually contains one living room and three bedrooms. It does not normally mean four bedrooms. Always verify the signed floor plan, actual bedroom count, Mamad, bathrooms and net room dimensions. Marketing room count also does not resolve area differences, so compare the contract specification, balcony and storage separately rather than relying on a foreign portal's automatic translation.

### How much Mas Rechisha does a non-resident pay?

Mas Rechisha, Israeli purchase tax, depends on the law and the buyer's facts at the transaction date, including residency, other property ownership and any qualifying status. Non-residents should not assume resident treatment or use an old online rate table. Run the official Israel Tax Authority simulator, then obtain a written case-specific calculation before signing because the tax can materially change the all-in budget.

### Can a non-resident obtain an Israeli mortgage?

Potentially, but approval, loan-to-value, affordability and documentation are bank-specific. Lenders may assess foreign income, tax residence, credit, debt, source of equity, property stage and appraisal. An early indication is conditional and may expire before the final draw. Ask how foreign income is treated, which documents must be refreshed and what happens if the bank values the apartment below the contract price.

### What is a Chok HaMecher guarantee?

This guarantee arises under the Sale (Apartments) (Assurance of Investments of Home Purchasers) Law, not the separate provisions governing specifications and bedek periods. A bank guarantee or approved alternative can protect buyer payments in defined circumstances, but it is not an unconditional refund for every defect, delay or fall in value. Verify the mechanism, beneficiary, amount, voucher route, issue timing, realization conditions and replacement with the buyer's lawyer for every installment.

### What is the Construction Input Index?

Madad Tesumot HaBniya is the Central Bureau of Statistics index tracking a basket of residential construction inputs. It is not a house-price index. For applicable contracts, only a legally permitted portion of payments may be linked under the signed clause. Check the base index, indexed percentage, calculation dates and stop date, and model it separately from foreign-exchange and mortgage-interest risk.

### What is Form 4, and can I move in without it?

Form 4 is the common buyer term for the occupancy and permanent-connection approval process, although current document names and procedures can vary. Do not move in merely because the apartment looks finished or the developer permits furniture delivery. Ask for the relevant official occupancy evidence, permanent utilities, safety-system completion and the contract's handover conditions, then have your lawyer confirm that lawful possession can occur.

### What is the difference between Tabu and ILA registration?

Tabu is the Israel Land Registry, where an official extract records registered rights and encumbrances. The ILA is the Israel Land Authority, commonly called Rami from its Hebrew acronym, and it manages many state-land rights, often through leasehold structures. Some new apartments are temporarily recorded by a registering company. Your lawyer should identify today's enforceable right, transfer requirements, fees and the path to registering the individual apartment after completion.

### Is buying off-plan cheaper than buying near completion?

Not always. Early sales may offer choice or an incentive, but they carry longer delivery, design, index, FX and financing exposure. A near-complete unit provides more physical evidence but may require rapid payment and offer fewer choices. Compare dated quotations for exact units through [PROJECT_LINK], then calculate the all-in stressed cost. The headline discount alone does not measure which stage offers better value.

### How do I check an Israeli developer before buying?

Identify the exact seller company, not only the brand. Review its company extract, land rights, permit status, project lender, statutory payment protection and executing contractor. Visit comparable completed buildings and ask residents about delivery, defects, elevators, water, acoustics and management cost. Your lawyer should verify legal records, while an engineer, appraiser or architect addresses technical quality and value where needed.

### What happens if a developer delivers the apartment late?

Compensation depends on the contract date and facts. Contracts signed from July 7, 2022 generally follow Amendment 9's tiered comparable-rent framework after the first month, while earlier contracts may follow the former 60-day rule and different multipliers. Preserve notices and rental evidence, do not sign a waiver, and ask an Israeli property lawyer to calculate the claim and analyze any asserted exemption.
