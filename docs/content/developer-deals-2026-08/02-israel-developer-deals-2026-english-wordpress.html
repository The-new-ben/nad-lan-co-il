Israel Developer Deals 2026: 20/80, 10/90, Apartment Auctions and Discounts That Actually Save Money

Approximately 85,310 newly built apartments were still waiting for buyers in Israel at the end of March 2026. At the sales pace measured at the time, that inventory represented roughly 32 months of supply. Tel Aviv alone had about 10,540 unsold new apartments, Jerusalem about 10,370, Lod about 3,160 and Ra'anana about 1,530.

Those figures explain why a billboard saying "Pay 20% now and 80% at delivery" no longer feels especially inventive. Developers have moved on to sealed-bid apartment auctions, rent payments, subsidized mortgages, trade-in programs, price-protection mechanisms, service-charge holidays and offers that postpone mortgage payments until years after the keys are handed over. For an overseas buyer, the central question is not simply whether an Israeli developer is offering a discount. It is whether the apartment is competitively priced after purchase tax, foreign-exchange movement, financing, index linkage, legal costs, management fees, remote ownership and the practical cost of keeping a property in Israel while living somewhere else.

Foreign nationals can generally purchase most privately held residential property in Israel. The difficult questions tend to begin after that sentence. They concern tax status, financing, the legal nature of the land rights, source-of-funds compliance, Hebrew contracts, remote signatures and what happens when 80% or 90% of the price becomes payable several years after signing.

A buyer in New York, London, Paris, Toronto, Johannesburg, Sydney or Dubai should therefore compare every Israeli new-build offer twice: once in shekels and once in the currency from which the purchase will actually be funded.

The Most Important Number Is Not Printed on the Billboard

The number that matters most is the economic price of the apartment.

The contract price is only the starting point. A serious comparison should deduct the value of deferred payments, subsidized interest, an exemption from the Construction Input Index and upgrades that the buyer would genuinely have purchased. It should then add purchase tax, legal fees, mortgage costs, valuation fees, currency conversion, interim rent, furnishing, apartment modifications, service charges, delay risk and the possibility that the bank will value the property below the contract price.

For an overseas owner, two more items belong in the formula:

  • The cost and risk of converting dollars, pounds, euros, Canadian dollars or another currency into shekels.
  • The cost of managing, inspecting, insuring and operating the apartment when the owner is not in Israel.

A new apartment is no longer sold as one product. It is sold like an airline ticket. There is a base fare, baggage, seat selection, cancellation terms, financing and a different final price depending on when and how the booking is made.

Two buyers can purchase identical apartments in the same building and pay radically different economic prices. One may convert funds when the home currency is strong, receive an index exemption and negotiate a furniture credit. The other may sign at the same contract price but convert at a weaker exchange rate, pay indexation and finance the final balance at a higher rate.

A direct discount is usually the easiest benefit to compare. There is no need to forecast interest rates, calculate present value or guess the Construction Input Index. The contract price is lower, the required mortgage may be smaller, purchase tax may be lower, and a future buyer will see a lower recorded acquisition price rather than a high list price supported by a private financing benefit.

That does not mean a direct discount is always the best offer. In a market where developers resist reducing reported contract prices, a well-structured 20/80 deal with no indexation can be worth more than a 2% discount with rapid payments.

The problem begins when the purchaser compares the offer with the developer's own list price instead of comparing it with real transactions, competing projects and the total cost of ownership.

What Foreign Buyers Are Really Searching For

A person typing "new apartments in Israel for sale" may be at the start of the process. Someone searching "20/80 payment plan Israel" is already comparing financing structures. Someone asking "can a non-resident get a mortgage in Israel?" may have selected a unit and discovered that the salesperson's promise is not the bank's promise.

The most useful way to understand international search intent is to separate the property's job from its marketing category.

The future-Aliyah apartment

This is not necessarily a conventional investment property. The apartment may sit empty for part of the year, be used by family or be rented until the owner relocates.

The key questions are:

  • Is the apartment suitable for permanent life, not only holidays?
  • Are there schools, synagogues, healthcare, public transport and community services nearby?
  • Will the building's service charges remain affordable after retirement?
  • Does the payment schedule match the probable Aliyah date?
  • Could the buyer qualify for an Oleh purchase-tax benefit, and when should the contract be signed?
  • What happens if Aliyah is delayed by two years?
  • Can the unit be rented without expensive furnishing changes?

For this buyer, "buy an apartment in Israel before Aliyah" is not merely a tax query. It is a timing query.

The family-base buyer

This owner may spend six to twelve weeks a year in Israel. The apartment must be secure, easy to close, easy to reopen and professionally managed.

A staffed lobby, reliable maintenance and underground parking may have genuine value. A large private garden that requires weekly care may be less useful. A charming older walk-up may be emotionally attractive but operationally inconvenient for aging parents, young children, luggage and long absences.

The yield investor

This purchaser asks about rental yield in Israel, but the correct number is net yield after:

  • Purchase tax.
  • Legal costs.
  • Mortgage interest.
  • Currency conversion.
  • Building management charges.
  • Municipal tax during vacancies.
  • Property management.
  • Repairs and insurance.
  • Israeli tax on rental income.
  • Home-country tax treatment.
  • Tenant turnover.
  • Exit costs and possible capital-gains tax.

A 3.5% gross yield can become a much smaller net return.

The capital-preservation buyer

This purchaser may accept modest current income in exchange for a high-quality apartment in central Tel Aviv, Jerusalem or Herzliya. The property is one component of a diversified international balance sheet.

For this buyer, legal certainty, title, developer strength, building quality, long-term liquidity and the ability to resell to both Israelis and foreigners may matter more than annual rent.

The child or student apartment

Parents may buy near a university, employment center or family. A compact apartment can appear efficient, but Israeli purchase tax and foreign-buyer transaction costs make short holding periods expensive.

The parents should ask whether the apartment still works after the child leaves.

The luxury and lifestyle buyer

A luxury buyer is not comparing only price per square meter. The comparison includes privacy, protected view, lift access, lobby service, pool quality, backup systems, parking, storage, acoustic performance, delivery certainty and the social character of the building.

In this market, a "discount" can be meaningless if the apartment has an inferior orientation or a future tower will block the view.

Why Developers Prefer Almost Every Incentive Except a Clear Price Cut

Assume a developer is selling an apartment for NIS 3 million.

The company could reduce the price to NIS 2.8 million. The transaction would be simple to understand. The next buyer would immediately ask for the same price. Earlier buyers might object. Appraisers and lenders would see the lower transaction. The new figure could become the market reference for the remaining inventory.

Alternatively, the developer can keep the contract price at NIS 3 million, defer 80% for three years, exempt the balance from the Construction Input Index and pay some financing costs.

To the buyer, the alternatives may be economically similar.

To the developer, they are very different.

A lower contract price may affect:

  • Remaining units in the project.
  • Bank-lending covenants.
  • Appraisals.
  • Revenue recognition.
  • Buyers who signed earlier.
  • Landowners in combination transactions.
  • Market perception.
  • Pricing of future stages.

A financing benefit can transfer value without visibly resetting the building's price per square meter.

Israel's State Comptroller examined this issue and found that deferred-payment incentives can cause the price reported in transaction databases to exceed the apartment's true economic price. In a representative 20/80 analysis, the timing benefit was estimated at approximately 5.3% to 7% of the home price compared with progress-based construction payments. State Comptroller

This is similar to a hotel that refuses to reduce the room rate but includes breakfast, airport transfers, lounge access and late checkout. The invoice still shows the full room price. The guest may receive substantial value, but the published rate remains intact.

Developers also need sales velocity. Early contracts can help satisfy presale conditions, demonstrate demand and unlock project financing. An attractive payment plan can produce signatures from buyers who do not want to transfer most of the capital during construction.

The Bank of Israel responded by limiting developer-subsidized bullet and balloon loans and by increasing the regulatory cost of projects with a high proportion of materially deferred payments. It did not prohibit 20/80 sales. It attempted to reduce a market structure in which too many purchasers reached delivery before discovering that they could not fund the balance. Bank of Israel

For foreign buyers, the lesson is simple:

A high contract price with a valuable incentive can still be a good deal, but only after the incentive is converted into money and compared with the correct alternative.

Why the exchange rate changes the meaning of 20/80

Suppose an American buyer agrees to purchase an apartment for NIS 3 million under a 20/80 plan.

The first payment is NIS 600,000. The final payment is NIS 2.4 million in three years.

Assume, purely for illustration, that the exchange rate when the contract is signed is NIS 3.50 per dollar.

The final NIS 2.4 million would represent approximately $685,700 at that rate.

If the dollar weakens and the exchange rate falls to NIS 3.15 per dollar, the same final payment requires approximately $761,900.

Nothing changed in the Israeli contract. The apartment still costs NIS 3 million. Yet the dollar cost of the final payment rose by roughly $76,200.

The common mistake: "I am buying a NIS 3 million apartment with only NIS 300,000"

You are not.

You are committing to pay NIS 3 million. The NIS 300,000 is the entry ticket to the obligation. A 10/90 agreement is a polite way of sending your future self a bill for NIS 2.7 million.

Before signing, show the complete payment schedule to the bank or an independent mortgage adviser. Do not rely on an approval in principle issued today. It is not a promise that the bank will lend the same amount in three or four years, at the same interest rate, against the same income and the same valuation.

  • Existing overseas debt.
  • Credit history.
  • Tax returns.
  • Source of capital.
  • The Israeli property's appraised value.
  • Compliance requirements.

20/80, 10/90, 15/85 and 5/95: One Idea, Different Levels of Risk

20/80 payment plans in Israel

The 20/80 payment plan has become the symbol of Israel's new-build market. The buyer generally pays 20% at signing or shortly afterward and the remaining 80% close to delivery.

The attraction is obvious:

  • Most of the capital stays with the buyer.
  • The main mortgage is postponed.
  • The buyer may continue earning investment income on retained funds.
  • In some offers, the deferred balance is exempt from index linkage.
  • A future home can be secured before Aliyah, relocation or retirement.

The risk is equally obvious. Eighty percent of the apartment price becomes payable in a compressed period.

On a NIS 3 million apartment, that is NIS 2.4 million. A foreign buyer should not assume that an Israeli bank will fund 75% simply because the apartment is the buyer's only property in Israel. Non-resident lending practice is often more conservative.

When 20/80 can make sense for a foreign buyer

It can be rational when the purchaser has a credible and documented liquidity event, such as:

  • A bond or deposit maturing before delivery.
  • A property sale planned conservatively.
  • Business proceeds with a high degree of certainty.
  • A future move to Israel that may change financing status.
  • A deliberate FX-conversion strategy.
  • A portfolio allocation where sufficient liquid assets already exist.

It is not a reliable plan when the funding strategy is:

  • "The apartment will rise in value."
  • "The shekel will probably weaken."
  • "The bank will certainly finance it."
  • "We can sell before delivery."
  • "My income will be higher by then."
  • "Someone in the family will help if needed."

10/90 payment plans

A 10/90 offer lowers the psychological entry price even further. A NIS 3 million apartment appears accessible with NIS 300,000.

Economically, the buyer has taken on a NIS 2.7 million future obligation.

This structure may suit someone who already knows where the final capital will come from. It is dangerous for a buyer who treats the deferred amount as tomorrow's problem.

For a foreign buyer, 10/90 concentrates three risks on the same date:

  1. Mortgage approval risk.
  2. Exchange-rate risk.
  3. Property-valuation risk.

If the shekel strengthens, the bank values the apartment below the contract price and overseas income weakens, all three problems arrive together.

15/85 payment plans

The 15/85 structure is a common middle ground. Developers sometimes combine it with a full index exemption, subsidized loan or interest contribution.

The difference between 10% and 15% may look small. On a NIS 4 million apartment, it is another NIS 200,000 at the beginning.

For some foreign purchasers, the extra early payment reduces the balance exposed to currency movement. For others, it removes liquidity that could remain in the home currency.

5/95 and almost-no-deposit offers

A 5/95 structure deserves slower analysis. Not every transaction of this kind is inherently defective. The issue is the enormous gap between the initial emotional experience and the actual obligation.

On a NIS 3 million apartment, the buyer pays NIS 150,000 and commits to another NIS 2.85 million.

That begins to resemble an expensive option on a future apartment more than a conventionally funded purchase.

Developer Loans: The Developer Pays the Interest, but the Debt May Be Yours

In a typical Israeli developer-loan structure, a bank advances credit in the buyer's name and transfers funds to the project. The developer pays some or all of the interest for a defined period.

From the developer's perspective, cash enters the project.

From the buyer's perspective, cash payment is postponed.

From the bank's perspective, the borrower has taken a loan.

The phrase "developer loan" can therefore be misleading. The developer may subsidize the loan without being the lender.

A foreign buyer should obtain written answers to the following:

Question Why it matters
Who is the legal borrower? The loan may appear as the buyer's personal debt
Is the interest paid monthly or accrued? Accrued interest can create a surprise balance
When is the principal due? A bullet payment may coincide with the final apartment payment
Does the loan reduce future mortgage capacity? Existing debt affects underwriting
What happens if the purchase is cancelled? Bank debt may survive a dispute with the developer
Who pays appraisal, insurance and opening fees? "Interest-free" does not always mean cost-free
Is the benefit transferable on resale? Many incentives are personal
Can the loan be refinanced? Exit flexibility may be limited

For a non-resident, the file may include tax returns, payslips, employment contracts, business accounts, foreign bank statements, credit information, passports, proof of address, corporate documents and evidence of the source of the purchase funds.

The process should begin before the reservation agreement, not after the contract has been negotiated.

When most transactions in a project are sold through incentives, the promotion is no longer exceptional. It becomes the project's real commercial price list.

The Construction Input Index: An Unglamorous Benefit That Can Save Real Money

The Israeli Residential Construction Input Index is commonly called Madad Tesumot HaBniya.

It tracks changes in residential construction inputs. A developer contract may link part of the unpaid price to this index.

Under the statutory framework applying to contracts signed from July 2022, the first 20% is not indexed and only a defined part of later payments may be exposed, unless the agreement gives the purchaser better protection. In practical illustrations, up to about 40% of the total price can remain exposed.

On a NIS 3 million apartment, 40% is NIS 1.2 million.

  2. How much is paid later?

  • How the building is managed.
  • Whether neighbors and community fit.

Why a sealed auction can be dangerous

The auction uses a first-price sealed-bid structure.

You do not know what other buyers are offering.

Bid too little and you lose.

Bid too much and you win, then discover that your offer was higher than everyone else's.

Economists call this the winner's curse.

The emotional version sounds like this:

I have already arranged the guarantee, studied the project and told the family. Another NIS 100,000 will make sure we win.

That extra NIS 100,000 can become substantially more after mortgage interest, purchase tax and opportunity cost.

A foreign buyer's auction rule

Never ask:

How much do I need to bid to win?

Ask:

What is the highest price at which this exact apartment remains a good purchase for me?

The maximum should be calculated before the auction opens. It should reflect:

  • Exact floor.
  • Direction and view.
  • Internal area.
  • Balcony.
  • Parking and storage.
  • Expected delivery.
  • Payment plan.
  • Indexation.
  • Purchase tax.
  • Mortgage and FX risk.
  • Management charges.
  • Comparable new and resale transactions.
  • Ease of renting.
  • Likely buyer pool on resale.

Then the bidder stops at that number.

A foreign buyer may be able to bid from London, Paris or New York. That does not mean all legal review can safely take place after winning.

Gindi VOGUE in Sde Dov: When the Promotion Actually Changes the Price

In June 2025, Gindi Holdings launched VOGUE in Sde Dov with an advertised entry price beginning around NIS 49,000 per square meter. Nearby projects were being marketed at approximately NIS 75,000 to NIS 80,000 per square meter.

Within less than a week, the company reported hundreds of signed or reserved apartments out of 708. Calcalist

This campaign matters because it was not merely a free kitchen or delayed instalment.

It changed the entry price.

For a foreign buyer searching "Sde Dov apartments for sale," the lesson is not that every VOGUE apartment was necessarily cheap. The lesson is that a sufficiently large direct price change can reset expectations throughout a new district.

Why could the launch be so aggressive?

One explanation lies in the underlying land transaction and timing. Gindi won two Sde Dov plots for 708 homes in a tender that reflected lower land values per apartment than earlier transactions in the district.

Lower land cost can give a developer more room to sell quickly at a lower price while preserving project economics.

Foreign buyers should therefore ask more than, "What is the discount from the brochure?"

They should ask:

  • What did the land cost?
  • At what planning stage was the apartment sold?
  • Which floors carried the advertised entry price?
  • Was the price for a small unit, weak orientation or early phase?
  • What did buyers pay in nearby projects?
  • How much development risk remained?
  • What will the building cost to operate?

Sde Dov is not one project. It is a new coastal district with multiple developers, different land costs, different amenity programs and different delivery dates.

Azorim 10-0: NIS 1 Million Today, NIS 1 Million in Ten Years

Azorim advertised a structure offering eligible buyers up to NIS 1 million without interest, indexation or monthly payments for ten years, subject to project, lender approval and campaign terms.

The principal remains due at the end of the period.

The economic value comes from time.

4% NIS 675,600 NIS 324,400
5% NIS 613,900 NIS 386,100

At a 5% discount rate, deferring NIS 1 million for ten years without interest is worth approximately NIS 386,000 in present-value terms.

That is a substantial benefit.

It is also a substantial balloon.

A buyer aged 38 at signing will be 48 when the principal becomes due. A foreign buyer may be living in a different country, using a different income source and facing a different exchange rate.

The irresponsible approach is to assume that a future sale will solve everything.

For a wealthy purchaser with capital invested elsewhere, the structure can be efficient. For a buyer using the campaign to reach beyond a sustainable budget, it can defer the affordability problem for a decade.

This campaign moves the pain point beyond handover.

For a family paying rent, that can be useful. The family may enter the new apartment, stop paying rent and only later begin repaying the loan.

For a foreign buyer, eligibility must be checked carefully. A campaign stating "up to 70% financing" does not mean a non-resident will automatically receive 70%.

The bank may still apply a lower practical loan-to-value, require more documentation, discount foreign income, limit the loan term or exclude certain borrowers.

The buyer must also understand:

  • Whether interest is paid or merely deferred.
  • Whether principal amortization begins immediately after the benefit.
  • Whether the monthly payment then jumps sharply.
  • Whether the loan can be refinanced.
  • Whether the apartment can be rented during the subsidized period.
  • What happens if delivery is late.
  • What happens if the buyer becomes an Israeli resident before completion.

Av-Gad introduced a trade-in structure across fourteen urban-renewal projects.

If the apartment sells above the floor, the additional proceeds remain with the owner. If it does not sell before the new apartment is delivered, other mechanisms may apply under the program's terms and company discretion.

This campaign is most relevant to foreign buyers who already own an Israeli apartment.

Aviv Melisron and the 0.99% Mortgage Headline

Aviv Melisron published a mortgage benefit under which the company would participate in the interest on a fixed, unlinked loan covering up to 30% of the apartment price for up to fifteen years.

The company's maximum contribution was 3.51 percentage points a year, while the buyer's rate could not fall below 0.99%.

If the lender's rate exceeded 4.5%, the buyer would pay the amount above 4.5% as well. At a lender rate of 5%, for example, the buyer would pay 1.49% and the company would fund 3.51%.

Illustrative value on a NIS 4 million apartment

Thirty percent of NIS 4 million is NIS 1.2 million.

A NIS 1.2 million, fifteen-year loan at 4.5% produces a monthly payment of approximately NIS 9,180.

At 0.99%, the payment is approximately NIS 7,177.

The difference over the full term can approach NIS 361,000 before fees and changes in assumptions.

That is a real benefit.

A foreign buyer must still ask:

  • Is the campaign open to non-residents?
  • Which lender provides the loan?
  • How is foreign income treated?
  • What is the minimum and maximum borrower age?
  • Is life insurance available?
  • What happens on early sale?
  • What happens on refinancing?
  • Is the subsidy personal and non-transferable?
  • Is the loan available on every apartment?
  • What is the cash price without the mortgage benefit?

A subsidized mortgage can be better than a direct discount for someone who will use the loan for the full term.

It may be less valuable to a cash buyer or someone expecting to sell in three years.

Aura Price Protection: Insurance Against a Falling Market?

Aura marketed a mechanism allowing eligible buyers to cancel under defined circumstances if official prices declined.

The structure was intended to address the fear of signing before a price fall.

The headline sounds simple.

The value depends on questions that are not simple:

  • Which official index controls?
  • Is it national, district, city or new-home data?
  • What are the starting and ending dates?
  • Is any decline sufficient?
  • Does the buyer receive every payment back?
  • Is indexation refunded?
  • Are legal or financing costs refunded?
  • What happens to purchase tax?
  • Does the buyer forfeit another discount by selecting the option?
  • Can the right be exercised while the buyer is in default?
  • Does the mechanism apply to a foreign buyer?
  • What happens if the buyer's currency weakens even though Israeli prices fall?

For an American buyer, the apartment could fall 2% in shekel terms while becoming more expensive in dollars because of the exchange rate.

Price protection must therefore be measured in the purchaser's economic reality, not only in an Israeli index.

  1. Is the future purchase price fixed when the tenancy begins?
  2. How much of the rent is credited toward the purchase?

What Does Value for Money Mean in an Israeli New Development?

Value for money does not mean the cheapest apartment.

It means the relationship between everything the buyer receives and everything the buyer will pay.

A useful score can be built from six components:

Component Suggested weight What to examine
Economic price per square meter 25% Price after discounting incentives and adding compulsory costs
Location and accessibility 20% Transport, employment, community, schools, retail and daily routes
Planning and delivery certainty 15% Permit, land rights, project finance, construction stage and delivery
Apartment and building quality 15% Layout, specification, parking, storage, orientation and systems
Ownership cost and liquidity 10% Management fees, municipal costs, tenant demand and competing inventory
Foreign-buyer friction 15% Tax, FX, remote management, bank process, language and source-of-funds requirements

Price per square meter is not enough

The marketed price per square meter may use a method that does not allow direct comparison between projects.

Request the total price and separate:

  • Main internal apartment area.
  • Balcony.
  • Garden.
  • Storage.
  • Parking.
  • Number and type of parking spaces.
  • Floor.
  • Orientation.
  • Future view.
  • Expected service charge.
  • Delivery date.
  • Construction Input Index exposure.
  • Buyer-change cost.
  • Registration and connection charges.

A four-room apartment of 100 square meters at NIS 3 million costs NIS 30,000 per square meter.

A four-room apartment of 110 square meters at NIS 3.2 million costs about NIS 29,091 per square meter.

If the second also has a proper storage room and conventional parking while the first has a mechanical parking system, the value gap widens.

Israeli room count can confuse foreign buyers

In Israeli listings, the living room is ordinarily included in the advertised room count.

An Israeli “four-room apartment” usually means:

  • Living room.
  • Three bedrooms.

It does not usually mean four bedrooms.

A foreign buyer searching “four-bedroom apartment in Tel Aviv” should therefore inspect the actual floor plan rather than relying on the local room count.

The protected room, known as a Mamad, may function as one of the bedrooms, but its dimensions, window, ventilation and furniture layout should be checked.

Gross yield is not net yield

An overseas investor should calculate:

Annual rent
minus management fees
minus municipal and owner expenses
minus insurance
minus property-management fees
minus repairs and replacement reserve
minus vacancy
minus tax
minus currency and transfer costs
divided by total cash invested

A 3.5% gross yield can become a much lower net yield in a tower with a pool, concierge and high maintenance.

A deferred-payment deal can improve the initial return on cash paid, but it does not change the final capital required.

The investor should calculate yield on the entire equity needed to complete the purchase, not only on the signing deposit.

Strange but Useful Comparisons

A 20/80 apartment is a phone installment plan with a NIS 2.4 million balloon

Mobile companies taught consumers to think about the monthly payment rather than the total price.

Israeli developer promotions use a related technique, except they sometimes remove even the monthly payment. The advertisement presents the deposit instead of the obligation.

An apartment auction is eBay without the visible leading bid

The buyer competes against an unknown number.

The urge to add another NIS 100,000 can feel small relative to the apartment price. It is still NIS 100,000.

Trade-in is a car dealership for an asset with a kitchen and land rights

A dealership pays less than open-market value for a car in exchange for convenience.

The same logic applies to an apartment, except that 6% of NIS 2 million is NIS 120,000.

An index exemption is insurance

Nobody knows the future index.

The exemption transfers risk to the developer. Its value increases with the exposed balance and time to delivery.

Developer rent support is credit-card cashback

The buyer makes a very large purchase and receives money back over time.

The cashback is valuable only after confirming that the underlying product was competitively priced.

A residential pool is a permanent resort fee

A hotel resort fee lasts a few nights.

A tower's pool costs money every month, including winter, vacancy and years when the owner rarely visits.

A Jerusalem buyer should compare:

Buying an Apartment in Israel from Abroad: The Due-Diligence Sequence

Foreigners can generally purchase most private residential property in Israel. The main differences usually arise in tax, financing, documentation and the legal nature of the land or registered rights.

A buyer should not assume that every Israeli apartment is held through the same title structure. Rights may be recorded in the Land Registry, through the Israel Land Authority, through a registering company or through an interim project structure.

The NadLan guide to buying property in Israel from abroad and the foreign-buyer guide to new-build projects provide a broader process framework.

Step 1: define what the apartment must do

  • A home for our child.

  • A combination of personal use and long-term rental.

A mixed purpose is acceptable.

An undefined purpose produces confused decisions.

Gindi VOGUE in Sde Dov: When the Promotion Actually Changes the Price

In June 2025, Gindi Holdings launched an aggressive sales campaign for VOGUE in Sde Dov with a publicized entry price of approximately NIS 49,000 per square meter.

At the time, nearby projects were being marketed at around NIS 75,000 to NIS 80,000 per square meter. Within less than a week, approximately 450 of the project’s 708 apartments were reported as sold or reserved, including signed contracts and buyers who paid a reservation amount.

This example matters because the campaign did not merely add a kitchen or postpone payment. It changed the entry price.

Later remaining units were publicized from around NIS 59,000 per square meter, meaning even buyers who missed the first launch received a new reference point.

One explanation was land cost. Results from Israel Land Authority tenders in central Sde Dov in February 2025 reflected a material reduction in land value per unit compared with earlier rounds. Gindi won two compounds for 708 apartments for approximately NIS 1.24 billion including development costs.

When the land basis is lower, a developer can sell rapidly at a lower price and still preserve project economics.

The lesson is simple:

A real discount is not measured against last month’s presentation. It is measured against the land basis, competing transactions, planning stage, delivery date, floor, orientation and risk.

Sde Dov is also a location where amenities can change the entire ownership equation. A foreign buyer considering a part-time coastal residence should compare the complete Sde Dov district guide and individual project pages rather than looking only at price per square meter.

A lower price per square meter may conceal:

  • A lower floor.
  • A partial rather than full sea view.
  • A smaller or less usable balcony.
  • Mechanical parking.
  • A different building within the compound.
  • Higher service charges.
  • A later delivery date.
  • A more exposed location during years of surrounding construction.
  • Restrictions on apartment changes.
  • A weaker management package.

Azorim 10-0: NIS 1 Million Today, NIS 1 Million Ten Years Later

At the end of 2025, Azorim publicized an offer under which qualifying buyers in selected projects could receive up to NIS 1 million with no interest, no indexation and no monthly repayment for ten years.

This is one of the most economically interesting developer promotions because the value is hidden inside time.

The present value of NIS 1 million due in ten years is approximately:

At a 5% discount rate, postponing NIS 1 million for ten years without interest has an illustrative value of about NIS 386,000.

There is another side. The principal still exists at the end of ten years.

A buyer aged 38 will be 48 when the amount falls due. A buyer aged 58 will be 68.

Without a savings or refinancing plan, the benefit can become a large mid-life or retirement balloon.

For a foreign buyer, the loan can also create a ten-year currency position. The amount is due in NIS, while the buyer’s income or assets may remain in dollars, euros or pounds.

A disciplined buyer can create a dedicated sinking fund and treat the future repayment as though it were already being paid monthly. The structure can be attractive to someone with substantial assets who values liquidity. It is less suitable for someone using the deferral to stretch into a property that is fundamentally unaffordable.

For an overseas buyer, the analysis is different.

  • Can rental income legally and practically service the later loan?
  • Is projected rent conservative?
  • How long will it take to find a tenant?
  • Who manages the property?

  • Does the service charge reduce net rent materially?
  • Does the developer subsidy end on a fixed date if delivery is delayed?
  • Is interest capitalized?
  • What is the payment after the promotional period?
  • Can the loan be refinanced by a non-resident?

The principal does not disappear during the free period.

How much does a similar apartment cost in a nearby project without the rent-support campaign?

If the comparable apartment costs NIS 300,000 less, the developer is not paying your rent. You are paying for the support through the purchase price and receiving it back gradually.

Foreign-buyer treatment of rent support

A non-resident should also ask:

  • Is the support described as a price discount, reimbursement or separate payment?
  • Is it paid to an Israeli bank account?
  • Is Israeli tax reporting required?
  • Does it affect purchase-tax value?
  • Can it be assigned?
  • Does it continue if the apartment is sold?
  • Does it continue if delivery is delayed?
  • Does it depend on the buyer paying rent elsewhere?
  • Is the amount fixed in NIS?
  • What happens if the buyer becomes an Israeli resident during the period?

Tax treatment depends on the legal and accounting structure. The marketing description is not enough.

YTOWN and its amenities

YTOWN is planned as a tower of approximately 26 storeys and 146 apartments. Publicized facilities included:

  • A double-height lobby intended to function as an art gallery.
  • A wellness floor.
  • Gym.
  • Treatment rooms.
  • Jacuzzi.
  • Residents’ club.
  • Work area.
  • Rooftop spaces for yoga and Pilates.
  • Japanese-style landscaping.

A family that works partly from home and uses the gym, work area and residents’ club may receive genuine value.

A foreign investor renting to a tenant who does not value those facilities may experience them mainly as service charges.

Av-Gad Trade-In: Selling an Apartment the Way You Exchange a Car

Av-Gad’s trade-in program was publicized for 14 projects in Tel Aviv, Ramat Hasharon, Herzliya, Bat Yam, Holon, Ramla, Ra'anana, Hod Hasharon, Kfar Saba, Petah Tikva and Rishon LeZion.

The publicized structure worked broadly as follows:

  1. The buyer’s existing apartment received an appraisal.
  2. A floor price was generally set at the appraised value minus 6%.
  3. The company appointed and paid a marketer.
  4. If the apartment sold above the floor price, the excess remained with the buyer.
  5. If it did not sell by delivery, additional mechanisms could apply under the program terms.

For an existing apartment valued at NIS 2 million:

Component Amount
Appraised value NIS 2,000,000
6% reduction NIS 120,000
Floor price NIS 1,880,000
Potential saving on a 2% brokerage fee Up to about NIS 40,000
Illustrative remaining price of certainty About NIS 80,000 before other costs

If the trade-in avoids bridge finance, temporary rent, storage and two moves, the price of certainty may be reasonable.

If the apartment could be sold easily at full appraised value, the 6% reduction is expensive.

For most first-time foreign buyers, the program is irrelevant because there is no existing Israeli property to exchange.

It may be useful for:

  • Returning Israelis.
  • Future olim who already own an apartment in Israel.
  • Foreign residents upgrading an Israeli second home.
  • Families dealing with an inherited Israeli apartment.
  • Older owners who want to avoid managing a sale remotely.

Do not assume the program accepts property outside Israel. A trade-in structure designed around Israeli valuation, marketing and legal transfer will normally require project-specific confirmation before any overseas property is considered.

The trade-in is not “full value for your old apartment.” It is the purchase of timing certainty at a defined discount.

A 0.99% Mortgage: The Number Behind the Headline

In campaign rules published by Aviv Melisron in May 2026, the developer offered to participate in the interest on a loan of up to 30% of the apartment price for up to 15 years.

The company would contribute up to 3.51 percentage points of interest so that the buyer paid at least 0.99%.

The 0.99% figure was not necessarily an absolute ceiling.

If the lender’s rate exceeded 4.5%, the buyer would pay the portion above 4.5% as well. At a 5% loan rate, for example:

  • Developer contribution: 3.51%.
  • Buyer interest: 1.49%.

On a NIS 4 million apartment, 30% equals NIS 1.2 million.

An illustrative NIS 1.2 million loan over 15 years produces:

Interest rate Approximate monthly payment
4.5% About NIS 9,180
0.99% About NIS 7,177

The cumulative difference over the term can approach hundreds of thousands of shekels before fees, early repayment and product changes.

This is a benefit with genuine economic value, but the buyer must compare it with the cash price without the promotion.

A foreign buyer must also ask:

  • Is the underlying loan available to non-residents?
  • Is foreign income accepted?
  • In what currency is the loan denominated?
  • Can the subsidy survive a change in residency?
  • What happens on early sale?
  • What happens on refinancing?
  • What happens if the borrower repays from abroad?
  • Is the benefit personal and non-transferable?
  • Which exchange rate applies to overseas payments?
  • Does the lender require an Israeli bank account?

A beautiful subsidized rate has no value if the buyer cannot obtain the loan.

Aura’s Price Protection: Insurance or Marketing Language?

Aura introduced a mechanism that allowed certain buyers to cancel if housing prices declined, subject to dates and conditions.

The concept was commercially interesting. Instead of promising that prices could only rise, the developer addressed fear of a fall.

The value depended entirely on the details:

  • Who determines whether prices declined?
  • Which apartment is used as the comparison?
  • Is the comparison against the project, city, district or national index?
  • How large must the decline be?
  • When can the right be exercised?
  • What money is refunded?
  • Is the refund indexed?
  • Is there a cancellation charge?
  • Does the buyer give up another incentive?
  • Does the right survive a delay?
  • Is it available to a non-resident?
  • What happens if the buyer’s home currency moves adversely while NIS prices remain stable?

A cancellation right is like travel insurance. It can be valuable at the moment it is needed, but not every buyer should pay a high hidden premium for it.

For a foreign buyer, a price-protection mechanism that looks attractive in shekels may not protect the home-currency result. The apartment could remain stable in NIS while becoming 12% more expensive in dollars because the shekel strengthened.

Rent-to-Own in Mevaseret Zion: Renting as an Option to Buy

Under a Rent-to-Own model, the resident occupies the apartment as a tenant and may choose to buy later. Some or all of the rent may be credited toward the purchase if the option is exercised.

A Rent-to-Own structure was publicized for selected four- and five-room apartments, garden apartments and penthouses in an ISA Group project in Mevaseret Zion marketed through Magma.

The attraction is simple. The buyer can experience the apartment before making the final commitment.

It is almost a test drive for a home.

The resident learns:

  • How much traffic is heard.
  • How sunlight behaves.
  • Whether parking is convenient.
  • How the building is managed.
  • Whether the neighbors and community fit.
  • How long the journey to Jerusalem takes.
  • Whether service charges match the promise.
  • Whether the apartment remains comfortable in winter and summer.

Four questions determine whether the arrangement is attractive:

  1. Is the purchase price fixed at the beginning or set later?
  2. What portion of rent is credited?
  3. What happens to the credit if the tenant decides not to buy?
  4. Who pays maintenance, insurance and repairs during rental?

A foreign buyer should add:

  1. Can the option be exercised through a power of attorney?
  2. When does purchase tax become due?
  3. Is the option assignable?
  4. Can rent be paid from abroad without repeated compliance delays?
  5. Does immigration status affect the eventual purchase?
  6. Is the option price denominated entirely in NIS?

If the future price is not fixed, the buyer may pay a premium for several years and then discover that the apartment moved beyond reach.

If the price is fixed and the market falls, the option may become expensive.

Free Management Fees, Furniture and a Complimentary Kitchen: Money or Confetti?

A small benefit is not necessarily a bad benefit. It should simply remain small in the conversation.

Free management fees

If management fees are NIS 700 per month and the developer pays them for three years, the nominal value is NIS 25,200. At a 5% discount rate, the present value is roughly NIS 23,400.

On a NIS 2.71 million apartment, that is less than 1%.

It is pleasant. It is not a reason to choose the project.

The benefit is more meaningful in a tower where the service charge is NIS 1,500 per month. Three years equal NIS 54,000.

The catch appears in year four. The owner begins paying NIS 18,000 per year.

A foreign buyer should investigate what happens during the subsidized period:

  • Is the service level fully funded?
  • Does the developer cover unsold apartments?
  • Is a temporary low budget creating deferred maintenance?
  • Is a reserve fund being established?
  • Are pool, security and concierge costs included?
  • Does the subsidy expire on a fixed date or three years from handover?
  • Who pays during phased occupancy?
  • What happens if common facilities open later than apartments?

Furnished apartments

There is a difference between “furnished apartment” and a binding list of valuable items.

Request an annex showing:

  • Brand.
  • Model.
  • Dimensions.
  • Quantity.
  • Warranty.
  • Installation.
  • Delivery date.
  • Replacement right.
  • Cash credit if unavailable.
  • Suitability for rental use.

A sofa shown at NIS 30,000 is not worth NIS 30,000 to you if you would have bought a different sofa for NIS 12,000.

A NIS 20,000 television does not create full value if the buyer already owns one or plans to rent the apartment unfurnished.

For a foreign owner, furniture may have additional value because it reduces the need to coordinate suppliers from abroad. It may also have less value if the items are fragile, difficult to replace or unsuitable for tenants.

The correct value is the cost the buyer would otherwise spend, not the figure printed in the campaign.

Upgraded kitchens

A kitchen incentive can be excellent if it replaces an upgrade you already intended to order.

Check whether it includes:

  • More cabinets.
  • Taller units.
  • Better internal fittings.
  • Island.
  • Worktop.
  • Appliances.
  • Electrical changes.
  • Plumbing changes.
  • Installation.
  • Design meeting.
  • Warranty.
  • Delivery coordination.

Sometimes rejecting the developer’s kitchen produces only a very small credit. Do not accept “kitchen worth NIS 80,000” without seeing both the standard specification and actual credit.

The right place to examine the promise is the Israeli new-apartment specification, not the rendered living room.

How to calculate whether a pool is worth it

Suppose service charges in a project with a pool are NIS 700 per month higher than in a competing building. That is NIS 8,400 per year.

A family currently paying for a country club, gym, coworking membership and children’s activities may recover much of that value through actual use.

An investor may receive only NIS 300 more in monthly rent because of the amenities.

The investor then pays NIS 700 to generate NIS 300 of additional rent.

The pool improves the brochure and weakens cash flow.

A foreign second-home owner may value a pool and concierge more highly because the building replaces services that would otherwise need to be arranged individually. The same owner may use the property only eight weeks per year and pay for all twelve months.

Amenities are worth money when the buyer uses them, can charge for them, or reasonably believes they improve resale liquidity.

Otherwise, they are a compulsory subscription attached to the apartment.

What Does Value for Money Mean in an Israeli New Development?

Value for money does not mean the cheapest apartment.

It means the relationship between everything the buyer receives and everything the buyer will pay.

A useful score can be built from six components:

Component Suggested weight What to examine
Economic price per square meter 25% Price after discounting incentives and adding compulsory costs
Location and accessibility 20% Transport, employment, community, schools, retail and daily routes
Planning and delivery certainty 15% Permit, land rights, project finance, construction stage and delivery
Apartment and building quality 15% Layout, specification, parking, storage, orientation and systems
Ownership cost and liquidity 10% Management fees, municipal costs, tenant demand and competing inventory
Foreign-buyer friction 15% Tax, FX, remote management, banking, language and source of funds

Price per square meter is not enough

The marketed price per square meter may use a method that does not allow direct comparison between projects.

Request the total price and separate:

  • Main internal apartment area.
  • Balcony.
  • Garden.
  • Storage.
  • Parking.
  • Number and type of parking spaces.
  • Floor.
  • Orientation.
  • Future view.
  • Expected service charge.
  • Delivery date.
  • Construction Input Index exposure.
  • Buyer-change cost.
  • Registration and connection charges.

A four-room apartment of 100 square meters at NIS 3 million costs NIS 30,000 per square meter.

A four-room apartment of 110 square meters at NIS 3.2 million costs about NIS 29,091 per square meter.

If the second has a proper storage room and conventional parking while the first has mechanical parking, the value gap widens.

Israeli room count can confuse foreign buyers

In Israeli listings, the living room is ordinarily included in the advertised room count.

An Israeli “four-room apartment” usually means:

  • Living room.
  • Three bedrooms.

It does not usually mean four bedrooms.

A foreign buyer searching “four-bedroom apartment in Tel Aviv” should therefore inspect the floor plan rather than rely on the local room count.

Gross yield is not net yield

An overseas investor should calculate:

Annual rent
minus management fees
minus municipal and owner expenses
minus insurance
minus property-management fees
minus repairs and replacement reserve
minus vacancy
minus tax
minus currency and transfer costs
divided by total cash invested

A 3.5% gross yield can become a much lower net yield in a tower with a pool, concierge and high maintenance.

A deferred-payment deal can improve the initial return on cash paid, but it does not change the final capital required.

The investor should calculate yield on the entire equity needed to complete the purchase, not only the signing deposit.

Strange but Useful Comparisons

A 20/80 apartment is a phone installment plan with a NIS 2.4 million balloon

Mobile companies taught consumers to think about the monthly payment rather than total price.

Israeli developer promotions sometimes remove even the monthly payment. The advertisement presents the deposit instead of the obligation.

An apartment auction is eBay without the visible leading bid

The buyer competes against an unknown number.

It is easy to add NIS 100,000 “to be safe.” Financed over 25 years, that NIS 100,000 may cost far more.

Trade-in is a car dealership with a living room and protected room

At a car dealership, the owner receives less than open-market value for convenience.

On a NIS 2 million apartment, a 6% discount equals NIS 120,000.

The convenience is real. So is the cost.

An index exemption is insurance

Nobody knows whether cumulative indexation will be 2%, 6% or 10%.

The waiver transfers a defined risk to the developer.

Rent support is credit-card cashback

You make a very large purchase and receive money back over time.

The question is not only how much is returned. It is whether the original product price was competitive.

A pool is a permanent resort fee

At a hotel, the resort fee lasts several nights.

In a tower, the pool is charged every month, including winter, periods abroad and vacancy.

A 10/90 deal is a call option with furniture

The buyer pays a relatively small amount today to control a much larger future asset.

Unlike a tradable financial option, the contract may contain severe default consequences, transfer restrictions and no easy exit.

Which Buyer Should Choose Which Promotion?

Future olim buying before Aliyah

A future oleh may want to secure a home before immigration, particularly in Jerusalem, Tel Aviv, Ra'anana, Modi'in, Netanya or another community with strong family and social connections.

A deferred-payment plan can align delivery with the planned move.

The buyer must model two versions:

  1. Signing and completing as a non-resident.
  2. Signing or completing after obtaining Israeli status.

The analysis should include purchase-tax treatment, mortgage eligibility, timing of benefits, foreign income, future Israeli income, currency conversion, delivery timing and whether the apartment will be rented in the interim.

Do not choose the contract date for emotional convenience. Tax and financing consequences may be substantial.

Diaspora families seeking an Israeli base

This buyer may care less about maximizing rental yield and more about community, family proximity, synagogues, walkability, holiday access, security, storage and reliable management.

A managed building with concierge services may justify higher fees.

A 20/80 plan can allow the family to secure the apartment while retaining investments abroad.

The main risks are currency and underuse. A beautiful apartment that sits empty for ten months still generates service charges, municipal expenses, insurance and maintenance.

Foreign investors

A foreign investor should be suspicious of a tiny deposit.

Bank financing for a non-resident may be limited. Purchase tax is high. Gross residential yields in prime locations are often modest.

A NIS 3 million apartment can require NIS 1.5 million equity if financing is 50%, approximately NIS 240,000 purchase tax, legal and banking expenses, furniture, service-charge reserve, management and currency costs.

A 10% signing payment does not change the final arithmetic.

Use the purchase-tax calculator and investment cash-flow calculator before describing return as a percentage of money paid so far.

Tel Aviv second-home buyers

A near-complete apartment with reliable management may be worth more than a deeper presale discount in a district that remains a construction zone for years.

Prioritize ease of opening and closing the apartment, security, remote climate control, leak detection, package management, guest access, parking, lift reliability, walkability, service-charge transparency and future view protection.

Jerusalem foreign buyers

Jerusalem’s international buyers often prioritize family, community, religion and long-term use rather than yield alone.

Compare established neighborhoods, new transport-led projects, elevators, parking, Shabbat walkability, topography, accessibility, protected rooms and aging-in-place suitability.

A large deferred payment may suit a family preparing for Aliyah, but future Israeli residency does not automatically guarantee mortgage approval.

Parents buying for children

Parents abroad may buy for a child studying, serving, marrying or beginning a career in Israel.

Important questions include who owns the apartment, whether the purchase is a gift or family investment, who pays tax, who receives rent, what happens if the child moves and whether the unit remains easy to rent and sell.

Older buyers and retirees

Long deferrals are not always suitable.

A balloon due in ten years may look comfortable today, but the buyer should examine age at repayment, retirement income, life insurance, mortgage term, succession planning, accessibility, elevators, protected space, medical access and service charges.

A trade-in, near-complete apartment and predictable management budget may be more valuable than a large theoretical financing benefit.

Deal Cancellations: The Bill for 2023 Is Arriving in 2026

By August 2026, the Tax Authority had recorded 1,821 cancellations of new-apartment purchases originally signed in 2023 to 2025, compared with 1,294 at the beginning of the year. That was a 41% increase.

The cancellation rate for transactions signed in 2023 reached 4.3%, compared with approximately 0.5% for 2021 transactions measured after a similar period. Purchases signed in 2024 had already reached a 2.3% cancellation rate even though many delivery dates had not yet arrived.

Not every cancellation resulted from a 20/80 promotion.

Transactions fail because of divorce, unemployment, business loss, change of plans, developer delay, legal disputes, mortgage refusal, low valuation, failure to sell another property, exchange-rate movement, inability to document funds or death.

The timing nevertheless overlaps with contracts where buyers initially paid 10% or 20% and later had to find the balance.

What causes a foreign buyer to default?

Foreign buyers face additional failure points:

  • The home currency weakened materially.
  • The buyer expected a 50% mortgage but received less.
  • The bank refused to recognize part of foreign income.
  • The appraisal came below contract price.
  • Funds held in a company or trust could not be transferred quickly.
  • Tax documents were incomplete.
  • A power of attorney was not accepted.
  • The buyer assumed the contract could be assigned.
  • The expected purchaser of the contract did not appear.
  • Closing costs were underestimated.
  • Service charges made the investment unattractive.
  • Home-country tax treatment changed.

Cancelling an off-plan apartment is not returning the keys. There may be no keys yet.

It can constitute contractual default leading to compensation, loss of payments, interest, legal expenses, claims for additional loss, enforcement against guarantors and difficulty obtaining future credit.

Read the default and cancellation section as carefully as the apartment plan.

Buying an Apartment in Israel from Abroad: The Due-Diligence Sequence

Foreigners can generally purchase most private residential property in Israel. The main differences arise in tax, financing, documentation and the legal nature of the land or registered rights.

A buyer should not assume every Israeli apartment is held through the same title structure. Rights may be recorded in the Land Registry, through the Israel Land Authority, through a registering company or through an interim project structure.

Step 1: define what the apartment must do

Write one sentence:

This apartment must serve as...

Possible endings include our permanent home after Aliyah, a family base for eight weeks a year, a long-term rental investment, a home for our child, a retirement apartment or a capital-preservation asset.

A mixed purpose is acceptable. An undefined purpose produces confused decisions.

Step 2: set the maximum budget in shekels

Do not set only a dollar, euro or pound budget.

Create:

  • Maximum contract price in NIS.
  • Maximum all-in cost in NIS.
  • Maximum all-in cost in home currency.
  • Exchange-rate stress limit.
  • Maximum mortgage.
  • Minimum liquidity reserve after closing.

Step 3: shortlist exact apartments

A project name is not a shortlist.

Record building, entrance, floor, apartment number, room count, actual bedrooms, internal area, balcony, parking, storage, direction, view, delivery, payment plan, price, indexation and management estimate.

Step 4: appoint an independent Israeli lawyer

The developer’s lawyer does not represent the buyer.

The buyer’s lawyer should investigate developer entity, land rights, planning, building permit, project finance, Sale Law security, draft agreement, apartment schedule, parking, storage, technical specification, registration, taxes, powers of attorney, assignment restrictions, delivery, delay and cancellation.

An English explanation is useful, but the Hebrew agreement may remain the controlling document.

Step 5: prepare source-of-funds documentation

Israeli banks and lawyers may need to understand where the money came from.

Documents can include salary statements, tax returns, bank statements, investment statements, sale agreements, inheritance documents, trust documents, company accounts, dividend records, loan agreements, beneficial-ownership information and gift documentation.

A legitimate source can still be delayed if the file is disorganized.

Step 6: obtain mortgage assessment before signing

The bank should know the buyer’s residency, citizenship, income country, income currency, employment type, age, requested loan, property, payment schedule, other debt, intended use and source of equity.

Do not rely on a generic statement that foreigners receive 50%.

Fifty percent is a ceiling or common reference, not a promise.

Step 7: prepare the power of attorney

A foreign buyer can often complete much of the transaction through an Israeli lawyer under a properly authenticated power of attorney.

Depending on country and document, authentication may involve an Israeli consulate, local notary, apostille, Israeli notary and bank-specific forms.

The document should be prepared by the Israeli lawyer or bank, not improvised abroad.

Step 8: verify Sale Law payment protection

New-build payments should be transferred only through the protected mechanism established for the project.

The buyer should verify project account details, payment voucher, amount, guarantee issuance, beneficiary name, apartment reference, conditions for release and treatment at handover and registration.

A campaign does not justify transferring money outside the protected structure.

Step 9: plan the currency route

Choose bank or regulated FX provider, timing, documentation, transfer limits, hedging policy, Israeli receiving account and emergency alternative.

Do not discover two days before payment that a bank requires more documentation.

Step 10: inspect before delivery

A new apartment is not automatically defect free.

A professional inspection should address moisture, waterproofing, windows, doors, flooring, slopes, drainage, plumbing, electricity, air conditioning, balcony, storage, parking, common areas and differences from the specification.

Step 11: budget for the period after the keys

The owner may need property management, insurance, furniture, utilities, internet, Arnona, building charges, tax registration, tenant search, repairs, keyholding, cleaning and periodic visits.

Receiving the keys is not the end of the purchase. It is the start of ownership.

1. Ask for an identified apartment

Not “three rooms from NIS 2.4 million.” Ask for building, floor, apartment number, area, orientation, balcony, parking and storage.

2. Request two offers

Request price with the campaign and price without it. If no alternative is provided, calculate one.

3. Compare transactions, not advertisements

An advertisement is a seller’s request. A transaction is a price a buyer accepted.

Use official transaction data and the NadLan property-value estimator as starting points.

The tax obligation does not disappear because the developer delayed the apartment payment.

Do not wait until a transfer is due.

How to Negotiate with an Israeli Developer in 2026

The weakest opening question is:

What promotion do you have?

That allows the salesperson to decide which currency of discount to offer.

The better question is:

What is the lowest economic price for this exact apartment, including every incentive, index term and compulsory cost?

Compare three properties, not one project

A serious comparison should include:

  1. The preferred new-build apartment.
  2. A competing new-build apartment.
  3. A completed or resale apartment in the same market.

A resale property may lack a 20/80 plan, but it offers immediate use, immediate rent and visible physical reality.

Read the new versus resale comparison.

Break the package into currencies of value

A developer may have flexibility in one category and not another.

Possible negotiation trades include:

  • Furniture exchanged for price reduction.
  • Higher initial payment exchanged for discount.
  • Earlier payment exchanged for index exemption.
  • Management-fee holiday exchanged for storage.
  • Design package exchanged for air conditioning.
  • Payment-plan improvement exchanged for a less popular floor.
  • Lower assignment fee.
  • Better kitchen credit.
  • Written management-cost estimate.
  • Additional time for foreign-bank compliance.
  • A turnkey handover package for a remote owner.

The buyer should prefer benefits with high personal value and low marketing inflation.

Timing can improve leverage

Negotiation may become more productive near month end, quarter end, year end, before a public company reports, before a bank presale target, when completed inventory remains, after a competing project cuts prices or when the buyer can sign and fund quickly.

Do not sign a reservation request before review

A reservation form may contain a non-refundable amount, short deadline, defined apartment, price commitment, waiver and campaign conditions.

Send it to the buyer’s lawyer.

Israel Compared with Developer Incentives Abroad

Developer incentives are not uniquely Israeli.

Every housing market tries to solve the same problem: the buyer wants the home, but the price, deposit or monthly payment is too high.

United States: developers buy down the mortgage rate

The American campaign often targets the monthly payment through mortgage-rate buydowns, closing-cost support and upgrade allowances.

The Israeli 20/80 campaign often postpones the mortgage itself.

United Kingdom: the developer adds to the deposit

UK deposit-boost schemes may supplement a buyer’s own deposit and help reach a lower loan-to-value bracket.

A foreign buyer accustomed to British financing may find Israel’s typical non-resident leverage unusually low.

Dubai: the developer finances after handover

Dubai has used post-handover plans under which a significant part of the price is paid after keys are delivered.

An example structure may be 10% at launch, 35% during construction, 5% at handover and 50% afterward.

Israeli buyers should not assume a 20/80 plan includes post-handover developer credit. In most cases, the final 80% is due around delivery.

Australia: the discount may come through tax

Some off-plan incentives operate through purchase-tax concessions rather than hidden financing value.

That is more transparent because the property price does not disguise the benefit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in Israel?

Yes. Foreign citizens and non-residents can generally purchase most privately held residential property in Israel. Citizenship is not normally required. The legal form of the rights, land status, tax and financing must still be checked.

Can I buy an apartment in Israel without travelling to Israel?

Many parts of the process can be completed remotely through an Israeli lawyer and a properly authenticated power of attorney. Remote completion does not reduce the need for physical, legal and technical due diligence.

What is a 20/80 payment plan in Israel?

The purchaser generally pays 20% near signing and 80% near completion or delivery. The benefit is deferred financing and retained liquidity. The risk is a very large future payment, together with mortgage, appraisal and currency uncertainty.

Is 20/80 a real discount?

Yes, deferred payment has economic value. The value depends on length of deferral, alternative payment schedule, interest rate, indexation, use of retained capital, currency, mortgage terms and apartment price relative to comparables.

What is a 10/90 deal?

The buyer pays 10% initially and 90% later, commonly near delivery. On a NIS 3 million apartment, that means NIS 300,000 initially and NIS 2.7 million later. The low entry payment should not be confused with the total equity requirement.

Is 10/90 more dangerous than 20/80?

Generally, it creates a larger completion obligation. Whether it is unsuitable depends on future capital, financing and currency planning. A wealthy buyer with a known liquidity event may use it efficiently. A buyer relying on future appreciation may be taking substantial risk.

Can a non-resident obtain an Israeli mortgage?

Yes. A common practical reference is financing of up to approximately 50% of the lower of contract price and bank valuation, subject to bank, borrower, property, income, age and documentation. Some buyers receive less.

Why is a current mortgage pre-approval not enough?

An approval in principle is time limited. The bank will reassess the borrower when the mortgage is granted. Income, rates, policy, age, currency, property value and debt can all change.

What happens if the bank values the apartment below the contract price?

The bank may calculate LTV from its appraised value. If the contract price is NIS 3 million but the valuation is NIS 2.7 million, a 50% loan may be limited to NIS 1.35 million rather than NIS 1.5 million. The buyer funds the difference.

What purchase tax does a foreign buyer pay?

As a general orientation, a non-resident commonly falls under the additional-property schedule, often 8% up to the relevant threshold and 10% above. Personal status can change the calculation. Use the official simulator and obtain advice before signing.

Can a future oleh receive lower purchase tax?

A qualifying new immigrant may be eligible for reduced treatment, subject to statutory conditions, timing and value limits. The benefit should be examined before the contract. Do not assume it can automatically be reclaimed later.

Does a 20/80 campaign postpone purchase tax?

Do not assume that tax is postponed merely because the developer delayed apartment payments. Tax follows statutory transaction timing, not the marketing slogan.

What is the Construction Input Index?

It measures changes in residential construction-input costs. A contract may link part of the unpaid price to it. If the exposed balance rises with the index, the final price in shekels increases.

Is an index exemption better than a NIS 100,000 discount?

It depends on the exposed balance and future index movement. On a NIS 3 million apartment with NIS 1.2 million exposed, a 6% cumulative increase adds NIS 72,000. At 10%, it adds NIS 120,000. The buyer can negotiate for both.

What is a developer loan?

It is usually bank credit granted to the buyer, with the developer subsidizing interest or payments for a period. The buyer may remain the legal borrower.

Is a developer loan free money?

No. Even when the developer pays interest, principal remains debt. There may also be fees, insurance, appraisal costs and conditions.

What does “0% at signing” mean?

It may mean the first payment is financed through a loan or another arrangement. It does not mean the apartment is purchased without obligation. Identify who advances the money, who owes it, what security is issued and when repayment is due.

Can I sell an off-plan apartment before delivery?

Only if the agreement permits assignment and conditions are satisfied. The developer may require consent, fee, minimum payment, mortgage documentation and tax clearance.

Does an apartment auction guarantee a discount?

No. The minimum bid may be below a market reference. The winning bid is the highest valid bid. Competition can remove the discount.

How should I choose a maximum auction bid?

Start with comparable transactions and adjust for floor, orientation, area, balcony, parking, storage, delivery, payment schedule, indexation, tax, management, mortgage and currency. Set the maximum before bidding.

Is developer-paid rent taxable?

The answer depends on documentation and personal tax position. It may be treated as a discount, reimbursement, payment or another form of benefit. Obtain Israeli and home-country advice.

Does furniture reduce purchase tax?

A separate movable-property component can have tax implications, but treatment depends on the genuine transaction and documentation. A marketing label does not automatically remove value from the taxable acquisition.

Is trade-in worthwhile?

It is worthwhile when the value of certainty exceeds the discount from open-market value. Compare appraisal reduction, brokerage saved, bridge finance, rent, storage, double move, time and risk.

Does a pool increase apartment value?

It can improve use, tenant appeal and resale differentiation. It also increases management and replacement costs. The effect depends on location and buyer market.

Is a boutique building better than a luxury tower?

A boutique building often has fewer residents, less complex equipment and lower management requirements. A luxury tower may offer security, concierge, pool, gym, views and professional management. The better choice depends on use and budget.

What gross rental yield should I expect in Israel?

Yield varies by city, unit, tenant profile and building cost. Prime Tel Aviv and Jerusalem properties often produce relatively modest gross yields. Secondary cities may offer higher gross yields but greater vacancy, management and resale risk.

Should I buy in Tel Aviv for investment?

Tel Aviv may suit a buyer seeking liquidity, central-city demand and capital preservation. It is less compelling for someone seeking high current yield. Compare it with alternative financial assets, not only other Israeli apartments.

Is Sde Dov a good investment?

Sde Dov offers a rare large-scale new coastal district. Strengths include new infrastructure and proximity to the sea. Risks include high entry price, years of construction, competing supply, high charges and dependence on district execution. The exact apartment matters more than the label.

Is Jerusalem better for foreign buyers?

Jerusalem has the strongest concentration of foreign-resident purchases, especially among Americans. It may be better for community, family and future-Aliyah use. It is not automatically better for rental yield.

Why do French buyers often consider Netanya?

Netanya combines coastline, French-speaking communities, synagogues, services and a broader price range than prime Tel Aviv. The city is not one market. Ir Yamim, South Beach, the center and northern neighborhoods differ.

Is Haifa a good value alternative?

Haifa offers a lower average entry price and may provide attractive housing and rental opportunities. Its micro-markets differ sharply, so local management and neighborhood knowledge are essential.

Can I use short-term rentals to increase yield?

Short-term rental depends on municipal rules, tax, building regulations, insurance, management and seasonal demand. Some buildings restrict it. Projected vacation-rental returns should not be treated as guaranteed.

What is Arnona?

Arnona is municipal property tax. The amount depends on municipality, classification, area and discounts. Responsibility between owner and tenant should be defined in the lease. An empty apartment may still create liability.

What are Va’ad Bayit and management fees?

Va’ad Bayit refers to common-building charges. In a large tower, a professional management company may operate lifts, cleaning, security, landscaping, pool, gym and systems.

How is money protected in a new development?

Payments should be made through the project’s protected mechanism and secured under the relevant Israeli Sale Law framework, commonly through bank guarantees. The buyer’s lawyer should verify every payment and guarantee.

Do I need an Israeli bank account?

It depends on the transaction, lender, developer and payment process. An Israeli account can simplify taxes, utilities, mortgage payments and management, but opening one as a non-resident involves compliance. Begin early.

Can I pay the developer in dollars or euros?

Most Israeli new-home obligations are denominated in shekels. A developer may quote an approximate foreign-currency amount, but the binding obligation should be checked. Do not assume a brochure fixes the exchange rate.

How should I manage exchange-rate risk?

Calculate the shekel obligation by date. Decide how much to convert early, how much to leave exposed and whether regulated hedging is appropriate. Model the home currency 5%, 10% and 15% weaker.

Is now a good time to buy property in Israel?

There is no single answer. The 2026 environment includes high unsold inventory, a lower new-home price index than a year earlier, significant incentives, lower policy rates than the peak, high absolute prices and currency volatility. The correct question is whether a specific apartment reaches your economic price and fits your life.

Should I wait for a better campaign?

Waiting can improve bargaining power. It can also cost rent, lose a preferred unit or expose the buyer to a different exchange rate. Set a maximum economic price rather than trying to identify the exact bottom.

What should the incentive addendum include?

It should state exact apartment, contract price, benefit amount, payment schedule, indexation, lender, borrower, interest responsibility, fees, eligibility, conditions for losing the benefit, treatment on delay, assignment, furniture specification and relationship to the sale agreement.

What is the best Israeli developer deal in 2026?

The strongest general package is a competitive underlying price, direct discount, full index protection, payment schedule matched to real liquidity, clear statutory security, manageable building and an exact apartment with a strong layout and orientation.

The weakest package is a high underlying price, tiny initial payment, huge future balance, unclear indexation, uncertain foreign mortgage, expensive amenities and gifts the buyer does not need.

The Bottom Line: Do Not Buy the Promotion

Israel’s 2026 new-build market does not have one price list.

It has packages.

One apartment is sold with 20/80.

Another comes with 10/90.

A third includes three years of rent support.

A fourth is sold through a sealed auction.

A fifth includes a pool, spa, coworking lounge and concierge.

A sixth offers trade-in.

A seventh postpones mortgage payments until after the buyer receives the keys.

Any one of them can be a good purchase.

Any one can also be too expensive.

The first rule is to ignore the word “deal” for an hour.

Place every payment, benefit, tax and expense on a timeline.

Convert every incentive into present-value shekels.

Then convert the full transaction into the buyer’s home currency under several exchange-rate scenarios.

Compare the exact apartment with real alternatives.

Calculate purchase tax, financing, management, vacancy and exit costs.

The second rule is to examine the day after the campaign ends.

The day the developer stops paying rent.

The day the management-fee holiday expires.

The day the subsidized interest ends.

The day 80% or 90% of the apartment price becomes due.

A good deal must still work on that day.

The third rule is to separate emotional value from financial value without pretending that one is illegitimate.

A Jerusalem apartment near family may be worth more to you than its rental yield.

A managed coastal home may make life in Israel possible for a family living abroad.

A Sde Dov residence may be a long-term lifestyle purchase rather than an income asset.

Those are rational reasons, as long as they are named and priced honestly.

The final rule is the simplest:

A strong developer incentive improves an apartment that already makes sense. It should not make an unaffordable apartment look affordable for the fifteen minutes it takes to sign a reservation form.

The promotion is temporary.

The apartment, the debt and the building’s running costs will remain long after the billboard comes down.

What Does Value for Money Mean in an Israeli New Development?

Value for money does not mean the cheapest apartment.

It means the relationship between everything the buyer receives and everything the buyer will pay.

A useful scoring model is:

Component Suggested weight What to measure
Economic price per usable square meter 25% Price after financing value, tax and index exposure
Location and accessibility 20% Employment, transport, community, schools and daily needs
Legal and delivery certainty 15% Land rights, permit, project finance and construction stage
Apartment and building quality 15% Layout, orientation, specification, parking and storage
Running cost and resale liquidity 10% Management charges, rental demand and future buyer pool
Foreign-buyer friction 15% Tax, FX, remote management, bank process, language and compliance

Do not compare Israeli room counts with foreign bedroom counts

An Israeli four-room apartment usually means:

  • Three bedrooms.
  • One living room.

A three-room apartment usually means:

  • Two bedrooms.
  • One living room.

A listing that translates "four rooms" into "four bedrooms" is wrong.

The protected room, known as a Mamad, may function as one bedroom, but its dimensions, window, ventilation and furniture layout should be checked.

Do not trust one area figure

An Israeli apartment can have several area measurements:

  • Contract area.
  • Sale Law specification area.
  • Municipal Arnona area.
  • Marketing area.
  • Appraiser's area.
  • Net usable internal floor space.

A 110-square-meter advertised apartment may not provide 110 square meters of walkable internal space.

For every apartment, record:

  • Internal contract area.
  • Balcony area.
  • Garden area.
  • Storage.
  • Parking.
  • Wall thickness and shafts.
  • Corridor space.
  • Room dimensions.
  • Ceiling heights.
  • Orientation.
  • Floor.
  • Future obstruction.

Two four-room apartments can have entirely different practical value.

Gross yield is not net yield

An overseas investor should calculate:

Annual rent
minus management fees
minus municipal and owner expenses
minus insurance
minus property-management fees
minus repairs and replacement reserve
minus vacancy
minus tax
minus currency and transfer costs
divided by total cash invested

A 3.5% gross yield can become a much lower net yield in a tower with a pool, concierge and high maintenance.

A deferred-payment deal can improve the initial return on cash paid, but it does not change the final capital required.

The investor should calculate yield on the entire equity needed to complete the purchase, not only on the signing deposit.

A 20/80 apartment is a smartphone installment plan with a NIS 2.4 million final bill

Phone companies trained consumers to focus on the monthly payment rather than the total cost.

A deferred apartment plan goes further. It focuses attention on the deposit and makes the remaining obligation feel distant.

The number to remember is not 20%.

It is 80%.

A sealed apartment auction is eBay without seeing the leading bid

You are competing against an invisible number.

The urge to add another NIS 100,000 can feel small relative to the apartment price.

It is still NIS 100,000.

Trade-in is a car dealership for an asset with a kitchen and land rights

A dealership pays less than open-market value for a car in exchange for convenience.

The same logic applies to an apartment, except that 6% of NIS 2 million is NIS 120,000.

Nobody knows the future index.

Its value increases with the exposed balance and time to delivery.

Developer rent support is credit-card cashback

The buyer pays a relatively small amount today to control a much larger future asset.

How to Negotiate with an Israeli Developer in 2026

The first mistake is entering a sales office and asking:

What promotion do you have?

That allows the salesperson to choose the currency in which the discount is delivered.

The better question is:

What is the lowest economic price at which I can acquire this exact apartment, including every incentive and every compulsory cost?

Step one: compare three apartments, not one project

Compare at least:

  1. An apartment in the preferred project.
  2. An apartment in a competing new project.
  3. A recent or resale apartment in the same area.

A new-build versus resale comparison is especially important when the developer offers financing but the resale apartment is available immediately and avoids years of rent, indexation and delivery risk.

For a foreign buyer, the resale alternative may provide:

  • Physical inspection.
  • Existing management history.
  • Known service charges.
  • Existing rental evidence.
  • Immediate use.
  • Less FX exposure over time.

The new apartment may provide:

  • Modern protected room.
  • Better systems.
  • New specification.
  • Statutory developer obligations.
  • Flexible payment.
  • Wider unit choice.

Step two: break the package apart

Tell the salesperson:

Please provide the exact unit price, complete payment schedule, index mechanism, credit value, estimated management fee, every compulsory cost and the price without the incentive. I will compare the net economic result.

That is more useful than asking whether there is “another discount.”

Step three: trade one benefit for another

Not every benefit is valuable to every buyer.

Ask whether you can:

  • Exchange furniture for a price reduction.
  • Exchange a management-fee holiday for a kitchen upgrade.
  • Pay earlier for a discount.
  • Increase the deposit in return for full index exemption.
  • Receive storage or parking instead of a design package.
  • Change floor or orientation while preserving the promotion.
  • Cap indexation.
  • Reduce the assignment fee.
  • Extend the payment deadline to allow foreign-bank compliance.
  • Receive an English summary and binding document index.
  • Include a professional handover representative.
  • Include appliance installation for a remote owner.
  • Obtain a defined property-management package.

A developer may have a marketing budget that cannot be used to reduce the reported apartment price but can fund interest, rent, furniture or management fees.

The objective is to move that budget to the place where it has the highest value for the buyer.

Step four: negotiate at the right time

Buyer leverage may increase:

  • At the end of a month, quarter or year.
  • Before a public company reports results.
  • Before a project-finance presale target.
  • When only less popular units remain.
  • Close to delivery, when completed inventory is expensive to finance.
  • When a new phase opens and creates internal competition.
  • After a competitor launches at a lower price.
  • When the developer has many similar units.
  • When an FX move temporarily reduces overseas demand.
  • When the buyer can transfer verified funds quickly.

Step five: do not sign a reservation request before review

A reservation or purchase request may contain:

  • Non-refundable seriousness fee.
  • Short deadlines.
  • Price-change clauses.
  • Broad permission to draft a contract.
  • Limited refund events.
  • Confirmation that the buyer inspected documents.
  • Consent to a specific payment structure.

Send it to the buyer's own Israeli real-estate lawyer.

The developer's lawyer does not represent the buyer, even where the buyer contributes toward project-registration legal fees.

Israel Versus Other Markets: Different Incentives, the Same Problem

United States: developers sell the monthly payment

In the United States, builder incentives commonly include mortgage-rate buydowns, closing-cost contributions, upgrade allowances and discounts on quick-move-in inventory.

The American campaign often targets the monthly payment because that number determines loan qualification and household affordability.

The Israeli 20/80 campaign often postpones the mortgage itself.

United Kingdom: the developer adds to the deposit

British developers have used deposit-contribution programs in which the buyer provides a deposit and the developer adds a further percentage, subject to conditions and caps.

The United Kingdom also supports the availability of high loan-to-value mortgages through a government guarantee framework.

The contrast is useful:

  • In Israel, developers often postpone the need for capital.
  • In the UK, the developer or government framework may help increase the recognized deposit.

A British buyer accustomed to 90% or 95% financing should not expect the same leverage from an Israeli bank as a non-resident.

Dubai: payment continues after handover

Dubai off-plan projects have frequently offered structures in which a significant part of the purchase price is paid after handover.

An example structure might be:

  • 10% at reservation.
  • 35% during construction.
  • 5% at handover.
  • 50% after handover.

The appeal is the possibility of renting the property and using rent to support later payments.

The risk is dependence on:

  • Timely delivery.
  • Strong rental demand.
  • Developer finance.
  • Continuing payment capacity.
  • Local service charges.
  • Ability to resell.

Israel's newer offers have begun moving in a similar direction where repayment starts after occupancy, but the legal, tax and mortgage frameworks remain different.

Australia: the discount may come through tax

Australian jurisdictions have periodically used stamp-duty concessions for qualifying off-plan purchases.

This is a more transparent incentive. The property price does not hide the benefit inside financing. The tax is simply reduced under the applicable rules.

The international conclusion

Developers in every market are trying to solve the same problem:

The gap between the property's price and what the household can comfortably pay today.

  • In the United States, they reduce the mortgage payment.
  • In the United Kingdom, they support the deposit.
  • In Dubai, they extend payment after handover.
  • In Australia, tax relief can reduce acquisition cost.
  • In Israel, they defer most of the price, subsidize interest and waive indexation.

The problem is not the existence of an incentive.

The problem begins when the incentive causes the buyer to stop looking at the total price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in Israel?

Foreign citizens and non-residents can generally purchase most privately held residential property in Israel. Citizenship is not normally required.

The legal form of the rights must still be examined. The property may be registered in the Land Registry, held through Israel Land Authority lease rights, recorded through a registering company or remain in an interim new-project structure.

Tax and mortgage treatment can differ from that of an Israeli resident.

Do I need Israeli citizenship to buy a Tel Aviv apartment?

No. A non-citizen can generally buy privately held residential property in Tel Aviv. The purchaser may face higher purchase tax, stricter bank underwriting and additional documentation.

Does buying an apartment give me residency or citizenship?

Property ownership and immigration status are separate. Buying a home should not be treated as a golden-visa program or an automatic route to residency or citizenship.

Can I buy an apartment in Israel without travelling there?

Many parts of the transaction can be completed remotely through an Israeli lawyer and an appropriately authenticated power of attorney.

Banks, tax authorities and project lenders may impose their own identification procedures. Confirm the full process before assuming no visit will be required.

What is a 20/80 payment plan in Israel?

The purchaser generally pays 20% near signing and 80% near completion or delivery.

The unpaid portion may or may not be protected from the Construction Input Index.

The benefit is deferred financing and retained liquidity.

The risk is a very large future payment together with mortgage, appraisal and currency uncertainty.

Is 20/80 a real discount?

Yes, deferred payment has economic value.

The value depends on the length of deferral, alternative payment schedule, interest rate, indexation, use of retained capital, currency, mortgage terms and price relative to comparable apartments.

A 20/80 plan can be valuable without changing the contract price.

It can also disguise an overpriced apartment.

What is a 10/90 deal?

The buyer pays 10% initially and 90% later, commonly near delivery.

On a NIS 3 million apartment, that means NIS 300,000 initially and NIS 2.7 million later.

The low entry payment should not be confused with the total equity requirement.

Is 10/90 more dangerous than 20/80?

It generally creates a larger completion obligation.

Whether it is unsuitable depends on future capital, financing and currency planning.

A wealthy buyer with a known liquidity event may use it efficiently.

A buyer relying on future price appreciation may be taking substantial risk.

Can a non-resident obtain an Israeli mortgage?

Yes.

A common practical reference is financing of up to about 50% of the lower of contract price and bank valuation, subject to bank, borrower, property, income, age and documentation.

Some buyers receive less.

A campaign promising more must be checked directly with the lender.

Why is a current mortgage approval not enough?

An approval in principle is time-limited.

The bank will reassess the borrower when the mortgage is actually granted.

Between contract and delivery, income, interest rates, policy, age, exchange rates, property value and debt can change.

A 2026 approval does not guarantee a 2029 loan.

What happens if the bank values the apartment below the contract price?

The bank may calculate loan-to-value using its own appraisal.

If the contract price is NIS 3 million but the valuation is NIS 2.7 million, a 50% loan may be limited to NIS 1.35 million rather than NIS 1.5 million.

The purchaser must provide the difference.

What purchase tax does a foreign buyer pay?

As a general 2026 reference, a non-resident is commonly subject to the additional-property schedule, with 8% applying up to the relevant threshold and 10% above.

Personal status can change the calculation.

Use the official Tax Authority simulator and obtain advice before signing.

Can a future Oleh receive lower purchase tax?

A qualifying new immigrant may be eligible for reduced purchase-tax treatment, subject to statutory conditions, timing and value limits.

The benefit should be examined before the contract.

A family should not assume it can sign as a non-resident and automatically reclaim the difference later.

Does a 20/80 campaign postpone purchase tax?

No general assumption should be made that tax is postponed simply because the developer delayed apartment payments.

The tax obligation follows the law and transaction timing, not the marketing slogan.

What is the Construction Input Index?

The Residential Construction Input Index measures changes in construction-input costs.

A contract may link part of the unpaid apartment price to it.

If the exposed balance increases with the index, the final price in shekels rises.

Is an index exemption better than a NIS 100,000 discount?

It depends on exposed balance and future index movement.

On a NIS 3 million apartment with NIS 1.2 million exposed:

  • A 4% cumulative increase adds NIS 48,000.
  • A 6% increase adds NIS 72,000.
  • A 10% increase adds NIS 120,000.

At 6%, the NIS 100,000 direct discount is larger.

At 10%, the full index exemption may be larger.

The buyer can also negotiate for both.

What is a developer loan?

It is usually bank credit granted to the buyer, with the developer subsidizing interest or payments for a period.

The buyer may remain the legal borrower.

The loan can affect credit capacity and the later mortgage.

Is a developer loan free money?

No.

Even when the developer pays interest, the principal remains debt.

There may also be fees, insurance, appraisal costs and conditions.

What does zero at signing mean?

It may mean the first payment is financed through a loan or another arrangement.

It does not mean the apartment is purchased without an obligation.

The buyer must identify who advances the money, who owes it, what security is issued, when repayment is due and what happens if the purchase fails.

Can I sell an off-plan apartment before delivery?

Only if the agreement permits assignment and the conditions are satisfied.

The developer may require consent, fee, minimum payment, mortgage documentation, tax clearance and a replacement buyer who meets project requirements.

A buyer planning to flip the contract must review this before signing.

Does an apartment auction guarantee a discount?

No.

The minimum bid may be below a market reference.

The winning bid is the highest valid bid.

Competition can remove the discount.

How should I choose a maximum auction bid?

Start with comparable transactions and adjust for floor, orientation, area, balcony, parking, storage, delivery, payment schedule, indexation, tax, management, mortgage, currency and personal use.

Set the maximum before the auction and do not exceed it.

Is developer-paid rent taxable?

The answer depends on how the campaign is documented and on the buyer's tax position.

It may be treated as a discount, reimbursement, payment or another form of benefit.

A foreign investor should obtain Israeli and home-country tax advice.

Does furniture reduce purchase tax?

A genuine movable-property component can have tax implications, but treatment depends on the real transaction and documentation.

A buyer should not assume that a marketing label automatically removes furniture value from the taxable acquisition.

Is trade-in worthwhile?

Trade-in is worthwhile when the value of certainty exceeds the discount from open-market value.

Compare appraisal reduction, brokerage saved, bridge-loan cost, temporary rent, storage, double move, time and risk of failing to sell.

Does a pool increase apartment value?

It can improve use, tenant appeal and resale differentiation.

It also increases management and replacement costs.

The effect depends on location and buyer market.

In an international luxury district, a pool may be expected.

In a yield-focused investment, it may reduce net return.

Is a boutique building better than a luxury tower?

A boutique building often has fewer residents, less complex equipment, lower management requirements and more privacy.

A luxury tower may offer security, concierge, pool, gym, view, professional management and backup systems.

The better choice depends on use and operating budget.

What gross rental yield should I expect in Israel?

Yield varies by city, apartment, price, tenant profile and building cost.

Prime Tel Aviv and Jerusalem properties often produce modest gross yields because acquisition prices are high.

Secondary cities may offer higher gross yields but can involve greater vacancy, management and resale risk.

Calculate net yield after purchase tax and all recurring expenses.

Should I buy in Tel Aviv for investment?

Tel Aviv may suit a buyer seeking liquidity, central-city demand and long-term capital preservation.

It is less compelling for someone seeking high current yield.

A Tel Aviv investment should be evaluated against alternative financial assets, not only other Israeli apartments.

Is Sde Dov a good investment?

Sde Dov offers a rare large-scale new coastal district in Tel Aviv.

Risks include high entry price, years of surrounding construction, future competing supply, high service charges and dependence on successful district development.

The exact apartment matters more than the district label.

A projected vacation-rental return should not be treated as guaranteed rent.

What is Arnona?

Arnona is municipal property tax paid to the local authority.

The amount depends on municipality, classification, area calculation and discounts.

An empty apartment may still create liability.

What are Va'ad Bayit and management fees?

The Bottom Line: Do Not Buy the Promotion

Israel's new-build market in 2026 is not a market with one price list.

It is a market of packages.

One apartment is sold with 20/80.

Another comes with 10/90.

A third includes three years of rent support.

A fourth is sold through a sealed auction.

A fifth includes a pool, spa, coworking lounge and concierge.

A sixth offers trade-in.

A seventh postpones mortgage payments until after the buyer receives the keys.

Any one of them can be a good purchase.

Any one of them can also be too expensive.

The first rule is to ignore the word "deal" for an hour.

Place every payment, benefit, tax and expense on a timeline.

Convert every incentive into present-value shekels.

Then convert the full transaction into the buyer's home currency under several exchange-rate scenarios.

Compare the exact apartment with real alternatives.

Calculate purchase tax, financing, management, vacancy and exit costs.

The second rule is to examine the day after the campaign ends.

The day the developer stops paying rent.

The day the management-fee holiday expires.

The day the subsidized interest ends.

The day 80% or 90% of the price becomes due.

A good deal must still work on that day.

The third rule is to separate emotional value from financial value without pretending that either is illegitimate.

A Jerusalem apartment near family may be worth more to you than its rental yield.

A managed coastal home may make life in Israel possible for a family living abroad.

A Sde Dov residence may be a long-term lifestyle purchase rather than an income asset.

Those are rational reasons, provided they are named and priced honestly.

The final rule is the simplest:

A strong developer incentive improves an apartment that already makes sense. It should not make an unaffordable apartment look affordable for the fifteen minutes it takes to sign a reservation form.

Before approaching a developer, use the NadLan foreign-buyer guide, explore the new-project catalog, calculate purchase tax, estimate the full transaction cost and test financing with the mortgage calculator.

The promotion is temporary.

The apartment, debt and building's running costs remain long after the billboard comes down.

A foreign resident generally falls into the higher residential purchase-tax brackets, commonly 8% and 10% according to the applicable threshold. The exact calculation depends on current law, value, residency, ownership and possible Aliyah-related relief. Obtain a personal calculation before signing.

Should I buy before or after Aliyah?

The answer depends on purchase tax, mortgage eligibility, timing of benefits, intended use and delivery date. Model both alternatives before fixing the contract date. Do not assume that an Aliyah benefit will apply to a contract already signed or that waiting will always produce a better result.

Does a 20/80 campaign postpone purchase tax?

Do not assume so. The tax obligation follows Israeli law and the transaction date, not the developer's payment slogan. A buyer may owe substantial purchase tax soon after signing even though most of the apartment price is not due for years.

What is the Construction Input Index?

The Residential Construction Input Index measures changes in residential construction costs. A developer contract may link part of the unpaid apartment price to it. If the exposed balance rises with the index, the final NIS cost increases.

Is rent support from a developer taxable?

It depends on the legal and accounting structure. It may be characterized as a discount, reimbursement, contractual payment or another form of consideration. A foreign investor should obtain Israeli tax advice and check home-country reporting.

Does free furniture reduce purchase tax?

Purchase tax is generally calculated according to the taxable real-estate transaction value. Furniture and incentives may affect the analysis depending on how the transaction is documented. A direct contract-price reduction usually has a clearer effect than a promotional furniture package.

Can I sell a 20/80 apartment before delivery?

Only if the contract permits it. Developers may require consent, an assignment fee, payment of a larger portion of the price or satisfaction of other conditions. Tax and mortgage consequences also apply. A buyer planning to sell on paper must review the assignment clause before signing.

What happens if the bank values the apartment below the contract price?

The bank may calculate financing using its own appraised value rather than the developer's price. If the apartment was bought for NIS 3 million and valued at NIS 2.7 million, the available loan may be calculated against NIS 2.7 million. The buyer must fund the gap.

Does an apartment auction guarantee a discount?

No. The minimum price may be low, but the winning bid is the highest valid offer. Competition can bring the result close to or above the price available through ordinary negotiation.

How do I set a maximum auction bid?

Start with comparable transactions and adjust for floor, orientation, area, balcony, parking, storage, delivery, specification and service charges. Subtract financing and index costs, add tax and remote-ownership expenses, then set a strict maximum before bidding.

Is a trade-in program worthwhile?

It is worthwhile when the value of timing certainty exceeds the discount from the appraisal. Compare the reduction with brokerage, bridge finance, temporary rent, storage, double moving costs and stress. It is mainly relevant to someone who already owns an Israeli property.

Do amenities increase apartment value?

Sometimes. A pool, gym and concierge may improve demand in luxury towers and among foreign second-home buyers. They also raise service charges and replacement costs. Value depends on local tenant demand, building quality and whether the facilities remain well operated.

Is a boutique building or a tower with a pool better for investment?

A boutique building generally has lower operating complexity and fewer expensive systems. A tower may offer views, security and facilities that attract certain tenants, but net yield may be reduced by management fees. Compare rent after all owner costs.

Why does the developer not simply reduce the price?

A lower contract price becomes visible in transaction reporting and comparable sales and can affect remaining inventory, prior buyers and project economics. Financing incentives can deliver value without visibly changing the list price.

Does a large promotion mean Israeli apartment prices will fall?

Not necessarily. A promotion may reflect a launch, inventory target, financing threshold or unit-specific problem. When promotions broaden across the market while inventory rises and sales weaken, they can reflect wider pressure.

Is Tel Aviv a good property investment for foreigners?

Tel Aviv may suit a buyer seeking long-term liquidity, central-city demand, personal use and capital preservation. It is less compelling for someone seeking high current yield. The investor must include purchase tax, management fees, low gross yield and the price paid for parking or amenities.

Is Sde Dov a good investment?

Sde Dov offers a rare large-scale new coastal district in Tel Aviv. Potential strengths include new infrastructure, proximity to the sea and a concentration of major projects. Risks include high entry price, years of surrounding construction, future competing supply, high management charges and dependence on successful district development. The exact apartment matters more than the district label.

Why do many foreign buyers choose Jerusalem?

Jerusalem combines family, community, religious, cultural and future-Aliyah motives. Those motives can support demand in selected neighborhoods. They can also create a foreign-buyer premium. Compare local Israeli transactions and do not rely only on English-language listings.

Why do French buyers often consider Netanya?

Netanya combines coastline, established French-speaking communities, synagogues, services and a wider range of prices than prime Tel Aviv. The city is not one market. Ir Yamim, South Beach, the center and northern neighborhoods have different values and tenant profiles.

Is Haifa a good value alternative?

Haifa offers a substantially lower average entry price than Tel Aviv, Herzliya or Jerusalem. It may provide attractive housing and rental opportunities. Its micro-markets differ sharply, so local property management and neighborhood knowledge are essential.

Can I use short-term rentals to increase yield?

Short-term rental depends on municipal rules, tax, building regulations, insurance, management and actual seasonal demand. Some buildings restrict such use. A projected Airbnb return should not be treated as guaranteed rent.

What is Arnona?

Arnona is the municipal property tax paid to the local authority. The amount depends on municipality, property classification and local area calculation. Responsibility between owner and tenant should be defined in the lease. An empty apartment may still create liability.

What are Va'ad Bayit and management fees?

Va'ad Bayit refers to common-building charges collected through the building committee or management arrangement. In a large tower, a professional management company may operate the property. Charges can cover elevators, cleaning, security, landscaping, pool, gym, systems and staff.

How is money protected in a new development?

Payments should be made through the project's protected mechanism and secured under the relevant Israeli Sale Law framework, commonly through bank guarantees. The buyer's lawyer should verify every payment and guarantee.

Do I need an Israeli bank account?

The answer depends on the transaction, lender, developer and payment process. An Israeli account can simplify tax, utilities, mortgage payments and property management, but opening one as a non-resident involves compliance and documentation. Begin early.

Can I pay the developer in dollars or euros?

Most Israeli new-home contracts and payment obligations are denominated in shekels. A developer may quote an indicative foreign-currency amount, but the binding obligation should be checked. Do not assume that a dollar figure in a brochure fixes the exchange rate.

How should I manage exchange-rate risk?

First calculate the shekel obligation by date. Then decide how much to convert early, how much to leave exposed and whether regulated hedging instruments are appropriate. Model at least the current exchange rate and scenarios in which the home currency is 5%, 10% and 15% weaker.

Is now a good time to buy property in Israel?

There is no single market answer. The 2026 environment includes high unsold inventory, a lower new-home price index than a year earlier, significant developer incentives, lower policy rates than the peak, high absolute prices, foreign-exchange volatility and uneven demand by city. The correct question is whether a specific apartment reaches the buyer's required economic price and fits the buyer's life.

Should a foreign buyer wait for a better deal?

Waiting can improve bargaining power. It can also cost rent, lose a preferred unit or expose the buyer to a different exchange rate. Set a maximum economic price. If a suitable apartment reaches that price and the financing remains safe, the decision does not require identifying the exact market bottom.

What should the promotion annex include?

It should state the exact apartment, contract price, benefit amount, payment schedule, indexation, lender, borrower, interest responsibility, fees, eligibility, conditions for losing the benefit, treatment on delay, treatment on assignment, furniture specification, payment dates and relationship to the sale agreement.

What is the best Israeli developer deal in 2026?

There is no single best promotion.

The strongest package normally combines:

  • Competitive base price.
  • Direct discount.
  • Full or broad index exemption.
  • Payment schedule matched to real funding.
  • Clear buyer-payment protection.
  • Reasonable management fees.
  • A specific apartment with strong fundamentals.

A weak package combines:

  • Inflated price.
  • Tiny initial deposit.
  • Huge final balloon.
  • Uncertain mortgage.
  • High service charges.
  • Gifts the buyer does not need.
  • No practical exit.

The Bottom Line: Do Not Buy the Promotion, Buy the Right Apartment at the Right Economic Price

Israel's 2026 new-build market does not have one price list. It has packages.

One apartment comes with 20/80.

Another includes an index exemption.

A third pays rent support.

A fourth is sold by auction.

A fifth includes a pool, spa, coworking area and children's club.

A sixth offers trade-in.

A seventh allows mortgage repayment to begin years after the key is delivered.

Any of them can be an excellent transaction.

Any of them can also be too expensive.

The first rule is to ignore the word “promotion” for a moment.

Place every payment, benefit and cost on a timeline. Convert every incentive into today's shekels. Compare real apartments. Add tax, finance, currency, interim accommodation, management and remote ownership.

Inspect the apartment, not only the payment schedule.

The second rule is to examine the day after the promotion ends.

The day the management-fee exemption expires.

The day rent support stops.

The day the developer no longer pays loan interest.

The day 90% of the apartment price must reach the project.

A good deal must remain workable on the day after the incentive.

The third rule is to remember that the lowest price is not always the best value.

A cheap apartment in a remote, early-stage project with uncertain infrastructure and delivery in seven years may be more expensive than a completed apartment.

A tower with a pool may be costly to maintain but ideal for a family that uses every facility or a foreign owner who needs a managed environment.

A trade-in may reduce the old apartment's value by 6% but save a year of pressure, bridge finance and remote coordination.

For an overseas buyer, one additional rule governs everything:

Calculate the purchase in shekels, but make the decision in the currency and life in which you actually operate.

A real developer incentive improves a transaction that was already financially and practically sound.

It should not turn an apartment the buyer cannot fund into one that appears affordable for fifteen minutes in a sales office.
