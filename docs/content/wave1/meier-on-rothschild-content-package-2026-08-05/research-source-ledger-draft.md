## Executive correction

The existing Hebrew page combines facts from two different Hagag-linked projects.

- **MEIER ON ROTHSCHILD / Rothschild Tower / Mayer Tower** is the completed residential tower at **36 Rothschild Boulevard, at the Allenby Street intersection**, designed by Richard Meier & Partners and developed through Berggruen Residential with the historical Hagag-Cohen partnership.
- **SHADAL / H-Shadal** is a separate project near Rothschild Boulevard and Shadal Street. Hagag describes SHADAL, not MEIER, as a mixed project involving residences, hotel use, business and trade.

This is not a naming nuance. It changes the address, use mix, status, floor description, buyer due diligence and search intent. The public MEIER content must not call the building Shadal Tower, place it at Rothschild and Shadal, or import SHADAL's hotel, office and trade program.

The strongest direct proof is Hagag's own About Us page, which lists "S.Meier on Rothchild" and "SHADAL Project" as separate projects with different descriptions. The 2025 Hagag filing continues to describe the SHADAL project independently and records SHADAL's purchase of parking and storage rights from Berggruen Residential in the existing Mayer Tower complex.

## Evidence and publication labels

Use these labels when converting this ledger into copy.

- **A - primary/official:** exchange filing, government planning document, architect's own project record.
- **B - first-party marketing:** developer or project marketing page. Useful, but promotional and sometimes undated.
- **C - reputable independent report:** established business or architecture publication.
- **D - discovery/claim inventory only:** the current nad-lan page, an aggregator, search snippet or broker listing.
- **Publishable:** supported by sufficiently strong evidence and safe when worded as shown.
- **Attribution required:** may be used only with its date/source or as a historical marketing claim.
- **Historical only:** true for the stated date, not proof of current inventory, services or price.
- **Do not publish:** false, conflated, unsupported, or materially misleading.

## Discrepancy reconciliation

### 1. Storeys: 37, 41 and 42

- The 2013 Berggruen Residential report repeatedly describes **37 residential storeys** and records the planning path from a 27-storey permit, to 32 residential storeys, and then to a permit for 37 residential storeys.
- Hagag's Berggruen page says **37 storeys over six basement floors**.
- Hagag's About Us page calls MEIER a **41-storey tower**.
- MeierArchitects and the completed-project press call it a **42-storey tower**, with Dezeen specifying 42 storeys above street level.

The sources are evidently counting different categories of level, but none of the reviewed documents supplies a single reconciliation key for marketing floor numbers, residential levels, lobby/retail, amenity, technical and roof levels. Do not invent one. For project-wide copy, use both conventions. For a unit, use its registered floor and the seller's plan, not a generic project counter.

### 2. Height: 154 versus 158 metres

- MeierArchitects: **158 metres**.
- Dezeen: **154 metres**.
- A January 2026 Globes report also uses **about 158 metres**.

Use "about 158 metres" and cite the architect. If precision matters, acknowledge the 154-158 metre published range. The reason for the difference may be measurement methodology, but that is an inference and must not be presented as fact.

### 3. Apartments: 121, 147 and 100

- The original contractor agreement covered 121 units in a 32-storey version, with an option to increase built area and unit count.
- The 2012 developer release and the 2013 financial report used **147 total units** after the additional rights were incorporated.
- At 2013 year-end, the company reported **112 of 147** sold.
- A May 2019 Globes report described **100 apartments in practice**, including six penthouses and duplexes.

The early marketing material explicitly allowed a floor to be configured as one, two, four or five homes, and the 2013 filing records an application involving apartment combinations. It is reasonable to infer that combinations reduced the practical apartment count, but a current condominium register was not reviewed. Public copy should not claim that either 147 or 100 is the exact current legal subunit count.

### 4. Completion: 2014, 2015, 2016 and 2017

- The October 2012 developer release targeted Q1 2014.
- The 2013 financial report expected buyer handover to begin in early 2015.
- The March 2016 Hagag filing said occupancy had already begun and the project was in final stages.
- ArchDaily assigns the project year 2016.
- Dezeen reported that Richard Meier had finished the work on 6 September 2017.
- Architect's Newspaper reported an official opening in the week before 11 September 2017.
- A 2026 Globes summary says the building opened in 2014, without reconciling phased construction/occupancy.

Approved wording: **"The tower is completed and occupied. Occupancy had begun by March 2016, while architecture publications documented final completion and an official opening in September 2017."** Do not state a single completion year without defining whether it means target, first occupancy, architectural completion or opening.

### 5. Developer and ownership

The project should not be framed as a simple current Hagag development. The evidence shows a dedicated Berggruen Residential company and changing historical ownership proportions. Hagag's role is real, but the partnership must be preserved in the wording. A buyer needs the present owner of the specific apartment and the present rights holder for each parking/storage attachment, not a decade-old project-company chart.

### 6. Pool description

- The 2012 developer release specified a **25-metre pool**.
- Hagag's English marketing page uses the phrase "olympic length pool."
- The January 2026 Globes report calls it **semi-Olympic**.
- Completion sources safely confirm a swimming pool without relying on the label.

Use "swimming pool" or "25-metre pool" with attribution. Do not call it a full Olympic pool.

## Buyer due-diligence questions

These questions should drive FAQs, calls to action and buyer interviews. They are more useful than generic luxury language.

### Unit and title

1. What is the exact subparcel/condominium unit number, and who is the registered owner?
2. Is there a current land-registry extract, condominium plan and bylaws package?
3. Are there mortgages, liens, caveats, attachments, pending claims or unpaid taxes?
4. What areas are registered for the apartment, terraces, storage and parking, and which measurement standard is used in the advertisement?
5. Is the advertised floor the legal/registered floor or a marketing floor?
6. Were units combined, and were all combination, structural and internal-layout changes permitted and registered?
7. Which fixtures, built-ins, smart-home components, lighting and furniture are included in the sale?

### View, light and privacy

8. What are the verified orientations and current views from each principal room?
9. Is the sea visible from living areas, bedrooms or only a terrace, and at what angles?
10. Are there approved or proposed nearby projects that could affect light, privacy or views?
11. How do the external louvers affect daylight, solar gain, cleaning access and the usable view?

### Parking, storage and access

12. Which parking spaces and storage room are legally attached to the unit?
13. Are the spaces conventional, tandem, mechanical or subject to management allocation?
14. What vehicle dimensions and EV-charging rules apply?
15. Are resident, guest, service and retail routes separated, and what are the loading/delivery hours?

### Building operation

16. Who is the current management company and when does its contract expire?
17. What are the current monthly management fees and what do they include?
18. What is the reserve fund, are there arrears, and are special assessments planned?
19. Which amenities are operating now, on what schedule, and are there booking or guest fees?
20. Is concierge/security service continuous, and what services are actually included?
21. What major repairs have been completed or proposed for glazing, louvers, elevators, waterproofing, pool/spa and MEP systems?
22. Are there engineering, facade, elevator, fire-safety and insurance reports available for review?
23. What restrictions apply to renovations, deliveries, rentals, short-term rentals, pets and commercial use?

### Economics

24. Is the quoted price an asking price or an accepted offer, and how long has the unit been marketed?
25. What are the last relevant closed transactions for genuinely comparable floor, orientation, area, finish and parking?
26. What purchase tax, legal costs, brokerage, renovation costs and recurring fees apply to this buyer?
27. For an investor, what documented long-term rent is achievable after vacancy, fees, repairs, tax and management costs?
28. Is there any developer/company inventory, or is the offer strictly a private secondary-market resale?

## Cross-check against frozen `00-source-ledger.md`

The frozen ledger remains the controlling publication source order. This audit found the following supported corrections, additions and cautions for editorial review. The frozen file itself was not changed.

### Supported corrections or qualifications

1. **The 158-metre figure is not merely an unsourced aggregator value.** The architect's own project record publishes 158 metres, 42 storeys and 33,000 sq m. ArchDaily and Dezeen publish 154 metres. The final articles should present 154 versus 158 as a direct public-source conflict, not dismiss 158 as unsourced. Source: https://meierarchitects.us/project/rothschild-tower
2. **The 147-apartment figure is a planned inventory, not a safe current unit count.** A 2013 audited TASE filing describes 147 planned apartments and 112 sold at year-end. A 2019 Globes report says the building contained 100 apartments "in practice." The safest treatment is `147 planned; later reporting described 100 in practice; current registered subunit count not verified`. Sources: https://mayafiles.tase.co.il/RPdf/887001-888000/P887948-00.pdf and https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001285623
3. **The January 2026 terrace description conflicts between Globes language editions.** The frozen English source says one 50 sq m balcony plus two 30 sq m balconies, implying 110 sq m in total. The Hebrew version says one 50 sq m terrace plus two additional terraces with a combined area of about 30 sq m, implying about 80 sq m in total. The article may quote the English description exactly because S14 controls parity, but it should not calculate or publish an aggregate terrace area as settled fact. Sources: https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-amnon-shashua-asking-nis-75m-for-tel-aviv-home-1001533085 and https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001533026

### Material evidence absent from the frozen source list

- The 2013 TASE annual report verifies the legal/project address, block and parcel, plan and permit history, 147 planned apartments, 112 sales at the reporting date, Dori as contractor, the historical ownership structure and the construction-period program.
