## 5. Entity and phrase failure rules

1. An exact-match phrase does not authorize repeating it mechanically.
2. A search result title is vocabulary evidence, not proof of price, view, room count, availability, or stage.
3. `Sea view`, `seafront`, `vue mer`, `у моря`, and `إطلالة بحرية` require apartment-specific evidence.
4. `Pre-construction` may be used only with the dated pre-permit evidence and must not imply a delivery date.
5. `Investment` opens a decision framework. It does not authorize yield, appreciation, rent, demand, or liquidity claims.
6. `From abroad` opens a document and process section. It does not authorize immigration, tax, mortgage, banking, currency-transfer, or remote-signing claims.
7. Competitor result titles may influence natural syntax. Their numbers, superlatives, scarcity, locations, and product facts never transfer.
8. Google AI Overview and translated autocomplete suggestions are excluded as source evidence.
9. Russian and Arabic autocomplete was unstable in the signed-in Chrome session and sometimes rewritten into Hebrew. Those rewritten suggestions were not accepted as target-language keyword evidence.
10. Every future page needs one self-consistent language, with no English fallback strings outside protected Latin names.

## 6. Intent conclusion

The live SERP supports four different decision paths built on one fact base:

- English: apartments for sale, overseas buying, relocation, price, plans, and evidence
- French: appartement neuf, remote dossier review, purchase confidence, price, and plans
- Russian: apartments for sale, numerical comparison, price categories, plans, and investment discipline
- Arabic: residential suitability, space, services, eligibility, price, and pre-payment documents

The exact project must lead every title and opening. Search intent decides which verified facts appear first. It never decides whether a fact is true.
