## Evidence labels used in this document

**Observed** means the wording was visibly present in Google autocomplete, a related-search surface, a People Also Ask surface, or a result title during this research session.

**Inference** means an editorial conclusion drawn from the observed wording. It is not a claim about all speakers of that language and must not be presented as demographic fact.

**Fact gate** means a statement that the future article may publish only after it is verified against the existing Hebrew project page and a suitable public primary or authoritative source. Search snippets and AI summaries do not pass this gate.

**Editorial direction** means a recommended content treatment. It guides how the article should answer buyer questions without inventing facts.

## Live Google setup and limitations

The searches were run in the user's real Chrome browser. English and French Google pages visibly used the requested interface language. Russian searches were moved to `google.ru` because the signed-in `google.com` interface initially rendered Hebrew controls despite `hl=ru`. The resulting `google.ru` pages rendered Russian controls and an Israel/Tel Aviv footer. Arabic searches were moved to `google.co.il` because the first `google.com` response was blank. The successful Arabic result pages rendered Arabic controls and an Israel/Tel Aviv footer, although one later page exposed `lang=iw` in the document while the visible search interface remained Arabic.

The browser session sometimes displayed a signed-in Google account and sometimes a signed-out interface on `google.ru`. Every result page used `pws=0`, and the Google footer explicitly said that results were not personalised where that message was shown. This reduces personalisation but does not turn the exercise into keyword-volume research. No search volume, keyword difficulty, click-through rate, or ranking forecast should be inferred from these observations.

Autocomplete in Russian and Arabic was visibly rewritten into Hebrew in the signed-in session. Those translated suggestions are not counted as Russian or Arabic keyword evidence. The Russian and Arabic phrase sets below therefore rely on target-language result titles and People Also Ask wording. English and French autocomplete remained usable and is included.

AI Overviews appeared in several searches. They were read only to understand question framing and vocabulary. No number, project attribute, legal statement, price, distance, apartment count, floor count, amenity, tax rate, delivery date, or availability statement from an AI Overview is approved for publication.

## Entity boundary and mixing controls

### Exact entity anchor

**Observed:** The exact-project search returned the official-looking result title `VOGUE - גינדי שדה דב` on `gindivoguetlv.gindih.co.il`, alongside results using `GINDI VOGUE` and `Gindi Vogue Sde Dov`.

**Editorial direction:** Keep the project label `GINDI VOGUE Sde Dov` intact at the start of every proposed title and H1. Do not shorten it to `Vogue`, `Gindi`, or `Gindi Tel Aviv` in metadata. The full form protects the entity from fashion-related noise and from other Gindi projects.

### Mixing matrix

| Search entity or surface | Risk | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `GINDI VOGUE Sde Dov` | Exact target | May be treated as the target entity, subject to fact verification. |
| `VOGUE - גינדי שדה דב` on the dedicated Gindi project domain | Strong entity anchor | Useful for name matching. Any project fact still needs direct source inspection. |
| `Gindi Sde Dov` without `VOGUE` | Ambiguous | Do not automatically merge. Confirm the page, unit, developer and project context. |
| `Gindi TLV` or `GINDI TLV` | Different project/brand context | Never import apartment details, amenities, address, renderings or sales language into this project. |
| Gindi Holdings versus Gindi Investments | Corporate-name ambiguity | Verify the legal/developer name from a current authoritative source before publication. Do not resolve from snippets. |
| `Vogue` fashion, magazine, clothing, social content | High lexical noise | Exclude. The title must keep `GINDI`, `Sde Dov`, `Tel Aviv` and apartment intent close together. |
| Private Facebook, Instagram or assignment listing | Volatile unit-level claim | Do not turn an individual listing into the project's baseline price, inventory, size, floor, view or availability. |
| `Rainbow`, `UTOPIA`, `ASHIRA`, `YAMA`, `FIRST` and other Sde Dov projects | District comparators | May inform SERP vocabulary and comparison questions. Their facts never transfer to GINDI VOGUE. |
| Generic Sde Dov district guides | Area-level context | District facts require their own source. They do not prove building or apartment facts. |
| Google AI Overview | Machine synthesis | May reveal question vocabulary only. It is not a factual source. |

### Entity failure patterns to block

1. A private resale or rights-assignment ad mentions one apartment. The article must not report its floor, area, balcony, price or view as the project's standard offer.
2. A result title says `Gindi Sde Dov` but omits `VOGUE`. The editor must inspect the source before deciding whether it refers to the same project.
3. A district page lists several developments. Building counts, apartment counts, amenities and timelines must remain attached to the source project only.
4. `Vogue` on its own can retrieve fashion content. Metadata, image alt text and opening copy should use the full project entity.
5. Search snippets can be stale, generated, truncated or mismatched. They are vocabulary evidence, not publication evidence.

## Editorial production brief for the future articles

### Title and H1 contract

1. Project name must be the first text in both title and H1.
2. Use the exact proposed language-specific phrase only after confirming it still fits the final fact set.
3. Use one H1 only.
4. Do not insert apartment inventory, price, view or stage into the title unless verified.
5. Keep the title readable. Do not append a chain of near-synonyms.

### Opening paragraph contract

The opening paragraph must answer the buyer's first five questions in order:

1. What is `GINDI VOGUE Sde Dov`?
2. Where in Tel Aviv is it, at the level the sources actually support?
3. Which building and apartment facts are verified?
4. Is a current project price publicly confirmed, estimated or unpublished?
5. What should a buyer check next before acting?

Use the required target-language equivalents of apartments, price and buying within the first 150 words. Do not announce the SEO process, keyword intent, research method or internal fact-checking system to the public reader.

### Content ordering contract

The four articles should use parallel H2 sections so their facts can be audited side by side:

1. Overview
2. Location and surroundings
3. Buildings and apartments
4. Prices and estimates
5. Developer
6. Project stages
7. Who it suits
8. Frequently asked questions
9. Sources

Within this shared skeleton, reorder subsections and examples to match each language's intent. English may surface foreign-buyer process earlier. French may surface remote-review mechanics earlier. Russian may surface the price and plan comparison earlier. Arabic may surface the apartment's practical family-use questions earlier. The source set and project facts remain identical.

### Fact hierarchy

1. Primary public source or official project material inspected directly
2. Municipal, planning or statutory source inspected directly
3. Established reporting inspected directly and dated
4. Existing Hebrew project page, only after its citations and publication status are checked
5. Independent estimate with transparent method and the exact required non-binding label

Search snippets, AI Overviews, social captions and private ads are outside the publication hierarchy. They may suggest questions to investigate but cannot establish the answer.

### Estimate treatment

If an estimate is included, each language must explicitly label it as non-binding in natural native wording. It must state the inputs, date and method. A district-level price range must not be presented as the project's price. A private asking price must not be presented as a developer price or a completed transaction.

### Calls to action

Every CTA should offer a clear next step without asserting inventory or affiliation. Use request language, not confirmation language. Good actions are requesting verified project details, asking which plans are currently available, comparing the information with the buyer's budget, and arranging a conversation request. Do not write `Reserve now`, `Choose your apartment`, `Last units`, `Guaranteed price`, or `Book a confirmed meeting` unless a real system and verified inventory support those statements.

## Final editorial recommendation

The live SERP does not support one literal translation strategy. It supports one shared fact base with four distinct decision paths. English should lead with apartments for sale and overseas-buyer practicality. French should lead with `appartement neuf`, purchase confidence and remote review. Russian should lead with `квартиры на продажу`, prices, plans and evidence-led investment comparison. Arabic should lead with `شقق للبيع`, residential suitability, space and clear pre-purchase checks in strong native RTL Arabic.

Across all four, the highest-risk error is entity leakage. Google mixes the dedicated project with generic Gindi results, private assignment listings, other Sde Dov projects, district guides and machine summaries. The content workflow must resolve each claim back to the exact project before writing it. Search results decide which questions deserve prominence. They do not decide which facts are true.
