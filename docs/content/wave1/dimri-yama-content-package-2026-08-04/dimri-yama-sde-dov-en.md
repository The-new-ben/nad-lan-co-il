### A practical plan review

Start with the usable daily arrangement:

1. **Entrance and storage:** Is there space for coats, shoes, luggage, a stroller or household equipment without narrowing the main circulation?
2. **Kitchen:** Is the kitchen open or separable, what worktop and storage lengths are shown, and are all appliances included in the technical specification?
3. **Living area:** Can the living and dining zones work at the same time? Where do glazing, doors and circulation reduce usable wall length?
4. **Bedrooms:** What bed, wardrobe and desk sizes fit after the door swing and structural elements are considered?
5. **Bathrooms:** Which fixtures are included, is there practical laundry space, and how are wet rooms ventilated?
6. **Protected space:** What protected-room or protected-space arrangement is documented for this exact apartment? The published project information does not provide a verified unit-by-unit answer.
7. **Balcony:** What is the separately stated balcony area, depth, shape, exposure and relationship to neighboring balconies?
8. **Daylight and heat:** Which directions face the main rooms, and what shading is shown? Western exposure may offer evening light and may also need heat and glare management.
9. **Privacy:** Can neighboring apartments, hotel areas, commercial activity, public routes or later buildings look into the principal rooms?
10. **Services:** Where are air-conditioning equipment, shafts, refuse areas, fire stairs and lift cores relative to the apartment?

Renderings are helpful for understanding the design language, but they are not measured apartment plans. ### Testing an investment case without inventing a return

DIMRI YAMA may interest a long-term investor because of its Tel Aviv location, large planned district and new-build format. Those characteristics are not a guaranteed yield or appreciation forecast. A useful investment model separates evidence from assumptions:

- **Entry cost:** current apartment price, tax, legal, finance, currency, upgrades and furnishing.
- **Holding cost:** municipal tax, management, insurance, maintenance, vacancy and property management.
- **Rent evidence:** comparable completed apartments, with date, area, condition, parking and location.
- **Net income:** rent less vacancy, management, repairs and non-recoverable costs.
- **Exit depth:** likely buyer pool for the specific room count, price band, floor, orientation and service charge.
- **District timing:** how much of the resale or rental story depends on infrastructure that is still planned.
- **Supply:** competing apartments expected across the wider Sde Dov build-out.
- **View risk:** whether later buildings could affect the outlook assumed in the purchase price.
- **Time horizon:** ability to hold through construction, district maturation and different market conditions.

No verified current rent, rental yield, appreciation rate or resale period was found in the public sources reviewed. A buyer can model scenarios, but the answer should remain a range of outcomes driven by stated assumptions. ## Who it suits

DIMRI YAMA could suit several buyer types, but the reason differs for each. The project should not be sold as universally suitable merely because it combines a coastal district, multiple building forms and a long amenity list. The right test is whether a particular apartment, cost and timeline fit the buyer's life.

### A primary-home buyer in Tel Aviv

A household planning to live in the apartment may value the range from smaller to larger planning categories, the choice between tower and lower buildings and the proposed common facilities. The harder question is timing. Sde Dov is still being built as a district, so the buyer should compare expected occupation with the opening of streets, services, schools, parks and transport that matter to daily life.

For this buyer, a sample-plan room count is only a starting point. Verify actual bedrooms, internal area, protected space, storage, laundry arrangement, lift access, parking, sunlight, privacy and noise. Test the route to work or relatives using current and expected conditions. If a school or childcare place is essential, confirm the institution, enrolment area and opening status with the responsible authority rather than relying on the master plan.

The project may be less suitable if the household needs an established neighborhood immediately, requires a confirmed school assignment now or cannot tolerate uncertainty around construction activity and future operating costs.

### A relocation buyer

Someone moving to Tel Aviv from abroad needs two synchronized plans: the property purchase and the household move. A delayed district service, documentation problem or currency transfer can matter as much as the apartment plan.

Before committing, align:

- immigration or residence status, without assuming a tax benefit;
- employment and income currency;
- expected move date versus contractual handover;
- temporary housing if the schedule changes;
- school and childcare evidence;
- healthcare and banking arrangements;
- furniture, utilities and insurance;
- who will inspect and accept the apartment.

Do not let a future relocation date turn a corporate 2031 planning year into a personal move-in promise. Build a contingency around the contract and the actual stage of construction.

### An overseas buyer or second-home owner

DIMRI YAMA may appeal to a buyer who wants a Tel Aviv base but spends much of the year elsewhere. The key question is not whether a remote purchase is theoretically possible. It is who performs every task in Israel and what authority they hold.

Establish a remote-purchase chain:

1. appoint an independent Israeli lawyer and verify the precise scope of representation;
2. obtain understandable explanations of the agreement, plans, specification and payment protections;
3. identify any translation required for informed signing;
4. confirm the exact form, limits and authentication of a power of attorney;
5. ask the relevant consular mission or professional whether personal attendance, notarization or an apostille is required for the buyer's documents;
6. prepare source-of-funds and identity material for banks;
7. test currency conversion and transfer timing before each NIS instalment;
8. appoint someone to review notices, changes and payment requests;
9. arrange independent snagging and representation at handover;
10. organize insurance, utilities, furnishing, key control and ongoing property management.

Some stages may be coordinated at a distance, but the required process depends on citizenship, residence, country, documents and bank. Do not assume that “remote” means no personal appearance.

The extensive planned facilities may benefit a second-home user, yet they can also create meaningful recurring fees even when the apartment is empty. Request the management-cost model and rules for vacant units.

### A long-term investor

An investor may be interested in the scale of Sde Dov and the possibility that a completed mixed district attracts a broad residential market. That thesis has variables, not guarantees. At entry, compare a current apartment-specific price with the full acquisition cost. During ownership, model management charges, municipal tax, insurance, vacancy, maintenance and professional management. For exit, consider the unit's room count, efficient plan, floor, orientation, verified view, parking, service charge and the competing supply that will arrive across the district.

The December 2025 launch price and Q1 2026 corporate averages are useful historical context. They do not establish today's fair value or future rent. A credible rental scenario needs comparables from completed apartments, with a date and matching size, condition, parking and micro-location. A credible resale scenario needs more than a general belief that Tel Aviv prices rise.

DIMRI YAMA may be less suitable for an investor who requires an immediate rental stream, a guaranteed yield, a short predictable exit or low service charges. The project was at an early reported construction stage in March 2026, and its shared-facility package is likely to require an operating budget that was not publicly verified here.

### A buyer comparing lifestyle facilities

For a buyer who will genuinely use pools, wellness areas, workspace and resident rooms, the planned amenity range is a meaningful differentiator. Compare access, hours, crowding, staffing and cost, not only the number of facilities. The hotel and commercial components should be assessed separately from the residential facilities.

This project may suit someone who values a serviced residential environment and is willing to pay a proportionate management fee once it is known. It may suit less well if the buyer prefers a simple building with low common expenditure, does not plan to use the facilities or wants exclusive private control of the entire courtyard.

### A buyer deciding between the tower and lower buildings

Choose the tower if the actual apartment offers the preferred plan, verified orientation and a service model the buyer accepts. Choose a lower building if its street relationship, lift dependence, plan or cost structure works better. Do not assume that the tower is automatically more prestigious or that the lower building is automatically quieter.

For each candidate, score:

- apartment efficiency;
- documented outlook;
- heat and daylight;
- lift and entrance arrangement;
- proximity to hotel, commerce, service areas and public routes;
- parking and storage;
- facility access;
- estimated management cost once supported;
- payment schedule;
- resale audience.

The winner should be the apartment whose evidence fits the buyer, not the building with the tallest number.
