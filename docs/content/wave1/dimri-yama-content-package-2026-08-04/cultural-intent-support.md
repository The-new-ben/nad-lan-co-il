# DIMRI YAMA Sde Dov: cultural intent support for EN, FR, RU and AR

Research date: 4 August 2026

## Scope and evidence boundary

This is a content-strategy support document, not a project article and not a project fact ledger. It does not establish any fact about DIMRI YAMA, its buildings, apartments, prices, views, amenities, permits, construction stage or delivery. Every such statement in the final language pages must come from the project's approved source ledger.

This document also does not replace the mandatory live Google Chrome research. It contains native vocabulary, buyer questions, cultural friction points and phrasing patterns derived from public information and language practice. The root writer must still record the live localized search evidence for each language before choosing a primary phrase, title or H1.

The four language audiences are not four fixed personality types. English, French, Russian and Arabic pages will each receive readers with different citizenship, residence, family and investment situations. The useful editorial distinction is the decision they are trying to make, not their ethnicity. Cultural adaptation should widen the page's usefulness without turning a search tendency into a stereotype.

## Universal buyer-decision spine

Every language page should answer the same ten buyer decisions with the same verified project facts. The order and emphasis can change by language.

| Decision | What the reader needs | Honesty gate |
|---|---|---|
| Identify the offer | Project name, city, Sde Dov context, developer, published building and apartment totals | If a figure differs between sources, state the conflict or use the approved safe range |
| Understand the location | Actual lot or published location, relation to the sea, park, roads, services and planned transport | Do not turn district proximity into a unit-level sea view or a walking-time promise |
| Compare buildings | Number and height of buildings, apartment mix, balconies, parking, storage and amenities | Say "not published" where current unit-level data is unavailable |
| Build an all-in budget | Current asking price if published, purchase tax, professional costs, financing, indexation and ongoing building costs | Never convert an old marketing example into current inventory or a current price list |
| Buy from abroad | Identification, representation, document language, signature verification, bank transfers and handover representation | Present questions and checkpoints, not a promise that every stage can be completed remotely |
| Test financing | Equity, mortgage eligibility, loan-to-value, income and currency exposure, payment schedule | Do not quote a personal borrowing limit or tax status without a professional assessment |
| Assess the build timeline | Planning, permit, construction and contractual delivery milestones | A developer target is not a contractual handover date |
| Test daily-life fit | Apartment layout, protected space, lifts, sun, noise, schools, parks, access and management | Do not call a project family-friendly without evidence for the relevant facilities and layouts |
| Test the investment case | Price per square metre, realistic rent evidence, vacancy, costs, resale liquidity and time horizon | No guaranteed yield, appreciation or future demand |
| Choose the next action | Request the current price list, plans, specification, payment schedule and contract pack | A CTA should invite verification, not manufacture urgency |

## English audience support

### Decision profiles to serve

The English page should not assume that every reader is a foreign investor. It should visibly support at least four routes:

1. A buyer relocating to Tel Aviv who needs a primary home and wants to understand when the district will function as a lived neighbourhood.
2. A non-resident buying from abroad who needs a verifiable process, document list and local representation plan.
3. A long-term investor who wants to model rent, holding costs, resale and district maturation without promotional yield claims.
4. A family or second-home buyer with ties to Israel who cares about apartment practicality, protected space, access, maintenance and periods when the home is empty.

Do not merge these routes into "international buyers" as if they share one budget or motive. A relocation buyer may value school timing and commuting more than yield. A second-home buyer may care more about property management and lock-up-and-leave practicality. An investor needs a separate calculation.

### Native vocabulary bank

Use terms according to the verified offer and the actual reader question:

- new-build apartment, new development, apartment in a new residential project
- off-plan purchase, buying before completion, purchase from a developer
- available apartment, current inventory, unit mix, floor plan, internal area, balcony area
- building, tower, lower-rise building, building entrance, floor, orientation
- sea-facing, sea view, partial sea view, open outlook, direction of exposure
- price list, asking price, price per square metre, purchase tax, payment schedule
- mortgage, loan-to-value, equity contribution, monthly repayment, foreign-currency exposure
- building permit, construction stage, target completion, contractual delivery date
- technical specification, buyer protections, bank-backed payment protection, snagging inspection
- management fee, building committee fee, municipal tax, insurance, maintenance and property management
- gross rent, net rental yield, vacancy, holding costs, resale liquidity and investment horizon
- relocation, primary residence, second home, family base and long-term hold

"New construction" is natural in North American English. "New build" is natural in British English. A Tel Aviv project page can use both naturally, but the live query evidence should decide which phrase owns the title and H1. "Apartment" is clearer than "flat" for the broad international audience unless the SERP strongly favours British wording.

### Practical concerns to answer

- Which facts are currently published by the developer or planning authorities?
- Is the price current, historical, estimated or not published?
- What does the published apartment count include, and is a live unit schedule available?
- How do tower and lower-rise options differ in height, lift dependence, outlook and likely service costs?
- Which orientations are documented for a specific apartment rather than inferred from a rendering?
- Is the sea distance a straight-line distance, a mapped walking route or a general district description?
- What is operating today and what belongs to the future Sde Dov plan?
- Which payment protections and project-finance arrangements are documented for this project?
- What costs sit outside the headline apartment price?
- Which documents can a buyer review before a visit or reservation decision?

### Remote-purchase issues

The useful English content is a verification chain, not the claim that the transaction is "fully remote". Explain the questions a buyer should resolve:

- Who represents the buyer independently, and what is the exact authority in any power of attorney?
- In which language will the contract, technical specification and schedules be reviewed?
- Does signature verification require a notary, apostille or appearance at an Israeli mission in the buyer's country?
- Which bank account receives each payment, and which compliance documents will the bank require for source of funds?
- How will exchange-rate changes between the buyer's home currency and NIS affect later instalments?
- Who verifies plans, changes, notices, construction milestones and payment requests while the buyer is abroad?
- Who attends the pre-handover inspection and records defects?
- Who receives keys, arranges utilities, insurance, furnishing and ongoing management if the owner is not in Israel?

The official consular material confirms that signature or affidavit services can require personal attendance and advance preparation. Therefore, do not promise that remote means no travel or no in-person step. Phrase it as a process to confirm for the buyer's jurisdiction and documents.

### Investment questions

Investment coverage should show a calculation structure without inserting invented numbers:

1. Purchase price and verified apartment area.
2. Purchase tax based on the buyer's actual status on the transaction date.
3. Legal, financing, currency transfer, furnishing and handover costs.
4. Recurring municipal, building, insurance and management costs.
5. Rent supported by comparable completed apartments, not projected marketing copy.
6. Vacancy, maintenance and letting costs.
7. Net yield before and after financing.
8. Resale scenario by apartment type, floor, orientation and district maturity.
9. Risks created by construction timing, changing interest rates, future supply and an unverified view.

Useful native questions include: "What is the realistic all-in cost?", "What would make this apartment easier to resell?", "How much of the investment case depends on the future neighbourhood?" and "What evidence supports the rent assumption?"

### Family and community needs

Cover daily-life evidence, not a generic "family-friendly" label:

- usable internal space and storage
- protected room or protected-space arrangement, only when documented
- lift count and service continuity
- stroller, bicycle and accessibility routes
- parking and visitor access
- daylight, heat exposure, privacy and traffic noise
- existing and planned childcare, schools, play areas and healthcare
- travel time to work or relatives based on an identified route and mode
- opening sequence of the surrounding district, not just the final master-plan vision

Avoid assuming that an English-speaking buyer needs an "expat community" or international school. Those should be addressed only when the reader asks or when the local evidence is relevant.

### Culturally awkward claims to avoid

- "The safest investment in Israel"
- "Guaranteed appreciation" or "guaranteed rental income"
- "Beachfront" when the project is inland or separated from the shore
- "Sea view" as a project-wide attribute when only some units may have it
- "Perfect for expats" without explaining the practical basis
- "Move-in ready" for a project that is not complete
- "World-class" and "iconic" without specific architecture or amenity evidence
- "Minutes from everything" without mapped routes
- "Foreigners can complete everything remotely" as an unconditional promise

### Native phrasing patterns

Prefer:

- "The decision starts with the apartment, not the rendering. Compare the building, floor, orientation, internal area and payment schedule before comparing slogans."
- "The project is in a coastal district, but a sea view must be verified for the specific apartment."
- "If the current price list is not public, request the dated inventory and compare the full acquisition cost, not only the headline price."
- "For a buyer abroad, the practical question is who checks each document, payment and construction notice in Israel."
- "Treat future transport and public facilities as planned infrastructure until their status and opening dates are verified."

Avoid translation-shaped phrases such as "apartment from contractor", "delivery of the apartment" when "handover" is clearer, and "number of rooms" without explaining the Israeli convention where necessary.

### CTA and FAQ language

CTAs should invite a useful next step:

- Request the current price list and floor plans
- Compare buildings and available apartments
- Ask for the payment schedule and specification
- Check what can be completed from abroad

FAQ seeds:

- Is there a current price list for DIMRI YAMA?
- Which apartments may have a sea view, and how is that verified?
- Can a non-resident obtain an Israeli mortgage?
- What should an overseas buyer review before signing?
- Which Sde Dov services exist now and which are planned?
- What ongoing costs should be included in an investment calculation?

## French audience support

### Decision profiles to serve

The French page should separate these paths rather than presenting Alyah as the motive of every francophone reader:

1. Achat d'une résidence principale for a household already living in Israel.
2. Préparation d'une future installation or Alyah, with timing and status treated as individual questions.
3. Résidence secondaire or pied-à-terre for regular stays in Tel Aviv.
4. Investissement patrimonial with a long horizon, rental and resale analysis.
5. Achat à distance by a buyer whose legal, tax and banking life remains partly outside Israel.

"Communauté" can matter to some buyers, but the page must not invent a French enclave, school network or social environment around the project. If community access is relevant, name the verified service or route. Do not substitute cultural reassurance for location evidence.

### Native vocabulary bank

- appartement neuf à Tel Aviv, programme neuf, projet résidentiel neuf
- achat sur plan en Israël, achat auprès d'un promoteur
- promoteur, constructeur, permis de construire, avancement des travaux
- tour résidentielle, immeuble de hauteur intermédiaire, bâtiment, étage
- plan d'appartement, agencement, surface intérieure, balcon, orientation
- appartement avec vue mer, vue partielle, dégagement, exposition ouest
- grille de prix, prix demandé, prix au mètre carré, budget global
- taxe d'achat, Mas Rékhicha when a familiar transliteration helps, échéancier de paiement
- apport, financement, crédit immobilier, risque de change EUR/NIS
- cahier des charges, notice technique, garanties de paiement, date contractuelle de livraison
- livraison, remise des clés, visite de réception, réserves and gestion du bien

Use "pièces" carefully. A French reader may apply a French counting convention to an Israeli plan. The article should describe bedrooms, living area and total published room count from the source rather than silently equating systems. Likewise, distinguish surface intérieure, balcony and any marketed total. Never invent a "surface Carrez" for an Israeli apartment.

### The VEFA mental-model gap

French buyers know the language of achat sur plan, VEFA, appels de fonds, livraison and réserves. That vocabulary is useful for framing their questions, but an Israeli developer purchase must not be described as a French VEFA. The governing documents, professionals, payment protections, taxation and registration system are Israeli.

A clear native formulation is:

"L'achat sur plan en Israël ne doit pas être assimilé automatiquement à une VEFA française. Les réflexes sont proches, vérifier les plans, l'échéancier, les garanties et la livraison, mais le contrat et les protections applicables sont israéliens."

This is a cultural bridge, not legal advice. It tells the reader which assumptions need checking without pretending the two systems are equivalent.

### Practical concerns to answer

- Quel est le statut exact du projet au jour de la consultation?
- Le prix affiché correspond-il à un lot disponible, à un exemple ancien ou à une estimation?
- Quelle surface est privative and what is the separate balcony area?
- Combien de pièces and how many bedrooms does the plan actually contain?
- Quelle différence entre la tour and the other buildings?
- L'orientation and sea view are they documented for the apartment concerned?
- Quel est l'échéancier de paiement and what may be indexed or adjusted under the contract?
- Quelles dépenses must be added to the purchase price?
- Which facilities are already operating and which belong to the future district?
- How will the apartment be inspected, handed over and managed if the buyer lives abroad?

### Remote-purchase issues

The French page should turn "acheter depuis la France" into a concrete file:

- choix d'un conseil indépendant en Israël
- version and explanation of the contract and technical schedules
- procuration, scope of powers and signature authentication
- possible appointment at an Israeli consulate and need for certified translation
- source-of-funds file and bank transfer timetable
- EUR/NIS exchange exposure across instalments
- dated inventory, plan, orientation and specification before any commitment
- representation at pre-handover inspection and key handover
- utilities, insurance, furnishing, letting and building management after handover
- coordination with the buyer's tax residence outside Israel

Official French consular information shows that some notarized declarations for use in Israel require personal attendance and may require a certified translation if the document language is not understood by the consular representative. Therefore, use "une grande partie du suivi peut être organisée à distance, selon les documents et le pays" rather than "tout se fait à distance".

### Investment questions

Use "investissement patrimonial" only when the content actually discusses horizon, costs and exit. Do not use it as an elegant synonym for a guaranteed gain.

Questions worth answering:

- Quel est le coût d'acquisition total en NIS and in the buyer's reference currency?
- Quelle rent assumption is supported by completed comparable units?
- What are the charges when the apartment is vacant or used intermittently?
- Does the floor, orientation, plan or parking improve resale depth?
- What future supply is expected in the wider district?
- What part of the thesis depends on infrastructure that is not open yet?
- What is the net yield after management, vacancy, tax, maintenance and financing?
- Who manages tenant issues or the empty apartment from abroad?

Avoid publishing a yield band unless the source ledger contains a reproducible rent and cost comparison with a date.

### Family, installation and community needs

For a future resident, cover:

- number of actual bedrooms and usable storage
- protected room or protected-space solution if verified
- pram, bicycle and accessibility circulation
- lifts, parking, visitor access and deliveries
- sun, heat, privacy and noise by orientation
- existing versus planned crèches, schools, parks, health services and shopping
- public transport available on the intended move-in date
- management of the home before permanent installation

Keep Alyah content distinct. A buyer may be eligible for a benefit, may plan to become eligible later, or may never make Alyah. The page should say "selon votre statut personnel à la date de l'achat" and send the reader to the current official source, not promise a reduced tax rate.

- "Achat totalement sécurisé" or "zéro risque"
- "Avantage fiscal garanti pour les futurs olim"
- "Le quartier préféré des Français" without data
- "Une communauté francophone à votre porte" without verified services
- "Vue mer" for the entire project
- "À deux pas de la plage" without a mapped route
- "Rentabilité assurée"
- "Le meilleur investissement de Tel Aviv"
- excessive use of prestige, exceptionnel, unique and exclusif without facts

Avoid literal structures such as "appartement du contracteur", "prix de l'appartement du projet" or a repetitive "il est important de" opening. French copy gains trust from exact nouns and calm transitions, not from reassurance adjectives.

## Russian audience support

The Russian page should support more than an investor looking for yield:

1. Покупатель для собственного проживания already in Israel.
2. Семья planning relocation and needing a realistic district timeline.
3. Нерезидент buying remotely and coordinating documents, banking and handover.
4. Инвестор comparing long-term rent, resale liquidity and risk.

Investment, distance to sea and price clarity deserve strong coverage because they are high-friction decisions, not because every Russian-speaking buyer shares the same motive.

### The Russian legal mental-model gap

Readers familiar with Russia may expect a ДДУ, эскроу-счет, Росреестр and акт приема-передачи within the Russian legal system. Those concepts help identify the questions the reader will ask, but they must not be mapped onto an Israeli transaction as equivalents.

Useful phrasing:

"Покупка квартиры у застройщика в Израиле оформляется по израильским правилам. Ее нельзя автоматически приравнивать к российской схеме ДДУ и эскроу. Сравнивать нужно функции: какой договор подписывается, как защищаются платежи, когда и как оформляются права, кто проводит приемку."

This avoids both jargon dumping and false equivalence.

### Investment questions

Russian-language investment content works best as a comparison, not a sales monologue:

- Полная стоимость входа: price, purchase tax, professional costs, currency and furnishing.
- Цена за квадратный метр: which area definition is used?
- Арендная ставка: what completed comparable supports it?
- Валовая and net yield after fees, vacancy, maintenance, management and financing.
- Ликвидность: which apartment size, floor, parking and orientation widen the resale audience?
- Горизонт: how long until the district and project are complete enough for the thesis?
- Риск предложения: how much new inventory may compete in wider Sde Dov?
- Вид: is the view protected or could future construction change it?
- Выход: what taxes, selling costs and time may apply based on the owner's status?

The article should never write "доходность X%" from an agency claim without showing date, rent basis, price basis, costs and whether the figure is gross or net.

## Arabic audience support

### Decision profiles to serve

The Arabic page must not assume one audience. It may reach Arabic-speaking Israeli citizens and residents, families moving within the metropolitan area, buyers elsewhere in Israel, and overseas readers. These groups have different legal status, banking access and daily-life priorities.

Support at least four paths:

1. أسرة تبحث عن مسكن رئيسي and needs space, education, access and daily services.
2. مشتري محلي يقارن بين مشروع جديد and an established neighbourhood.
3. مشتري من خارج البلاد needs documents, translation, banking and representation.
4. مستثمر wants a separate rent, costs and resale model.

Family space and education should receive practical depth, but the page should not imply that every Arabic-speaking reader has children or wants a large apartment.

### Native vocabulary bank

- شقة جديدة في تل أبيب, مشروع سكني جديد, شقة من المطور
- شراء شقة على المخطط, شراء قبل اكتمال البناء
- المطور العقاري, المقاول المنفذ, رخصة البناء, مرحلة التنفيذ
- برج سكني, مبنى, طابق, مدخل المبنى
- مخطط الشقة, توزيع الغرف, المساحة الداخلية, مساحة الشرفة
- عدد غرف النوم, غرفة المعيشة, مخزن, موقف سيارة
- اتجاه الواجهة, اتجاه النوافذ, الإضاءة الطبيعية, الخصوصية
- إطلالة بحرية, إطلالة جزئية, اتجاه نحو البحر, and these must not be treated as synonyms
- قائمة الأسعار الحالية, الشقق المتاحة, السعر للمتر المربع
- ضريبة الشراء, التكاليف الإضافية, جدول الدفعات
- الدفعة الأولى, التمويل العقاري, نسبة التمويل, مخاطر سعر الصرف
- المواصفات الفنية, حماية أموال المشتري, الموعد التعاقدي للتسليم
- معاينة الشقة, تسجيل العيوب, استلام المفاتيح
- رسوم إدارة المبنى, الضريبة البلدية, التأمين, الصيانة
- الإيجار المتوقع, العائد الإجمالي, العائد الصافي, الشغور, سهولة إعادة البيع
- سكن رئيسي, منزل ثان, انتقال عائلي, استثمار طويل الأجل

Use clear Modern Standard Arabic. Limited local terms can be explained once when they help, for example الطابو as a familiar local reference or غرفة محمية if the project documents actually confirm the protected-space arrangement. Do not fill the article with Hebrew transliterations when a clear Arabic term exists.

### Practical concerns to answer

- ما المعلومات المنشورة حاليًا about the project and apartment inventory?
- هل السعر حالي, historical, estimated or unpublished?
- ما المساحة الداخلية الفعلية and what is the separate balcony area?
- How many bedrooms does the plan contain, beyond the marketed room count?
- What is the difference between tower and other buildings in daily use and costs?
- What is the apartment's orientation and what does that mean for sun, heat and privacy?
- Is a sea view documented for the chosen apartment?
- What is the actual walking route to the seafront?
- Which schools, childcare, parks, shops and transport exist now and which are planned?
- What are the payment schedule and total acquisition costs?

### Remote-purchase and language-access issues

Arabic content must acknowledge a real practical issue: official information may be available in Arabic while forms or transaction systems remain in Hebrew. The Israel Tax Authority's Arabic purchase-tax page, for example, states that the information is in Arabic but the simulator itself operates in Hebrew. This does not justify a broad claim that all services are inaccessible in Arabic. It does justify telling the buyer to verify the language of every operative document and system.

Questions to cover:

- ما اللغة الملزمة للعقد and technical annexes?
- هل يحتاج المشتري إلى ترجمة معتمدة before signing?
- من يشرح differences between the Arabic summary and Hebrew contract?
- ما نطاق التوكيل exactly, and how is the signature authenticated?
- هل يتطلب الإجراء حضورًا شخصيًا at an Israeli mission?
- What identification and source-of-funds documents will the bank require?
- How will foreign currency be converted to NIS for later instalments?
- Who checks project notices and payment requests in Israel?
- Who attends apartment inspection and handover?
- Who manages utilities, insurance, furnishing and leasing afterward?

Official Arabic consular information says a person signing a notarized declaration for use in Israel may need to appear personally and that a translation may be required when the representative does not understand the document's language. Phrase remote purchasing as a coordinated process, not a promise of zero travel.

### Investment questions

- ما التكلفة الكاملة للشراء, including tax, professional costs, currency and furnishing?
- ما تعريف المساحة used for the price per square metre?
- What completed comparable supports the proposed rent?
- Is the yield gross or net after vacancy, management and maintenance?
- What monthly building costs continue when the apartment is empty?
- Which plan, floor, parking and orientation may help resale?
- How much of the case depends on future district facilities?
- Could later construction affect the current outlook?
- What is the intended holding period and exit route?

Avoid importing Gulf freehold, escrow or residency-by-investment language into an Israeli project page. Arabic property vocabulary can be shared across markets, but the legal regime cannot.

### Family and community needs

This section should be especially concrete:

- عدد غرف النوم usable by the household
- internal storage and laundry practicality
- protected room or documented protected-space solution
- privacy between bedrooms and living area
- balcony safety and usability
- sun, heat and shade by orientation
- lifts, accessibility, pram and bicycle movement
- parking, visitor arrival and deliveries
- existing and planned nurseries, schools, playgrounds and healthcare
- route to work and family support networks
- building management, monthly fees and service continuity

Do not write "مناسب للعائلات" as a free-standing slogan. Show the plan, services, access and costs that allow a family to decide for itself.

### Culturally awkward claims to avoid

- "فرصة العمر"
- "استثمار آمن بلا مخاطر"
- "عائد مضمون"
- "إطلالة بحرية" as a project-wide promise
- "على البحر مباشرة" without exact geographic proof
- "مثالي للعائلات" without layout and services evidence
- "حي هادئ وآمن" without defined evidence and context
- "مناسب للمجتمع العربي" without verified services or access
- "كل الإجراءات متاحة بالعربية"
- "يمكن إتمام الشراء بالكامل عن بعد" as an unconditional claim
- overuse of فاخر, استثنائي, حصري and أيقوني instead of data

### Native phrasing patterns

Prefer:

- "ابدأوا بالشقة نفسها: المبنى، الطابق، المخطط، المساحة الداخلية، اتجاه الواجهة وجدول الدفعات."
- "القرب من الساحل لا يعني أن كل شقة تتمتع بإطلالة بحرية. يجب التحقق من الإطلالة للشقة والطابق والاتجاه المحدد."
- "إذا لم تُنشر قائمة أسعار حالية، اطلبوا قائمة مؤرخة بالشقق المتاحة."
- "في الشراء من خارج البلاد، السؤال العملي هو: من يراجع كل وثيقة وكل دفعة وكل إشعار بناء داخل إسرائيل؟"
- "يجب الفصل بين الخدمات القائمة اليوم والمرافق المخططة للحي في المستقبل."

Avoid literal translations such as وحدة ديارية, مقاول المشروع when المطور is intended, and long ceremonial openings. Strong Arabic buyer copy begins with the project facts and the decision.

### CTA and FAQ language

CTAs:

- اطلبوا قائمة الأسعار والمخططات الحالية
- قارنوا بين المباني والشقق المتاحة
- احصلوا على جدول الدفعات والمواصفات الفنية
- تحققوا من خطوات الشراء من خارج البلاد

FAQ seeds:

- هل نُشرت قائمة أسعار حالية لمشروع DIMRI YAMA؟
- كيف يمكن التحقق من الإطلالة البحرية لشقة محددة؟
- هل يستطيع غير المقيم الحصول على تمويل عقاري في إسرائيل؟
- ما المستندات المطلوبة للشراء من خارج البلاد؟
- ما التكاليف التي تضاف إلى سعر الشقة؟
- ما المرافق الموجودة اليوم في Sde Dov وما الذي لا يزال مخططًا؟

## Cross-language consistency rules for the final writers

### One fact, four native explanations

The final pages must share one immutable project ledger. Cultural adaptation changes the order, examples, questions and explanations. It does not change numbers. If the approved ledger says a price is unpublished, every language says it is unpublished. If a sea view is unverified, no language may upgrade it to a promise.

For every material statement, assign one label before drafting:

- verified project fact
- verified district or planning fact
- developer-presented feature
- historical price example
- estimate, not binding
- general buying-process guidance
- unknown or unpublished

This prevents a district-level fact from becoming an apartment feature and prevents a general process guide from becoming legal advice for a specific buyer.

### Coastal-language control

Keep these propositions separate in all languages:

1. The project is in Sde Dov or a coastal district.
2. The site has a measured relation to the shore.
3. A mapped walking route reaches a named beach access.
4. A building faces a certain direction.
5. A specific apartment on a specific floor has a documented sea view.
6. That view is permanent or protected from future development.

Each proposition needs its own evidence. Never infer propositions 3 to 6 from proposition 1.

### Price and investment control

- Label every price with source date, apartment identity and whether it is current, historical or estimated.
- Do not convert NIS into EUR, USD, GBP, RUB or another currency without a dated rate and a clear indication that the conversion changes.
- Do not publish yield without rent basis, vacancy, recurring costs, tax scope and gross/net label.
- Do not promise appreciation from future neighbourhood plans.
- Keep purchase-tax language conditional on buyer status and transaction date.
- Present mortgage questions through official Bank of Israel rules and an individual bank assessment, not a universal approval statement.

### Remote-purchase control

Every language should explain the same remote workflow: identity, independent representation, translation, authority to sign, signature verification, source of funds, payment protection, construction notices, inspection, handover and ongoing management. The wording must remain conditional because the required steps depend on citizenship, residence, country, documents and bank.

### Cultural-quality control

- Do not translate the English draft sentence by sentence.
- Do not use nationality as a proxy for tax status.
- Do not make Alyah the default francophone story.
- Do not make yield the default Russian-language story.
- Do not make relocation the only English-language story.
- Do not make family size or Gulf-investor status the default Arabic story.
- Avoid fake local community claims in every language.
- Use short native sentences and concrete buyer questions.
- Keep the project name and official brand spelling identical across languages.

### Handoff to mandatory live search research

The live Google Chrome researcher should test, not assume, the vocabulary bank above. For each language, they should record the exact query, interface language, Israel location, date, suggestions, People Also Ask when present, result titles and dominant intent. The primary phrase chosen for the title and H1 must come from that evidence. This support document supplies hypotheses and decision coverage only.

## Final editorial test

Before a language draft is accepted, a native editor should be able to answer yes to all of these:

- Does the opening identify the project and answer a real buying question before lifestyle language?
- Can a reader distinguish verified facts, developer presentations, estimates and unknowns?
- Is the cultural emphasis expressed as choices rather than assumptions about the reader?
- Are price, view, delivery, transport and yield claims bounded by evidence?
- Does remote-purchase coverage identify real documents and responsibilities rather than promise convenience?
- Does the page explain the buyer's familiar legal vocabulary without claiming that foreign and Israeli systems are equivalent?
- Are family, relocation, second-home and investment paths visibly distinct?
- Does the CTA request current documents instead of creating false urgency?
- Would the page still be useful if every adjective such as luxury, exclusive and iconic were removed?
- Has the final title, H1 and opening been chosen only after the separate live Google Chrome research?
