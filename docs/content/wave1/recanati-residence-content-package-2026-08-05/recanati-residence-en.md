# RECANATI RESIDENCE - apartments for sale north Tel Aviv, prices and buyer guide

RECANATI RESIDENCE is a completed residential project in Ramat Aviv Gimel, Tel Aviv, with 198 apartments across three 16-floor buildings. The published mix runs from 3 to 5 rooms and includes mini-penthouses and penthouses. For anyone searching for apartments for sale north Tel Aviv, the practical question is no longer whether the project will be built. It is which specific apartment can be bought now, at what price, from which seller, with which registered rights, and on what handover terms. The current official project page presents the complex as ready for immediate occupancy, while a project partner describes it as occupied. Neither source publishes a current apartment list or price table. Buying therefore starts with a fresh, dated unit sheet and a document check, not with an old advertisement.

The complex stands on the western side of Recanati Street beside Beit Tzuri Garden. It replaced 96 older homes through an urban-renewal process and was completed during 2023. Its developer is now known as Ashtrom Residences, formerly Ashdar, working with Enav. This guide looks at the real buyer decision: the neighborhood, the built environment, apartment-level checks, historic price evidence, ongoing costs and the difference between a project fact and a right attached to one unit. Nad-Lan is an independent information site. It is not the developer or its sales representative.

## RECANATI RESIDENCE overview

RECANATI RESIDENCE belongs to a category that buyers often describe too loosely as a new project. It is new compared with the low-rise homes it replaced, but it is not a plan waiting to become a building. The three buildings are complete. Residents have moved in. Shared spaces can be seen. The route from the street to the lobby can be walked. A buyer can test light, noise, privacy, elevator service and the condition of a particular apartment instead of trying to infer them from a rendering.

That changes the nature of the decision. In an off-plan purchase, the buyer spends much of the review on planning status, construction security, specifications, milestones and delivery risk. In a completed project, those questions do not disappear, but the centre of gravity moves. The critical file is the specific apartment. Who owns or has authority to sell it? Is it a remaining developer unit, a resale from an original owner, or a resale from an earlier purchaser? What is registered? Which plan forms part of the agreement? Are parking and storage rights attached, and if so, where and how? What charges are already operating in the building? What defects or finishing items remain inside the apartment or common property?

The verified project scale is clear. There are 198 apartments in three buildings. The current official presentation uses 16 floors. The published mix includes 3-room, 4-room and 5-room homes, along with mini-penthouses and penthouses. The official page also says there are more than 15 metres between the buildings and three underground parking levels. These are project-level facts. They do not establish the size, floor, outlook, parking allocation or storage rights of an apartment being offered today.

The distinction matters because the old development program was divided between replacement homes and homes intended for developer marketing. Regulatory material records 96 replacement apartments for the former owners and 102 units allocated to developer sales. That history does not tell a buyer who the current seller is. A replacement owner may now be reselling. An original developer unit may have changed hands. A broker may advertise a unit without holding the complete current file. The seller's identity and authority must be checked for the actual transaction.

The present commercial status also needs precise reading. Ashtrom's current page says immediate occupancy. Enav describes the project as occupied. Both descriptions fit a completed complex. Neither guarantees that a unit matching a buyer's requirements is available. They also do not replace a contractual possession date. If a current unit is offered, its seller should provide the price, payment timetable, condition, legal status and date on which possession can be delivered. The words immediate occupancy are useful context, not a complete contractual answer.

## Location and surroundings

The official location description places RECANATI RESIDENCE on the western side of Recanati Street, beside Beit Tzuri Garden, in Ramat Aviv Gimel. Ramat Aviv Gimel is part of north Tel Aviv's established residential fabric. The buyer can inspect the real approach to the building and observe how the immediate area works on a weekday morning, in the afternoon and after dark. That direct evidence is one of the strongest advantages of considering a completed apartment.

Beit Tzuri Garden is the nearest named public-space anchor in the official project description. Adjacency to a garden can be attractive, but it is not a single uniform benefit. A garden-facing apartment may receive a more open outlook. It may also experience voices, play activity, lighting or maintenance at certain hours. An apartment facing inward, toward the street or toward another building will have a different balance of privacy and sound. The word garden should lead to a site visit, not to a blanket premium.

The spacing of more than 15 metres between the buildings is another project-level design fact. It suggests that the site plan was intended to create separation within the complex. It does not guarantee an unobstructed view or complete privacy from every room. The result depends on the apartment's height, exact angle, window position, balcony depth, neighbouring facade and any mature landscaping. Stand at the principal windows and balcony at the time of day that matters to you. Check the view while seated as well as standing. Marketing photographs are rarely taken from normal eye level throughout a home.

Some buyers make a sea view part of their north Tel Aviv shortlist. No inspected source verifies a sea view for a particular RECANATI RESIDENCE apartment. Visit in clear and hazy conditions if that feature affects the price you are willing to pay. Daily convenience should be measured around the buyer's own routine. A map label cannot tell a parent how the school run feels, a commuter how reliable a connection is at the needed hour, or an older resident whether the final walking segment is comfortable. Create a weekly route list. Include work, school or nursery, medical care, groceries, sport, family visits and the places reached most often after dark. Test the routes from the actual building entrance. Do not rely on a distance calculated from the historic middle of the 5-15 site.

School and nursery research needs a date. A nearby institution is not automatically the assigned institution for a specific address, and catchment rules can change. Buyers who depend on a particular framework should verify the exact current address, registration rules, age group, language, transport and capacity directly with the responsible authority or institution. The project source does not publish a guaranteed school assignment.

Parking deserves the same local test. Three underground levels establish substantial parking infrastructure, but they do not tell you whether the offered apartment includes one space, two spaces or none. They do not identify the location, width, turning path, height clearance, proximity to the lift, charging rights or whether the space is a normal, tandem or mechanically dependent arrangement. Bring the car you actually use. Drive the route. Open the doors. Check the agreement and plan after the physical test.

For a buyer who does not drive every day, the value calculation shifts toward walking, cycling, public transport and reliable taxi access. Test the route in heat and rain, with shopping or a stroller if relevant. Check whether the lobby approach is step-free. Observe bicycle access and storage rather than assuming it exists. ## Buildings and apartments

The three completed buildings create a different buying environment from a single isolated tower. A buyer should identify not only the apartment but also the building within the complex. Entrance position, distance to the garden, internal circulation, lift arrangement, service access, refuse route and relation to the other two buildings can influence daily experience and resale appeal.

The official project page describes the homes as 3 to 5 rooms, mini-penthouses and penthouses. Israeli room counts include the living room. An English-speaking buyer should not automatically convert a 4-room apartment into a four-bedroom home. In ordinary Israeli usage, it will often mean a living room plus three bedrooms, but the signed plan and current layout control. Rooms may also have been combined or divided after construction. Count usable spaces on site and compare them with the approved and contractual plan.

No current official unit schedule was published in the sources inspected for this guide. That means there is no reliable public table connecting apartment number, area, floor, direction, balcony, parking, storage and price. A broker card or portal listing can begin the conversation. It should not be the final evidence. Ask for a dated seller-issued sheet and the documents that support every field.

Area is especially easy to misunderstand. A listing may use a marketing area, municipal area, registered area, sale-agreement area or a calculation that includes a share of walls. These numbers need not match. Ask the seller to label the measurement convention. Obtain the plan with dimensions. If price per square metre will drive negotiation, calculate it more than one way and state which area is in the denominator. A price divided by an undefined area creates false precision.

For an apartment inspection, begin with layout rather than finishes. Check the path from entrance to living room. Look for wasted corridor space. Test whether the dining position blocks circulation. Measure the wall intended for a sofa or television. Confirm that bedroom doors can open with the proposed furniture. Check whether a safe room, if present, works as the intended bedroom or study. Consider where luggage, cleaning equipment, sports gear and seasonal items will go if no storage room is attached.

Light should be assessed by direction and obstruction, not by floor number alone. A high floor may still face another building. A lower floor may receive good light across a garden. Ask for a compass reading, then verify it against the plan and real sun path. Visit at more than one time if natural light matters. Switch off all artificial lighting during part of the viewing. Photographs taken with wide-angle lenses and bright exposure can hide the actual depth and brightness of a room.

Ventilation requires a similar check. Open the relevant windows and balcony doors. Identify whether the home has openings on one facade or more than one. Test how air moves through the kitchen and wet rooms. Inspect extraction systems. A marketing description such as three directions should be supported by the plan and the real openings, not by a generic project diagram.

Noise changes by hour. Listen with windows open and closed. Check street traffic, garden activity, lifts, refuse collection, mechanical systems, parking gates and neighbouring building services. Visit during a busy period and a quieter period. If the apartment sits near a lift core, plant room, garage ramp or shared terrace, ask whether that relationship appears in the plan and whether residents report recurring disturbance.

Water and envelope checks remain useful in a relatively new completed home. Look around window frames, balcony thresholds, ceiling corners, wet-room joints and service shafts. Ask for the apartment's defect and repair history. Inspect after rain where possible. A new appearance does not eliminate maintenance or workmanship questions. A qualified inspector can distinguish cosmetic finishing from an issue that needs engineering or waterproofing attention.

The shared property deserves its own review. Walk all practical routes: entrance, mail area, lifts, stairs, parking, refuse room, bicycle access and emergency exits. Observe cleaning and wear. Ask for the building or complex budget, recent meeting records, reserve position, insurance, service contracts and any major planned expenditure. Three 16-floor buildings with lifts, landscaping and underground parking require ongoing management. The exact cost was not published in the project sources and should not be guessed.

Parking rights must appear in the legal and contractual file. A physical space shown during a viewing is not enough. Check its number, plan location and attachment to the apartment. If there is electric-vehicle charging, establish whether the electrical infrastructure, meter, permission process and future capacity are documented. If the space is narrow or beside a column, measure it. A nominal parking benefit can become daily friction.

Storage follows the same rule. The project description does not promise a storage room for every apartment. If one is offered, inspect its location, ventilation, moisture, access and actual dimensions. Confirm that the right is attached to the apartment and that the room shown is the room identified in the documents. Do not value a storage room that exists only in a listing sentence.

Buyers should also ask whether changes were made after the original handover. A moved wall, enclosed balcony, altered plumbing line, added air-conditioning system or merged room may be useful, but it needs review against approvals, building rules and the registered or contractual plan. A completed project can already contain owner modifications. The newer age of the building does not guarantee that every interior remains in original approved form.

If the seller is the developer or an affiliated entity, ask which specification and warranty framework applies to the exact unit and whether it has already been occupied. If the seller is a private owner, request the original purchase agreement and handover documents where relevant, along with evidence for upgrades, repairs and warranties that may be transferable. The legal relationship and remedies differ. The project name alone does not answer them.

A practical document checklist for a serious candidate includes seller identification and authority, current title or rights extract, cadastral or company-registration material where applicable, original and current sale documents, the signed apartment plan, parking and storage plans, municipal file checks, tax and charge clearance requirements, building-management records, possession status and a list of included fixtures. A lawyer should tailor the list to the registration stage and seller type.

The [guide to buying a new apartment](https://nad-lan.co.il/new-projects/) can help organize contractor and project questions. RECANATI RESIDENCE is already complete, so the guide should be adapted. Add actual-condition inspection, occupied-building accounts, seller-specific authority, existing management obligations and the exact possession mechanism. Do not use a generic checklist as a substitute for a lawyer and engineer who have the unit documents.

The strongest feature of a completed apartment is verifiability. Use it. Sit in each main room. Measure furniture walls. Check mobile reception and internet options. Run taps and showers where permitted. Test shutters, windows and air conditioning. Ride the lift at a busy hour. Walk from the parking space to the apartment carrying what you normally carry. A buyer should pay for the home that exists, not for the best version imagined from a brochure.

## Prices and estimates

No current official asking-price table was published on the inspected RECANATI RESIDENCE project page. No dated current unit schedule connects a price with an apartment number, area, floor, direction and rights. A responsible current price answer is therefore: obtain a fresh written offer for the exact unit. Any number copied from an old portal, historic campaign or undated sales message may no longer describe an available apartment.

The regulatory record does provide historical transaction context. It records one Building C sale in 2023 for a 237 sqm unit at NIS 14.083 million excluding VAT, equivalent in that filing to NIS 59,422 per sqm excluding VAT. This is a dated large-unit transaction, likely at the high end of the mix. It is not a current asking price. It is not an average for 3-room, 4-room or 5-room apartments. It is not directly comparable unless the subject unit has similar size, position, specification, rights, sale date and tax basis.

That example is useful because it shows how much context a price needs. The transaction year matters. The area definition matters. VAT treatment matters. A large penthouse-like unit may carry a different rate per square metre from a standard family apartment. Parking, storage, terraces and outlook affect value but may not sit in the headline area. A current buyer should reconstruct the comparison instead of lifting the final number.

Start price analysis with the exact current offer. Record the total asking price and every attached right. Then identify comparable completed transactions from the same complex if available through reliable records. Prefer similar building, floor band, area, room count, condition, exposure, parking and storage. If same-project evidence is thin, widen gradually to genuinely comparable completed buildings in Ramat Aviv Gimel. Explain every adjustment. Do not jump directly to Sde Dov or central Tel Aviv luxury towers because they appear in search results.

A live listing is an asking price, not a completed transaction. It can show seller expectations and competing inventory. It cannot prove where a deal will close. Note how long the listing has been visible, whether the same unit appears through several agents, whether its area description changes and whether the price includes fixtures or unusual rights. Duplicate advertisements should not be counted as separate supply.

Price per square metre is a diagnostic tool, not a valuation by itself. Calculate it using the registered or contractually defined interior area, then show any alternative calculation separately. Do not blend terraces, gardens or parking into the denominator without explanation. Two apartments with the same headline room count can differ significantly in efficient interior area. A lower rate may reflect less usable space, lower floor, poorer exposure, work required or a weaker rights package.

The purchase budget extends beyond the agreed price. It may include purchase tax, legal fees, valuation, engineering inspection, registration and bank charges, currency conversion, financing costs, insurance, immediate repairs, furniture, management charges and municipal tax. The correct mix depends on the buyer, seller, financing and apartment. Build the full budget before making an offer so that a negotiated price reduction is not consumed by costs that were ignored.

Purchase tax in Israel depends on the buyer's status, other property ownership, transaction date and current law. The [purchase-tax calculator](https://nad-lan.co.il/purchase-tax-calculator/) is useful for an initial scenario. It is not a tax ruling. A buyer living abroad, planning immigration or changing tax residence should obtain advice based on the actual signing date and personal facts before committing to a price ceiling.

Financing needs an apartment-specific path as well. A bank may consider borrower income, residence, currency, credit history, loan-to-value policy, property registration and valuation. An online [mortgage calculator](https://nad-lan.co.il/mortgage-calculator/) can test monthly-payment scenarios. The bank's approval, appraisal and final terms control. Immediate occupancy can compress the time available for financing, source-of-funds checks and currency transfers, so those steps should start before signing pressure develops.

Foreign-currency buyers should separate property price risk from exchange-rate risk. Decide which currency funds are held in, when conversion may occur, who bears transfer fees and whether the payment timetable creates several conversion dates. A seemingly small currency movement on a large Tel Aviv purchase can exceed the cost of a finish upgrade. This is not a prediction about rates. It is a reason to model scenarios and coordinate the contract timetable with the bank and advisers.

Ongoing costs affect both homebuyers and investors. Ask for the current management charge for the exact apartment, the latest approved budget, reserve balance, insurance arrangements and any special assessments. Obtain the municipal tax classification and recent bill rather than estimating from area alone. Add utilities, parking or charging costs, insurance and routine maintenance. A completed complex provides actual accounts, so there is little reason to rely on a promotional estimate.

For an investment review, collect realistic rent evidence for comparable apartments, not just advertised top-end rents. Deduct vacancy, management, repairs, insurance, municipal costs when vacant, tax where applicable and transaction costs. Then test the resale market for the same room count and price band. The inspected sources do not publish a verified current yield for RECANATI RESIDENCE, and this guide does not invent one.

Liquidity is often more useful than a headline yield. Ask how many genuinely comparable units sold in a reasonable period, how wide the gap was between asking and transaction prices, and whether demand depends on an unusually small buyer group. A very large unit may have prestige and scarcity but a narrower resale pool. A well-planned family apartment may attract broader use demand. These are hypotheses to test with current evidence, not promises.

Negotiation should be tied to the file. A strong offer can explain its basis: verified comparable transactions, current competing units, condition, missing rights, renovation cost, management obligations, possession timing and financing certainty. An arbitrary discount request is easy to reject. A documented offer gives the seller a clear path to agreement.

If information is incomplete, do not fill the gap with a polished range. Ask for the documents. A numerical estimate would be non-binding even with good inputs; without a current unit identity and rights package, it would be noise. The most useful price result at the early stage is a comparison worksheet with blank fields visibly marked until evidence arrives.

Before transferring a reservation payment or signing a short form, have a lawyer review whether it is binding, refundable, conditional and paid to the correct party. A completed unit can create urgency because it appears ready to use. Readiness does not make a preliminary document harmless. The legal effect comes from its wording and the surrounding transaction.

## Project stages

RECANATI RESIDENCE began as an urban-renewal plan for the historic Recanati 5-15 site. The statutory plan became effective in October 2012. It provided for the replacement of 96 older apartments with 198 homes in three buildings. The long path between planning and completion is part of the project's identity, but a current buyer is purchasing in the completed result, not joining the original renewal process.

Regulatory material separates the 198 apartments into 96 replacement homes and 102 homes allocated for developer marketing. That allocation explains the economics and resident mix of the original project. It does not establish current inventory. By 2026, apartments from either group may be owner-occupied, rented or resold. The current transaction type must be established from the seller's documents.

The 2023 annual report records completion of buildings 1 and 2 in March 2023 and building 3 in June 2023. This is stronger than repeating an earlier forecast. It provides a dated completion history for all three buildings. The current official project page now presents immediate occupancy, while Enav describes the project as occupied.

Completion does not mean every legal and administrative step is identical for every apartment. Registration, subdivision, transfers, mortgages, municipal records and common-property arrangements can progress on different timetables. Ask for a current rights extract and a clear explanation of how title will transfer in the proposed deal. Do not infer registration status from the building's physical appearance.

The practical current stage is unit selection and transaction verification. First obtain a dated unit identity, price and rights schedule. Then perform legal and physical checks. Next confirm financing and tax. Only after those stages should the buyer finalize negotiation and contract terms. The ready building allows these workstreams to use actual evidence.

An occupied project also has an operating history. Ask for real management accounts, lift and system service records, insurance, meeting minutes and any special assessment under discussion. Walk the shared property. Speak with residents where appropriate and respectful. Treat anecdotal comments as leads to verify, not as conclusive evidence.

The possession stage needs a detailed protocol even if the apartment is empty. Record condition, meter readings, keys, access devices, included fixtures and defects. Photograph material surfaces and systems. State who pays charges from which date. If the unit is occupied by a tenant or seller, the agreement needs a clear vacation and possession mechanism. The phrase immediate occupancy cannot resolve those facts.

If the buyer intends to renovate, approval and timing should be checked before purchase. Building rules may govern work hours, lift protection, waste removal, contractor insurance and changes to exterior elements. Structural, plumbing, facade or safe-room changes may require professional and municipal review. A completed apartment can be customized, but not every alteration is a private interior choice.

For remote purchase, use decision gates. Gate one is an evidence package. Gate two is a live continuous tour and local professional inspection. Gate three is legal and tax review. Gate four is financing and funds readiness. Gate five is a negotiated draft with a workable possession plan. A buyer who cannot pass a gate should pause rather than compensate with optimism.

Current availability should be rechecked at several points: before a viewing trip, before professional expense, before a reservation document and before signing. A unit may be withdrawn, sold or repriced. Ask for written confirmation with a date and identify who issued it. That practice is more reliable than describing the project as sold out or available based on undated public pages.

The final stage is not merely receiving keys. It includes registration or the agreed interim rights process, transfer of parking and storage rights, insurance activation, management-company onboarding, municipal-account changes and closure of documented defects or retentions. Build these tasks into the transaction timetable.

The project may suit a buyer who wants a completed apartment in an established north Tel Aviv neighborhood and prefers to inspect the real home before committing. It may appeal to households that value a multi-building complex, underground parking infrastructure and proximity to a named garden. It may also suit a buyer whose relocation timing makes an off-plan delivery window impractical.

It is not automatically right for every buyer seeking a luxury apartment in Tel Aviv. The public sources do not establish a current price, unit choice or apartment-specific view. The building scale and ongoing management may not suit someone seeking a small intimate block. A buyer who wants the sea immediately outside the building should not infer that experience from generic Tel Aviv marketing.

Remote buyers may find a completed project easier to verify than an off-plan one because the apartment and shared areas exist. The project may be less suitable for a buyer whose budget depends on an unverified discount, unusually low management charge or guaranteed rent. A disciplined shortlist uses needs that can be tested. Set the maximum total budget including tax and costs. Define minimum usable rooms and furniture dimensions. Specify acceptable orientations and noise. State whether parking, storage, accessibility or immediate possession is essential. Then compare actual units. Do not begin with a project name and bend the household around whatever happens to be offered.

When a unit appears suitable, request the current price, unit sheet, plan, rights extract, parking and storage schedule, building charge, municipal bill, possession status and seller identity in one package. Compare the documents with the viewing. If the two stories differ, investigate before negotiating.

### Is the project in Neve Avivim?

No current first-party project source inspected for this guide identifies it that way. The official Ashtrom page uses Ramat Aviv Gimel. Neve Avivim appears to be a mix-up with separate nearby Ashtrom projects.

### How many buildings and apartments are there?

The verified current project total is three buildings and 198 apartments. The current official source describes the buildings as 16 floors. An isolated contractor page displays 66 apartments, which is exactly one third of 198 and cannot be used as the project total.

### What apartment types were published?

The official project page lists 3-room, 4-room and 5-room apartments, mini-penthouses and penthouses. It does not publish a current unit-by-unit schedule with areas, floors, directions or prices. Ask for a dated sheet for the apartment offered now.

### Is RECANATI RESIDENCE complete?

Yes. The 2023 regulatory report records completion of buildings 1 and 2 in March 2023 and building 3 in June 2023. Enav says the project is occupied. Ashtrom currently presents immediate occupancy.

### Does immediate occupancy mean I can receive keys at once?

Not by itself. It is a current project status. The actual possession date depends on the selected unit, seller, payment timetable, legal status, current occupancy and signed agreement. Obtain the proposed possession clause in writing.

### What is the current price?

No current official price table was found on the inspected project page. Current price must be requested for a specific unit with its area, floor, rights and payment terms. An undated portal price should not be treated as a current developer offer.

### Is there a reliable historic price reference?

The 2023 regulatory report records one 237 sqm Building C unit at NIS 14.083 million excluding VAT, or NIS 59,422 per sqm excluding VAT. It is a dated large-unit transaction, not a current average or a price estimate for another apartment.

### Are apartments currently available?

Current unit-by-unit inventory was not published in the inspected official source. Older reports and stale indexed sales wording cannot establish today's availability. Ask for same-day written confirmation identifying the apartment and seller.

### Does every apartment receive parking and storage?

The project has three underground parking levels. That does not prove an allocation to every apartment. The public source does not promise a storage room for each home. Check the specific agreement, plan and rights record.

### Is there a sea view?

No inspected source guarantees a sea view for a particular unit. Verify the actual apartment's floor, direction and current outlook in person or through a continuous live tour. Do not rely on a generic Tel Aviv or Sde Dov image.

### Who developed the project?

The current attribution is Ashtrom Residences, formerly Ashdar, together with Enav. The Ashdar name appears in historic documents because the official brand change took place in February 2025.

### Why do some sources show a different floor count?

Current Ashtrom material and the later regulatory report use 16 floors. Earlier planning or partner descriptions used 17 or 18 under different counting conventions. For a purchase, the apartment's contractual floor and building documents matter more than a historic marketing count.

### What should a remote buyer request first?

Request a dated unit and price sheet, seller identity, current rights extract, signed plan, parking and storage schedule, building charge, municipal bill, possession status and proposed payment timetable. Then arrange an independent continuous tour, legal review and physical inspection.

### What should I check during a viewing?

Check layout, dimensions, light, ventilation, noise, privacy, water marks, windows, wet rooms, systems and alterations. Walk the lobby, lifts, parking and storage routes. Visit at the times that matter to your routine and compare what you see with the signed plan.

### How should an investor evaluate it?

Use current comparable rents, realistic vacancy, management and maintenance costs, purchase tax, financing, currency exposure and resale evidence for the exact unit type. The project sources do not publish a verified yield, so no return should be assumed.

### What is the next useful step?

Choose one actual apartment and request its complete current evidence package. A project-level description cannot answer price, view, parking, storage, condition or possession for that unit. Compare the written file with a real inspection before making a binding commitment.
