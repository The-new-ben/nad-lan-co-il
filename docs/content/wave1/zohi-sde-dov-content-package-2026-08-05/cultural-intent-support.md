# ZOHI Sde Dov multilingual cultural-intent research support

Research date: 5 August 2026, Israel time  
Scope: English, French, Russian, and Arabic search intent for a future ZOHI Sde Dov project article  
Output type: research and editorial brief only  

This document is not a project article and does not establish any project fact. It records wording that was visibly present in localized Google results, then converts that evidence into an editorial strategy. Search-result titles, snippets, related searches, People Also Ask questions, and AI-generated summaries are vocabulary and intent evidence only. They must never be copied into a project fact ledger.

## Evidence labels used throughout

- **Observed evidence** means wording or interface behavior directly visible during the recorded Chrome session.
- **Editorial inference** means a proposed content decision based on the observed pattern. It is not a demographic claim and it is not a Google ranking claim.
- **Fact gate** means a rule that prevents search wording, competitor copy, or an unverified assumption from becoming a factual statement.
- **Unknown** means the available search observation cannot answer the question.

The cultural sections describe useful buyer situations, not fixed traits of English, French, Russian, or Arabic speakers. A reader's language does not prove nationality, residence, religion, wealth, investment objective, or migration plans. Each article should offer the relevant pathway without assigning it to every visitor.

## Query-owner contract

The intended owner is one future project-specific page for **ZOHI Sde Dov** in each language. The owner task is to help a buyer evaluate the named project: identify it, understand only the verified offering, place it in the Sde Dov and Tel Aviv context, interpret the published price status, and know what to verify before making contact or purchasing.

The page must exclude these intents:

- a general directory of all Sde Dov projects;
- a history of the former Sde Dov airport;
- a generic Tel Aviv apartment-listing page;
- short-term rentals, hotels, holiday apartments, or rental searches;
- a page about UTOPIA, Rainbow, YAMA, FIRST, ASHIRA, GINDI, or another neighboring project;
- a promise of investment return, rent, resale value, sea view, delivery, availability, or financing;
- a legal, tax, immigration, mortgage, or currency-transfer guide pretending to replace a qualified professional;
- an official developer sales page or a claim that the independent editorial site represents the developer.

The blind-librarian test for every future language page is simple: after reading only the Title, H1, and opening paragraph, a reviewer must be able to answer "this is the ZOHI Sde Dov buyer page in Tel Aviv" and must not mistake it for a generic Sde Dov guide or another project.

## Live Chrome setup and limitations

All searches were run in the user's real Chrome session on desktop. Google was set to Israel with `gl=il`; interface language was set separately with `hl=en`, `hl=fr`, `hl=ru`, or `hl=ar`; and `pws=0` was used. The visible footer located the session in Israel and, where shown, specifically Tel Aviv based on the IP address. Viewport observations ranged from 998 x 317 to 1098 x 349 CSS pixels because the connected Chrome window changed size between tabs.

English and French rendered with `html lang="en-IL"` and `html lang="fr-IL"`. Russian and Arabic searches retained the requested target-language controls and target-language queries, but the rendered document reported `html lang="iw"`. Russian organic results became materially more Russian when `lr=lang_ru` was added. For Arabic, `lr=lang_ar` improved Arabic result language for a broad Tel Aviv buying query, but returned no results for the exact ZOHI query. Removing `lr=lang_ar` surfaced the official project result plus Arabic property-result titles, while also allowing Hebrew and English material. Both conditions were deliberately preserved as evidence.

The account state was visibly signed in for the English, French, and Arabic tabs. The Russian Google domain displayed a sign-in control and was therefore treated as signed out. The English, French, and Arabic result footers visibly stated that results were not personalized; `pws=0` was also present. This remains one manual observation, not a rank distribution, search-volume estimate, or keyword-difficulty measurement. Autocomplete and result composition can vary with time, device, account, and Google experiments.

### Observed intent pattern

English results repeatedly combined `apartments for sale`, `new apartments`, `sea view`, `beachfront`, `prices`, `investment`, and the ability to buy from abroad. The remote-buyer query produced an AI summary and organic results centered on foreign buyers, legal representation, tax, finance, payment, and remote process. Those answers are not accepted as facts, but the question set is clear: an English-language buyer may need the project explained together with the mechanics of evaluating a purchase from outside Israel.

### Editorial inference

The English article should serve three overlapping situations without assuming that every English reader is foreign:

1. a resident comparing new Tel Aviv projects;
2. a relocating household that needs city and neighborhood orientation;
3. an overseas buyer who needs an explicit remote-due-diligence path.

The voice should be direct and practical. Define Israeli terms the first time they appear. If an authoritative source uses Hebrew planning terminology, retain the term in transliteration and immediately explain it in plain English. State prices first in ILS; optional foreign-currency illustrations must be dated, sourced to an exchange-rate source, and labeled as non-binding illustrations. Do not convert a published ILS figure and then present the converted number as a project price.

The article should answer: What exactly is ZOHI? What has been officially published? What remains unpublished? Where is the project in relation to the district, coast, transport, and daily services? Which apartment categories are verified? Is a price list public? What must an overseas buyer verify about representation, signatures, money transfer, financing, tax status, warranties, construction linkage, and delivery? The process questions should link to qualified guides or professionals rather than offering personal legal or tax advice.

### Exact primary-signal recommendation

- **Title:** `ZOHI Sde Dov Tel Aviv apartments for sale - prices, plans and buyer checks`
- **H1:** `ZOHI Sde Dov Tel Aviv apartments for sale - prices, plans and buying guide`
- **Primary owner query:** `ZOHI Sde Dov Tel Aviv apartments for sale`
- **Secondary natural vocabulary:** `new apartments in Sde Dov`, `Sde Dov apartments for sale`, `buying from abroad`, `apartment plans`, `price status`, `sea-view verification`

The Title says the page covers prices and plans; it does not claim that a current price list or downloadable plans exist. The opening must resolve that status immediately. If no current project price is published, say so in the first paragraph and direct the reader to the later methodology for comparing verified market evidence.

### English no-go language

Avoid `guaranteed investment`, `best opportunity`, `beachfront`, `unobstructed sea view`, `available now`, `starting from`, `high return`, `safe investment`, `move-in date`, or `foreign buyers can simply...` unless the exact claim has current, attributable support. Do not use `relocation` as if it proves aliyah. Do not call every apartment a residence, unit, condo, or flat interchangeably; use `apartment` as the main noun and explain project terminology where needed.

### Observed intent pattern

French results used `appartement neuf`, `projet neuf`, `à vendre`, `immobilier`, `investir`, `vue mer`, and explicit France-to-Israel purchase language. The remote-purchase query surfaced result copy about French-speaking accompaniment, video visits, remote signing, and buying from France, Belgium, or Switzerland. Again, those process claims require independent sourcing. As intent evidence, they show that a French page should not stop at architectural description. It needs a legible path for a buyer who cannot visit every week and may compare values in both shekels and euros.

### Editorial inference

The French article should feel written for a francophone buyer, not translated from English or Hebrew. Use `appartement` and `pièces` consistently. Explain that Israeli room-count conventions may not map exactly to French bedroom-count expectations. Never silently translate an Israeli `X-room` label into `X chambres`; preserve the original category and explain the convention.

Remote purchase deserves a dedicated practical subsection: documents to request, role of an independent lawyer, how to review plans and specifications, how to document representations, what a power of attorney is for, how payment milestones are verified, how currency movements can change the effective euro cost, and which questions must be answered before a remote reservation or contract. Each legal or tax statement still needs a current authoritative source and a non-advisory frame.

Aliyah, a second home, family proximity, and community can be offered as reader pathways, but they must never be assumed. A French-speaking visitor in Israel may be a long-term resident; a reader in France may be investing; a Belgian, Swiss, Canadian, or other francophone buyer may have a different tax and banking context. Phrase the choices conditionally: `si vous préparez une installation`, `si vous achetez à distance`, `si le bien est destiné à votre famille`, or `si vous comparez un investissement`.

- **H1:** `ZOHI Sde Dov Tel Aviv appartement à vendre - prix, plans et guide d'achat`

### French no-go language

Avoid `opportunité unique`, `dernier bord de mer`, `placement sûr`, `rentabilité garantie`, `vue mer imprenable`, `prix de lancement`, `disponible`, `livraison prévue`, `accompagnement complet`, or `achat sécurisé` without direct and current evidence. Do not translate `purchase tax` mechanically; use the correct French term for the verified Israeli tax context and keep the original Hebrew name in parentheses if it helps precision. Do not say `résidence` when the source only proves an apartment project, and do not imply that a French-speaking sales contact is an independent adviser.

## Cross-language fact contract

All four future articles must be generated from one language-neutral fact ledger. Cultural adaptation changes questions, ordering, examples, and explanations. It never changes the underlying project facts.

### Mandatory fact-ledger fields

For every publishable claim, record:

1. canonical fact ID;
2. exact claim in a language-neutral working form;
3. value and unit;
4. scope, such as project, building, apartment type, individual unit, district, or city;
5. fact type, such as official statement, planning record, permit record, reported transaction, current asking price, historical asking price, or editorial estimate;
6. primary source URL;
7. source publisher and document title;
8. publication date if available;
9. verification date;
10. exact source excerpt kept internally for audit;
11. allowed wording and prohibited extrapolation;
12. status: verified, superseded, conflicting, not published, or estimate;
13. four-language rendering check;
14. reviewer decision.

### Source order

Use the most direct source available:

1. official municipal, planning, permit, cadastral, or statutory record for planning and legal status;
2. official project or developer publication for the offering, design, apartment categories, amenities, team, and marketing status;
3. current public-company filing when applicable;
4. attributable reporting from a reputable publication for transactions, launch events, or dated statements;
5. property portals and brokerage listings only as clearly labeled asking-price or listing examples, never as the project's official inventory;
6. editorial calculation only when inputs are verified, method is visible, date is stated, and the result is labeled as a non-binding estimate.

Google snippets, AI summaries, autocomplete, related searches, PAA questions, social captions, and copied portal descriptions are excluded as project-fact sources. They may identify a question worth researching. They may not answer it.

### Fields that must remain identical in substance

- exact project identity and brand;
- city and neighborhood;
- developer and partner identities;
- planning lot, parcel, or address status;
- number and type of buildings;
- floor counts and how they are counted;
- apartment or unit total;
- apartment categories and published size ranges;
- parking, storage, balconies, shelters, and specifications;
- published amenities;
- architect, designer, contractor, and consultants;
- permit, construction, marketing, and delivery status;
- published availability;
- every price, price range, currency, date, and evidence class;
- payment terms and conditions;
- distances and travel times;
- view and orientation claims;
- source list and source-access dates.

Language-local formatting may change punctuation, date order, decimal separators, digit shape, currency label, and explanatory context. The normalized value cannot change. A fact that is missing in one language cannot appear in another merely because a competitor wrote it there.

### Required treatment of unknowns

If an item has not been published or cannot be verified, say so plainly in the target language:

- English: `This information has not been published in the sources reviewed.`
- French: `Cette information n'a pas été publiée dans les sources consultées.`
- Russian: `Эта информация не опубликована в проверенных источниках.`
- Arabic: `لم تُنشر هذه المعلومة في المصادر التي تمت مراجعتها.`

Do not fill the gap with a nearby project, a district average, a visually inferred count, an AI answer, a sales-agent message, or a model-generated approximation. A district estimate may still be useful when it is clearly labeled, independently sourced, and never presented as the ZOHI price.

### Required estimate labels

- English: `Estimate, non-binding.`
- French: `Estimation non contractuelle.`
- Russian: `Оценка, не является обязательной.`
- Arabic: `تقدير غير ملزم.`

The label must sit next to the number, not in a remote disclaimer. Every estimate needs a date, inputs, method, scope, and reason it is useful. If those elements are unavailable, omit the estimate.

### Price evidence classes

Every price statement must identify one of these classes:

- official current project asking price;
- official historical launch price;
- current private listing asking price;
- reported completed transaction;
- district-level published range;
- editorial estimate.

Never combine classes into one seamless range. Never say `prices start at` based on a single listing or an old campaign. Never convert a district price per square meter into an individual apartment price without verified area, included spaces, floor, orientation, taxes, linkage, and date. Currency conversions do not create a new evidence class.

### View, coast, and distance gates

`Sea view`, `vue mer`, `вид на море`, and `إطلالة بحرية` are unit-level or orientation-level claims unless a source explicitly defines a broader scope. A coastal district does not prove a view. A rendering does not prove a protected future view. Publish the claim only when the exact building or apartment orientation is verified, the measurement source is recorded, and future obstruction is not hidden.

Map distances must state whether they are straight-line, walking, driving, or transit distances. The start point must be the verified project location, not the center of Sde Dov. A planning lot must not be written as a postal street address.
