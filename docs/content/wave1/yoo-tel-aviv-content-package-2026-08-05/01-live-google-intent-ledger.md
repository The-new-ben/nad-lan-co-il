# YOO Tel Aviv - live Google intent ledger

Research date: 2026-08-05

Method: searches were performed in the user's real Chrome browser on Google with Israel as the country context, Tel Aviv or Tel Aviv-Yafo shown as the detected location, personalization disabled where exposed, and a separate interface language for English, French, Russian, and Arabic. The search URLs used the target-language `hl` value, `gl=il`, and `pws=0`. Evidence was taken from visible autocomplete suggestions, organic result titles, People Also Ask, and competitor-title language. AI-generated summaries were inspected only to understand question language and were not accepted as factual sources.

Search results change. The phrases below are a dated research capture, not a permanent volume ranking.

## Cross-language decision

All four language products concern the same completed resale complex. None should be written as a new-build launch, contractor presale, or developer inventory page. The shared informational spine is:

- what YOO Tel Aviv is and where it sits in Park Tzameret;
- what the original plans and credible records establish;
- what a resale buyer must verify at apartment level;
- how current price and availability evidence should be interpreted;
- how building management, alterations, parking, storage, view, access, and future transport affect the decision.

The order and language of buyer questions should respond to the target-language search evidence. Cultural intent is a content hypothesis based on search patterns and cross-border buying friction, not a claim that every speaker has the same priorities.

### English content signals

- Prefer `apartment`, `resale apartment`, `completed residential towers`, `Park Tzameret`, `Nissim Aloni`, `asking price`, `completed transaction`, `management fees`, `parking rights`, and `approved alterations`.
- Explain Israeli room-count conventions instead of assuming they match US or UK bedroom counts.
- Give NIS first for Israeli transaction facts. A converted currency can only be a dated convenience calculation, not the advertised project price.
- Address foreign and relocation buyers without implying that foreign ownership, financing, tax, or residency outcomes are automatic.
- Avoid `off-plan`, `pre-construction`, `delivery`, `developer price`, and `new apartments` unless used to explain why they do not describe this project.

## Shared question architecture for the eventual four products

The facts remain fixed, but the lead buyer questions should be ordered as follows:

| Language | First buyer concerns to answer |
|---|---|
| English | Current resale availability, relocation or foreign-purchase workflow, building costs, apartment-level due diligence |
| French | Existing resale versus new program, remote purchase, room and area conventions, charges, currency and representation |
| Russian | Current unit price and status, living versus investment, distance and location reality, area, parking, ongoing costs |
| Arabic | Verified availability, family-usable space, total purchase cost, education and services, rights and permitted alterations |

This is not permission to create different facts. It is a relevance map for presenting the same evidence in a useful order.

## Search-language traps to avoid

- Competitor adjectives such as `luxury`, `prestigieuse`, `элитные`, and `فاخرة` are not verified attributes by themselves. If used, they must be supported through specific features rather than repeated as empty labels.
- `Sea view`, `vue sur la mer`, `вид на море`, and `إطلالة بحرية` are apartment-level claims.
- Search titles often omit whether a price is an asking price. Future content must label it.
- Room counts in Israeli listings cannot be translated into bedrooms without explanation.
- A listing title does not prove current availability after the capture date.
- An AI Overview does not qualify as a source for legal eligibility, price, floor count, facilities, or project status.
- Google wording is an intent signal, not permission to copy a competitor's article or to stuff exact phrases unnaturally.

## Research output for future titles and openings

The eventual title and first paragraph for each language should be written only after the fact baseline is locked. Each must:

- lead with YOO Tel Aviv or the culturally natural YOO project form;
- include the strongest observed purchase phrase naturally;
- identify Tel Aviv and Park Tzameret;
- say in the first paragraph that the towers are completed and purchases are generally resales;
- include apartment, price, and buying language in the target language without implying live stock or a verified project price;
- give at least one hard, source-scoped fact early, such as two towers and the original up-to-297-apartment plan;
- avoid opening with the site's independence statement or internal process language.

## Evidence limitations

- Google does not expose stable search volumes in this browser workflow. Phrase strength here means repeated relevance and commercial clarity in live results, not a claimed monthly volume.
- Russian autocomplete changed to Hebrew during the session, so Russian suggestions were excluded rather than reconstructed.
- Arabic project-specific organic coverage was thin. The ledger combines exact project autocomplete with a broader Arabic Tel Aviv apartment-buying SERP to capture real buyer questions.
- Search localization was confirmed through interface language, Israel location, Tel Aviv-area footer, and query parameters. It does not simulate every city, device, or signed-in user.
