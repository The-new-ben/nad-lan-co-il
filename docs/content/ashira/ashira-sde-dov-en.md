# ASHIRA Sde Dov - New apartments Tel Aviv: prices, buildings and buyer guide

ASHIRA Sde Dov is a planned four-building residential project in Tel Aviv with 406 apartments on lot 101 in the Eshkol area of the new Sde Dov district. The approved composition includes a 35-storey tower, a 16-storey building and two 8-storey buildings. A full building permit was publicly reported in March 2026, and spring 2026 reporting placed construction at the foundation and basement-raft stage. A current public price list and live apartment inventory have not been published. That makes the buying decision less about reacting to a headline price and more about requesting a dated offer, identifying the exact building and home, checking the contract package, and separating what exists today from what is still planned for the wider district.

## Overview

ASHIRA sits inside one of Tel Aviv's largest new urban districts, but a buyer is not purchasing a general idea called Sde Dov. The contract concerns one home in one building, with a particular floor, orientation, specification, payment schedule and delivery commitment. The district story matters because it shapes the long-term setting. The apartment-level documents matter because they determine what the buyer actually receives.

The strongest public planning source is Tel Aviv Municipality's architectural and environmental design page for lot 101. It describes an approximately 7.4-dunam site and four residential buildings containing 406 homes. Of those, 202 are allocated to tower construction and 204 to perimeter or mid-rise construction. The plan also includes about 3,400 square metres of open space, described as roughly 46 percent of the lot, plus 500 square metres for a kindergarten and municipal offices. These are planning facts. They do not provide a current list of apartments for sale.

The project's official marketing presents a broad residential package: apartments, penthouses, shared leisure areas, workspaces, children's facilities and commercial space at ground level. That is useful for understanding the intended positioning. It is not a substitute for the sale plan, technical specification, management agreement or dated inventory. A serious comparison begins by putting each statement into one of three groups: approved planning data, developer marketing, or contract-level information supplied for the selected home.

### What makes the project different

The project combines several building scales rather than placing every home in one uniform tower. That matters to a buyer. A home in a 35-storey tower can have a different lift dependency, entrance experience, outlook, exposure and management-cost profile from a home in an 8-storey building. The 16-storey building creates another middle option. The choice is therefore not simply between room counts. It is also between building types and positions within the site.

The approved plan's open-space allocation is another meaningful part of the proposition. The municipality describes a shared courtyard, planting and public passage or stay easements. Buyers should read those terms carefully. An open area inside the project boundary is not automatically a private garden for residents. Some spaces may serve public movement or public use. The plan can still produce a pleasant setting, but ownership, access, maintenance and operating responsibility need to be understood rather than assumed.

ASHIRA is also being marketed as an amenity-rich development. The developer presents an indoor semi-Olympic pool, a children's pool, jacuzzi, spa, salt room, collagen tanning area, dry and wet sauna, gym, yoga and wellness areas, a private cinema, children's activity spaces, a residents lounge, bar and workspace. These features can materially influence daily life and ongoing costs. Buyers should confirm which amenities appear in binding documents, how they will be delivered, who may use them, and how their operation will be funded.

### What is known and what is not

The project name, developer, planning lot, number of buildings, approved building heights, total number of homes, planning team, open-space allocation and reported permit status are publicly supported. A current apartment-by-apartment inventory is not. Current asking prices are not. Exact apartment areas, balconies, parking spaces, storage rooms, floor positions, orientations, specifications and views are not available as a verified public schedule.

That distinction prevents an expensive mistake. A visual selector, sample plan or marketing rendering may help a buyer understand a concept. It cannot prove that a home is available, that its area is correct, or that its view will remain open. The working rule is simple: use public information to decide what to investigate, then use dated contractual documents to decide what to buy.

## Location and surroundings

ASHIRA is planned on lot 101 in Eshkol, the southeastern planning area of Sde Dov in North Tel Aviv. Tel Aviv Municipality describes Eshkol as an area near Levi Eshkol Street and S.Y. Agnon Street, intended to connect the established Kochav HaTzafon and Lamed area toward the coast. The location therefore sits between existing North Tel Aviv neighborhoods and a district that is still being built.

That boundary position can appeal to buyers who want a new project without treating the entire surrounding city as hypothetical. Established North Tel Aviv streets and neighborhoods sit beside the planning area. Much of the new Sde Dov fabric, however, will emerge over years. A buyer should assess both sides of that reality. Existing North Tel Aviv can provide present-day services and connections. The new district can bring future public space, commerce, employment and transport, but also years of construction activity and changing access routes.

### The wider Sde Dov plan

The municipality's Sde Dov page describes a former airport area being transformed into a mixed urban district. The master plan, TA/4444, was approved in August 2020. Municipal figures describe approximately 1,300 dunams, around 16,000 planned homes and a planned population of about 40,000 people. The intended mix includes housing, offices and business, retail, hotels, public buildings, open spaces and a coastal park.

These district figures provide scale, not a delivery timetable for every street. A plan for 16,000 homes tells the buyer that Sde Dov is a major city-building exercise. It does not show which school, shop, park segment or transport stop will operate on the day the apartment is handed over. The responsible question is not only, "What will the district eventually contain?" It is also, "What is expected to be usable when I need it?"

The Eshkol detailed plan, TML/3001, was approved in May 2021. The municipality reports 4,844 planned homes in Eshkol, often rounded to about 4,800, as well as approximately 45,000 square metres of retail space and 145,000 square metres of employment space. These are Eshkol-wide numbers. They must not be confused with ASHIRA's 406 homes or its own ground-floor commercial component.

### A coastal district is not the same as a beachfront apartment

Sde Dov is a coastal Tel Aviv district, and the broader plan includes a coastal park. That does not make every project first line to the sea. It also does not establish a permanent sea view from every home. ASHIRA's official material places the project within the district and presents the area's relationship with the coast, but no verified public unit schedule establishes the view from a particular apartment.

For a buyer comparing homes, the useful questions are precise:

- Which direction does the main living area face?
- Which direction do the balcony and bedrooms face?
- What is the apartment's contractual floor?
- Which existing or planned buildings stand between it and the coast?
- Is the claimed view direct, angled, partial or only a potential high-floor outlook?
- What does the approved planning around the site permit in the future?
- Is the salesperson's statement reflected in a signed document?

The same discipline applies to distance. "Near the sea" may be a fair district description, but it is not an exact measurement. A buyer who cares about daily beach access should map the real pedestrian route from the project entrance, not draw a straight line from the lot boundary. Construction diversions, future crossings and the location of the chosen building can change the practical route.

### Transport today and the future Green Line

Road and public-transport access must be divided into what operates today and what is planned. The municipality says the Green Line is intended to pass through Sde Dov, with four stations along the continuation of Ibn Gabirol. NTA describes the Green Line as a future connection from Holon and Rishon LeZion through Tel Aviv toward Herzliya and the north.

NTA's current published schedule points to 2028 for a southern portion and 2030 for full commercial operation. Those are future infrastructure dates and can change. They are not a promise that a particular ASHIRA resident will have an operating station at a stated walking distance on a contractual handover date.

A transport review should therefore have two columns. The first records current bus routes, roads, cycling options and walking connections observed during a site visit. The second records planned infrastructure, its responsible authority and its latest published schedule. Buyers relocating from abroad should repeat the check at the time of purchase and again before moving, because a district under construction can change quickly.

## Buildings and apartments

ASHIRA's approved composition brings together a 35-storey tower, a 16-storey building and two 8-storey buildings. The municipal plan allocates 202 of the project's 406 homes to tower construction and 204 to perimeter or mid-rise construction. Those figures describe the approved project. The presence of four buildings creates meaningful choices. Height is the obvious difference, but it is not the only one. Building position affects the route from the street, relationship to the shared courtyard, exposure to commercial frontage, lift use, outlook and potential construction nearby. The same room count can feel very different in two buildings. Buyers should compare the whole position before comparing the finish package.

### Understanding the four-building composition

The 35-storey tower is likely to attract buyers who value tower living, a more vertical residential environment and the possibility of higher-floor outlooks. None of those preferences proves a sea view, and a higher floor does not remove the need to check orientation and future plans. Tower buyers should also ask about the number and type of lifts, backup systems, shared systems and the cost model for operating them. Those details are not published in the verified public inventory.

The 16-storey building may appeal to someone looking for a scale between a high-rise tower and the two lower buildings. It could provide a different balance of lift dependence, common-area intensity and connection to the site. The public sources do not publish an apartment schedule that allows a factual comparison of its unit mix with the other buildings.

The two 8-storey buildings offer the lowest approved scale in the project. Some buyers may prefer a shorter vertical journey or a closer relationship to the courtyard and street. A lower building is not automatically quieter or more private. Its exact position relative to public passages, retail, vehicle access, planting and neighboring buildings matters.

This is why a building-choice discussion should include a marked site plan. Ask the seller to identify all four buildings by the same names or numbers used in the contract. Locate the entrances, refuse rooms, loading areas, parking ramps, public easements, commercial units, kindergarten allocation and main plant rooms. Then place the proposed apartment on that plan.

### Apartment areas and plans

No verified current public unit schedule provides the areas of available ASHIRA apartments. That means an article cannot responsibly list typical apartment sizes as current stock. Sample plans may exist in marketing material, but a sample is only relevant when it is tied to the exact apartment offered and matches the contractual sale plan.

When reviewing a plan, move beyond total square metres. Check the dimensions of the main living space, bedrooms and circulation. Look for structural columns, service shafts, window widths, storage inside the home, the route from the entrance to the living room, and the relationship between the kitchen and balcony. Confirm how the seller defines internal area and what is excluded from it.

For relocation buyers, compare the plan with the household's current routines rather than its current property label. A plan that works as a full-time family home may differ from one suited to a Tel Aviv base used several months a year.

### Orientation, sunlight and outlook

Orientation can affect light, heat, wind, privacy and the experience of the balcony. It can also influence energy use. Ask for a north arrow on the sale plan and site plan, then verify that the apartment is marked consistently on both. Marketing labels such as "west" or "toward the sea" need to match the actual geometry.

An outlook is not only a compass direction. The distance to the next building, height of podium elements, trees, public spaces and future construction all affect what is visible. A sales rendering may use a viewpoint that is not the viewpoint of the apartment. Request an apartment-specific view study if outlook is central to the decision, and ask what assumptions it makes about surrounding approved plans.

Sunlight should be considered by room and season. A western living room can deliver attractive late-day light and also stronger afternoon heat. A northern exposure may be more even but less bright at some times. A low-floor home facing a landscaped courtyard can feel connected to greenery while experiencing more activity. There is no universally best direction. There is only a direction that fits the buyer's priorities and the verified site context.

### Balconies, parking and storage

Public sources reviewed for this guide do not provide verified apartment-by-apartment balcony, parking or storage details. Do not assume that every apartment receives the same allocation. These items can materially affect value and daily use, so they belong in the written offer and contract package.

For a balcony, check net usable dimensions, not only area. Identify structural columns, planters, railings, drainage, outdoor equipment and privacy relative to neighboring balconies. For parking, confirm the legal and operational form, dimensions, access route, charging provisions and whether the space is conventional or part of a mechanical system. For storage, confirm location, area, height, ventilation and attachment to the apartment's rights.

None of those details should be inferred from another apartment in the project. They are unit-specific.

### A buyer's apartment comparison sheet

Use the same fields for every apartment considered. A simple comparison sheet prevents one salesperson's strongest talking point from dominating the decision.

Record:

1. Building and contractual apartment number.
2. Contractual floor and floor-count convention.
3. Room count and room dimensions.
4. Internal area definition.
5. Balcony area and usable dimensions.
6. Orientation of living room, balcony and bedrooms.
7. Current and planned buildings in each direction.
8. Parking and storage rights.
9. Technical specification and permitted substitutions.

A beautiful apartment can still be the wrong purchase if its cash-flow dates do not suit the buyer, its management costs exceed the expected budget, or its orientation conflicts with daily use. The comparison sheet keeps product, money and timing in the same decision.

Historical figures can help a buyer understand how the project was positioned at specific dates. They should not be used to calculate today's budget without a current offer. ### What a useful written price should contain

A headline price is only the beginning. Ask for a schedule that makes the following explicit:

- Exact apartment and building.
- Internal area and stated measurement basis.
- Balcony, parking and storage included in the offer.
- Contract price in NIS.
- Value-added tax treatment where relevant to the quoted sum.
- Reservation or application payment, if any.
- Payment milestones and dates.
- Any linkage to a construction input index and the contractual formula.
- Legal, registration and processing charges payable by the buyer.
- Upgrade or change-order pricing.
- Conditions attached to a promotion or financing arrangement.
- Offer expiry date.

Do not compare two projects until both quotes are normalized. One may include parking and storage while another lists them separately. One may require a large early payment while another defers more of the consideration. One may expose a larger portion to indexation. The lower headline figure is not always the lower total commitment.

### Purchase tax and buyer status

Purchase tax depends on the buyer's circumstances and the rules in force when the transaction occurs. Residency, ownership of other homes and eligibility for a particular treatment can change the result. Language, nationality or an intention to make Aliyah should never be used as a shortcut for a tax conclusion.

Use the site's [purchase tax calculator](https://nad-lan.co.il/purchase-tax-calculator/) for an initial scenario, then verify the position for the actual buyer and date with an appropriate Israeli professional and the current Tax Authority rules. A calculator is most useful when the input assumptions are explicit. It is not a tax ruling.

Foreign-currency buyers face another variable. The contract price and taxes are normally assessed in NIS, while savings or income may be in dollars, pounds, euros or another currency. A move in the exchange rate can change the home-currency cost even when the NIS price is fixed. Build a buffer rather than relying on one conversion captured on the day of the sales meeting.

### Mortgage and payment timing

A project under construction can create a long financial timeline. The buyer may pay deposits and milestones before receiving the home, while also funding rent or another mortgage. A remote buyer may need additional time for account opening, source-of-funds review, document authentication and lender approval.

The [mortgage calculator](https://nad-lan.co.il/mortgage-calculator/) can help test an initial loan size, monthly payment and interest sensitivity. It cannot promise bank approval or the final rate. Run more than one scenario: the expected loan, a smaller loan funded by more equity, and a stress case with higher finance costs or an exchange-rate movement.

Match the finance plan to the contractual payment dates. A mortgage approval that expires before a major milestone is not a complete solution. Ask when the lender can value an apartment under construction, what documentation it requires, and which payments must come from equity first. For a buyer abroad, the route for transferring funds and demonstrating their source should be planned early.

### Estimating the total commitment

No current official price means this guide cannot publish a reliable ASHIRA total-cost estimate. Once a dated apartment quote is obtained, create a personal acquisition budget. It can include purchase price, purchase tax, legal advice, lender costs, valuation, currency conversion, document authentication, travel, upgrades, furniture, moving and a contingency reserve. Include interim housing costs if the move depends on a future handover.

Ongoing costs also belong in the decision. An amenity-rich four-building project can require significant management and maintenance, but no verified public budget has been published. Ask for the best available estimate, the assumptions behind it and the process for setting charges. Pool heating, spa operation, staffing, security, cleaning, lift maintenance, insurance and reserve funding do not disappear because the purchase price is paid.

### How to compare price without a public price list

Obtain like-for-like written offers. Compare NIS per apartment and, cautiously, the seller's area basis. Then compare building, floor, orientation, outdoor space, parking, storage, specification, payment structure and estimated management cost. A mechanical price-per-square-metre calculation can mislead when area definitions or included attachments differ.

Use recent verified transaction data for area context where available, but do not assume a district average values a specific unbuilt apartment. New-build contracts can include payment structures and specifications that are not reflected in a simple resale comparison. An independent valuation may be useful, especially when the purchase requires substantial leverage.

For investment analysis, avoid inserting an assumed rent merely to produce a yield. ASHIRA has no completed operating history, the district is developing, and the chosen apartment is not identified in public data. A responsible model needs a specific apartment, realistic completion timing, achievable long-term rent evidence, vacancy, management, maintenance, tax, furnishing and financing. Until those inputs exist, yield is unknown.

### Construction changes and buyer selections

Projects can evolve within the applicable approval and contract framework. Buyers should understand what the seller may change, how material changes are communicated, and which apartment selections have deadlines. Late decisions about electrical layouts, kitchens, flooring or plumbing can be costly or unavailable.

Create a written selection calendar. Record the standard specification, every upgrade, its price, the drawing revision and the payment confirmation. Do not rely on a showroom conversation. Before possession, review the apartment with an appropriate professional, compare it with the sale plan and specification, and document defects clearly. Check systems, finishes, openings, drainage, measurements and the attachments to the apartment. Understand which common areas are complete and which may still be under work.

An amenity-rich development may deliver parts of the shared program on a schedule different from the apartment itself. The contract should address the relationship between apartment handover, common-area completion and ongoing construction. The public 2030 target does not answer those apartment-level questions.

## Who it suits

ASHIRA may fit several buyer routes, but the same apartment will not serve each route equally. A household moving to Tel Aviv, an overseas buyer seeking a base, and an investor all examine the project through different cash-flow, timing and use questions. The right starting point is the intended use, not the language spoken by the buyer or the prestige attached to a new district.

### Buyers planning a full-time move to Tel Aviv

The project may suit buyers who want a new home in North Tel Aviv and are comfortable moving into a district that will continue developing. The combination of several building scales, planned open space, resident amenities and connections toward established neighborhoods can be attractive for daily living.

The tradeoff is timing. A full-time household needs more than an apartment. It needs practical routes to work, school, childcare, groceries, healthcare, recreation and support networks. Some needs may be met in established nearby neighborhoods. Other district facilities remain planned. Families should map the first years after handover, not only the district's eventual form.

For a relocation, the contractual date matters more than the public 2030 target. Build a plan for temporary housing and a delayed move. Verify school arrangements close to the relevant year. Decide whether one or more adults can tolerate ongoing construction around the new district. If the answers remain comfortable under a slower scenario, the project may be a reasonable candidate.

### English-speaking and overseas buyers

People comparing new apartments in Tel Aviv often have distinct needs: relocation, a future Aliyah plan, a second home, support for family in Israel, or investment. These routes should not be collapsed into one "foreign buyer" profile.

ASHIRA may interest an overseas buyer who values a new-build process and has time to organize Israeli legal representation, banking, source-of-funds documentation and remote decisions. A buyer who cannot visit frequently should require a stronger evidence trail: dated plans, recorded video walks, written answers, translated explanations where needed, and one controlled repository for all revisions.

Citizenship, residency, land status, tax treatment and mortgage access require individual review. No general statement about English-speaking buyers can answer them. Keep NIS as the authoritative contract currency and treat any dollar or pound conversion as a dated personal budgeting tool.

### Buyers making Aliyah or planning a later relocation

A buyer planning Aliyah may value securing a future Tel Aviv home before the move. The risk is that personal timing and construction timing can diverge. Immigration, employment, school and tax status may all change before completion. A property decision should still work if the move occurs earlier or later than expected.

Consider whether the apartment can be managed while empty, used by family, or rented under a compliant arrangement if the relocation date changes. Ask who will attend inspections, make upgrade decisions, receive formal notices and manage handover. The project may fit when these responsibilities are assigned before signing.

Do not assume a benefit or tax rate based solely on an intention to move. Verify eligibility at the relevant transaction date and again when circumstances change.

### Second-home buyers

The amenity program and North Tel Aviv location may appeal to a buyer seeking a city base rather than a full-time home. A residents lounge, workspace, pool and wellness areas can make intermittent stays more convenient if they are delivered and operate as presented. Ground-floor commerce and future district services could also support a lock-and-leave routine.

Second-home buyers should focus on management. Who checks the apartment during an absence? How are deliveries, repairs and access handled? What are the rules for guests? What fixed monthly costs continue when the home is empty? Does the apartment have the storage and internal layout needed for personal belongings between visits?

The buyer should also avoid paying a premium for a view or amenity based only on imagery. A second home used for short periods can be especially sensitive to operating rules, management quality and construction around the building.

### Families comparing usable space

The project's marketed room range and planned children's amenities may attract families. The lot plan's kindergarten allocation and wider district planning add to that interest. Yet family suitability depends on details not published in a current inventory: bedroom dimensions, storage, parking, stroller routes, lift capacity, balcony safety, acoustic conditions and the timing of actual education services.

Choose through a weekday routine. Trace the path from parking or street to the apartment with shopping, a stroller or sports equipment. Check whether bedrooms can handle the required beds and desks. Understand how children reach open space and which areas are public. Ask how construction zones will be separated during phased work.

The word "family" does not describe one household. A couple with a baby, a household with teenagers, and an intergenerational family need different plans. The apartment should be tested against the real routine.

### Long-horizon investors

ASHIRA may interest investors who want exposure to a large new Tel Aviv district and can accept construction and district-maturity risk. The wider plan has substantial housing, retail, employment, public space and future transport components. That scale can create a long-term urban story.

It can also create competition. Thousands of planned homes across Sde Dov and Eshkol mean future rental and resale supply. The timing of infrastructure, surrounding building work, management charges and the exact apartment's efficiency can all influence demand. A general belief in Tel Aviv is not a unit-level investment case.

Build the analysis around a specific apartment and a conservative holding period. Use verified comparable rents when the delivery date approaches. Deduct vacancy, management, maintenance, tax, furnishing and finance. Stress-test a later handover and slower district completion. Do not publish or rely on a yield until the rent and cost inputs are supported.

An investor who needs immediate income should recognize that ASHIRA is under construction. The capital is committed before the apartment can produce rent. A completed property may better fit that objective.

### Buyers who may prefer another project

ASHIRA may be a poor fit for someone who needs a home soon, requires a guaranteed public-transport opening, wants a mature street with no major construction nearby, or will proceed only if a permanent sea view is certain. It may also be unsuitable when the budget has no room for indexation, currency movement, fit-out, interim housing or future management charges.

A buyer who dislikes large shared amenity systems may prefer a simpler building. Someone who wants a verified low monthly charge should wait for stronger operating-cost information or compare a less service-intensive project. A buyer who needs current unit prices to compare online may find the enquiry-based sales process inefficient.

Walking away is a valid outcome of due diligence. A high-profile district does not make every apartment right for every buyer.

### A practical fit test

Before requesting a reservation, answer these questions in writing:

- What will the apartment be used for during its first five years?
- What is the maximum all-in NIS budget?
- How much timing flexibility is available?
- Which building scale is preferred, and why?
- Is a particular outlook essential or only desirable?
- Which amenities will actually be used?
- What monthly management range can the household absorb?
- Which current services are non-negotiable at handover?
- Who will manage legal, banking and construction decisions locally?
- What fact would cause the buyer to choose another home?

This exercise turns a broad interest in Tel Aviv new developments into a specific purchase brief. It also makes the sales conversation more productive because the buyer can ask for relevant apartments rather than the most heavily promoted option.
