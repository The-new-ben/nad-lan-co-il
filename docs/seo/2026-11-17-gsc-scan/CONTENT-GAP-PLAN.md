## D. The writing standard ("better than everyone", AI-answer ready)

Every item below goes through the same pack. Evidence for the AI-answer parts: Seer Interactive, May 2026, 8,500 US/English keywords. Directional for Hebrew, not proof.
1. **Intent sheet:** 1-5 owned intents with every synonym and inflection (law 9.11), the owner URL, kw_guard and `tools/seo/cannibal_check.py` output pasted.
2. **Manual Google search by the writing session** in Ben's Chrome, with screenshots saved and sent (11.11 law): suggestions, page one, "people also ask", AI answer and its sources (or "absent"). The top 4-5 competitor pages read in full and recorded with retrieval time.
3. **Answer first:** under the H1, 2-3 sentences with the number the searcher wants (a rate, a threshold, an amount), dated, sourced.
4. **A working tool** that computes the reader's own case (an AI answer cannot). Server-rendered labels, works on a 375 px phone, no data stored without consent.
5. **One table of official data** with source and date (Tax Authority, Kol Zchut, gov.il, the land registry, CBS). Outbound links to the official source (Seer: cited pages link about twice as often to .gov sources).
6. **Internal links:** up to the hub with the money word, down to children with their own names, across to the tool pages (Seer: cited definitional pages carry about 69% more internal links).
7. **Schema:** Article + BreadcrumbList (+ the tool's WebApplication where it fits). Seer found FAQ/HowTo-heavy sites underperformed about tenfold in AI Overviews; do not add FAQ blocks for markup's sake. Questions the searcher really asks stay, as normal headings.
8. **Length follows the intent.** Seer: 5,000+ word guides took 4.4% of definitional slots. Ben's flagship standard (5,000+ words with proof of superiority) stays for flagship hubs; for a narrow answer the depth goes into the tool and the table, not the word count.
9. **Proof table** against the top 4-5 (the existing proof-of-superiority format), Claude Design board before any visible change, source audit before and after release, link sent to Ben.
10. **Who writes the body:** per Ben's process (ChatGPT Pro per the 28.9 decision, or Claude as on recent flagships). Ben to confirm per article.

### I3. One side effect to settle: glossary terms renamed into sentences
- In a glossary entry the H1 is the term's name. Renaming it to match a title turns a dictionary term into a headline, and that name is what the /glossary/ index lists and what other blocks print.
- Repo code (`plugins/nadlan-config/inc/glossary-autolink.php`; the live file may differ) links a term from running text only where the **exact term name** appears. A sentence-long name can never match, so a renamed term stops gaining automatic links. Not confirmed live.
- Checked live on 10 professional pages that linked to the two terms on 17.11: 9 still link (the "מונח במילון" block), 1 is a retired page. So no link loss seen so far.
- Proposal: for glossary entries keep the term as the name ("טופס 4", "מפקח בנייה מטעם דיירים") and let the title open with the same term, or move the page's H1 to a separate field in code. Decide before round 2 touches more glossary terms. The glossary has 144 terms.
