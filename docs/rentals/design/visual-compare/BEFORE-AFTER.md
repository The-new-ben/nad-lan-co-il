## What was counted
On every product screen: photographs or renders inside the interface, a map (yes or no), a 3D view (yes or no), whether every item shows its state without a press, what the small recurring marks are (photos, illustrations, filled icons, line icons), the share of the screen that is picture, map or 3D (steps of 10%), and the words on the screen. Counting, not rating.
A helper session collected and counted 45 screens of 17 products on 20.11.2026 (vendors' own pages and store listings; nothing was blocked). This session checked the counts by eye on 7 of the 45 (DoorLoop, Landlord Studio, Arthur, TenantCloud, Airbnb, Matterport, HomeZada): they match.

## What the numbers say
- The rental-management field is a field of tables and line icons: on 25 of 29 screens there is no picture, no map and no 3D at all, and no leader shows a 3D view anywhere on these screens. So "as good as the rental products" is a low bar; that is the standard Ben saw and rejected.
- The products people call beautiful put a picture, a map or a 3D view on about 38% of the screen and use about 43 words where the rental products use about 70.
- The day screen built this week sits with the rental field (0%). Board 5 moves the opening to the level of the best-looking group, and adds what none of the 17 has on one screen: every property of the landlord on a 3D map with its state shown without a press.

## Limits, said plainly
- Vendor screens are staged marketing frames, often with a caption and a phone bezel, so shares and words include those.
- Airbnb's frames are the guest app, Hemlane's are the tenant app, Dira360's come from its demo video; AppFolio and Stessa show almost no real interface.
- Board 5 is a design measured on lab renders, not a built product, and nothing was measured on a real phone. The same table is re-measured on the built product in stage 7.
