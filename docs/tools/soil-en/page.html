As of 15 October 2026, seven soil test reports for plots in the Sde Dov district, the former airfield in north Tel Aviv, have been published in the Ministry of Environmental Protection database. Every one of them measured PFOS above the direct-contact threshold the reports use for Sde Dov, in 60 of the 101 boreholes drilled. The highest value was on plot 303: 598 times the threshold, at a depth of half a metre. The map below shows every published plot and borehole, the stage each plot is at, and the report itself.

What has been published, plot by plot

The reports were written by environmental consultants under "Even Derech", the national programme for treating state-owned land, and published in the freedom-of-information database of the Ministry of Environmental Protection. Each report lists the boreholes on the plot, the depths tested and the value of every compound. The number worth looking at is the highest PFOS value against the threshold:

  • Plot 303, retail and employment: 598 times the threshold, at half a metre. Above the threshold in all 16 boreholes.
  • Plot 301, retail, employment and tourism: 94 times, at one metre. Above the threshold in 22 of 23 boreholes.
  • Plot 2270, residential and employment: 25 times, at two metres. Above the threshold in 3 of 10 boreholes.
  • Plot 2206, residential and employment: 20 times, at 10 metres. Above the threshold in 13 of 26 boreholes.
  • Plot 2255, residential and employment: 16 times, at 10 metres. Above the threshold in 4 of 12 boreholes.
  • Plot 2701, public open space: 5.6 times, at 8 metres. Above the threshold in one borehole of 4.
  • Plot 822, a future road in the north of the district: 1.2 times, at two metres. Above the threshold in one borehole of 10.

On plot 303, four boreholes were drilled in an area marked in advance for testing and twelve outside it, and every one of them measured a value above the threshold. The developer said that the cost of treatment, if it is needed, is negligible against the cost of the project, and that the project will be marketed meeting every required standard.

In July 2026 a list was published of nine plots where findings were reported: 103, 105, 106, 107, 301, 303, 2102, 2206 and 2207. For some of them no report has been published in the database yet. On two of them the developers said that no exceedances were found and that the Ministry approved, and on one more the developer said that more than 98% of the samples were within the values and that the soil where exceedances were found has been fully removed. On the map, each plot is marked by what has been published about it.

Where the compounds came from

PFAS are a family of very persistent compounds that were part of firefighting foam for decades. Sde Dov operated as an airfield from 1938 until July 2019, and the compounds are believed to be linked to the use of such foam at the airfield and at the nearby fire station. Between 2020 and 2023 the soil was investigated and treated for fuels and metals, and according to the Ministry of Environmental Protection announcement of February 2026, PFAS were not tested at the time. In January 2026 a report was submitted to the Ministry defining 12 areas for testing, among them the runway and the aircraft parking aprons. In February the Ministry, together with the Israel Land Authority, published the preliminary findings and the guidelines for plot owners.

Buying in the district from abroad: what to check

The published tests were carried out on plots before construction, and soil treatment, where it is required, is done before construction or during excavation. Under Tel Aviv municipality guidelines, a plot the municipality marks as requiring a soil survey needs the approval of the Ministry of Environmental Protection before a building permit is issued, and in February 2026 the Ministry instructed the municipality to anchor the PFAS guidelines in permits. These are the questions to put to the developer, in writing:

  • Was the plot tested under the Ministry guidelines of 12 February 2026, and when?
  • Did the Ministry adopt the findings? If treatment is required, was the treatment plan approved?
  • Was the report published, and if not, can you receive it?
  • Could the treatment delay the delivery date? Israel's Sale (Apartments) Law sets compensation for late delivery, so the delivery date in the contract matters.
  • What do the contract and the sale specification say about the soil? Have an Israeli real estate lawyer go over those clauses: buying property in Israel from abroad and the sale specification of a new apartment.

When you buy from abroad, keep every answer in writing: a written answer is what your lawyer can work with. How to read a soil test report

A report gives its values in nanograms per kilogram of soil, and sometimes in micrograms or milligrams per kilogram: a thousand nanograms make one microgram, and a million make one milligram. The direct-contact threshold is the level above which the soil requires attention. In the Sde Dov reports it is 6,321.4 ng/kg for PFOS, 1,896.4 for PFOA and 39,824.6 for PFHxS. Each depth is measured separately, so the same borehole can show a high value at half a metre and a low one at three metres. Private landowners in the Large Block

The testing has also held up the allocation of rights to the private landowners in the district. In May 2026 the lottery was postponed to August, and on 30 August the Herzliya Magistrate's Court stopped it and required the Israel Land Authority to file an update by 6 October. As of mid-October it has not been published whether the update was filed. Holders of rights in the Large Block who are thinking of selling can follow apartment and land prices in the district in the Sde Dov price guide.

The published tests were carried out on empty plots before construction. They do not deal with existing buildings.
