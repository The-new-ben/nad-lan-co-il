# Decision Log

## D-01 — One familiar portal system, not a novel visual experiment

**Decision:** Use familiar portal conventions from Zillow/Yad2/Madlan/Rightmove and mature off-plan portals, then differentiate through Israeli project depth, source truth, 3D and foreign-buyer readiness.

**Reason:** The immediate business job is trust. A visitor should understand the product within seconds without reading an explanation of the concept.

## D-02 — Image-rich, but never image-faked

**Decision:** Real or approved project media is the primary visual layer. Generated concept media is allowed only in internal review or as a visibly labelled fallback. Sketches and blueprints move to secondary editorial/3D explanation contexts.

**Reason:** Image scarcity makes the portal look unfinished; deceptive imagery destroys investor and buyer trust.

## D-03 — Premium comes from curation and consistency, not emptiness

**Decision:** Increase useful inventory density while keeping disciplined spacing, typography, crop ratios and badge limits.

**Reason:** The portal must feel active and established, while avoiding the noisy advertising density of weaker regional examples.

## D-04 — The previous “never a marketplace/listings board” rule is superseded

**Decision:** The owner’s 2026-07-21 direction explicitly replaces that earlier positioning. NadLan should use a marketplace/portal discovery grammar while retaining its editorial and evidence advantages.

**Reason:** The earlier sparse editorial model conflicts with the owner’s presentation goal and with the dominant trust patterns in the benchmark corpus.

## D-05 — Five review views, one canonical visual language

**Decision:** Show homepage, list/map catalog, project page, foreign-investor journey and developer-completion journey. Do not present five unrelated visual moods.

**Reason:** Stakeholders need to judge whether the product system works end-to-end, not vote on superficial styles.

## D-06 — WordPress remains the single source of truth

**Decision:** Use the existing block theme, CPTs, taxonomies, meta, revisions, Studio and REST surfaces. Do not create a detached static inventory or a second editorial database.

**Reason:** The owner requires a by-the-book WordPress operating model that remains manageable after implementation.

## D-07 — No unsupported portal counts or lead promises

**Decision:** Counts must be generated from published, quality-approved records. Price and lead claims require approved evidence. Until then use honest language such as “request current price” and “availability requires confirmation.”

**Reason:** Fabricated scale may create a short-term impression but makes the product less credible under diligence.

## D-08 — Cards expose evidence, not just marketing copy

**Decision:** Every card uses a stable order: visual, project/developer, location, price policy, unit/status facts, limited proof badges, freshness/source and actions.

**Reason:** This is the strongest repeated pattern across mature portals and off-plan products.

## D-09 — Badges are evidence-backed state

**Decision:** Limit the visible badge family and define a field/evidence requirement for every badge. “Verified,” “3D,” “Floor plans,” “Foreign-buyer ready” and “Sponsored” cannot be manually decorated without proof.

**Reason:** Uncontrolled badges become noise and can misrepresent status.

## D-10 — Project pages behave as decision mini-sites

**Decision:** A serious project page contains media, structured facts, availability language, floorplans/documents, amenities, map/context, team, costs/process, freshness and a named contact.

**Reason:** This is where developers and investors will judge whether NadLan is a complete product.

## D-11 — Foreign readiness is operational, not a translated headline

**Decision:** HE/EN parity, currency/unit controls, remote-viewing workflow, total-cost/process education, timezone-aware contact and source/legal caveats are required.

**Reason:** Global portals and Israel-focused foreign-buyer specialists show that translation alone is insufficient.

## D-12 — Exact 3D unit selection requires exact data

**Decision:** The current prototype can demonstrate authored targets and approximate selection. “Click any window” requires official segmented geometry or unit IDs from BIM/GLB/IFC/facade mapping.

**Reason:** The existing repository source notes and QA explicitly establish this technical boundary.

## D-13 — Presentation happens only after a launch cohort passes a gate

**Decision:** Do not promote an empty or demo-heavy portal. Assemble a minimum cohort of complete, approved projects before external outreach.

**Reason:** An active storefront is itself the proof that the developer offer works.

## D-14 — This branch is documentation only

**Decision:** No WordPress, plugin, theme, template, live content, credential, deploy or merge action occurs without a separate explicit owner approval.

**Reason:** Direct owner instruction on 2026-07-21.
